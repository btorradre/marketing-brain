# AI Voiceover VSL B-roll

Explain **why each scene belongs at this line**, then source or generate assets that fulfill that purpose. Use the observed reference as evidence of a visual approach, not as a universal template or proof of conversion.

## Let narration and the visual premise drive the ad

AI voice describes audio production, not a visual style. Do not default to animation or claim a reference uses AI audio based on its look. Determine whether the target is faceless, has occasional presenter returns, follows one protagonist or uses a sequence of examples. If it is fully animated, also apply the animated skill; if a recurring speaker anchors most of the argument, apply the presenter skill.

Choose the coverage structure from the concept:

| Structure | Main visual engine | Why it works for this kind of line |
|---|---|---|
| Native footage montage | Realistic actions and settings, punctuated by diagrams/product | Good when lived experience, attempts and outcomes need concrete examples. |
| Physical demonstration | Hands, real object/model and a readable change | Good when position, fit, material or use can be inspected directly. A continuous demonstration can progress without constant cuts. |
| Narrated protagonist | The same identifiable person or character acts through the story | Good when cause and consequence belong to one experience; unrelated people must not be presented as the same documented case. |
| Animated explanation | Coherent generated world and process views | Good when invisible or imagined actions drive the concept; preserve its animation style across the story. |

## Map the argument, then the imagery

For each line identify whether the viewer needs recognition, a contrast, a cause, a demonstration, a result or an instruction. The next picture should add that step. A noun match alone produces disconnected stock footage.

- Use a lived action for an experience: a dog shaking beside a sleeping owner shows the cost of the disturbance; it is more specific than another generic dog portrait.
- Use a physical demonstration when the cause is visible: show a gap, contact, pressure, fit or movement, then the feature that changes it. The visible demonstration is illustrative, not clinical validation.
- Use an analogy only when its relationship is clear. Map its elements to the actual idea and return to the relevant subject before the metaphor becomes a separate story.
- Use scientific animation for an invisible process only when needed. Give each step a distinct view and observable change; do not fill explanatory lines with unrelated cellular loops.
- Use a photo when a static detail is the evidence or identification task. Give it enough readable time; unnecessary motion can reduce comprehension.
- Use an accurate product action for routine and a specific regained activity for payoff. The payoff should answer the problem established earlier.

## Time coverage to speech and visible action

Save provisional durations during planning. Once the selected narration exists, align scene entries to meaning-bearing phrases and let necessary actions complete. Preserve approved voice speed and caption alignment during visual revisions. Do not invent universal cut intervals, regenerate voice to fit weak footage or mistake a camera pan for a cut.

When a single demonstrated setup carries several lines, plan the changing actions and points of attention within it. Do not manufacture separate “new B-roll” by recropping the same clip. For separate inserts, satisfy the workspace’s distinct-scene requirement. Use quiet holds when they aid inspection; use faster changes when the argument genuinely changes examples or states.

## Source/generate and inspect

Write searches and generation briefs around a visible verb, subject, setting and framing. Example original target: close view of hands seating the actual insert inside the approved bag, then a different overhead packing action demonstrating accessible compartments. Why: the first action establishes fit; the second makes the use benefit inspectable. Product fidelity and real geometry govern selection.

For each selected clip record usable action start/end, native audio handling, source or provider, identity continuity and what would make it fail the line. Use GPT Image 2/Google Omni under the shared workflow when generation is needed, not because the narrator is synthetic. Review the actual sequence with final voice before claiming synchronization. Reject random pretty footage, misleading before/after continuity, premature result reveals and visuals that force the listener to imagine the missing causal step.

## Work from this concept, not its label alone

Read the shared [B-roll storytelling skill](../broll-storytelling/SKILL.md) for reference inspection, per-scene reasoning, asset provenance and production handoff. Read [ad editing style](../ad-editing-style/SKILL.md) when analyzing or specifying cuts, transitions, zooms, captions and sound. Use one shared editing plan rather than conflicting plans.

Inspect the actual reference media and narration. Record shot boundaries, visual actions and changes of medium; inspect consecutive source frames around mapped boundaries. A thumbnail, transcript or automated watch manifest cannot establish a frame audit. Separate observed picture, inferred intention and proposed target choice. State the reviewed spans and audio limitations honestly. Read [reference examples](references/reference-examples.md) for this skill’s empirical basis and limits.

For every beat and intentional hold, deliver: exact approved line; visible action; medium; full-frame/inset/split/hold choice; entry and exit cue; provisional duration; incoming/outgoing transition; source or generation brief; asset gap; **Why this scene, here**. Explain the line’s job, what the picture adds, why it suits this concept, and why it enters/exits there. “Adds engagement” is not a reason. A still can be the correct asset when its static detail is the information; use video when change over time matters.

## Production and selection contract

Save/update the concept’s `edit/editing-plan.md` before generating ad assets, voice or a timeline. Include the hook, mechanism/product order, captions/audio direction, CTA/end hold, asset gaps and delivery QA; timing remains provisional until final narration alignment. Planning adds no new approval gate. Research/skill creation alone does not authorize producing an unrelated ad.

When sourcing is requested, search for the specific subject + action + setting + framing, inspect the actual candidate media, select usable in/out points and save its URL, creator/license status and local asset ID. TikTok is a discovery source, not automatic permission for commercial reuse. If the right permitted footage cannot be found, generate an illustrative scene within the target’s authorization rather than silently accepting a weak match. Keep found, generated, illustrative and verified evidence assets distinguishable.

Use GPT Image 2 for first frames/stills and Google Omni for video in this workspace; check the available provider capabilities before promising duration or format. Use approved identity/product references and inspect selected outputs. DaVinci Resolve is the editor; verify the live connection and preserve existing work. HyperFrames and Remotion are banned. A storyboard delivery follows Cut Room’s skill and includes the actual selected first asset set and working board URL. Follow the shared skill and workspace rules for these operations.

Audit the whole sequence for visual variety: each separate B-roll beat needs a distinct composition, subject detail and action. A crop, mirror or recolor is not a new scene. Consistent presenter coverage and a deliberately held demonstration are not excuses to label repeated inserts as fresh B-roll. On visual-only revisions preserve approved narration speed, word alignment and captions.

Do not turn dramatized mechanisms, unrelated footage or generated people into purported scientific evidence, real customer outcomes or verified professional endorsements. Use supported target claims; the reference’s reach does not validate its claims or prove that its visual choices caused performance.
