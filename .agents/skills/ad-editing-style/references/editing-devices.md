# Editing devices and diagnosis

Use this reference to distinguish changes that can look similar in a finished video. Name the observed behavior first; choose an implementation only when adapting it.

| Device | Diagnose and record | Explain its role at this cue |
|---|---|---|
| Direct cut | Consecutive-frame picture replacement, no visible overlap; note continuing audio/text | New referent, action stage, scale, response or contrast |
| Jump cut / punch cut | Same setup with discontinuous pose/time/framing; alternate camera origin may be unknown | Compress a pause or emphasize a thought; check for clipped speech or awkward gesture |
| Match cut / cut on action | Matching shape, screen position, direction or continued action across sources | Preserve orientation or connect ideas; a similar color alone may not establish deliberate matching |
| J / L audio cut | Incoming scene audio leads its picture / outgoing scene audio continues after its picture | Prepare the next scene or carry emotion/context across it; continuous voiceover over B-roll is not automatically a J/L cut |
| Dissolve / visible overlap | Both pictures coexist over consecutive frames; record onset, dominance change and completion | Connect related states, time or scale where the overlap supports comprehension |
| Fade through black / white | Outgoing dims/brightens, intermediary state, incoming emerges; black/white may be brief | Mark a chapter or experiential change; distinguish from a one-frame flash or exposure artifact |
| Flash / light leak | Temporary bright/color wash; inspect whether picture and captions are affected together | Accentuate a specific handoff; do not assume a camera flash was captured |
| Whip / directional blur | Lateral/vertical smear and handoff direction, settling frames, possible scene displacement | Carry momentum or mark a quick shift; preset and captured pan may be unresolved |
| Radial zoom transition | Radial streaking/scaling around a center across a picture handoff | Travel between scales or emphasize entry; distinguish from an ordinary within-shot push |
| Wipe / occlusion reveal | Moving boundary or foreground object reveals another image | Maintain a spatial relationship, reveal or conceal; identify mask edge and direction |
| Digital punch-in/out | Abrupt tighter/wider framing, relatively fixed perspective if same source | Isolate a word/reaction/detail; hold long enough to read it |
| Continuous push / pull | Gradual scale/framing change, anchor and trajectory; perspective change may indicate physical/virtual travel | Direct attention, reveal context or follow an unfolding explanation |
| Pan / tilt / tracked crop | Frame travels across subject/document or follows action | Reveal a specific detail at its narration cue; avoid cropping evidence before it can be read |
| Subject motion / morph | Image state or subject changes within continuing scene | Complete the demonstrated process; not necessarily a post-production transition or speed effect |
| Freeze / slow / fast / ramp | Repeated frames or apparent rate change; inspect intermediate frames/audio and original if available | Make a brief moment readable or compress known time; absolute speed factor requires source evidence |
| Inset / split / keyed presenter | Layer geometry, crop/mask, border, entry/exit and underlying picture continuity | Annotate without losing speaker, compare, or guide; avoid face/caption/product collisions |
| Arrow / highlight / diagram | Appearance cue, target position, hold duration and removal | Direct attention to a named element; do not create unsupported proof by highlighting fabricated data |
| Caption / emphasis label | Exact displayed words, wrapping, placement, replacements, active-word behavior, box/outline/shadow | Support comprehension and emphasize the actual phrase; caption timing is independent of shot timing |
| Grade / texture change | Changes in color, luma, contrast, grain or blur, and affected layers | Mark mood/state or unify sources; identify an observed shift without inventing a LUT |
| Music / SFX / silence | Audible entry/exit, rhythmic relationship, transient and speech overlap | Punctuate an action or chapter, build/release tension, or give a line space; no SFX assumed from visuals |

## Measuring without false precision

- Use source pixels or normalized image coordinates for observed framing/placement and label the reference resolution. A finished image can establish how large a face appears, not necessarily an editor's scale value.
- Compare stationary landmarks to infer a crop change; perspective/parallax can suggest camera travel. If movement is ambiguous, say “apparent push toward the subject; capture versus post-production unverified.”
- Record a transition's total affected span separately from clip overlap and editorial handoff. They can differ. Describe perceived acceleration/deceleration without inventing an exact easing curve.
- Separate picture treatment from caption/graphic treatment. Text staying crisp through a blurred picture transition implies separately treated layers but does not reveal the source application or effect stack.
- Sound analysis can measure the finished track's level and peaks. It does not reveal the original fader settings, music stem level or sidechain threshold. Do not prescribe reference-exact dB settings without that evidence.

## Pacing is more than cutting speed

Analyze shot duration, inset duration, caption density, internal action, narration rate/pauses, music and change in explanatory scale together. A held moving demonstration can be more active than several static cuts. A very brief symptom insert can communicate recognition but would be too short for reading a document.

Mark acceleration and release by story section, supported by intervals. Explain why the local pace changes. For a target edit, assign the time each action or piece of information needs, then align to final speech. Avoid treating every noun as a cut cue or every section change as a reason for a flashy transition.
