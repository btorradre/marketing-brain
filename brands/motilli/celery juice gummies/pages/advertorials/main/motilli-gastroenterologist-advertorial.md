# A Gastroenterologist Explains Why Every Remedy Her GLP-1 Patients Try Fails — And What She Finally Tells Them Instead

*Millions of GLP-1 users are treating their sulfur burps, bloating, and constipation from the wrong end of their digestive system. One GI doctor watched it happen to her own mother before she intervened.*

**By Dr. Megan Walsh, MD — Board-Certified Gastroenterologist | Updated April 2026**

---

I've been a practicing gastroenterologist for fourteen years. I run a GI motility clinic. I see the inside of people's stomachs for a living.

And I couldn't help my own mother.

Not at first.

She started Mounjaro last March after her endocrinologist suggested it. She'd been fighting the same twenty-five pounds since menopause. Within six weeks she'd lost eleven pounds and told me — with genuine wonder in her voice — that she'd gone an entire Saturday without thinking about food.

By week ten she called me and said, "Megan, I need to ask you something and I need you to answer as my doctor, not my daughter."

She described sulfur burps so constant she'd stopped eating lunch with her friends. Bloating that made her look pregnant by 4 PM. And constipation — six days without a bowel movement, then an entire morning in the bathroom.

I knew exactly what was happening. I'd been watching the same pattern in my clinic for two years.

**Here's what I told her — and what I now tell every GLP-1 patient who sits across from me.**

## Your Medication Is Working Exactly as Designed. That's the Problem.

Semaglutide and tirzepatide — the active compounds in Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, Zepbound — work by slowing **gastric motility**. They tell the muscles in your stomach wall to contract less frequently. Food stays in the stomach longer. You feel full. You eat less. You lose weight.

This isn't a side effect. It's the primary mechanism. The drug is doing exactly what it was engineered to do.

But your stomach is now a slow-moving system. And slow-moving systems have consequences.

**When food sits in a slowed stomach, bacteria begin to ferment it.** This fermentation produces hydrogen sulfide gas — the same compound that gives rotten eggs their smell. That gas rises. You burp it. You can taste it. You can smell it. Everyone around you can smell it.

The fermentation also creates pressure. Gas expands. Your stomach distends. That's the bloating — the feeling of being inflated from the inside, the reason you change into loose pants every evening, the reason you stopped tucking in your shirt.

And because the stomach muscles aren't contracting with normal force, **nothing moves downstream efficiently.** Contents that should transit through your system in 4-6 hours now take 12, 18, sometimes 24 or more. That's the constipation. Not because your colon isn't working — but because your stomach isn't sending anything down to it.

One system. Three symptoms. One root cause.

## Why I Watch My Patients Spend Hundreds on Remedies That Can't Work

This is the part that used to frustrate me. Now it just makes me sad.

By the time most GLP-1 patients sit in my office, they've already built what I privately call **"the cabinet."** It's the collection of things they've tried:

- Gas-X or simethicone (designed to break up gas bubbles in the **intestine**)
- Charcoal capsules (absorbs gas in the **lower GI tract**)
- Miralax (osmotic laxative — pulls water into the **colon**)
- Dulcolax (stimulates contractions in the **colon wall**)
- Fiber supplements (adds bulk in the **colon**)
- Peppermint capsules (relaxes smooth muscle in the **intestine**)
- Probiotics (colonize the **large intestine**)
- Ginger root (mild effect on **intestinal** motility)

Do you see the pattern?

**Every single one of these works downstream — in the intestine or colon.** They're designed for a digestive system that's moving at normal speed but has a problem at the end of the line.

Your problem isn't at the end of the line. **Your problem is at the beginning.** Your stomach has slowed down. The food is fermenting before it even reaches your intestine. You're standing at the wrong end of a clogged pipe with a plunger.

I've watched patients spend $80, $120, $200 a month layering supplements that pharmacologically cannot address the issue. Not because the supplements are bad products. Because they were designed for a different problem.

One of my patients — a woman named Catherine, sixty-one, retired teacher, on Wegovy for eight months — brought in a plastic bag to her appointment with eleven different products she was taking daily for her GI symptoms. I laid them out on my exam table and explained to her that not one of them was designed to work where her problem was actually occurring.

She looked at me and said: *"Then why did nobody tell me that?"*

I didn't have a good answer.

## What Actually Needs to Happen

Once you understand that the issue originates in the stomach — not the colon — the solution criteria become very specific:

**1. The stomach muscles need to be encouraged to contract again.**

Not a laxative. Not a stimulant that pushes from below. A **prokinetic** — something that supports the stomach's own muscular contractions. The same contractions that GLP-1 medications are designed to suppress.

The compound with the most promising natural prokinetic profile is **apigenin** — a flavonoid found in concentrated levels in celery. Apigenin supports gastric motility through interaction with smooth muscle cells in the stomach wall. It doesn't override the medication. It doesn't interfere with the appetite-suppressing mechanism. It gently supports the muscular activity that keeps food moving.

**2. The sulfur gas needs to be neutralized at the source.**

Not absorbed after it's already been produced and distributed through the intestine (that's what charcoal and Gas-X attempt). **Chlorophyll** — the compound that makes plants green — binds to hydrogen sulfide molecules and neutralizes them. In the stomach. Before they rise as burps. Before they push down as bloating pressure.

**3. The nutritional gap needs to be addressed.**

When your stomach is processing food at half speed, you're absorbing nutrients at half efficiency. I run labs on my GLP-1 patients routinely. The pattern is consistent: **folate trending low. B6 trending low. Vitamins A and K below optimal.** These aren't dramatic deficiencies that show up as acute symptoms. They're slow erosions — the kind that show up months later as thinning hair, brittle nails, afternoon fatigue, brain fog.

These three criteria — **motility support, sulfur neutralization, nutritional replacement** — are what I now consider the minimum standard for GLP-1 digestive support.
