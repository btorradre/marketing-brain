## Primary Avatar: "The GLP-1 Survivor"

### One-Line Summary
A 50–70 year-old woman who found her "miracle" weight loss drug and is now paying for it with her digestive dignity — enduring in silence, terrified to complain, terrified to stop.

### Demographics at a Glance
| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Age | 50–70 (peak 45–55) |
| Gender | Female (80% of non-diabetic GLP-1 users) |
| Income | $75K–$150K+ household |
| Life Stage | Peri/post-menopausal, Gen X / Boomer |
| Medication | Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, or Zepbound |
| Diet History | 40+ years — every major diet trend |

### The Core Psychological Profile: "Secrecy Fueled by Fear"

**1. Addiction to "The Silence" (Food Noise)**
She's lived with constant mental chatter about food for 40 years. GLP-1 silences this for the first time ever. She describes it as "life-changing" and "peaceful." She will tolerate extreme physical discomfort (vomiting, nausea) to maintain this mental peace. The fear of the noise returning is more powerful than the fear of side effects.

**2. The Secrecy and Shame Spiral**
Despite medical validation, she harbors deep shame. She hides severity of side effects from her prescribing physician out of fear they'll discontinue the medication. She may hide nausea from her spouse to avoid judgment. She suffers in the bathroom in silence.

**3. The Negotiation with Herself**
Every week she performs a cost-benefit analysis:
- Cost: Nausea, constipation, sulfur burps, social shame, malnutrition
- Benefit: Weight loss, silence of food noise, compliments, fitting into clothes
- She consistently chooses to pay the cost — but RESENTS it

**4. The Hierarchy of Pain**
1. **Constipation** — the most physically debilitating (10/10 pain, ER visits, enemas)
2. **Sulfur Burps** — the most socially devastating ("clear a room," "rotten eggs")
3. **Nausea** — the most persistent (daily, low-grade misery)
4. **Bloating** — the most identity-threatening ("I look 6 months pregnant" — defeats the purpose of weight loss)

## Secondary Avatar: The Diarrhea Subavatar

### Profile
Women on GLP-1s experiencing recurring diarrhea — often alternating with constipation ("the pendulum"). Equally debilitating but less discussed publicly.

### Key Differences
- Fear is about **losing control** (accidents in public) vs. inability to go
- Social shame is MORE acute — diarrhea can happen publicly, constipation is private
- Has tried: Imodium, BRAT diet, shot timing changes, probiotics
- Often told "it means it's working" by doctors
- May experience "fecal urgency" — the sudden, uncontrollable need

### Awareness Breakdown
- **Solution-Aware: 45%** ← largest actionable segment
- Problem-Aware: 30%
- Product-Aware: 15%
- Unaware: 5%
- Most Aware: 5%

### Key VoC Quotes
> "The diarrhea comes out of nowhere — I can't trust my body anymore."

> "I have to know where every bathroom is before I leave the house."

> "My doctor said it means the medication is working. That's not an answer."

### Positioning Opportunity
**No product currently targets recurring GLP-1 diarrhea as its primary value proposition.** This is completely unoccupied territory in the market.

## Avatar Decision-Making Model

### What Makes Her Buy
1. **Mechanism explanation** — she needs to understand WHY this works when everything else failed
2. **Peer testimonials with specifics** — "went from 5 episodes a day to 1"
3. **Healthcare provider endorsement** — "my gastroenterologist recommended this"
4. **One-problem focus** — she trusts specialists over generalists
5. **Ingredient transparency** — she reads labels, researches compounds

### What Makes Her Scroll Past
1. Generic "digestive support" language — too vague, she's heard it
2. Celebrity endorsements — signals marketing, not medicine
3. "All-in-one" positioning — too good to be true
4. Clinical jargon — sounds like the doctors who dismissed her
5. Attacking her medication — she LOVES her shot, don't touch it

### The Three Beliefs She Must Hold Before Purchase
1. "My problem is upstream (stomach), not downstream (colon) — that's why laxatives failed."
2. "Celery has specific prokinetic compounds that can gently wake up my stomach."
3. "This product is designed specifically for women on GLP-1s, not a repurposed fiber supplement."
