Do probiotic yogurts help with constipation? Here's my hot take as an obesity doctor. GLP-1s slow how fast food moves through your digestive system. It's not just your stomach. It's the stomach, the small intestine, and the colon. If you're having constipation, fiber and water are the first-line treatments. The recommended amount of fiber is twenty-five to thirty-five grams a day, ideally from food that you eat. Increase it gradually because increasing fiber too fast on a slow gut can make bloating and gas worse. Probiotics like yogurt and kefir often have an added bacterial strain called Bifidobacterium lactis. It has modest evidence for improving transit time. It's not dramatic, but it's not nothing either. If fiber and water alone aren't cutting it, and for many patients they aren't, the next moves are magnesium oxide or citrate, osmotic laxatives like polyethylene glycol, or fiber supplements.
