## PART 1: PRODUCT IMAGE CAROUSEL CRO AUDIT

You have 8 images in the carousel. I'm analyzing each for conversion impact, sequencing logic, and what's missing vs. what the top competitors are doing.

---

### Image 1 — Product Hero Shot

**What it does right:** Clean product photography on a split cream/green background. Benefit callouts on the right (Natural Detoxification, 5g Prebiotic Fiber Per Serving, Daily Green Boost) give the shopper three fast reasons to care. The jar is the visual anchor. Heart-shaped gummies scattered around it add tactile appeal.

**What's wrong:**

The three benefit callouts are *feature statements*, not *outcome statements*. "5g Prebiotic Fiber Per Serving" tells me what's in the gummy — it doesn't tell me what happens to my body. Compare this to Alevia, which leads with "Support your body from the inside out" — outcome-first, ingredient-second.

"Daily Green Boost" is vague and meaningless to someone scrolling Amazon or a product page. Boost to what? Energy? Digestion? Skin? It doesn't earn the click.

**Fix:** Replace the three right-side callouts with outcome-driven language: "Flattens Bloating Fast" / "Feeds Your Good Gut Bacteria" / "Detoxes Without the Juice Hassle." Keep the icons — just rewrite the text beneath them.

---

### Image 2 — "Go green. Feel clean. Every day."

**What it does right:** Strong brand tagline. The subline ("From 5 grams of fiber to full-body detox support, here's what makes Motilli different") sets up differentiation. Clean Label Project Certified badge and Vegan badge in the bottom-right corner add trust.

**What's wrong:**

The three benefit bullets are unfinished and unclear. "Stops Bloating & Supports" — supports what? This looks like a design error where the copy got cut off. That's a conversion killer. It signals sloppiness to anyone who actually reads.

"Restores Your Daily green boost" has a casing mismatch ("Daily" capitalized, "green boost" lowercase) and the phrase still doesn't mean anything concrete.

**Fix:** Complete the copy. "Stops Bloating & Supports Healthy Digestion." "Restores Your Daily Energy & Regularity." Proof every image for truncation errors before publishing. This one image alone could be costing you perceived credibility.

---

### Image 3 — Testimonial (Sarah K., 42)

**What it does right:** This is the strongest image in the carousel. Real person holding the product. Star rating + customer count at top ("Rated 4.9/5 | Trusted by 30,000+ Happy Customers"). The quote is specific and emotionally resonant: "The only gummy that actually gave me my mornings back." The supporting copy hits multiple outcomes (bloating gone, feels lighter, skin clearer, feels like herself again).

**What's wrong:**

The headline quote is underlined on "gave me" and "my mornings back" — which gives it a handwritten/editorial feel. Good design choice, but the testimonial text below uses a single quotation mark to open ('After just a week...) and a different style to close. Inconsistent punctuation is a small but noticeable trust signal issue.

The image doesn't include a product shot. Alevia pairs every testimonial with a visible product image or bottle — this reinforces what they're endorsing. Without the jar visible, the testimonial exists in a vacuum.

**Fix:** Add a small product jar in the corner of the testimonial image. Fix quotation consistency. This image should be image 2 or 3 in the carousel — it's currently well-placed.

---

### Image 4 — Ingredients Wheel

**What it does right:** Visually clean. Four ingredients in a circular layout with real-food photography (celery stalks, green liquid chlorophyll, green apple, white fiber powder). Parenthetical benefit descriptors under each ingredient (Natural Detoxification, Deep Green Cleanse, Natural Flavor, 5g Digestive Support).

**What's wrong:**

"Deep Green Cleanse" for chlorophyll is marketing language that doesn't mean anything specific. It sounds like a juice cleanse brand, not a digestive support mechanism. A skeptical shopper reads this and thinks "buzzword."

"Natural Flavor" under Green Apple is a wasted slot. Every competitor uses this space to communicate a *benefit*, not a product attribute. You have four ingredient slots and you're burning one on "it tastes good."

There's no mechanism education here. Alevia explains *how* amla supports mitochondrial function. Motilli just lists ingredients with vague descriptors. This is a missed belief-shift opportunity.

**Fix:** Replace "Natural Flavor" with "Tastes Like Candy, Works Like Medicine" or simply remove Green Apple from the wheel and replace it with a fourth functional ingredient or benefit. Under Chlorophyll, write "Neutralizes Sulfur Gas & Odor." Under Prebiotic Fiber, write "Feeds Beneficial Gut Bacteria." Make every slot do conversion work.

---

### Image 5 — "Why Choose Motilli?"

**What it does right:** Four trust badges (Clinically Tested Actives, Manufactured in the USA, Clean Label Project Certified, Vegan & Non-GMO). Six check-mark bullet points below. Green background with natural food border imagery.

**What's wrong:**

This is a pure trust-signal image with zero emotional pull. Every bullet is a feature/attribute: "No Artificial Colors," "No Synthetic Flavors," "No Gelatin," "5g Fiber Per Serving," "Real Celery Juice Extract," "Green Apple Flavor." None of these answer *why I should choose Motilli over doing nothing.* They only matter to someone who's already decided to buy a celery gummy and is comparing labels.

Alevia's trust section includes *outcome-based comparisons* ("Guaranteed Clinical Potency" / "Cellular Power Building vs. Symptom Masking"). Motilli's trust section reads like a supplement facts label in image form.

**Fix:** Split this into two images. Image A: Trust badges with a "clean ingredients" angle (keep the four badges). Image B: "Why Motilli Works When Others Don't" — reframe the bullets as comparative advantages tied to outcomes, not inputs.

---

### Image 6 — Comparison Chart (Motilli vs. Other Brands)

**What it does right:** Classic comparison table format. Five rows with checkmarks for Motilli and X's for competitors. Categories: Clinically Tested Actives, Clean Label Project Certified, Real Celery Juice (Not Powder Filler), 5g Prebiotic Fiber Per Serving, No Artificial Colors or Flavors.

**What's wrong:**

This comparison only works if the shopper already knows what these categories mean and why they matter. "Real Celery Juice — Not Powder Filler" is the only row that creates a real competitive wedge. The rest are certifications and specs that don't trigger an emotional response.

Alevia's comparison table includes lines like "100% Active Ingredient Concentration" and "Cellular Power Building vs. Symptom Masking" — these create a clear "us vs. them" narrative. Motilli's comparison reads like a spec sheet.

The generic white bottle representing "Other Brands" is fine visually, but there's no named competitor or recognizable silhouette. It looks like you're comparing yourself to nothing.

**Fix:** Add a row for the outcome: "Stops Bloating in 48 Hours" (checkmark) vs. (X). Add a row for "90-Day Money-Back Guarantee" (checkmark) vs. (X). Lead with the rows that create the strongest emotional contrast, not the rows that read like a compliance checklist.

---

### Image 7 — Supplement Facts Panel

**What it does right:** Standard supplement facts layout. Shows serving size (2 Vegan Gummies), servings per container (30), calories (10), full ingredient breakdown with daily value percentages. Dosage instructions at the bottom (Take 2 gummies daily, with water, anytime).

**What's wrong:**

This is necessary for compliance and for detail-oriented shoppers, but it's placed as image 7 of 8. That's the right placement — no issue there. However, the "Other Ingredients" line at the bottom lists "Celery Juice, Chlorophyll, Chlorophyll, Sodium Copper Chlorophyllin, Sodium Copper Chlorophyllin, Like Inulin, Copper Chlorophyll Sugars." This looks like a copy-paste error with duplicate ingredients listed. That's a credibility problem if anyone reads it closely.

**Fix:** Clean up the Other Ingredients line. Remove duplicates. This should be exact and match your actual label. If this is a mockup for ads, make sure it's been proofread against the real label.

---

### Image 8 — 90-Day Risk-Free Trial

**What it does right:** Clean, high-contrast guarantee badge design. "Feel Clean Guarantee" naming is on-brand. Copy is specific: "If you don't feel noticeably lighter — less bloating, better digestion, more energy — within 90 days, simply [ask] for a full refund. No questions asked. No return required."

**What's wrong:**

This is solid but could be stronger. Alevia calls theirs "The Empty Bottle Promise" — which is more visceral and memorable. "Feel Clean Guarantee" is on-brand but doesn't hit as hard as "empty the entire bottle and we'll still refund you."

The image is also text-heavy with no product shot. Adding the jar or a gummy in the corner reinforces what you're guaranteeing.

**Fix:** Consider testing "The Empty Bottle Promise" or "The Flat Stomach Promise" as guarantee names. Add a small product visual. This image should always be last in the carousel — it's currently in the right position.

---

### CAROUSEL SEQUENCING RECOMMENDATION

Current order: Hero → Benefits → Testimonial → Ingredients → Trust → Comparison → Supp Facts → Guarantee

Recommended order: **Hero → Testimonial → Comparison → Ingredients (rewritten) → Trust Badges → Benefits (rewritten) → Supp Facts → Guarantee**

Rationale: Move social proof earlier. The testimonial image is your strongest asset — it should be image 2. The comparison chart creates differentiation — it should come before the ingredient breakdown so the shopper understands *why these ingredients matter* before seeing what they are.

## PART 2: WEBSITE CRO AUDIT (trymotilli.co)

---

### Above the Fold

**Current state:** Product image gallery on left, product details on right. Title reads "Motilli Digestive Health Gummies." Price is $29.99 (compare-at $49.98). Subscription option at $24.39/month. Bundle tiers: Buy 1 (30-Day), Buy 2 Get 1 Free (90-Day, "Most Popular"), Buy 3 Get 2 Free (150-Day, "Best Value").

**What's working:** Bundle structure is solid and mirrors Alevia's exact tiering format (Buy 1 / Buy 2 Get 1 / Buy 3 Get 2). "Most Popular" and "Best Value" labels are proven conversion psychology. Subscription option creates recurring revenue. Price anchoring against $49.98 makes $29.99 feel like a deal.

**What's not working:**

The page opens with a product title that reads "Motilli Digestive Health Gummies – Motilli v2." The "Motilli v2" suffix looks like an internal SKU name that leaked into the storefront. This immediately signals "beta product" or "unfinished store" to a cold-traffic visitor. Remove it.

There's no headline copy above the fold that communicates the *problem this solves*. Compare to Alevia, which leads with "Your Cells Are Losing Energy. Alevia Supports the Source." Motilli's above-the-fold experience is: product name + price + variants. There's no hook.

**Fix:** Add a benefit-driven subheadline directly under the product title: "The Celery Juice Gummy That Stops GLP-1 Bloating, Sulfur Burps & Constipation — In Days, Not Weeks." This immediately communicates who it's for and what it does.

---

### Social Proof

**Current state:** One testimonial visible (Margaret P., Verified Buyer, about Ozempic constipation). Five-star rating shown. No aggregate review count or rating widget visible on the product page.

**What's missing:**

Alevia displays "Rated 4.8/5.0 | Excellent" three times across their page. They have 6+ carousel testimonials. Rosabella has 134 reviews with a full review widget.

Motilli has *one* testimonial and no visible review count on the product page. Your product images say "Rated 4.9/5 | Trusted by 30,000+ Happy Customers" — but the actual website doesn't reflect this. That's a congruence gap. If a shopper sees 30,000+ in the ad images but lands on a page with one testimonial and no review widget, trust drops immediately.

**Fix:** Install a review widget (Judge.me, Loox, or Stamped) and display aggregate ratings prominently. Add at least 5-8 testimonials in a carousel format. If you have 30,000+ customers, show the review count. If that number is aspirational, remove it from the images — incongruence kills conversion faster than having fewer reviews.

---

### Mechanism Education (Collapsible Sections)

**Current state:** Five accordion sections: How Does It Work, What Motilli Helps With, When Will I See Results, Who Can Use It, How Long Until I Get It.

**What's working:** The mechanism copy is actually strong. The "How Does It Work" section explains that celery juice extract acts as a natural prokinetic, chlorophyll neutralizes hydrogen sulfide gas, prebiotic fiber rebalances gut bacteria, and vitamins replenish depleted nutrients. The "What Motilli Helps With" section lists 7 specific symptoms (constipation, sulfur burps, nausea, fatigue, brain fog, bloating). The results timeline (Days 1-3 through Week 4+) is a proven conversion device that Alevia also uses.

**What's not working:**

All of this is hidden behind collapsible accordions. On mobile, the majority of shoppers won't click to expand five separate sections. The most persuasive copy on the entire page — the mechanism explanation, the symptom list, the results timeline — is invisible by default.

Alevia presents their mechanism education, timeline, and comparison table as full-width, always-visible sections. Nothing is collapsed. The shopper scrolls through the entire persuasion arc without clicking anything.

**Fix:** Uncollapse the most critical sections. "How Does It Work" and "What Motilli Helps With" should be always-visible sections with dedicated design treatment (icons, illustrations, custom layout). Keep "Who Can Use It" and "How Long Until I Get It" as accordions if you need to manage page length — those are informational, not persuasive.

---

### GLP-1 Positioning

**Current state:** The hero copy opens with "GLP-1 medications slow your entire digestive system — and your gut pays the price." The "Who Can Use It" section names Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, and Zepbound. Margaret's testimonial references Ozempic by name.

**What's working:** This is a strong niche positioning. You're not competing in the generic "gut health gummy" market — you're targeting the specific, massive, and growing GLP-1 user base with a specific set of side effects (constipation, sulfur burps, nausea, fatigue). This is the right move.

**What's not working:**

The GLP-1 angle isn't in the product title, the meta description, or the above-the-fold area in a way that a cold-traffic visitor immediately understands. If someone clicks a Facebook ad about Ozempic side effects and lands on a page that says "Motilli Digestive Health Gummies" with no immediate GLP-1 reference, there's a congruence break.

myNuora positions as "Vaginal & Gut Health Probiotics for Women" right in their page title. Motilli should do the same for GLP-1.

**Fix:** Update the product subtitle or add a badge above the title: "Designed for GLP-1 Users" or "The #1 Gummy for Ozempic & Mounjaro Side Effects." This creates instant ad-to-page congruence.

---

### Pricing & Offers

**Current state:** $29.99 single bottle. Bundles: Buy 2 Get 1 Free, Buy 3 Get 2 Free. Subscription at $24.39/mo. Free shipping over $45.

**Competitive comparison:**
- Alevia: $39.99 single / $79.99 for Buy 2 Get 1 / $119.99 for Buy 3 Get 2 (higher price point, but same structure)
- Motilli is priced lower, which could signal "cheaper product" or "better value" depending on how it's framed.

**What's not working:**

The subscription is framed as "$24.39/month with subscription" — this is the boring, standard Shopify subscription display. Alevia frames theirs as "Save 25% with Automatic Refills! Zero Commitment, Cancel Anytime." The language matters. "Automatic Refills" sounds effortless. "$24.39/month with subscription" sounds like a recurring charge.

The free shipping threshold of $45 is smart because it pushes the single-bottle buyer ($29.99) toward the 2-bottle bundle to hit free shipping. But this isn't called out explicitly anywhere. There's no "Add 1 more to unlock FREE shipping!" nudge.

**Fix:** Reframe subscription copy: "Subscribe & Save 20% — Cancel Anytime, No Commitment." Add a shipping threshold nudge in the cart or near the CTA.

---

### Missing Elements (Competitive Gaps)

**No explainer video.** Alevia doesn't have one either, but top-performing supplement pages increasingly use short (30-60 second) product videos above the fold. For a GLP-1 audience dealing with real digestive distress, a talking-head video from a founder or nutritionist explaining the product would be high-impact.

**No before/after or user-generated content.** The single testimonial is text + stock-looking photo. Real UGC (unboxing, daily routine, selfie-style) performs significantly better for trust-building.

**No clinical study references or citations.** The images say "Clinically Tested Actives" but the website doesn't link to or reference any specific studies. Alevia at least references AMPK activation and mitochondrial pathways. Motilli claims clinical testing without substantiation on the page — this is a trust gap.

**No email capture or exit-intent popup.** No mechanism to capture visitors who don't buy. Alevia has email/SMS capture infrastructure. You're losing every visitor who bounces.

**No sticky add-to-cart bar.** The code references a sticky ATC but it appears disabled. On long-scroll pages, a persistent CTA button is critical for mobile conversion.

## PART 3: COMPETITIVE BENCHMARKS — WHAT TO STEAL

---

### From Alevia (getalevia.com)

**Steal their page structure.** Alevia's page flows: Hero with hook → Educational problem framing → Mechanism explanation (always visible, not collapsed) → Results timeline → Comparison table → Social proof carousel → Pricing bundles → Guarantee. Every section is open, visible, and designed. Nothing is hidden behind an accordion. This is the gold standard for supplement product pages.

**Steal their guarantee naming.** "The Empty Bottle Promise" is more memorable and viscerally reassuring than "Feel Clean Guarantee." It tells the shopper: use the entire product, and if it doesn't work, we'll still pay you back. That's stronger risk reversal.

**Steal their bundle framing.** Alevia's bundle display shows the per-bottle price, the total savings percentage, and the "Most Popular" / "Best Value" labels — all in a visually distinct card format. Motilli's bundles work but could be more visually differentiated.

**Steal their copy-first approach.** Alevia leads with "Why Feeling 'Off' Is Not a Willpower Problem" — a belief-shift headline that reframes the shopper's self-blame into a biological explanation. Motilli's equivalent content exists in the accordions but isn't positioned as a headline.

---

### From Rosabella (tryrosabella.com)

**Steal their review density.** Rosabella has 134 reviews displayed with a full widget, verified buyer badges, and star ratings on the product page. Their entire conversion strategy is built around social proof volume. Motilli's product images claim 30,000+ customers but the website shows 1 testimonial. Fix the gap.

---

### From myNuora (mynuora.com)

**Steal their niche clarity.** myNuora's page title is "Vaginal & Gut Health Probiotics for Women." There's zero ambiguity about who this product is for. Motilli should achieve the same clarity for GLP-1 users — the product title or subtitle should make the target audience unmistakable.

---

## PRIORITY ACTION ITEMS (Ranked by Conversion Impact)

**1. Fix the social proof gap.** Install a review widget. Display aggregate ratings. Add 5+ testimonials. If you claim 30,000+ customers in images, prove it on the page. This is the single highest-impact change.

**2. Uncollapse the mechanism education.** Your best copy is hidden. Make "How Does It Work," "What Motilli Helps With," and the results timeline always-visible, designed sections — not accordions.

**3. Add a benefit-driven subheadline above the fold.** Something that immediately communicates: this is for GLP-1 users, it stops bloating/constipation/sulfur burps, and it works fast.

**4. Remove "Motilli v2" from the product title.** This looks like an internal label. It undermines credibility.

**5. Fix the truncated copy on Image 2.** "Stops Bloating & Supports" is unfinished. "Restores Your Daily green boost" has casing issues. Proof every image.

**6. Fix the duplicate ingredients on Image 7 (Supplement Facts).** Chlorophyll and Sodium Copper Chlorophyllin are listed twice. This is a credibility issue.

**7. Rewrite image benefit callouts from features to outcomes.** Replace "5g Prebiotic Fiber Per Serving" with "Feeds Your Good Gut Bacteria." Replace "Daily Green Boost" with "More Energy, Less Bloat."

**8. Enable the sticky add-to-cart bar.** It's in the code but disabled. Turn it on.

**9. Add email capture / exit-intent popup.** You're losing every non-buyer with no way to follow up.

**10. Reframe subscription copy.** Change from "$24.39/month with subscription" to "Subscribe & Save 20% — Cancel Anytime."
