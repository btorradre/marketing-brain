## THE AWARENESS CORRECTION

The structure swipe sells to a **solution-aware** buyer: already on omeprazole, already tried to wean, already burned by rebound. It never has to convince her she has a problem.

Ours is **problem aware**. She knows she feels awful but lacks the causal link, and most of these women have not connected it to the shot or settled on laxatives as their category. So the load-bearing beat moves. For the swipe it is *why your method failed*. For us it is *why this is happening at all*, and the laxative teardown demotes from premise to education. The drawer appears at 1:22 as a future she is walking toward, never as a present she is assumed to live in.

## COPY OF RECORD

If you're on a GLP-1 and you haven't gone in days, this is the reason nobody gave you.

Four days. Maybe eleven. There's a brick sitting in your stomach, and the burps clear a room.

And you've been drinking the water and adding fiber this whole time, which is exactly what they told you to do, so at some point you started wondering what's wrong with you.

Nothing is. What's going on is a signal.

Between your brain and your gut there's a signal running all day. It tells your stomach when to empty and your intestines when to move things along. Think of it as a motor. The motor on a conveyor belt, that's probably closer.

Your GLP-1 works by turning that signal down. That's how it quiets the food noise and keeps you full for hours. It's doing its job.

But the same signal runs the belt. So you turn it down, and the whole line slows. Your stomach slows first, then the small intestine, and the colon last.

Food sits instead of moving on. And food that sits ferments, which is where the sulfur burps come from. Everything behind it backs up. That's the brick.

That one made me mad when I found it. The weight comes off because the medication quiets your appetite. Everything happening in your gut is just riding along beside it, and you've been paying for it every day like it was the price of the results.

I mean, a laxative pulls water into your colon. And your colon is the end of the belt. So you're forcing the exit of a line whose motor is still idling. One trip, nothing rebuilt, and the next backup starts that same day.

So you take it again. And the amount creeps up, because a belt that never got its motor back needs more push every time. That's how a drawer fills up. Give it a year and the only thing moving your gut is something you took.

Your doctor probably called it harmless. A large 2023 study in the journal Neurology, using UK Biobank data, found regular laxative use was associated with roughly fifty to sixty percent higher dementia risk, and the association was strongest for the osmotic kind.

Association is not causation. Nobody is saying a laxative causes anything. It's a published finding sitting next to a habit nobody thinks twice about.

And more fiber, the bulky kind, just piles freight onto a belt that isn't moving. Which is why it made you feel worse. You assumed you needed more of it.

Nothing you've tried has been aimed at the motor.

There are three specific things that support it. Apigenin, concentrated from celery juice. A low viscosity soluble fiber, the kind that dissolves instead of swelling into bulk, so it holds water in the stool. And a chlorophyll complex, which neutralizes the sulfur gas behind the burps.

None of them force anything. They support the rhythm your gut already had.

A company called Motilli put all three into one gummy. It's the one I point people to, because the celery extract is standardized for a real dose of apigenin instead of a trace.

Three before bed with a full glass of water. The burps go first, inside the first week. Then the brick starts lifting over the next two, and mornings get predictable again.

And you never touch the shot. The weight loss and your gut are two separate systems, so you keep every pound of it, and you stop paying for something that was never part of the deal.

Everything's on their site, getmotilli.com. They run a ninety day guarantee instead of thirty, because a rebuild takes longer than a month.

So if you've been counting days again, go look before you reach for the laxative one more time. They're running a sale on it right now.

That is the banned construction and the previous audit wrongly passed it. Now leads with the positive and carries no negation: "The weight comes off because the medication quiets your appetite. **§3 Banned transition, 1 hit**

"Almost nobody gets told this part" is semantically the banned "Here's what most people don't realize." §3 requires a transition to carry information rather than announce it, so the beat now opens "Your doctor probably called it harmless," which sets up the same contrast and does work.

**§4 Banned qualifier, 1 hit**

"usually inside a week" used a banned hedge on a results claim. Now "inside the first week," matching the live listicle, which states it absolutely. Kept: "roughly fifty to sixty percent" and "Association is not causation" are locked compliance language, hard-locked by the naturalizer, not weak hedging.

**§5 Dense paragraph block, 1 hit**

The study beat ran six sentences. Split into the finding and the qualifier, with a performance note ensuring no dramatic pause between them.

**Near-misses kept deliberately**

- "Nothing is." reads like negation-then-positive but is a literal answer to the question the previous sentence poses, not a contrast device.
- "Food sits... And food that sits ferments." Word echo across sentences, but they do not open on the same word, and the naturalizer prefers repeating a word to reaching for a synonym.

**Clean on first scan:** no rhetorical section openers (zero question marks in the script), no "Imagine" opener, no triple short sentence stacks, no emoji, no exclamation points.
