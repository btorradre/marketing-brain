# My Stomach Felt Like Cement For 9 Days Straight. The Reason Every Laxative Failed Me Had Nothing To Do With My Colon, And Fixing It Took 3 Weeks

Day 9 was the one that broke me.

I sat on the edge of the tub at 2 am doing math on my phone. Counting back to the last time I had gone.

My name is Dana. I am 52. I started Mounjaro last March and I have lost 40 pounds since.

That night I would have given a lot of it back for one normal morning.

[IMAGE IDEA: A woman's hand holding the waistband of jeans away from her bloated stomach, taken looking down at herself in a bathroom mirror. Slightly blurry, bad overhead bathroom lighting, phone visible in the mirror. Looks like a photo she took to show someone.]

It started small. Bloating after dinner. Pants that would not button by 4 pm.

Then it got bad. 6 days without going. Then 9.

My stomach felt like wet cement. Hard, heavy, stuck.

Then came the burps. They tasted like rotten eggs.

I burped in the car with my daughter. She rolled down her window and did not say anything. She did not have to.

I bought everything. Miralax. Fiber gummies. Senna tea. Magnesium. Prune juice.

I hid the prune juice behind the orange juice so my husband would not ask.

Some of it worked once. None of it worked twice.

[IMAGE IDEA: An open bathroom cabinet, shot straight on with a phone. Crowded shelves of laxative boxes and supplement bottles, some tipped over, a few opened. Ordinary home lighting, nothing arranged.]

My doctor told me to drink more water and eat more fiber. He said it was a common side effect.

I was already drinking 80 ounces a day. I was already eating fiber.

So I started saying no to things. Dinner with friends. My son's game. A trip we had planned for a year.

I was thinner than I had been in 20 years. And I was hiding in my house.

One night I could not sleep. I was on my phone at 1 am reading about all of it again.

I found a long post from a nurse who works with people on these medications. It stopped me cold.

She said we are all treating the wrong organ.

[IMAGE IDEA: A dark bedroom, phone screen glowing, showing a wall of text. Face not visible, just the light on the sheets. Grainy, shot in near darkness.]

Here is how she explained it.

Think of digestion like a highway. Food gets on at your stomach. It gets off at your colon.

These medications do not slow down your colon. They slow down your stomach.

So food sits up top. For hours. Sometimes days.

It sits and ferments. That is where the rotten egg smell comes from.

It dries out and hardens. That is the cement feeling.

By the time anything reaches your colon, it is already a brick. Most of the time nothing gets there at all.

That was the part that got me. There was nothing in my colon to push out. The jam was way up top.

Then I understood my medicine cabinet.

Miralax pulls water into the colon. Nothing was in my colon.

Fiber adds bulk to move things through the colon. I was adding cars to a road that was already closed.

Senna squeezes the colon. You can squeeze an empty tube all day.

Every single thing I bought was aimed at the wrong end.

[IMAGE IDEA: A notepad on a kitchen table with a simple pen drawing of a road with an on ramp and an off ramp, arrows and a few scribbled words. Coffee cup edge in the frame. Handwriting is messy.]

So I looked up what actually works on the stomach. Three things kept coming up.

Apigenin. It is in celery. It supports the nerve that tells your stomach to push food along.

Chlorophyll. It grabs the sulfur gas and cancels it out before it comes up.

A soft, low bulk fiber. It softens what is stuck without adding more bulk.

Wake the stomach up. Handle the gas. Soften what is sitting behind it.

I went looking for one thing with all 3. That took me a while.

Most stomach products had nothing for the gas. Chlorophyll drops did nothing for movement. Most fiber was the bulky kind that made me worse.

Then I found a small brand called Motilli. It is a gummy made for people on these medications.

Celery juice concentrate for the apigenin. Chlorophyll. The soft low bulk fiber. All 3, in the right forms.

I ordered it that night.

[IMAGE IDEA: A short square jar with a white cap sitting on a kitchen counter next to a coffee maker, cap off, a few dark forest green heart shaped gummies visible inside. Morning light through a window, some crumbs on the counter.]

I took 2 in the morning with my coffee.

The first few days, nothing. I almost gave up.

Day 5, the bloating eased a little. I was not sure it was real.

Day 9, I went. On my own. No Miralax.

I sat there and cried. That is a strange thing to cry about. I did not care.

By week 3 I was going every other day and the burps were gone. I had coffee with a friend and never once thought about my breath.

By week 6 it was most mornings. My stomach was soft again. I could button my pants at 8 pm.

I am still on Mounjaro. I am still losing weight.

I went to my son's game last month and stayed the whole time.

My husband said I seem like myself again. He did not know I had been gone.

[IMAGE IDEA: A phone photo taken from the bleachers at a youth baseball game, slightly crooked, a chain link fence partly in the way, other parents in the frame. Late afternoon sun. Nothing centered.]

There are really only 2 ways to go from here.

You can keep doing what you have been doing. More water. More fiber. More Miralax. And keep waiting for your body to adjust to something it is not going to adjust to on its own.

Or you can try something that works on your stomach instead of your colon, which is where the problem actually is.

That is the whole choice.

One more thing. Motilli only sells through their own website.

I have seen copies on Amazon. A woman in my group ordered one. Different ingredients, far less of everything. It did nothing for her.

If you try it, get it from the real place.

I wish someone had told me this in March. I lost 6 months to a problem nobody was looking at correctly.

You do not have to choose between the weight loss and feeling human. I did not know that.
