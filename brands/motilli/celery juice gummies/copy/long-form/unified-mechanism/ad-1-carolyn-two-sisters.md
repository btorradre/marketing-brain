TWO SEPARATE SYSTEMS

Here's the part no one had explained to either of us.

The weight loss happens in one system — your brain and your appetite. The medication turns down the hunger signals. That's the dial it's turning. That's what makes you eat less and lose weight.

The constipation happens in a completely different system. Down in your colon. Different machinery. Different wiring entirely.

And once I understood that, the whole trade-off fell apart. Because if the two things run on separate systems, then you can fix one without touching the other. You don't have to turn off the weight loss to fix the gut. They were never connected the way Dana thought they were.

So what is actually happening in that second system?

Your colon is a muscle. To move, it needs a signal telling it to squeeze and push. That signal is a short-chain fatty acid called butyrate, and you don't make it yourself — the bacteria living in your colon make it, by fermenting the fiber that passes through. They produce butyrate, butyrate tells the muscle to contract, things move. That's the whole system, working quietly, your entire life.

Now here's what the GLP-1 does to that second system, as a side effect — not as its purpose.

The medication slows everything down. That's how it works on the appetite side: slower digestion, you feel full longer. But that slowdown reaches all the way down to the colon. And when things slow down, less material reaches those bacteria. Less to eat means fewer of them, and fewer of them means less butyrate, and less butyrate means a weaker signal, and a weaker signal means an even slower colon.

And then it feeds on itself. Slower colon, less reaching the bacteria, fewer bacteria, less signal, slower still. Every week the loop pulls tighter. The medication holds that initial slowdown in place, so the loop can't break on its own.

That's why it got worse for both of us instead of better. We weren't failing to adjust. We were stuck in a loop that tightens by design.

And I sat there and realized: every single thing in our drawer was aimed at the wrong place.

More fiber doesn't fix this. Fiber is just the raw material those bacteria eat. If the bacteria themselves have been starved down, throwing more fiber in doesn't help — it just piles up in a colon that isn't moving and bloats you worse. That's exactly what the fiber gummies did to Dana. More in, nothing moving it through.

Probiotics don't fix this. They drop new bacteria into a gut whose entire environment has been disrupted — and those bacteria can't take hold in conditions that won't support them. You're sending in workers and there's nowhere for them to live.

Laxatives don't fix this. They force one movement by overriding the whole system. They do nothing about the missing bacteria or the missing signal. Tomorrow you're right back where you were, reaching for the box again.

None of it was rebuilding the thing that had actually broken: the bacterial community that makes the signal.
