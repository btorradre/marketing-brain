## Production rules

**MUST**
- Cast a HeyGen avatar that reads 55–65, suburban married woman. NOT a 30-something car-talker. Wrong demographic kills the whole script.
- Same avatar face and same wardrobe across talking head AND every lifestyle frame (HeyGen still → Nano Banana 2 i2i).
- Same restaurant + husband + outfit in the 0:10 cold-open and the 3:21 callback. Single biggest credibility lever.
- Settings are home interiors: kitchen counter, bathroom mirror, dining table, school auditorium, car passenger seat, restaurant. **No car-talker. No bathroom-mirror selfie of bare midriff. No gym, no glam.**
- One voice for the entire ad — ElevenLabs clone, age-shifted to 55–65.
- Burned-in captions on every talking-head segment — sans-serif, white with black drop shadow, bottom third, max 6 words per line, word-by-word sync.

**MUST NOT**
- No hard CTA. No price overlay, no offer card, no "tap to buy" button, no guarantee graphic, no 3-bottle pack hero, no end-screen CTA. The whole ad stays in "telling a friend" register.
- No red-X graphics over named competitor products (legal). Generic categories only if naming on screen.
- No doctor in a white coat anywhere. This avatar is hiding from her doctor — a doctor in frame breaks the entire premise.
- No gastroparesis mention. Per the angle research: "Do not market purely on gastroparesis. That's a scary medical term users are in denial about. Meet them at symptom level." Use the symptoms, not the diagnosis.
- No "buttoning my jeans" / weight-loss-before-after / body transformation visuals. The win-state is **social re-entry** — dinner with husband, granddaughter's recital, restaurant.
- No constructed metaphors ("locked door," "gut stall," "reward-blocking syndrome"). Describe the mechanism literally (food ferments, hydrogen sulfide gas, malabsorption).
- Do not screenshot a real Facebook group — mock the group name.
