## Transcript argument, psychological triggers and open loops

The central argument has six steps: the attempted remedy may be mismatched → medication changes digestion → that supposedly explains several symptoms → common remedies supposedly fail for a shared reason → these two ingredients supposedly address that reason → an ordinary person describes relief and returning to normal life. This is a persuasive argument map, not verification of the underlying biology.

| Loop | Open | Intermediate development | Payoff | Motilli adaptation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Why might my attempts be backfiring? | 0–5s: counterintuitive medication conflict | 8–27s gives a causal story; 27–45s contrasts remedies | 45–59s defines an alternative and introduces its first ingredient | Ask why digestion changed; explain before recommending a purchase. Do not imply medication should be stopped. |
| Why hasn't the usual approach helped? | Around 5–8s: explanation promise | 27–43s differentiates supposed actions of alternatives | Around 43–45s: failure is framed as understandable | Preserve empathy and differentiation; remove the assertion that laxatives universally fail on GLP-1s. |
| What would the right solution need to do? | Around 45s: unbranded requirements | 50–69s ingredient/action/benefit sequence | Magnesium and ginger are presented as meeting the criteria | Teach ingredient role and routine criteria; introduce Motilli only after that teaching. |

The research cue around 60 seconds is an authority device, not automatically an open loop. The day-specific outcomes are evidence claims, not open loops. The capsule-visibility sequence at 90–96 seconds is a small demonstration payoff. Calling every transition an open loop would obscure what actually creates curiosity.

Other triggers: relief from self-blame; fear that an attempted fix is worsening things; perceived mechanistic certainty; highly specific short timelines; reassurance against cramping/urgency; future scenes of coffee and brunch; low effort; money-back protection; and a final slogan that echoes the hook. These are interpretations of the creative, not measured reasons for performance.

## B. Three useful features a first-time writer might skip

These are observed in the supplied reference and qualitatively in the Omyo transcript family; they are not findings across five independent ads.

1. **Explain the viewer's previous disappointment.** The failed-alternatives block occupies about 17% of SOS's total runtime. It provides a reason to reconsider, beyond saying the new product is better.
2. **Define the solution before naming the brand.** The short requirements bridge makes the product feel like the next step in an argument. Delaying only a logo while naming the product early would miss that function.
3. **Resolve more than the efficacy objection.** The final third covers discomfort fears, ordinary-life outcomes, routine, financial risk and transparency. A first draft often jumps directly from ingredients to CTA.

## C. Motilli adaptation and claim choices

Use the [outline](outline.md) for beat, target duration, intended message, proof element and draft copy. Use [script.txt](script.txt) as the clean spoken script. Expected speech is 112–120 seconds at 145–155 wpm, plus a four-second end hold. First spoken and visible brand occurs around 80 seconds at the central planning pace, approximately two-thirds through.

The new speaker is an informed AI brand presenter. The argument is: understand the change → distinguish the jobs of different remedies → evaluate daily supplements by ingredients and routine → meet Motilli and see its formula/directions. This preserves the reference's explanatory sequence while narrowing the medical promise to what the available evidence supports.

Motilli's current seller page lists celery juice powder, chlorophyll and FOS/prebiotic fiber, and instructs two gummies before bed with a full glass of water. Those details support formula and convenience copy. They do not establish that the finished product restarts stomach contractions, neutralizes sulfur burps, reverses medication effects or produces relief on a particular day. The supplied magnesium/ginger, bromelain/berberine, biofilm and IM8 clinical claims do not transfer to Motilli. [Current product page](https://getmotilli.com/products/motilli-3-bottle-90day-reset).

The current refund policy allows a request within 90 days of receipt, including opened bottles. Approval and any requested return still apply. The draft uses the actual policy without adding no-questions-asked or no-return promises. [Refund policy](https://getmotilli.com/policies/refund-policy).

Wegovy's prescribing information supports delayed gastric emptying and lists constipation among adverse effects. It does not support the reference's sweeping claim that all bowel contractions are suppressed or that laxatives are therefore futile. NIDDK describes several legitimate constipation treatments. The draft separates those facts from the sales argument. [Wegovy prescribing information](https://www.wegovy.com/prescribing-information.html), [NIDDK treatment guidance](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/digestive-diseases/constipation/treatment).

ISAPP supports the general prebiotic role described in the draft. This is category-level information; a demonstrated prebiotic dose or constipation result for Motilli was not established. [ISAPP: what is a prebiotic?](https://isappscience.org/resource/what-is-a-prebiotic/).

The reference's outcome-proof slot is therefore adapted to a verifiable formula/routine demonstration. **This is a weaker evidence type than a documented customer result or product trial.** No clinical success rate, user count, doctor recommendation or first-person recovery is invented. If documented Motilli proof is supplied, the highest-value next revision is to strengthen this slot and update the editing plan.
