## What the research establishes

| Ingredient | Relevant findings and bodily process | Implication for this VSL |
|---|---|---|
| Apigenin | A flavone, not a digestive enzyme. In a high-fat-diet mouse experiment, purified apigenin reduced colonic inflammatory markers IL-1β/IL-6 and oxidative-stress marker MDA, and normalized certain experimentally evoked colonic contraction responses. This suggests preclinical effects on inflammation and neuromuscular function. It does not establish stronger stomach contractions. | No reviewed human GLP-1 study establishes that this ingredient or the gummy reverses delayed gastric emptying. Do not turn “normalization of selected mouse-colon responses” into “wakes your stomach back up.” The seller lists celery powder, not a quantified apigenin dose. |
| Sodium copper chlorophyllin | A modified chlorophyll derivative investigated for odor control. An older nursing-home report described benefits including flatus, but a randomized crossover trial in 28 colostomy patients found no odor advantage over placebo. Odor intensity, gas volume, fermentation rate and abdominal distension are different outcomes. | The proposed “handles fermentation” job is not established by this evidence. Do not claim that it stops fermentation, removes trapped gas, or relieves GLP-1 bloating. |
| FOS prebiotic fiber | Nondigestible carbohydrate fermented by colonic microbes; human trials show it can increase bifidobacteria. Fermentation can also cause gas, depending on dose and tolerance. | “Feeds bacteria in the gut” is supportable general physiology. “Zero gas,” “zero bulk,” or “pushes food out of the stomach” is not established. This ingredient does not justify dismissing fiber as a category. |

Primary sources:

- [Apigenin mouse study, PLOS One (2018)](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0195502): 10 mg/kg/day purified apigenin, eight weeks, five animals per group. This was not a GLP-1 experiment or a human gastric-emptying trial. Under ordinary bath conditions overall electrically evoked contractions did not significantly change; selected pathway responses were normalized.
- [Cirsium flavonoids and isolated swine colon (2023)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38139112/): isolated tissue work illustrates context-dependent motor effects; it is not product efficacy evidence or human gastric-emptying evidence.
- [Chlorophyllin nursing-home report (1980)](https://agsjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1532-5415.1980.tb00124.x): descriptive report in 62 patients; abstract mentions odor, constipation and flatus. It cannot establish the proposed GLP-1 mechanism.
- [Randomized odor trial (1989)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2675439/): 75 mg three times daily, 28 colostomy patients, no subjective odor difference from placebo. This does not test the current gummy or demonstrate that its lower dose fails; it simply supplies no support for the claimed effect.
- [Short-chain FOS randomized human study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9915885/): fermentation and bifidobacterial effects; dose-specific findings cannot automatically be transferred to Motilli.
- [Oligofructose randomized trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4153283/): 16 g/day in healthy adults increased stool frequency and produced more flatulence. Different dose/population from Motilli; supports avoiding a universal no-gas claim.

## Product and UMP grounding

The previously captured [current seller listing](https://getmotilli.com/products/motilli-3-bottle-90day-reset) lists 50 mg celery juice powder, 540 mcg (0.54 mg) sodium copper chlorophyllin and 3 g FOS per two gummies. These are seller declarations, not independent batch verification. It does not quantify apigenin. Ingredient presence is not proof of the advertised clinical action.

[Human GLP-1 motility experiment](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/103/2/575/4644818) supports inhibitory effects on gastrointestinal motility. [Wegovy prescribing information](https://www.wegovy.com/prescribing-information.html) supports delayed gastric emptying and recognizes constipation and abdominal distension among adverse effects. Reduced appetite may reduce fiber intake; the podcast's assertion that fiber is always the first thing cut is not repeated. The revised UMP describes a possible contributor, not a diagnosis or the only cause of bloating.
