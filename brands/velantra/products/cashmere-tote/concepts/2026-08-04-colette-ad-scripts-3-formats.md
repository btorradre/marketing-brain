## The angle these all run on

**Hook:** the perfect fall bag. It is the search query already in her head, and it is the reference format's own hook ("if you're looking for the perfect summer tote, I have it").

**Taste anchor:** Loro Piana inspired. Lands immediately after the name, then cashes straight into a physical feature. Same construction the fall-straw VO uses with "it's Birkin-inspired, so it's fully structured, it holds its shape."

**Promise:** it holds her whole day and still reads as the nice bag.

**Mechanism (ours):** the brushed wool body is the only thing that gets to 50cm and still looks like something she'd wear, and it is cut to stand up on its own instead of sagging.

**Proof beat in every format:** the load-in. Things go in, silhouette barely changes.

**Rule of thumb for the lineage line:** the house name buys credibility, our physical features carry the proof. Never let "Loro Piana inspired" sit alone as a benefit. It is always followed by "so it's [our physical feature]."

### Why it's cut this way

**One cut per noun on the capacity line.** Shots 7, 8 and 9 land on "a laptop," "a water bottle," "and a sweater." The edit counts for her, so the claim gets proven three times in four seconds instead of asserted once.

**The proof beat is a set-down, not a hold.** Shot 4 is the only way to show "stands up on its own." Hands leave frame and the bag does not slump. Nothing she says could do that work.

**She ends holding it loaded.** Shot 10 closes on the bag with a sweater visibly in it, so the last thing on screen is the promise and the proof in one frame. This is the shot that makes the whole ad, and it is why A3 has to be generated with the bag full rather than empty.

**"No logo anywhere" stays on her.** A person turning a bag toward you and showing you nothing is on it beats a macro of the same thing. It also earns her a third on-camera beat so the review never stops feeling like a review.

### Continuity rules for the edit

1. **Every cutaway is face-free.** Hands, product, cropped-at-the-shoulder only. A different woman's face in a cutaway breaks the illusion that this is her own footage. Check `pack-water-bottle` before using, the seated frame runs close to the chin, crop if needed.
2. **Caramel throughout.** Several library macros are Espresso (`macro-belt-through-strap`, `macro-belt-curve-profile`). Do not mix. If a Caramel version of a needed macro does not exist, swap the shot rather than the colorway.
3. **A1 and A3 are the same setting, outfit, hair and light** so they read as one continuous take with cutaways over it, not two separate shoots.
4. **Mute the library clips' native ambient audio.** Omni bakes it in. The VO is the only voice track. A light room-tone bed underneath is fine.
5. **Cut from the early-to-mid stretch of each 10s clip.** Late frames drift.
6. `macro-felt-fibre` and the other Ken Burns renders cut like locked-off tripod shots, which is what the macro beats want anyway.

### Captions

House style, but **the capacity beat gets one word per card** synced to the cuts: "a laptop" / "a water bottle" / "a sweater." Everything else runs 4 to 8 words per card, centered lower third, above her head in the avatar shots.

## Production notes (all three)

**Voice.** One single seamless ElevenLabs take per script, never per-line splices. Female American, conversational, reads 40+.

**Pronunciation.** "Colette" is **co-LET**. "Loro Piana" is **LOR-oh pee-AH-nuh**. If the model mangles either, respell phonetically in the input until it lands. Captions still spell both correctly.

**Captions.** House style from the Sofia masters: white bold rounded sans with thin black outline, sentence case starting lowercase, no periods or commas, max 2 lines, 4 to 8 words per card, centered lower third. "Loro Piana" keeps its capitals in captions.

**Dialogue punctuation.** Commas and periods only in anything fed to a video model. No ellipses, no em dashes.

**Speaker register (hard rule, all formats).** The creator never speaks as the brand. Always "they're running a pre-order," never "our pre-order," never "we." First person plural anywhere in a UGC or review cut reads as paid placement and the recommendation stops being worth anything. The reference Marketing Studio test breaks this rule with "our summer sale"; do not copy that part of it.

**Generation.** Product stills through GPT Image 2 image-to-image off the canonical Colette master, three variants per scene, then pick. Any frame that reads as a 3D render gets regenerated. Video segments fixed at 6s. Seedance normal mode, 9:16, 720p.

**Greenscreen avatar.** Age 35 to 40 on camera, voice reads 40+, chroma green background, keyed clean with no fringe. Follow the V4-V8 identity-block prompt format.

## Guardrails applied

The lineage line is a taste anchor and always cashes into a physical feature, never stands alone · no competitor product is shown, described, priced or ranked, and no other brand is named · no origin claims and no "Italian leather" (that carve-out does not extend to this product) · material claim is brushed wool and cashmere-feel only, never a fiber claim · the bag is never called a work bag, that is the Margot's slot · no logo beat in all three, it is a top ICP driver · no flap language, the Colette is open top · **the belt is described but never claimed to adjust or cinch**, that is unverified on the physical sample · no personification · no em dashes anywhere · pre-order terms stated truthfully in every CTA, just never in the hook.
