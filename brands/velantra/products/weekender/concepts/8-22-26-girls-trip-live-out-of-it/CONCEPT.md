## The micro-angle, and why it is not the 8/06 girls trip cut

`GIRLSTRIP-VO-01` (8/06) is a **capacity** angle wearing a season: fall clothes are bulky, will it all fit. That angle ends the second the bag is zipped in her driveway.

This one starts there. **The problem is the three days AFTER she packs.** On a girls weekend she is in a rented house or somebody's guest room. There is no dresser, no closet, no drawer that is hers. Four women share one bathroom and one bench. So the bag does not get unpacked, it gets lived out of, and by Saturday night a soft duffel has collapsed into a pile on the floor that she is digging through in the dark.

That reframes structure from a spec into the thing she actually needs. **A bag that keeps its shape packed full or barely at all is a bag that is still a dresser on day three.** Every physical fact we own stacks onto it: the flap folds all the way back so the whole mouth is open and she can see everything at once, the interior is smooth caramel leather with a wide slip pocket so small things have a place, the structure means it stands rather than slumps.

**Durability check:** no season word anywhere, no destination dependency, no trend peg. It runs in February. It is also the first Weekender angle that is about the bag when she is *not carrying it*, which is roughly 90% of the trip and 0% of our existing creative.

No villain, no category attack, nothing compared. The dismissal ("you're digging through a pile on the floor") lives in her own lived proof beat over our product, never as a claim about bags we do not make.

# v4 — FINAL (2026-08-24). Two defects caught by Brooks, both fixed.

**Delivered:** `VEL-WEEKENDER-GS-LIVEOUT-02-v4.mp4`. 1080x1920, 30fps CFR, 35.69s, 1068 frames, -15.0 LUFS, TP -4.2 dBFS. Supersedes v2 and v3.

## What Brooks caught

**"The back of the bag is artifacted. There should be a handle on the bag."** The S5 frame ("no logo anywhere on it") showed the two teardrop stitched handle bases with **no handle rising out of them** — an orphaned base.

**The QA pass had already flagged it** ("the two teardrop bases are orphaned, no handle emerges from them") and it was logged as a minor editor note instead of a failure. That was the process error, not a QA miss.

## The correction, and the second defect it uncovered

Re-running the audit across all shipped frames specifically for this defect class found a **second** one: S6 (Charleston) had the front handle **drooping into a long slack loop reaching the bag's bottom edge**, anchored through a **plain round punched hole** instead of a teardrop stitched base, with the right belt strap tip **missing its gold end plate**. The first QA had flagged that too ("the front handle loop just reaches the bottom edge", "handles pass through punched round holes"), and it had also been passed as a note.

**S1, S2, S3, S4 and S7 audited CLEAN** — two handles each, every teardrop base with its handle, no orphans, no droops, no punched holes.

## 🔒 PROCESS LAW ADDED

**A missing, orphaned, drooping, duplicated or invented structural element is a FAIL, never a note.** Surface and tonal issues (colour cast, softness, texture) are notes. The distinction must be explicit in every QA brief, because "minor imperfection for the editor" was doing the work of hiding two genuine product-truth failures. Frame-QA briefs now demand an explicit per-frame count: how many handles, how many bases, does every base have its handle.

## How each was fixed

**S5 → S5r3.** Re-rolled with the handle-and-base rule pinned at the FRONT of the prompt. All four variants came back with handles. QA picked S5r3 as the only one showing the complete bag in frame at correct 18x14.5 proportions with a large uninterrupted plain back band. Animation drift capped at ~3% of frame width (only ~40px side margin).

**S6 → S6d2, via a shot change and an input change.** Four open-bag re-rolls all failed again: two lost the entire front-handle assembly off a fully visible band, one collapsed the bag into an oval basket, three grew invented gold hardware on the folded-back flap. Only a tight interior crop passed, and it won by exclusion with almost no product identity — unusable next to S7, which is already a tight crop.

**The fix was to change what the beat shows.** The VO line is "I took mine to Charleston in June and never actually unpacked" — it never needed to show packing (the packing action is the approved packvo clip at 23.3s). So S6 became **her arriving in the Charleston hotel room with the bag CLOSED**, which is the geometry that renders reliably.

**Then the real unlock: i2i off the hardware macro.** The closed-bag round still merged the two-bar staples into a single solid blade. Regenerating **image-to-image from `LC-macro-turnlock-flap.jpg`** instead of the closed-front hero fixed it outright — at 8x both staples resolve as two distinct flat gold bars with their own bevels and a full-height shadow channel.

## 🔒 GENERALISABLE: anchor i2i on the reference that shows the FITTING, not the whole product

When a specific small fitting keeps rendering wrong, stop hardening the words and change the i2i input to the macro photograph of that fitting. Prompt text lost to this staple across roughly a dozen attempts; the macro reference fixed it on the first try. Match the reference to the failing detail, not to the shot.

## Omni gotcha

Dense negative stacking ("never / no / NEVER") in a motion prompt triggers `Input blocked` on the Interactions API. Hit on both S7 and S6. Rewriting the construction lock in positive terms ("keeps its two-parallel-flat-bar form", "stays warm brass gold throughout") passes. Keep construction locks affirmative in Omni motion prompts; save the hard negatives for the GPT Image keyframe prompts, which accept them.

## 🔒 WHAT ACTUALLY FIXED THE FLAP

Two changes, both applied at once, after this geometry had failed roughly a dozen times across three runs:

1. **The i2i anchor moved off the closed hero and onto the real open-bag photographs** (`LC-open-flap-inner-face-interior.jpg` + `LC-open-flap-foldback-front.jpg`). Same law as the 8/24 staple fix: anchor on the reference that shows the FITTING.
2. **The flap-tab language moved to the very front of the prompt with explicit counts**, written as a left-to-right reading of the flap's bottom edge: one strap slot, keyhole tab, centre tab with the gold oval plate, keyhole tab, one strap slot. Plus the sentence "EXACT COUNTS: THREE hanging tabs, ONE gold oval plate on the centre tab only, TWO keyhole handle cutouts, exactly TWO narrow vertical strap slots." Pass 1 without the counts produced four slots on one candidate; pass 2 with them produced correct counts on every roll.
