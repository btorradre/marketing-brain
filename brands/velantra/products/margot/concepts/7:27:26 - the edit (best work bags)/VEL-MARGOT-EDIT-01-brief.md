## 2. Research — how the four losers were chosen

Brooks asked for an Apify IG-comment scrape of the work-bag category. **Apify is hard-blocked ("Monthly usage hard limit exceeded"), so the consideration set was triangulated instead** via TrendTrack Meta-ads recon + buyer/editor review mining. Full evidence and sources: `research/COMPETITOR-RESEARCH.md`.

The four archetypes cover the entire pool a US working woman names when asked "what work bag are you considering": Longchamp Le Pliage, Marc Jacobs The Tote, Béis/Dagne Dover organizers, Cuyana/The Row quiet luxury. Each is praised genuinely, then given its ONE documented work failure.

## 3. Standing-rule status (inherited from VEL-SOFIA-EDIT-02, Brooks's 7/25 instruction)

1. `feedback_velantra_no_competitor_comparisons` — **overridden for Edit comparison formats.** The comparison is the concept.
2. Designer names spoken as **taste anchors only** ("it is giving Marc Jacobs", "it is giving The Row") — no house's actual bag is ever in frame; the four losers are generated unbranded archetypes.
3. **Hermès and "Birkin" stay out** — standing, and doubly so here: the Margot's belt-and-turn-lock face reads Kelly-adjacent, so those names would turn a taste anchor into a dupe claim.
4. **Competitor prices never spoken** ("at that price I would baby it" quotes no number). $99.99 stays the only figure in the ad, spelled out as words: "under a hundred dollars" — the numeral bug from the Sofia v1 run is a standing law.

## 5. Final script

*Commas and periods only. 163 words · 90s generated. Every line sized to Seedance's native 1.6–2.0 words/sec.*

> Everyone wants a work bag that looks expensive and carries everything. Save this, I tested five and one won.
>
> Starting with the nylon one, every woman on my commute has it. So light and easy. But it slumps, and slump is not polished.
>
> This big leather one is giving Marc Jacobs. The leather is gorgeous. It is heavy before you even pack it.
>
> The viral organizer tote. There is a pocket for everything. So smart for travel. In a meeting it looks like luggage.
>
> This one is quiet luxury, it is giving The Row. Stunning. But at that price I would baby it every day.
>
> And then this one. Real structure, it stands up on its own. My laptop fits, my planner fits, and it stays this light. And this burgundy goes with every single thing I wear to work. And it is under a hundred dollars.
>
> That is the one that won, and it was not close. Tell me which one you would have picked.

## 8. QA gates

1. Frame QA on every keyframe **before** animation — burgundy vs brown/black drift, swirl grain vs pebble, exactly 1 belt + 2 clasp plates + 1 turn lock, silver never brass, no Margot signatures bleeding onto the four losers.
2. ≥4 frames per clip after generation, same checklist plus: bag never slumps, belt ends stay loose in their V, no strap self-fastening.
3. **Transcript every clip** — the price line especially ("under a hundred dollars", no numeral).
4. Energy check on the last sentence of every clip.
5. Re-audit after every regeneration — a passed audit belongs to the file that was audited, not its replacement.

**Two new laws from this run, saved to memory:**
1. **Ref-image room bleed.** The Camille reference image drags t2i scenes back to her native coastal room (rattan mirror, ocean window) even when the prompt describes a different room. Describing the new room is not enough — the scene lock must NEGATE what the ref would inject ("never the ocean, no mirror on any wall") and pin wardrobe bottoms ("dark blue jeans"). 9/9 held after the fix.
2. **Forearm carries need explicit anatomy** ("both handles looped over her right forearm, elbow bent, bag hanging below the forearm against her hip, same size as in the bag reference image") or i2i floats the bag at the wrong scale.
