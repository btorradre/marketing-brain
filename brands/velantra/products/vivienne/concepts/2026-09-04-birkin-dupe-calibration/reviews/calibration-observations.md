## Threshold and anchor tension

The rubric defines 3/4 as acceptable and 4/4 as exemplary. A script that is acceptable in every weighted criterion earns 75/100 and is blocked by the current threshold. Reaching 90 requires exemplary scores on criteria totaling at least 60% of the weight, assuming the rest score 3. This is mathematically coherent if the intention is to admit only mostly exemplary copy, but it conflicts with an informal expectation that meeting all stated acceptable floors clears a normal production gate.

Keep version 0.1.0 and the existing result intact. Do not reduce the threshold, inflate a score or silently reinterpret an anchor to get this concept through. Ask Brooks to label examples as usable, needs revision or reject, and to express preferences between unlabeled candidates. Compare those labels to criterion scores on several distinct concepts before deciding whether production acceptance and an aspirational excellence score should be separate. Record the rationale and version any future rubric change; check it on held-out concepts.

## What this review actually establishes

- Copy truth can be verified against the pinned ledger and visible master. An owner-approved generated image does not establish product testing, materials or a real customer experience.
- Customer voice and avatar fit currently refer to a documented adjacent-category hypothesis. There are no authenticated Vivienne buyer interviews in this packet. A 3 denotes fit to the supplied brief, not validated audience resonance; actual conversion outcomes remain separate.
- Spoken naturalness here is text read-through. No waveform, timed voice performance or pronunciation was inspected. Final audio quality must be independently checked when the take exists.
- The added presenter still and shot plan do not upgrade a script into a verified edited ad. A plan must not earn scores for completed motion, timing, cuts, chroma key or captions.
- The same independent reviewer assessing a revision is continuity of review, not inter-reviewer agreement. Another reviewer should judge a blinded candidate set before the team characterizes the rubric as reliable.
