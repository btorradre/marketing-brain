# CONCEPT B — "That's a really cute bag"
### Velantra Colette Wool Tote · 24 seconds · 9:16 · five versions

Burned on screen for the first 3 seconds: **"That's a really cute bag — where'd you get it?"**

A stranger stops a woman on the sidewalk to compliment her bag. She answers the way someone answers when they've been asked this before. In twenty-four seconds she gives the name, the material, the thing that surprises people, and the price. Nothing is pitched. She's just answering.

**She answers the question in the first line.** No warm-up, no preamble. The product is named by second two — the title card asked where the bag is from, so the very next thing out of her mouth is where the bag is from.

**Fire this concept first.** Cheapest per ad, most native to the format, fastest read of the idea.

---

## The workflow

**Step 1.** Generate her starting frame with GPT Image 2. One frame per woman. Three variants, pick the best.
**Step 2.** Animate that frame with Seedance 2.5 as the first frame. One pass, whole ad, audio included.

**The five starting frames are the same ones Concept A uses.** If you've already made them, skip Step 1 and go straight to the video prompt.

---

## The script

24 seconds · 5 shots · 4 cuts. **Identical for all five women** — the starting frame is the only thing that changes. Every line is sized to its beat. Never add words to a beat and never stretch a beat to fit a line.

| Beat | Window | Line | Doing |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | 00:00–04 | "Oh, thank you. It's the Colette, it's Velantra." | Answers the question immediately |
| **2** | 00:04–09 | "It's brushed wool, but it stands up on its own." | The surprise — soft material, real structure |
| **3** | 00:09–14 | "My laptop is in there right now and you can't even tell." | Capacity, shown not claimed |
| **4** | 00:14–19 | "It was a hundred and twenty dollars. I get asked about it constantly." | The payoff, plus social proof |
| **5** | 00:19–24 | "It's on pre-order right now, so go get yours below." | CTA |

**The Colette is a genuine pre-order** — it ships early October, so beat 5 is accurate as written. If that changes before you generate, the line changes with it.

**Check the price the morning you generate.** Beat 4 names $119.99. If it's moved, use the no-price ALT below rather than guessing.

**No-price ALT** if the price is in flux or you want a cut with no number in it — replace beat 4 with:
> "I get asked about it constantly. It's the only bag I've used since September."

---

## Non-negotiables

- The only brand named anywhere is Velantra. This concept names no other brand at all.
- No competitor is named, implied or criticised.
- She never speaks as the brand. "It's Velantra", "I got it for fall". Never "our", never "we".
- The interviewer never speaks and is never seen. Only a forearm and a mic at the right edge of frame.
- The question is a burned title card in post, first 3 seconds only. It is never spoken audio.
- The Colette hangs from its two short top handles, in the hand or on the forearm. **It can never go over a shoulder** — the handle drop is 16cm and physically cannot reach.
- No music in the generation. No on-screen text in the generation.
- Any frame that reads as CGI, a product render or a catalogue photo is a regenerate, not a ship.

# STEP 2 — Animate it

**Seedance 2.5.** Upload the approved starting frame and set it as `first_frame_url`.

```
model:           bytedance/seedance-2-5
first_frame_url: <the approved starting frame>
duration:        24
aspect_ratio:    9:16
resolution:      720p
generate_audio:  true
```

Three things that silently ruin a pass:
1. The runtime in FORMAT, the beat windows, and `duration` must all be **24**. Mismatch makes it compress and the delivery slides off the timeline.
2. Never write "add", "remove", "change" or "replace" — those reroute the job into edit mode and lock the duration. Write "lifts", "turns", "presses".
3. Never negate an object. "No table" produces a table. Say what IS there.

# Check before it ships

Pull frames at 3 to 4 per second, not 1.

1. Exactly 4 cuts. Cuts every two seconds means the timeline was ignored.
2. Same face either side of every cut.
3. Clothing and jewellery identical across every cut. Earrings vanish first.
4. Dialogue lands inside its beat window. Over half a second of drift means the beat was overfull.
5. Lip sync holds. If it goes soft, cut words — never add duration.
6. The bag: right colorway, one belt strap, gold hardware, no logos, open top, **no shoulder strap**.
7. **Beat 3 is the money shot** — the bag fills the lower half of frame. The wool must read as brushed fabric with visible fibre, not smooth plastic, and the body must hold its shape while she presses it.
8. The mic and forearm are still there and haven't changed shape or position.
9. Photoreal — sensor noise, blown highlights, imperfect focus, real creases. Clean, even and tack-sharp means it's a render. Reject it.
10. Transcribe it. Under-filled beats grow speech that isn't in the script, and confirm the spoken price matches the live price.
11. The CTA in beat 5 is intact and audible. If pre-order has ended by the time this ships, the line is wrong and the pass has to be rerun.

**If it comes back wrong,** fix with the cheapest tool: trim in post, then patch the audio, then regenerate the starting frame, then rerun. A rerun is 1,512 credits, so if a take is 95% there, patch it. Change one variable per retake — a retake reliably fixes what you named and breaks something else.

**Cost:** 24s × 63 credits/sec = 1,512 per version, 7,560 for all five. Starting frames are free if Concept A already made them.
