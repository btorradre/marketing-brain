# Velantra Naming System — "The Women"

**Status:** PROPOSAL — awaiting Brooks's approval on the cast before anything touches Shopify
**Date:** 2026-07-22
**Companion doc:** `Velantra_ Comprehensive Branding Kit.md`

---

## 1. The Problem

Right now the catalog reads like a dropshipping store: *Straw Tote, Boat Tote, Bow Tote, Weekender.* Descriptive names are category labels — they tell the customer what the product *is*, but nothing about who Velantra is. There is no thread connecting one product to the next, so every purchase is a one-off and every launch starts from zero.

## 2. The Story Spine

**Every Velantra bag is named for a woman.**

Jessica's telling (founder voice, used on the About page, PDP intros, launch emails):

> "I don't design for a market. I design for one woman at a time. She has a life, a schedule, somewhere to be — and by the time a bag is finished, I know her name."

Each bag gets a woman's name — just the name, **and that's where it stops on the page.** No epithet ("the summer one" states the obvious and cheapens the name), no vignette, no biography, no bag with a life of its own. Vestirsi names "the Eden" — no tagline, no backstory — and lets the name carry the equity while the copy stays understated and buyer-facing. Personifying the bag (giving it summers, schedules, errands) is a brand-law violation and reads forced — telling, not showing. Launches can still be framed as "Meet Eleanor," but "meeting her" means seeing the bag, not reading her diary.

Why this works for Velantra specifically:

1. **It's the quiet-luxury move.** Heritage houses name bags after women; fast fashion names them after what they are. The naming convention itself signals which world we belong to — without ever comparing ourselves to anyone (per brand law: no competitor comparisons).
2. **It sidesteps the origin trap.** Women's names carry warmth and provenance-feel without making any geographic claim. (Place-based naming — "the Positano," "the Milano" — would flirt with implied Italian/EU origin, which we can never claim. Names of women make no factual claim at all.)
3. **The seeds are already planted.** The PDP testimonials are signed Sofia L., Eleanor M., Margot R., Camille D., Anya K. The brand has been whispering these names since day one.
4. **It's infinitely extensible.** Every future product slots in. The system never runs out.

**Compliance guardrail:** the women are muses — a naming device, nothing more. Never present them as real, specific customers ("named after our customer Sofia from Nantucket"), and never narrate their lives on-page (no personification — see brand law). The name is a name; the copy is about the bag.

## 3. Naming Laws

- Feminine first names, 2–3 syllables, instantly pronounceable and spellable (AI VO + UGC scripts must handle them cleanly — no "Weekender"-style spelling failures).
- Timeless but not stuffy. Think grandmother's name that came back in style.
- Distinct first letters across the active line (no two bags starting with the same letter).
- No collisions with famous bag names or designers (rules out Anya — Anya Hindmarch; Kelly, Birkin, Marcie, Loulou, etc.).
- The definite article is mandatory: it's never "Sofia," always **the Sofia**. The article is what makes it an object of desire instead of a person.

Accessories (Bag Scarf, Horse Charm, Cherry Charm, Keychain, Organizer) stay descriptive — they are the finishing touches, not characters. Cross-sell copy stays product-forward ("knots at the handle; add the Bag Scarf") — never personified ("Sofia wears hers knotted at the handle").

**One trademark note:** Ferragamo had a "Sofia" satchel (largely discontinued). First names on handbags are weakly protectable and shared across many brands — risk is low at our price point — but worth knowing it exists.

## 5. PDP copy — name ONLY (no epithet, no vignette)

**KILLED — vignettes (2026-07-22) and epithets (2026-07-23), both by Brooks.** The vignettes were third-person biographies ("Sofia spends her summers near the water…") — personification of the bag, telling not showing, forced. The epithets ("the summer one," "the everyday one") state the obvious (a woven straw tote is self-evidently the summer bag), cheapen the name, and lower perceived value. The PDP adds exactly one brand line — the name — and nothing more:

The Vestirsi model (the reference PDP): name the bag, describe the bag, speak to the buyer. Vestirsi never gives "the Eden" a summer, a schedule, or a tagline — the name is just a name. Match that restraint.

## 6. Format & Surfaces

- **Shopify title:** `The Sofia — Woven Straw Tote` (name first, function second). The descriptor keeps category SEO ("straw tote bag" search volume) and instant comprehension; the name builds the brand. As equity grows, descriptors can shrink.
- **PDP:** H1 = "The Sofia" (name only — no epithet subhead), then the existing craft/function copy — no vignette in between.
- **Collection page:** each card shows the name only ("The Sofia") — clean, no epithet subtitle.
- **About page:** Jessica's "one woman at a time" narrative introduces the whole system.
- **Email:** launches become "Meet ___" campaigns; flows introduce the other women as cross-sells ("You met Sofia. This is Eleanor.").
- **Ads:** TOF hooks stay avatar-language and category-language ("this straw tote…", "boatkin" hooks unaffected) — the name pays off at MOF/BOF and on the PDP, where congruence matters.
