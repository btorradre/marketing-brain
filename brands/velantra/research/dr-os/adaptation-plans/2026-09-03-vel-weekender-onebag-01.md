## PART 2. AUTOPSY

**Angle.** The travel bag that pays for itself and keeps your things off each other. The
problem it names is logistical and financial: bag fees, and shoes touching clothes.

**Claims ladder, in order.**
1. Capacity beyond expectation (3 to 5 days)
2. Free ride past the airline (counts as a personal item)
3. Interior organisation (laptop pocket, pockets in the right places)
4. Separation (a sealed bottom compartment)
5. Hands free transport (trolley sleeve)
6. Looks acceptable in 2 contexts
7. Others notice it
8. Steep discount plus a second bag free

Note the shape: every rung is a FEATURE, and every feature is one a competitor could
add next season. The ladder is rented, not owned.

**Emotional engine per act.**
- Act 1 (0 to 15s) recognition. "I have bought bags before and been wrong."
- Act 2 (15 to 50s) competence. She is being shown how to pack well by someone who has.
- Act 3 (50 to 74s) relief and vanity, in that order. The neck and back line is the
  most honest sentence in the ad and it is placed where fatigue actually happens.
- Act 4 (74 to 81s) acquisitive warmth. The offer is followed immediately by a gift,
  which converts a discount into generosity.

**Promise plus identity upgrade.** Promise: you will stop paying for things you should
not have to pay for. Identity upgrade: you become the woman who has travel solved, who
does not arrive frazzled and does not get nickel and dimed. The identity is competence.

**Belief channeling.** The ad never argues. It converts every claim into a hand doing a
thing, and it lets the sound of a zipper close the argument 3 separate times. Doubt is
handled by pre-emption, not rebuttal: the bag is shown attached to the roller in the
very first shot, 50 seconds before the trolley sleeve is mentioned, so by the time she
names it the viewer has already accepted it.

**The golden nugget.** Not "I want a better duffel." It is: *she is tired of the moment
at the gate when the bag becomes a problem she has to solve in public.* Everything in
the ad is that moment, either prevented or paid for.
