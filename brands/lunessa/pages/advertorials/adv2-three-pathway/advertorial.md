## 11 years on a statin. My scan still found plaque.

I am 58. I live outside Fort Wayne, Indiana. I ran the front office at an elementary school for 21 years.

My mom had a stroke at 66. So I have been careful since my forties.

My doctor put me on a statin at 47. I took it every night. I never missed one.

It worked, at least on paper. My LDL went from 190 down to 98. It stayed there for 11 years.

Every single year, my doctor said the same thing. "Your numbers look great."

So I stopped worrying about it.

![Pill organiser, statin bottle and lab printout on the kitchen counter](final/IMG-01-statin-routine.png)
*Pill organiser, statin bottle and lab printout on the kitchen counter*

Last spring my clinic offered a carotid ultrasound for $99. My friend Peg talked me into going with her.

I only went to be nice. I expected them to tell me I was fine.

The tech got quiet. She kept going back over the same spot on my neck.

"How long have you been on cholesterol medication?" she asked.

"11 years," I said.

She did not say anything after that.

![Carotid ultrasound seen from the exam table](final/IMG-02-carotid-scan.png)
*Carotid ultrasound seen from the exam table*

My doctor called two days later. There was plaque in both carotid arteries. Enough that she wanted me to come in.

I sat at my kitchen table and cried.

I had done everything they told me to do. For 11 years. And my arteries were closing anyway.

When I asked her how that was possible, she said some people just progress. She offered me a higher dose.

That was the whole answer. More of the thing that had not worked.

![After-visit summary on the kitchen table](final/IMG-03-diagnosis-call.png)
*After-visit summary on the kitchen table*

My nephew Danny is a pharmacist. I called him that weekend, mostly to complain.

He let me finish. Then he asked me something that changed everything.

"Aunt Sue, do you know what a statin actually does?"

I said it lowers cholesterol.

"It stops your liver from making it," he said. "That is one job. Your body has three."

He explained it slowly and I wrote it down.

The first one is making it. Your liver produces cholesterol. A statin slows that down. That part everybody knows.

The second one is clearing it. Your body has receptors that pull LDL back out of your blood. In people like me, those receptors are lazy. A statin does nothing for them.

The third one is oxidizing. LDL that sits in your blood too long goes bad. That oxidized kind is what sticks to artery walls and builds plaque. A statin does nothing for that either.

![Handwritten note: 1. Making it, 2. Clearing it, 3. Oxidizing](final/IMG-04-three-pathways.png)
*Handwritten note: 1. Making it, 2. Clearing it, 3. Oxidizing*

"So your number went down," Danny said. "But everything already in there just kept sitting. And sitting LDL oxidizes."

That was my scan. That was the whole thing.

My blood test measured how much was in there. It never measured what it was doing.

11 years of good numbers. And two of the three running the entire time with nothing on them.

I thought about everything I had tried. Fish oil. Niacin. Those plant sterol drinks. Oatmeal every morning for two years.

Every one of them worked on the first thing. The same thing my statin was already doing.

I had been treating a third of my problem from six directions.

Danny told me to look at red yeast rice with CoQ10.

Red yeast rice slows production like the drug does. But it also has other compounds that wake up those clearing receptors. That covers two.

CoQ10 covers the third. At a high enough dose, it keeps LDL from oxidizing.

He warned me the amount matters. Most bottles use 50 or 100 mg of CoQ10. The research uses 200.

It took me a while to find one with both at the right amounts. Most had one or the other, or barely any of either.

I found Lunessa. 2400 mg of red yeast rice, standardized and tested, with 200 mg of CoQ10. All three in one thing.

It is a gummy, which felt a little silly to me at 58. I take two with my coffee.

Week 3, the ache in my legs eased up. I was not expecting that at all.

Week 8, I had bloodwork. My LDL came back at 72. It had not moved off 98 in 11 years.

This past March, I had the ultrasound done again.

Same plaque. No new plaque. Nothing had grown.

My doctor read it twice. She said stable is a very good word for someone with my history.

I sat in the parking lot again. Different reason this time.
