# Orelli Founder Film — Episode 1 Creative Brief

**Premise:** Two cousins announce the medicine they're building for their sisters, and for the 100 million adults who can't swallow pills.
**Format:** VO-driven B-roll film. Vertical 9:16. Runtime ~1:55. The founders' faces appear ON CAMERA exactly once: the final shot of the CTA. Every other second of the film is B-roll: backs, hands, over-the-shoulder, receipts, graphics. Archive photos (childhood, sisters) are exempt; they're receipts, not footage.
**Voiceover:** Brooks records the entire script as ONE continuous voice memo, conversational, like telling a friend.

**Caption system:**
- Small 1-4 word caption chips that change every 1-2 seconds. White = normal. Red = villain words. Yellow = payoff words.
- Full-frame black cards with one white word for the hardest punches.

---

## COLD OPEN — pick from 5 hook variants

Shoot all five in one session, same girl, same outfit. Ideally one of your sisters (she reappears later, which ties the hook into the story). Her face is fine on camera; the faceless rule applies only to the two of you. Every variant carries the same on-screen text: **"Watch this if you can't swallow pills."** Small, centered, plain white.

**HV1 — Bedroom (the reference replica).** Dim bedroom, one warm lamp, phone propped on the dresser. She sits on the bed in a tank top, pill in mouth, water bottle in hand. Head thrown all the way back, big gulp, gag, lurches forward, hands over her face. Exactly the energy of the reference TikTok.

**HV2 — Kitchen sink panic.** Harsh overhead light. She refills the same glass a second time, tries again, gags over the sink, water dripping off her chin, palms flat on the counter, head drops.

**HV3 — Mirror.** Bathroom mirror, phone on the counter. Pill on tongue, attempt, retch, she spits it into her hand and stares at herself, defeated.

**HV4 — Public stealth.** Dinner table. She tries to swallow discreetly, eyes water, she grabs someone else's drink and coughs into a napkin.

**HV5 — Aftermath (no face).** Macro: one pill sitting on the counter next to three empty glasses. A hand slams down a fourth. The quiet variant.

Direction for all: visceral but real. Throat working, eyes watering, true gag reflex. One or two takes, keep the imperfect one.

While she's in the room, also grab the **callback take** for the CTA: same outfit, same spot, but this time she just looks up at the camera, calm, small nod. Five seconds. It closes the loop at the end of the film.

## THE FILM, LINE BY LINE

---

**BEAT 1 — 0:00-0:04 — THE HOOK**

VO: *"Watch this if you can't swallow pills."*

VISUAL: One of the five hook variants above, full frame.

TEXT: The hook line, small centered white.

**BEAT 2 — 0:04-0:06 — THE BRIDGE**

VO: *"That's the problem we became obsessed with fixing."*

VISUAL: Whip cut from her mid-gag to the obsession desk: overhead shot of the kitchen table buried in printed studies streaked with highlighter, sticky notes, the open notebook, two coffee mugs. Two mugs says two people without showing anyone. A hand drops one more printout onto the pile as the line ends.

TEXT: White chip: **obsessed**

---

**BEAT 3 — 0:06-0:09 — THE NAMES**

VO (live sound, not voiceover): Bennett: *"I'm Bennett."* Brooks: *"I'm Brooks."*

VISUAL: The two of you at the table shot from BEHIND, backs of heads and shoulders only. As each voice speaks, a name chip lands under that side of frame: **BENNETT** left, **BROOKS** right. Two voices, two backs, zero faces; the duo is established and the face payoff is saved for the end. Alt coverage: each name lands on that founder's hands, Bennett cracking the notebook open, Brooks clicking a pen.

---

**BEAT 4 — 0:09-0:15 — THE DREAM**

VO: *"We're cousins, and since we were kids, we've had the same dream. Build something together. Something that actually matters."*

VISUAL: Two or three childhood photos of you and Bennett punched in fast: little kids together, awkward teens, recent. The photos are the proof of "since we were kids," and they quietly show who the two backs belong to.

TEXT: White chip on the first photo: **since we were kids**

---

**BEAT 5 — 0:15-0:26 — THE SISTERS**

VO: *"Turns out, the idea was sitting at our own dinner tables. Both of our sisters struggle to swallow pills. We grew up watching them gag on them, hide them in food, or just skip the medicine and push through the pain."*

VISUAL: Family photos with the sisters, then three staged inserts: a macro of a pill buried in a spoonful of yogurt. An untouched pill sitting next to a full glass of water. An alternate take of the hook girl struggling. Between clips, punch full-frame black cards.

TEXT: Black cards: **GAG.** / **PANIC.** Red chip on the last line: **push through the pain**

---

**BEAT 6 — 0:26-0:35 — THE NUMBER**

VO: *"Then we found a number that changed everything. Four in ten adults struggle to swallow pills. Over 100 million people, just in America, dealing with the same thing as our sisters."*

VISUAL: Three graphics plus one human insert, in this order:
1. Ten identical pill icons in a row. Four flip red one by one with a tick sound as "4 IN 10 ADULTS" lands.
2. Over-the-shoulder macro of a phone in hand, thumb scrolling the actual study, the 40% figure visible on screen. Shoot past the shoulder so no face enters frame.
3. An odometer counter rolling up to 100,000,000+ over a US map filling with dots.
4. A screen-recorded grid of real pill-struggle videos tiling the whole frame (blur usernames).

---

**BEAT 7 — 0:35-0:42 — THE VILLAIN**

VO: *"The pain relief aisle has known this for forty years, yet the best they came up with was bitter liquid medicine and chalky tablets for children."*

VISUAL: The children's medicine aisle at CVS, filmed slow across the shelf of kids' liquid medicine and chewables. Then an adult hand holds a children's bottle up to the lens, kids' shelf soft behind it, no face in frame. A grown man's hand dwarfing children's medicine IS the argument. Then a timeline graphic: 1986 to 2026, the identical caplet icon repeating at every decade tick. Nothing changed.

TEXT: Red chips: **bitter liquid** / **chalky tablets** / **for children**. Black card: **40 YEARS**

---

**BEAT 8 — 0:42-0:45 — THE DECISION**

VO: *"That was it. The idea we'd been waiting for."*

VISUAL: The turn, played entirely in hands: the notebook flips shut, a palm slaps down flat on the cover, then a fist bump over the table between the two of you, faces out of frame. Three quick shots, rising energy.

---

**BEAT 9 — 0:45-0:56 — THE REVEAL**

VO: *"So we went all in on a chewable called Orelli, 500 milligram of acetaminophen without the hard to swallow defect. The same pain medicine that's in the pill in your cabinet. No water needed and you just chew it."*

VISUAL, phrase by phrase:
- "So we went all in" lands on a full-frame black card: **ALL IN.** Punch out of the card into the reveal.
- "a chewable called Orelli": the notebook page where the name was first written, then the clean Orelli wordmark. Hold the name a full beat so it registers.
- "500 milligram of acetaminophen": macro of the notebook spec line, "500 mg" circled twice in pen.
- "the pill in your cabinet": POV insert, a hand swings open a bathroom medicine cabinet, one generic white pill bottle inside, label turned away from the lens (Hard Rule 5).
- "No water needed and you just chew it": back at the table, two fingers slide a full glass of water out of frame. Cheap, clean, and it says everything.
- Button on the beat: packaging render or sketch, if approved.

TEXT: Black card: **ALL IN**. Yellow chips: **500 mg** / **no water** / **you just chew it**

---

**BEAT 10 — 0:56-1:07 — THE THREE HARD LINES**

VO: *"And we drew three hard lines from day one. It had to be the same dose as the pill, not some children's version. It had to actually taste good. And it had to be made just as safely as the pill it replaces."*

VISUAL: The whiteboard framed tight, hand and marker only, written in real time, one line per spoken line, marker squeak audible:
- "three hard lines from day one": the hand writes **1. FULL DOSE** as the first line is spoken.
- Line 1 insert: macro of a children's chewable bottle's dosage panel next to an adult 500 mg panel (grab this while you're already at CVS for Beat 7), or the children's bottle from Beat 7 getting pushed off the edge of the table.
- Line 2: back to the whiteboard, **2. TASTES GOOD** written and underlined twice. No tasting footage exists yet and none is allowed (Hard Rule 8), so the double underline carries the intent.
- Line 3: **3. SAFE AS THE PILL**, then a stack of printed spec and compliance paperwork dropped on the table, macro of a highlighter running across one line. Generic pages only, nothing that identifies the manufacturer; the reveal stays in Beat 13.

TEXT: Black card in: **THREE HARD LINES**. White chips as each lands: **same dose** / **actually tastes good** / **safe as the pill**

---

**BEAT 11 — 1:07-1:17 — THE BITTERNESS DEMO**

VO: *"We learned fast why nobody's done this. That medicine is one of the most bitter things in the pharmacy, and hiding a full adult dose in a chewable that tastes good is a chemistry problem most factories won't even touch."*

VISUAL: The demo, shot faceless. Crush a plain white tablet with the back of a spoon, macro on the powder, a fingertip takes a crumb up and out of frame. Then the reaction with no face: he shoots up from the table and lunges for the sink, filmed from behind, faucet blasting, glass slamming the counter, head stays out of frame or turned away. The lunge and the water grab are the receipt. Do not act it. It will be genuinely awful.

TEXT: White chip: **the most bitter thing in the pharmacy**

---

**BEAT 12 — 1:17-1:22 — THE REJECTIONS**

VO: *"We called manufacturer after manufacturer. 99% of them told us it can't be done."*

VISUAL: Fastest cutting stretch of the video. Email screenshots with the rejection lines highlighted. A call log scrolling. The phone face-up on the table mid-call, speakerphone UI visible, a pen tapping next to it harder and harder. The whiteboard list of company names crossed off one by one until almost every name is struck; the near-empty board IS the 99%. Blur company names and numbers.

TEXT: Red chips rapid-fire over the evidence: **No.** / **No.** / **No.** Black card as the line lands: **99%**. Red chip on the last words: **can't be done**

---

**BEAT 13 — 1:22-1:29 — THE ONE THAT SAID YES**

VO: *"Then we found the one that didn't say no, an FDA-registered drug manufacturer whose scientists were already deep at work on this exact problem."*

VISUAL: The tone flips, still faceless. The phone on the table again, but this time the tapping pen drops, and two fists slam together above the phone. A chair scrapes back, papers fly up through frame. Shoot it all from behind or with heads out of frame; big body energy, zero faces. Then the authority evidence: the signed agreement's signature page with terms blurred, facility or equipment photos. The script no longer names the manufacturer; the name or website may still appear on screen ONLY if both confirmations in Hard Rule 7 clear.

**BEAT 14 — 1:29-1:42 — THE PLAN**

VISUAL: "That was always the plan" lands on a receipt instead of a face: macro of the early notebook page where the plan was actually written, something like "find a real drug manufacturer + partner" with a date on it, held steady, no cuts. The written plan is the proof the line claims. Then alternate: their side (facility, equipment) against your side (hands at the laptop with the brand work up, packaging sketches, the notebook), faces never in frame.

## SHOOT PLAN (4 sessions)

**1. Hook day (with the sister or a friend):** all five hook variants, one or two takes each, same outfit. Extra takes double as Beat 5 B-roll. Plus the calm callback take for Beat 17 (five seconds, same spot, small nod at camera).

**2. Founders at home, all faceless coverage (backs, hands, over-the-shoulder):** obsession-desk overhead with two mugs (Beat 2), backs-at-table two-shot for the names (Beat 3), phone-scroll over-the-shoulder (Beat 6), notebook slam + fist bump (Beat 8), water-glass slide-out, notebook 500 mg spec macro, medicine cabinet POV with a generic bottle, notebook name page (Beat 9), whiteboard three-hard-lines writing hands-only + paperwork stack + highlighter macro (Beat 10), bitterness crush and the sink lunge from behind (Beat 11), speakerphone-on-table with tapping pen + whiteboard cross-off (Beat 12), yes-call fist slam, chair scrape, paper toss (Beat 13), plan notebook page macro + hands-at-laptop brand work (Beats 14, 17). PLUS the only on-camera setup of the film: the face-reveal two-shot, both versions (table look-up for the quiz cut, golden-hour turn for the follow cut).

**3. Errands:** children's medicine aisle at CVS or Walgreens, shelf pans and the bottle-in-hand-to-lens shot, no faces (Beat 7), plus the dosage-panel macro of a children's chewable next to an adult 500 mg panel while you're there (Beat 10), caplet macros at home.

**4. Archive pulls:** childhood photos of you two, family photos with the sisters, one warm sister photo, rejection emails and call logs, agreement signature page, facility photos from the manufacturer.

**Post:** the four graphics (pill counter, odometer map, 40-years timeline, struggle-video collage), quiz screen recording, caption chips, black cards, and both end cards (quiz cut and follow cut).

## HARD RULES

1. FACELESS RULE: the founders' faces never appear in filmed footage until the face reveal at the end of the CTA. Backs, hands, over-the-shoulder, silhouettes only. If a face sneaks into a take, crop it or reshoot. Archive photos are exempt. The hook girl's face is allowed throughout.
2. Announcement framing only. No taste claims, no "it works," no testimonials. Nothing exists in hand yet.
3. Never claim we invented or own the formula. "Partner" is the word, framed as the plan.
4. Sisters: real details only, both consent before faces or photos appear, no product opinions in their mouths.
5. Never say "FDA approved." Only "FDA-registered drug manufacturer," pending legal check.
6. Don't name Tylenol on camera. "The pill in your cabinet" carries it. The medicine cabinet insert in Beat 9 uses a generic bottle with the label turned away.
7. Recreate the hook clip. Never repost the original creator's footage.
8. Manufacturer name on camera needs two confirmations: the agreement allows it, and the exact name.
9. "It had to actually taste good" is a standard you set, never a claim it delivers. No tasting footage of Orelli appears anywhere; the taste gate is unpassed.
