## Dimension 1: Who She Is

**She is global, young, and she found this post through a person, not a product.** The section is dominated by women in their late teens to early 30s who follow Leana Deeb as an identity figure. The single most commercially important comment in the section:

> "never tried anything from bloom and will be doing so js cuz of u" — @sbittercookies

That is the entire mechanism in one line. No ingredient question, no price question, no efficacy question. Creator trust converts to first purchase directly. This validates the Orelli affiliate motion as designed: the announcement format works because the audience buys the person, and the product rides along.

**The Muslim wellness girl is a real, visible segment.** Leana Deeb's audience surfaces her loudly: "Are these HALAL?", "MashaAllah" comments throughout, "Do we have it in Dubai?". For Orelli this is actionable, not trivia. A chewable format raises the gelatin question immediately for this segment. If the chew is pectin-based or otherwise halal-clean, that is a stated, checkable purchase gate we can answer preemptively in FAQ and creator briefs. If it is gelatin-based, expect the question in every comment section and have the sourcing answer ready.

**She is international and blocked by shipping.** Brazil, Canada, Dubai, Europe all begged in the comments ("Bring to Brazil 🇧🇷 Pleaseaa", "when are they finally coming to Europppppeeeee"). Orelli is US-only. The same begging will appear under Orelli creator posts. Do not treat it as a problem, it reads as demand proof to every US viewer scrolling past it.

## Dimension 2: How She Buys

**The object is the product.** The most engaged non-creator comments are about the can and the color, not the drink: "this can is too much 😍", "the pink is just wow", "Pink 🥤 so sweet". Three separate commenters asked where the creator's *sweater* was from. She is shopping the entire frame. The product must survive as an accessory inside an outfit post. For Orelli, packaging is a conversion asset with the same weight as the formulation, and creator briefs should style the jar into the shot the way Bloom's can was styled into a fit pic.

**Flavor names are identity, not description.** "Strawberry rose" did the work here. "Berry flavored anything has my heart OMG PLEASE", "apple crisp... that's a STAPLE 🤌🏼". She collects flavors, ranks them against each other, and declares allegiance to them in public. Orelli flavor naming deserves the same romantic register, and flavor drops are a repeatable content event, not a line extension.

**She tolerates preorder friction when the want is high.** "It doesnt ship for 1-2 months 😢" sat in the section and people kept ordering. "I'm trying so hard to not drink my case 😭😭" shows the end state: case purchase, self-rationing, the product as a treat she manages. Both behaviors map directly onto the Orelli preorder launch. The Dec/Jan preorder window is not a handicap with this buyer, it is how her favorite brands already behave.

**She expects retail eventually.** "When would they be releasing them in stores?" DTC-only is accepted for hype products in their launch era. No urgency here, just a note that this ICP treats DTC as chapter one.

## Dimension 3: What She Fears

This is the layer that matters most for an OTC drug, and it runs quietly under all the hype.

**Health anxiety is present, public, and directed at the community, not the brand.** Four separate comments in a 90-comment hype section:

> "They said it is not healthy is that true" — @zainab.nabil19
> "does anyone know if it has stevia in it? i want to try but stevia is a migraine trigger for me" — @heartlikekhadija
> "Gives me a tummy ache as opposed to the powder 😢" — @crescentkunoichi
> (French) "I was a fan but when I see how many people needed vascular surgery because of these drinks I'm scared" — @layda_reader

Notice the pattern. She asks strangers in the comments, she cites secondhand scares ("they said"), and she parses delivery formats against her own body (ready-to-drink vs powder, stevia as a personal trigger). This ICP already thinks in formats and ingredients even while buying on aesthetics. For Orelli, a 500mg acetaminophen chew will draw the same class of questions louder, because it is a drug: "is this real medicine?", "is chewable acetaminophen safe?", "what's in the chew besides the acetaminophen?". These need seeded answers in captions, pinned comments, and creator FAQ before launch, because the section will answer them itself if we don't, with "they said it is not healthy."

**Ad-literacy is high and priced in.** The single most-liked comment (12 likes, more than double anything else): "I don't really like it's just marketing." She knows a #partner post is an ad and discounts it, and the post still converted the room. The lesson is the one already encoded in the Orelli announcement-not-testimonial law: polished sponsored gloss gets the marketing discount, personal announcement energy gets the parasocial pass. This section is direct evidence the law is correct.

## Dimension 4: The Negative Space

What is completely absent from 90 comments is as diagnostic as what is present.

- **Zero efficacy talk.** Nobody asked whether the energy drink gives energy. The functional benefit is assumed the moment the trusted creator holds it. Orelli parallel: nobody will ask whether acetaminophen relieves pain. Efficacy argument is wasted airtime with this buyer. Sell the format, the flavor, the object, the identity. (Claims stay clean regardless, it is a drug.)
- **Zero price talk.** Not one comment about cost. $29.99 will not be litigated in the comment section. Price objections for this ICP live at checkout, not in public.
- **Zero comparison shopping.** No one named Celsius, Alani, or any competitor. She is not cross-shopping in public, she is deciding whether this object belongs in her life.

## The Composite Picture

Put the four dimensions together and the Bloom buyer, who is the Orelli buyer, looks like this: a young woman who discovers products through creators she has a real relationship with, decides in seconds based on whether the object is beautiful and the flavor name sings, carries a low hum of ingredient anxiety she voices to the community rather than the brand, knows she is being advertised to and buys anyway when the delivery feels personal, tolerates preorders and shipping waits for products that feel like drops, and never once asks whether the product works or what it costs.

## What This Changes for Orelli

1. **Creator brief addition:** style the jar into the outfit/frame, never present it clinically. The packaging shot is the ad.
2. **Pre-seed the anxiety layer.** Draft and pin answers for "is this safe", "is chewable the same dose", "what else is in it", and the halal/gelatin question before the first creator post goes live.
3. **Flavor naming is a launch lever.** Romantic two-word names in the strawberry-rose register, and future flavors are content events.
4. **Preorder posture is native.** The Dec/Jan window mirrors how this ICP's favorite brands already launch. International demand comments are free social proof, leave them up.
5. **Do not argue efficacy in creator content.** The beachhead doc's format-friction insight carries the whole sale, this section confirms nothing else needs defending.

## Caveats

Leana Deeb's audience overweights the Muslim wellness segment relative to Bloom's general buyer base. For a fuller picture, the natural next mines are @bloomsupps' own post comments (brand-side sections carry more complaint VOC) and Bloom's TikTok comment sections, which run more candid than IG.
