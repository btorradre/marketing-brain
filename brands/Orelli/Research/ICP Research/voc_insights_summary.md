# Voice of Customer Insights: Pill-Swallowing & Gummy Preference

I have compiled a comprehensive Voice of Customer (VoC) database by scraping **3,457 real comments** across four major platforms. This includes 1,000 comments from YouTube videos on "how to swallow pills", 1,000 comments from TikTok medical and wellness creators discussing pill anxiety, 996 comments from Reddit threads discussing difficulty swallowing pills, and 461 comments from Instagram reels about pill-swallowing hacks and gummy vitamins.

### The Choking and Gagging Trauma
The most common language used by consumers isn't about the taste of the medication—it is overwhelmingly about the physical sensation and the fear of choking. Consumers frequently express sentiments like, "I really can't do this. I'm terrified of choking. I end up only swallowing the water." Another common experience is the physiological anxiety response: "Bro I always act confident when I’m about to swallow a pill, but once the pill is in my mouth my heart starts beating like crazy." Many also note the psychological block, stating, "It’s so weird. I can accidentally swallow chewing gum but can’t swallow a tiny pill." The marketing implication here is that the primary angle shouldn't just be that the product "tastes better." Instead, the messaging must emphasize "no more anxiety, no more gagging, and no more fighting your own throat."

### The Secret Shame Factor
Many adults feel deep, lingering shame about not being able to swallow pills, a feeling that often stems from childhood trauma of parents yelling at them to take their medication. The database is filled with confessions such as, "I'm a 29 year old and still struggling 😭😭" and "Who else has their parents yelling at them to swallow a stinking pill." Seeing others share this struggle provides immense relief, as evidenced by comments like, "These comments are making me feel better about not being able to swallow pills." This data validates that normalization is a powerful marketing tool. The confessional creator angle—focusing on "things I thought only I struggled with"—will resonate deeply with this audience.

### Desperate Workarounds
People are going to extreme lengths to avoid swallowing pills whole, highlighting a massive gap in the market for alternative formats. Consumers report, "48 here, i crush, chew or liquid," and "I personally think every medication should be available in liquid form. The tiniest tablet is scary to me." One of the most visceral pain points described is the taste of a dissolving pill: "The worse part is when you are trying to swallow and keep drinking water and it just stays there in your mouth. Then after a few seconds it starts to dissolve and you taste all that nastiness..." Orelli's soft chew should be positioned as the ultimate, dignified upgrade from crushing pills into applesauce or enduring the bitter taste of a dissolving tablet.

### The Medical Reality of Dysphagia
A significant portion of the Reddit comments highlighted actual medical conditions, such as Eosinophilic Esophagitis (EOE) or dysphagia. Users shared serious struggles: "I have eosinophilic esophagitis. Food and pills can get stuck in my throat very easily. When I say stuck, I mean even water won't go down." While your primary Ideal Customer Profile is the everyday wellness consumer seeking an aesthetic format upgrade, there is a highly motivated sub-segment of people with actual swallowing disorders. This demographic will purchase Orelli's soft chews out of pure medical necessity, providing a strong secondary market.
