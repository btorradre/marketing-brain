# Orelli — Angle & Design Matrix

Four angles, mined from the ICP research (VoC DB ~4K comments, brand consensus, beachhead). One **umbrella** angle (convenience) + three **emotional spears**. Each is a *specific situation in her mind*, not an abstract benefit. Every angle still resolves to the same line: **"It's literally just Tylenol. In a gummy."**

## Angle architecture (which buyer each indexes)

| | Angle | Buyer it indexes | Role |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| **0 — MAIN** | **Convenience** | Situational + broad (incl. casual strugglers) | The umbrella. Broadest net, lowest threat, "oh cool." |
| **1** | **The Choking Fear** | The dispositional sufferer (our locked avatar) | Deep spear — visceral, highest emotional charge. |
| **2** | **The Adult Shame** | The dispositional sufferer | Deep spear — identity + dignity + "being seen." |
| **3** | **The Untreated Pain** | The dispositional sufferer | Deep spear — the *cost* of avoidance, raises stakes. |

> Convenience catches everyone and reads as a product, not a confession. The three spears convert the high-intent avatar by naming a private situation she's never seen named. Run convenience as the broad always-on; rotate the spears as targeted ad sets.

---

## ANGLE 0 — CONVENIENCE (main / umbrella)

**The situation in her mind:** A headache hits and there's no water — at her desk, in the car, mid-flight, at the gym. Or she just doesn't want to do the whole production. *"People who swallow pills without water got me shook."* (6,300 likes) · *"the only time I've been able to swallow a pill was on an airplane and I was crying there too."*

- **Emotional driver:** ease / relief from friction. Low stakes, high relatability.
- **Internal monologue:** *"Wait, I can just… eat it? No water, no thinking about it?"*
- **Awareness:** most-aware — barely needs setup, lead with the product.
- **Hooks:**
  - "It's literally just Tylenol. In a gummy. No water, take it anywhere."
  - "POV: headache hits, zero water in sight. You're completely fine now."
  - "Adult pain relief you can take at your desk, in the car, mid-flight. No glass of water required."

## ANGLE 1 — THE CHOKING FEAR (spear)

**The situation in her mind:** The pill is sitting at the back of her throat and it *won't go down*. Her throat closes, her heart races, she gags, sometimes brings it back up. Her brain red-flags it as choking. *"I'm terrified of choking. I end up only swallowing the water."* (6,300) · *"Therapist: what's your biggest fear? Me: swallowing a pill."* (1,600) · *"My throat closes whenever I try to swallow one."* · *"then it melts in my mouth and makes me vomit."*

- **Emotional driver:** fear → release. The highest-charge theme in the entire dataset.
- **Internal monologue:** *"It's not that I won't. My body physically won't let me."*
- **Awareness:** problem-aware — open on Recognition, never on product.
- **Hooks:**
  - "POV: the pill's at the back of your throat and it just… won't go down."
  - "'I'm terrified of choking, I end up only swallowing the water.' If that's you—"
  - "Your throat closing up isn't willpower. It's a reflex. So stop fighting it." [chews gummy]

## ANGLE 2 — THE ADULT SHAME (spear)

**The situation in her mind:** She's a grown woman secretly buying children's grape medicine, hiding it from her doctor, mocked by her family. *"It's so tiring to have people make fun of you for not being able to swallow medicines."* (490) · *"I'm ashamed I'm already 19 and my younger brother does it effortlessly."* (831) · *"so embarrassed to tell my doctor I can't swallow pills."* · *"the bottle just thinks I'm a chubby 12-year-old."*

- **Emotional driver:** shame → vindication / dignity. The "being seen" conversion trigger.
- **Internal monologue:** *"I thought I was the only adult who still can't do this."*
- **Awareness:** problem-aware — Recognition → Relief ("you're not the only one") → permission.
- **Hooks:**
  - "POV: you're a grown adult sneaking children's grape Tylenol into your cart."
  - "I'm 24, I'm a nurse, and I still can't swallow a pill. Turns out it was never me."
  - "You're not childish. You were just never given an adult version."

## ANGLE 3 — THE UNTREATED PAIN (spear)

**The situation in her mind:** She's sick or in pain and she just… doesn't take anything, because the pill is scarier than the pain. She suffers through the headache, quits the antibiotics, tells herself she's "not that sick." *"I'd rather suffer than have to swallow one."* · *"sometimes I don't take any to convince myself I'm not sick."* · *"I'd rather deal with the most disgusting liquid than swallow a large pill."* (151)

- **Emotional driver:** defeat → resolution. Raises the stakes — this is what avoidance *costs* her.
- **Internal monologue:** *"I've let a whole day get ruined because the pill scared me more than the pain."*
- **Awareness:** problem-aware, but she's normalized the cost — name the cost first.
- **Hooks:**
  - "Be honest: how many times have you chosen the headache because the pill was worse?"
  - "I let migraines ruin entire days rather than fight a pill down. Not anymore."
  - "Stop choosing the pain. The pill was the only problem — we fixed the pill."

> **Cross-cutting proof element (use in all four):** taste. The VoC is full of *"it dissolves and you taste all that nastiness"* / *"makes me vomit."* A real first-bite reaction ("wait, this actually tastes good") is the single strongest proof beat across every angle — it pre-empts the #1 objection. See [taste dependency in strategy doc].

## How to use this matrix

- **Always-on = Angle 0.** It's the umbrella, lowest production friction, broadest reach. Most volume here.
- **Spears = Angles 1–3**, run as distinct ad sets / creator briefs so the emotional register stays pure (don't blend a shame hook into a convenience vignette — it blunts both, per the convenience-vs-anxiety split).
- **Test grid:** each column is a shippable creative concept. Brief one creator per angle to start (4 originals), read which *situation* gets the strongest "that's me" comment response, then pour volume into the winner's column.
- **Taste proof rides every angle** — it's the universal objection-killer, not its own angle.
- Every angle lands on the same line so the brand stays one thing no matter which door she comes through.
