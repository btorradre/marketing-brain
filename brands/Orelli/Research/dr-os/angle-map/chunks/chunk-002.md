# Orelli Beachhead Market Definition

**Product:** OTC adult-strength acetaminophen soft chew (500mg), $29.99, US-only DTC subscription  
**Stage 1 Message:** "It's all in the gummy."

---

## The Beachhead: Who Buys First

### One-Sentence Definition

**The woman who already takes OTC pain relief multiple times per month but actively dreads the act of swallowing the pill — and has never been told an alternative format exists.**

She is not a "wellness enthusiast browsing for the next supplement." She is a **current, frequent OTC pain relief buyer** who has a specific, visceral friction with the delivery format. The product does not need to create demand for pain relief — demand already exists. It only needs to present a format she did not know was available.

---

| Attribute | Beachhead Specification | Why This Narrows the Market |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Gender** | Female | Women are 1.5x more likely to use OTC analgesics than men due to menstrual pain, migraine prevalence, and higher healthcare engagement [1][2] |
| **Age** | 28–38 | Old enough to have an established, monthly pill-taking routine she resents; young enough to live on TikTok/Instagram where creators reach her [3] |
| **Income** | $65K–$130K household | Can absorb $29.99/month without deliberation; already pays convenience premiums (Instacart, pre-made meals, premium skincare) |
| **Geography** | US suburban/tier-2 metro | Austin, Charlotte, Denver, Nashville, Raleigh — high DTC penetration, high social media engagement, lower CPMs if you ever layer paid |
| **Life Stage** | Working professional or early-stage mom (0–2 kids) | Her schedule is compressed; she reaches for pain relief reactively and wants zero friction |

## Why She Cares (The Specific Pain)

This is not a "nice to have" product for her. She has a **compounding daily frustration** that she has normalized but never solved:

**The Core Insight from VoC Data:**

> "I'm literally an adult and I still can't swallow pills without gagging. I thought I'd grow out of it. I didn't." — YouTube comment, 321 likes

> "With all of the technology we have in 2018 you would think they would have better tasting meds now." — YouTube comment, 321 likes

> "I would rather get punched in the face than take my medicine." — YouTube comment, 100 likes

**Why she cares, quantified:**

1. **Up to 40% of US adults report difficulty swallowing pills** [4][5]. Among women, the prevalence skews higher due to smaller esophageal diameter and higher anxiety sensitivity around gagging [6].

2. **She is not non-compliant by choice.** Research shows that 32% of adults without diagnosed dysphagia still have "current medication swallowing difficulties" [7]. These people skip doses, crush pills into food, or use children's liquid formulations — all of which she finds embarrassing.

3. **The shame is the lock-in.** From our VoC scrape: the dominant emotional theme is not "I wish pills tasted better" — it is "I thought I was the only adult who still can't do this." The moment she sees a creator say "wait, you can't swallow pills either?" she feels seen. That is the conversion trigger.

**Her specific workarounds today (from VoC data):**

| Workaround | Frequency in VoC Comments | Emotional Tone |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Crushing pills into applesauce/yogurt | Very common | Embarrassment ("I feel like a child") |
| Using a specific soda/juice to force it down | Common | Frustration ("I have a whole system") |
| Cutting pills in half | Common | Resignation |
| Using children's liquid medicine | Moderate | Shame ("I'm 30 and I buy kids' Tylenol") |
| Simply skipping the dose / suffering through pain | Very common | Defeat ("I just deal with the headache") |
| Gagging/vomiting when attempting | Moderate | Visceral distress |

## How Often She Uses Pain Relief (Frequency = LTV)

This is the critical economic question: is she a one-time buyer or a subscriber?

**The data says she is a high-frequency user:**

| Data Point | Source |
|:---|:---|
| **23% of US adults use acetaminophen-containing medicine every week** | Consumer Healthcare Products Association [8] |
| **52 million Americans** use acetaminophen weekly | CHPA [8] |
| **84% of women experience menstrual pain**, with 43% reporting it occurs every period | BMC Women's Health meta-analysis [9] |
| **42% of women with menstrual migraine** use only OTC medications | AAN 2024 [10] |
| **22% of female college students** report pain on a daily or weekly basis | Journal of the American Pharmacists Association [11] |
| **80% of Americans** routinely reach for OTC pain pills for headaches, backaches, sore muscles | STAT News / Boston University [12] |
| **79% of OTC pain reliever users** say their usage has stayed the same or increased over time | CHPA 2022 survey [13] |

**What this means for Orelli's beachhead:**

The beachhead customer is not an occasional user. She takes OTC pain relief **2–4 times per week** across a rotating set of triggers:

- Headaches (tension, screen-related, hormonal) — 2–3x/week
- Menstrual cramps — 3–5 days/month, multiple doses per day
- Post-workout soreness — 1–2x/week
- Hangover recovery — 2–4x/month
- General body aches from desk work — ongoing

At $29.99/month for a subscription, she is paying roughly **$1/day** for a format that eliminates a friction she experiences multiple times per week. The value proposition is immediate and obvious: she already spends $8–$15/month on generic Tylenol that she hates taking. Orelli is a **$15–$20/month premium** for a dramatically better experience.

**Projected subscription retention:** High. Unlike supplements (where efficacy is debatable and churn is 40–60% by month 3), OTC pain relief has **immediate, undeniable efficacy**. She knows it works. The only question was the format. Once she switches, there is no reason to switch back.

## Why She Is Reachable (Channel Strategy)

The beachhead customer is not just identifiable — she is **actively self-selecting on social media right now.** Here is why she is reachable through a creator-led motion:

### 1. She Already Tells the Internet About This Problem

From our VoC scrape: there are **millions of views** on "can't swallow pills" content across TikTok and YouTube. This is not a latent, hidden problem — people are actively creating and engaging with content about it. The top videos have 1M–10M+ views. The comments are filled with people saying "I thought I was the only one."

This means:
- **Organic discovery is built in.** A creator who says "I can't swallow pills and I just found this" will get algorithmic distribution because the topic already has proven engagement.
- **Comment sections are lead lists.** Every person who comments "OMG ME TOO" on a pill-swallowing video is a qualified prospect.

### 2. She Lives on TikTok and Instagram (The Exact Platforms Where Creator Seeding Works)

| Platform | Her Age Band (28–38) | Usage Pattern |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **TikTok** | 34% of users are 25–34 [14]; she spends 95 min/day average | Discovery platform — she finds new products here via FYP |
| **Instagram Reels** | 55% of US users are female [15]; 28–38 is the core demo | Validation platform — she checks a brand's IG before buying |
| **YouTube** | Highest reach among 25–34 women | Long-form trust — she watches "honest reviews" before subscribing |

### 3. The Content Format That Converts Her Already Exists

She responds to **confessional/relatable content** — the exact format that Nano and Micro creators produce naturally:

- "POV: you're 30 and still can't swallow a pill"
- "Things I thought were normal but apparently aren't"
- "My embarrassing health hack I've never told anyone"
- "Wait… this exists??" (the discovery reaction)

These are **not scripted ads.** They are the organic content format that TikTok's algorithm rewards. A seeded creator who genuinely cannot swallow pills will produce this content authentically — no brief required.

### 4. She Is Cheap to Reach at Scale

| Reachability Factor | Why It Matters |
|:---|:---|
| **Self-identifying hashtags** | #cantswallowpills, #pillhack, #adultproblems — she is already tagged |
| **Low creator cost** | Nano/Micro creators (1K–50K) accept free product + 15–20% commission; no upfront fee |
| **High share rate** | Identity-driven content ("this is SO me") gets shared to friends via DM — free amplification |
| **No education required** | Stage 1 message means the ad IS the product explanation: "It's a gummy. It's pain relief. Done." |
| **Subscription-native audience** | She already subscribes to 2–4 DTC products (skincare, coffee, etc.); the subscription model is not a barrier |

### 5. The Funnel Is One Step

Unlike complex DTC funnels that require quiz → email → nurture → convert, Orelli's beachhead funnel is:

**Creator video → "Wait, this exists?" → Click link → Buy.**

There is no objection to overcome. She already wants pain relief. She already hates pills. The only information she needs is: "An adult chewable pain reliever now exists." That is the entire ad. That is the entire funnel.

## Summary: The Beachhead in One Paragraph

Orelli's beachhead customer is a US woman aged 28–38 earning $65K+ who takes OTC pain relief 2–4 times per week (headaches, cramps, soreness) and has struggled to swallow pills since childhood. She has never been told an adult chewable exists. She is reachable because she already self-identifies on TikTok and Instagram through confessional content about her "embarrassing" pill workarounds, she responds to relatable Nano/Micro creators who share her experience, and the conversion requires zero education — only awareness that the product exists. Her usage frequency (weekly+) and the undeniable efficacy of acetaminophen make her a high-retention subscriber. The entire go-to-market is: seed creators who genuinely share her problem, let them say "wait, this exists," and capture the demand that is already there.
