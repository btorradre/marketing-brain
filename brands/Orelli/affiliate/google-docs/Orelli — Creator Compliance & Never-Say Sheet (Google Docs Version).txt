ORELLI — CREATOR COMPLIANCE & NEVER-SAY SHEET

Creator-facing. This is not an attachment — it is a signed part of the Founding Creator agreement. No affiliate code is issued until it's acknowledged.


READ THIS PART FIRST

Orelli is not a supplement or a wellness product. It is an over-the-counter drug, regulated by the FDA. The rules for talking about it are stricter than anything you've done for a skincare or supplement brand, and the penalties are real — not "the brand gets a warning email" real. Fines for fake or unsubstantiated testimonials run up to $51,744 per violation, and they can land on both the brand and the creator.

None of this makes your video harder to make. It mostly just means your video is about you, and the product gets the last eight seconds. Which is the version that performs better anyway.

Three rules. That's it.


1. RULE 1 — YOU HAVE NOT TRIED IT, SO YOU CANNOT REVIEW IT

This is the big one.

Federal law, 16 CFR 255.1, says: "Endorsements must reflect the honest opinions, findings, beliefs, or experience of the endorser." And: "When the advertisement represents that the endorser uses the endorsed product, the endorser must have been a bona fide user of it at the time the endorsement was given."

Orelli does not ship until December. Nobody on earth has tried it yet — including us. So no video, caption, comment, or story can say or imply that you have.

You are making an announcement, not a review.

Completely fine, all true, paired against what creates real liability:
•  "I can't swallow pills" — never "I tried it" or "I've been using this."
•  "I've been buying children's medicine for years" — never "It tastes like candy" or "It tastes great."
•  "Someone finally made adult Tylenol as a gummy" — never "It actually works" or "It killed my headache."
•  "It's 500mg, same as Extra Strength Tylenol" — never "This is my new go-to."
•  "I preordered it" (only if you actually did) — never "It's so much better than Tylenol."
•  "It ships in December and I'm counting down" — never film yourself eating or holding a bottle.

If someone asks in the comments "does it taste good?" — the honest answer, and the only allowed one: "No idea yet honestly, it doesn't ship till December. I preordered because I finally found something I can actually take."

That answer performs better than a fake one. It reads as real, because it is.

Also: don't fake having the product. No holding a bottle, no eating a gummy, no unboxing, no "look what came in the mail." Use the product images from the Asset Pack, edited into your video. Everyone understands what a screenshot is.


2. RULE 2 — DISCLOSE, EVERY SINGLE TIME

You earn commission, so there is a material connection and it has to be disclosed. This is true even though we're not sending you free product.

Do all three:
1. In the caption — #ad or #affiliate, in the first line, before the "…more" cutoff. Not buried at the end of a hashtag pile.
2. On screen — a visible text overlay somewhere in the first few seconds. "#ad" is enough.
3. Use TikTok's built-in toggle — mark the post as a paid promotion or branded content in the posting flow.

Not acceptable: "sp," "collab," "thanks Orelli," "partner," disclosure only in a comment, or disclosure that requires tapping to see.

Say it out loud if it fits your style — "I'm working with them, obviously." It costs nothing and audiences respond well to it.


3. RULE 3 — ONLY SAY WHAT THE LABEL SAYS

Orelli's Drug Facts label allows exactly two claims:

Temporarily relieves minor aches and pains.
Temporarily reduces fever.

That's the complete list. You can describe the format freely — chewable, no water, adult 500mg dose, tastes like a gummy (as a description of what it is, not a review of how it tastes).

Never say, no exceptions:
•  "Cures," "heals," or "treats" anything. Reason: beyond the label.
•  "Works faster, better, or stronger than Tylenol." Reason: it's the identical molecule. Our angle is format, not potency.
•  "Safe for pregnancy," or any pregnancy claim. Reason: requires clinical substantiation we don't have.
•  "Safe for kids," or any child dosing. Reason: this is a 500mg adult dose.
•  Any dosing instruction beyond the label. Reason: dosing is the label's job, not yours.
•  "Take it with [other medication]." Reason: interaction advice is practicing medicine.
•  "Helps with anxiety, sleep, hangovers, or cramps" as a cure. Reason: off-label. "I get period cramps" is fine as your story; "it treats cramps" is not.
•  "Doctor recommended" or "clinically proven." Reason: not substantiated.
•  "All natural" or "chemical-free." Reason: it's acetaminophen.
•  Comparing to a competitor by name, negatively. Reason: legal exposure.

Saying "Tylenol" is fine and encouraged, as a factual comparison of the active ingredient. "It's the same active ingredient as Tylenol" is true and legal. "It's better than Tylenol" is not.


4. RULE 4 — PREORDER TERMS MUST BE CLEAR

People are paying $29.99 and up for something that arrives in months. Federal rules (16 CFR 435) require the timing to be unmistakable at the point of sale, and we need it in your content too.

Every video must include, on screen or spoken: "Ships December 2026."

Every caption must include: "Preorder — ships December 2026. Cancel anytime before shipping for a full refund."

Never say "get yours now," "in stock," "selling out," "shipping fast," or anything implying immediate delivery. The word "preorder" or "preordering" should appear in the caption.

Scarcity is fine if it's true. "Founding price ends Friday" is fine when it does. Made-up urgency is not.


5. THE 60-SECOND PRE-POST CHECKLIST

Before you hit post, confirm each of these:
•  Nothing in this video says or implies I've tried it.
•  No shot of me holding or eating the product.
•  #ad is in the first line of the caption.
•  #ad is visible on screen.
•  TikTok's branded-content toggle is on.
•  The only claim made is "relieves minor aches and pains" or "reduces fever" — or no claim at all.
•  No "better than Tylenol," no pregnancy, no kids, no dosing advice.
•  "Ships December 2026" is on screen or spoken.
•  Caption says "preorder" and mentions the refund.
•  My link and code are in the pinned comment.
•  The comment is actually pinned.


6. WHAT HAPPENS IF A POST ISN'T COMPLIANT

This is not a punishment system. It's that non-compliant posts create legal exposure for both of us.

1. We'll message you and ask for a specific fix, usually adding a caption line — 30 seconds of work.
2. Fix it and everything continues normally. This is what happens about 95% of the time. Genuinely, nobody gets in trouble for a missing hashtag they then add.
3. If it can't be fixed — for example, the video says you tried it — we'll ask you to take it down. Commission on that post is withheld.
4. Repeat issues after being asked end the partnership.

Your first post gets reviewed before we promote or repost it. Send it to us when it's up. Fast turnaround, usually within a couple of hours.


7. THE SHORT VERSION

•  You haven't tried it. Don't say you have. Announce, don't review.
•  #ad in the first line of the caption, and on screen.
•  "Relieves minor aches and pains" or "reduces fever." Nothing else.
•  "Ships December 2026," visible or spoken, plus "preorder" in the caption.
•  Your story is unlimited. Tell it however you want, as long as it's true.

Rule 5 isn't a rule, it's the point: the part of your video that's actually yours — the struggle, the shame, the kids' medicine in the cart — has no restrictions on it at all. That's the part that converts anyway.
