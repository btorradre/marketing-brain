# Research, Positioning, and Testing

## Evidence collection

Collect before synthesizing. Preserve raw material and provenance.

Useful sources include:

- current customers: support tickets, reviews, post-purchase surveys, interviews, calls, returns, cancellations;
- market conversations: forums, Reddit, comments, Q&A, search queries, communities;
- competitors: ads, landing pages, offers, reviews, active duration, funnel structure;
- product truth: formulation, specifications, manufacturing, pricing, guarantees, inventory, studies;
- performance: creative-level spend, delivery, attention, click, page, checkout, purchase, retention, refund, and chargeback data.

Tag competitor language separately. A competitor claim is not customer evidence and not product truth.

## Avatar synthesis

Weight evidence toward:

- specific pain and emotional cost;
- recurring language and metaphors;
- routines, triggers, failed solutions, and purchase moments;
- identity, feared future, desired future, and social consequences;
- awareness, sophistication, objections, and trust history.

Forum contributors often skew more aware than cold traffic. Distinguish the vocal expert from the quieter majority. Never invent a persona detail merely because it would improve a story.

## Product-market fit and positioning

A viable opening needs demand, a real product advantage, favorable economics, a communicable mechanism, and executable creative.

Common entry plays include:

- proven product or ingredient for an underserved sub-avatar;
- familiar avatar with a genuinely different mechanism;
- a better form factor that removes a recurring routine or compliance failure;
- a companion to an expanding behavior or category;
- a category reframe or identity position supported by product truth.

Evaluate demand trajectory, competitive density, pain intensity, timing, margins, retention potential, operational feasibility, and cross-channel fit. Compare multiple candidate niches rather than rationalizing the first idea.

The avatar-first test is whether the top pains, beliefs, vocabulary, product form, and friend-retellable explanation align. If the customer cannot repeat the reason why in her own language, the position is not finished.

## Angle discovery and saturation

An angle is not a topic or hook format. Cluster competitor creative by the emotional or explanatory doorway it uses. Scan the actual ad and page, not only the headline.

A white-space hypothesis needs:

- demonstrated demand;
- weak or absent competitive supply;
- a direct product connection;
- customer language supporting it;
- compliance and proof feasibility;
- enough variants to scale without mimicking one ad.

“Nobody is running it” can mean opportunity or lack of demand. Evidence decides.

## From winner to learning

For each winner, record:

- sub-avatar and awareness;
- angle and concept separately;
- hook type and opening loop;
- mechanism and solution criteria;
- emotional engine and credibility source;
- format, funnel destination, offer, and close;
- performance by the metric that matches its job;
- what was actually changed from the previous control.

Document transferable roles, not a sentence template. A winner can donate a hook, angle, format, proof device, visual, or close to a new test without cloning the whole ad.

## Creative velocity

Run a portfolio of controlled learning:

- exploit proven families with disciplined variants;
- reserve capacity for genuinely new angles, formats, and personas;
- change one primary axis per iteration when the goal is causal learning;
- create a new concept ID when multiple load-bearing variables change;
- use consistent naming and a tracker that connects creative inputs to funnel outcomes.

Engagement winners can donate hooks to conversion winners with weak openings. Conversion winners with weak attention can receive hook and first-frame tests. Do not discard a strong body because the hook failed.

## Test statement

Before production, write:

`We are testing [one change] because [evidence-backed hypothesis]. It should improve [specific stage metric] without materially damaging [downstream guardrail]. We will decide after [sufficient spend/time/sample].`

Thresholds come from brand economics, account history, audience temperature, placement, and test maturity. Do not universalize one account's CPA, CTR, hook-rate, or duration rule.

## Diagnosis-to-action map

- Weak first seconds or low qualified CTR: inspect angle, awareness match, hook, first frame, and promise.
- Strong hook but weak hold: inspect lead congruence, repetition, open loops, and delayed payload.
- Strong attention and click, weak page action: inspect technical LP views first, then promise and page congruence.
- Strong page action, weak ATC: inspect product bridge, offer, proof, and PDP continuity.
- Strong ATC, weak purchase: inspect shipping, price surprise, trust, UX, payment, and guarantee visibility.
- Strong first purchase, weak retention or high refunds: inspect expectation setting, product experience, fulfillment, and customer quality—not merely acquisition copy.

Fix the first bottleneck. Preserve working upstream signals while testing the suspected failure.
