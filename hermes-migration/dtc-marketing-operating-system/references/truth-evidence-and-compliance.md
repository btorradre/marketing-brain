# Truth, Evidence, and Compliance

## Non-negotiable evidence boundary

Use only facts supported by the current product truth, actual offer, verified source material, and applicable law and platform policy.

Never fabricate or imply the existence of:

- customers, testimonials, comments, review counts, community size, transformations, or survey results;
- doctors, researchers, journalists, founders, patients, bylines, credentials, quotes, or personal stories;
- studies, journal names, sample sizes, percentages, charts, pathology images, laboratory results, or citations;
- inventory constraints, deadlines, price history, sales volume, guarantees, bonuses, or scarcity;
- editorial independence, press coverage, certification, manufacturing origin, or endorsements.

Do not convert an unsupported claim into “lived experience,” a quotation, a character's discovery, or a visual. The substantiation requirement follows the message.

## Product truth before persuasion

Before writing or approving work, verify:

- exact product, formula/specification, dose or materials, form factor, and intended use;
- current price, bundles, subscription, guarantee, shipping, inventory, and launch status;
- allowed and prohibited claims;
- verified proof and explicit proof gaps;
- current mechanism decision when brand files conflict;
- current visual identity and canonical product references.

If current brand files conflict on a load-bearing fact, stop that claim or mechanism decision and surface the conflict. Do not select the version that makes better copy.

## Health and wellness

Use current legal and platform guidance for the market and channel. As a baseline:

- do not claim to diagnose, cure, treat, or prevent disease unless the product and evidence legally support that claim;
- do not tell customers to stop prescribed medication or replace medical care;
- do not use fear of disease, false pathology, or fabricated medical imagery;
- distinguish structure/function or general-wellness support from disease treatment;
- represent studies accurately, including population, intervention, endpoints, limits, and relevance to the actual product;
- do not imply that ingredient-level evidence proves the finished product produces the same result;
- keep timelines, magnitude, and certainty within the evidence.

Plain language is not permission to make a stronger claim. Translate the supported claim without enlarging it.

## Authority and narrators

Authority may be supplied by a real expert, real evidence, product demonstration, transparent founder knowledge, or verified customer experience. Credentials must be accurate and relevant.

Never invent a doctor narrator, composite patient presented as real, or “breaking ranks” professional. A fictionalized dramatization must be clearly presented as such and still cannot carry unsupported claims.

The collapse test is useful: remove the authority figure. If the explanation has no evidence or logic left, the authority was decoration. Add actual support rather than a stronger costume.

## Advertorial and native format

Native readability means matching the medium's clarity, pacing, and information design. It does not mean disguising an advertisement as independent journalism.

- disclose sponsored or advertorial status where required;
- do not copy a real publication's trade dress in a misleading way;
- do not use fake staff bylines, editors, review widgets, comment sections, or media logos;
- make the commercial relationship clear before it would affect a reasonable reader's interpretation.

## Customer language and consent

Keep source records for customer quotes. Follow the brand's consent and privacy process before publishing identifiable material. Remove or anonymize sensitive details when the quote is used for internal strategy. Do not splice separate customers into one first-person testimonial presented as real.

## Competitive claims

Explain a competing approach's failure mode only when it is accurate, relevant, and fairly stated. Avoid blanket attacks on professions, categories, or medications. Comparison should identify the actual criterion where approaches differ.

Do not infer that a competitor's long-running ad proves its claim. Duration can be a creative signal; it is not substantiation.

## Urgency and risk reversal

Use urgency only when the condition is real and current. Product-derived timelines, actual inventory, scheduled price changes, shipping cutoffs, or a real enrollment window can be valid. Evergreen fake countdowns and repeated “last units” are not.

State guarantee terms accurately and prominently enough to be understood. Never invent a longer guarantee to match a biological timeline.

## Review checklist

- Every factual claim has a traceable source.
- The source supports the exact wording, magnitude, population, and timeframe.
- People, credentials, experiences, and social proof are real and authorized.
- The product shown and described is the current product.
- The ad format is transparent about its commercial nature.
- The mechanism is understandable without becoming stronger than the evidence.
- The close, price, guarantee, urgency, and CTA match the live offer.
