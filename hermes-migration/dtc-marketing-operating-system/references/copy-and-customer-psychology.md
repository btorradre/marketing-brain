# Copy and Customer Psychology

## The persuasion model

Direct response begins with a mass desire that already exists. The writer cannot create that desire; the writer channels it through one relevant promise, explanation, and action.

The practical customer journey is:

`lived problem → recognition → accepted explanation → solution criteria → product evaluation → proof → restored life`

This is often described as nightmare → reframe → dream. Use it as a diagnostic lens, not a mandatory story template. Product-aware and most-aware customers may need little nightmare material. A cold, dismissed customer may need more recognition before she accepts new information.

## The classic five beliefs, modernized

The five beliefs are useful inventory, not compulsory sections:

1. **The experience is real.** Validate only to the degree the customer needs. Specific scenes are stronger than symptom lists.
2. **The current approach is incomplete.** Explain the exact failure mode; do not merely declare that alternatives fail.
3. **A more complete cause or constraint exists.** Bridge from an accepted premise. Do not manufacture a contrarian reframe for drama.
4. **A credible solution must meet specific criteria.** Establish the standard while nothing is being sold.
5. **This product credibly meets that standard.** Use verified proof, fit, risk reversal, and an honest timeline.

For each belief, mark `held`, `uncertain`, `incomplete`, `opposed`, or `irrelevant`. Confirm held beliefs quickly. Clarify uncertain ones. Bridge incomplete ones. Do not attack opposed foundational beliefs without evidence and a gradual path. Omit irrelevant ones.

## Nightmare and dream scenes

Pain and aspiration should be visible. A useful scene contains a moment, object, behavior, internal line, and consequence. Match early deposits with later payoffs when the format supports it: the object, place, or relationship that represented the problem can return with changed meaning.

Do not force a mirror callback into direct offer copy. Use it when it produces genuine emotional completion.

## Mechanism language

The mechanism answers “why this problem persists” and “what a solution must do.” Build it from the customer outward:

1. Start with a familiar observation.
2. Add one missing causal link.
3. Show the consequence in physical, picturable language.
4. State the solution criterion.
5. Introduce the product only after the criterion is understandable.

Prefer `physical verb + plain outcome`: “pulls water in,” “slows the movement,” “keeps the material from drying out.” Use technical terms only when the audience already uses them or the term itself is valuable; define it immediately.

Avoid:

- Latin or clinical vocabulary used only to borrow authority;
- multiple named pathways when one causal chain is enough;
- contrived numbered frameworks the customer must memorize;
- human agency assigned to organs, ingredients, or abstract objects;
- “the logic is the proof.” Logic makes a claim understandable; evidence makes it credible.

## Angle, concept, hook, and lead

- **Desire:** the existing market force.
- **Angle:** the specific wound, scenario, fear, aspiration, or explanatory doorway.
- **Concept:** who is speaking, how the message is delivered, its emotional temperature, and its relationship to the product.
- **Hook:** the first delivery of the angle.
- **Lead:** the section that expands the hook's emotional and informational world.

The hook does not need to sell the product. It must earn the next line. Use at least two forms of specificity when natural: concrete detail, scene, dialogue evidence, time, quantity, behavior, or real credential.

The lead must maintain causal continuity. It may be narrative, identity-led, educational, or authority-led. Choose by awareness and concept, not by a fixed story rule.

## Copy spine

A flexible spine is:

`hook → problem world → failed approach/failure mode → root cause or constraint → solution criteria → product fit → proof/results → close`

Not every asset needs every beat. The line-level test is whether each section enters on one belief state and exits on the belief state the next section requires.

Product integration is earned when criteria exist. It may be:

- early and direct for product-aware traffic;
- woven through the second half for a comparison or demonstration;
- delayed in a discovery narrative;
- brief in an editorial continuation;
- immediate in a most-aware offer.

## Voice and anti-AI discipline

Write in the customer's cognitive vocabulary, not merely at a low reading level. Preserve sentence shapes and expressions heard in real source material without copying a single individual's story as the brand's story.

Remove:

- false-contrast stacks such as repeated “not X, but Y” constructions;
- three clipped sentences used as synthetic drama;
- repetitive rhetorical questions and section openers;
- parallel sentence stacks, throat-clearing, filler, and promotional adjectives;
- abstract claims the reader cannot picture;
- performed relatability, exaggerated punctuation, and generic reassurance.

Do not replace these artifacts with stiff prose. Spoken work needs connective tissue, fragments, contractions, and thinking-aloud moments. Written work needs clarity, causal momentum, and varied sentence length.

## Proof and results

Use a skeptical reader's standard:

- actual demonstrations and artifacts;
- sourced customer reports with consent;
- verified studies accurately represented;
- realistic timeframes and imperfect but honest progress;
- comparison to the criteria already established;
- a guarantee only when its terms are real.

Never invent an “imperfect” number to make a story sound credible. Never place a claim in a character's mouth to avoid substantiation.

## Close

The close resolves the asset's governing tension and gives the correct next action. Return to the angle or restored-life outcome when useful. Do not re-teach the mechanism or restart the pitch. CTA directness rises with awareness and funnel temperature.
