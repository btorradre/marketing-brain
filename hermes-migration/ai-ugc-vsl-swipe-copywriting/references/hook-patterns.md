# Hook patterns: select by audience question

Editorial interpretations of observed advertising, not measured causes of conversion. Choose the audience question and payoff together. [Nuora expansion](nuora-study/NUORA-ANALYSIS.md) explains the additional price, authenticity, offer and skepticism patterns.

## recognition-and-validation — Specific experience contradicts reassurance

**Use when:** Problem-aware people who feel their repeated experience has not been explained.

**Appeal (hypotheses):** recognition; validation; relief from self-blame

**Build it:** Open with an observable recurring situation; acknowledge the unresolved question; give a supported explanation.

**Payoff and bridge:** The promised explanation must account for the opening experience before the product appears.

**Avoid:** Do not assert doctors are wrong, dismiss diagnoses, or borrow lab thresholds from the ad.

**Read:** [N1](cases/N1.md), [N3](cases/N3.md), [N4](cases/N4.md), [L2](cases/L2.md), [E1](cases/E1.md), [P1](cases/P1.md), [NU018](cases/NU018.md), [NU026](cases/NU026.md), [NU027](cases/NU027.md), [NU035](cases/NU035.md), [NU095](cases/NU095.md), [NU136](cases/NU136.md), [NU144](cases/NU144.md), [NU149](cases/NU149.md)

## failed-attempt-ledger — Attempt, cost or effort, disappointing consequence

**Use when:** Skeptical buyers who have already tried relevant alternatives.

**Appeal (hypotheses):** effort validation; anticipated regret; reason to reconsider

**Build it:** Select a few real attempts; show the specific gap they share; define what a new option needs to do.

**Payoff and bridge:** Move from the shared limitation into selection criteria, then product fit.

**Avoid:** Do not invent expenditure, personal failure, or claim every alternative is ineffective.

**Read:** [G1](cases/G1.md), [G2](cases/G2.md), [L2](cases/L2.md), [A1](cases/A1.md), [NH1](cases/NH1.md), [NU021](cases/NU021.md)

## preserve-the-gain — Keep a valued gain while resolving its trade-off

**Use when:** People reluctant to sacrifice something they value to solve another problem.

**Appeal (hypotheses):** loss aversion hypothesis; conflict resolution; regained agency

**Build it:** Name the gain and unresolved cost; explain the supported way the proposed solution addresses the conflict.

**Payoff and bridge:** Show evidence for both sides of the desired combination.

**Avoid:** Do not imply a supplement preserves treatment benefits or eliminates side effects without evidence.

**Read:** [G2](cases/G2.md), [G3](cases/G3.md), [Z2](cases/Z2.md), [F1](cases/F1.md)

## buyer-inspection — Inspect this before choosing

**Use when:** Solution-aware shoppers comparing formulations or features.

**Appeal (hypotheses):** agency; competence; reduced uncertainty

**Build it:** Turn the choice into an observable check; explain why the criterion matters; show the product meets it.

**Payoff and bridge:** Checklist match: the brand answers the inspection task.

**Avoid:** Specifications, exclusivity and comparative superiority require product-specific support.

**Read:** [N5](cases/N5.md), [A1](cases/A1.md), [SA1](cases/SA1.md), [NU012](cases/NU012.md), [NU038](cases/NU038.md), [NU043](cases/NU043.md), [NU048](cases/NU048.md), [NU091](cases/NU091.md)

## practical-use-question — How to use an already interesting category

**Use when:** Ingredient- or solution-aware viewers with a real use question.

**Appeal (hypotheses):** practical utility; relevance; earned authority

**Build it:** Answer the use question within approved directions, then explain the meaningful product distinction.

**Payoff and bridge:** Recommendation follows the useful answer and supported criteria.

**Avoid:** Do not reuse medical dosing or fabricate a clinician narrator.

**Read:** [R1](cases/R1.md), [NH1](cases/NH1.md)

## reverse-warning — An apparent complaint becomes an honest qualification

**Use when:** Product-aware viewers who have a reason to care about a review or real offer.

**Appeal (hypotheses):** expectation violation; curiosity

**Build it:** Name the product and a truthful surprising qualification; resolve the apparent warning promptly.

**Payoff and bridge:** Early reveal; explain the qualification and practical value.

**Avoid:** No fabricated complaint, miraculous outcome, or invented apology/discount.

**Read:** [R2](cases/R2.md), [U2](cases/U2.md), [NU002](cases/NU002.md)

## discovery-story — An unusual discovery becomes relevant to daily life

**Use when:** Problem-aware viewers who need a credible reason to consider a different approach.

**Appeal (hypotheses):** curiosity; narrative identification; earned trust

**Build it:** Start with a sourced discovery or founder decision; expand to a recognizable problem; explain the selection.

**Payoff and bridge:** The product is the next supported event in the story.

**Avoid:** Shock without relevance fails; invented first-person histories are not usable testimonials.

**Read:** [R3](cases/R3.md), [L3](cases/L3.md), [A2](cases/A2.md), [AR2](cases/AR2.md), [NU021](cases/NU021.md), [NU088](cases/NU088.md), [NU123](cases/NU123.md)

## success-with-hidden-cost — An outward success conflicts with felt experience

**Use when:** An audience whose desired identity or activity is threatened despite apparent progress.

**Appeal (hypotheses):** identity continuity; recognition; emotional contrast

**Build it:** Show the contradiction through an actual situation; identify what the person wants restored.

**Payoff and bridge:** Use the product rationale to connect to a supported activity, not an abstract transformation.

**Avoid:** Do not invent trauma, lab results, relationship dialogue, or promised recovery.

**Read:** [Z2](cases/Z2.md), [C2](cases/C2.md), [AR1](cases/AR1.md), [B2](cases/B2.md)

## one-analogy-explanation — One physical analogy makes the missing link understandable

**Use when:** Viewers who need an unfamiliar functional distinction explained.

**Appeal (hypotheses):** comprehension; uncertainty reduction

**Build it:** State the literal supported process, map one familiar analogy to it, and reuse that mapping only while useful.

**Payoff and bridge:** Derive a feature requirement from the explanation.

**Avoid:** An analogy cannot establish clinical efficacy; do not mix metaphors or invent a hidden cause.

**Read:** [Z1](cases/Z1.md), [N2](cases/N2.md), [L1](cases/L1.md), [C1](cases/C1.md), [E2](cases/E2.md), [NU095](cases/NU095.md), [NU136](cases/NU136.md)

## staged-progress — Small developments lead back to ordinary participation

**Use when:** Buyers whose skepticism concerns what actual use or progress would look like.

**Appeal (hypotheses):** credible expectations; future-use imagination; identity restoration

**Build it:** Use documented milestones and remaining uncertainty; finish with a concrete activity.

**Payoff and bridge:** Product use and relevant evidence lead into the outcome sequence.

**Avoid:** Do not invent days-to-results or imply an individual timeline is typical.

**Read:** [Z2](cases/Z2.md), [R4](cases/R4.md), [G3](cases/G3.md), [NU007](cases/NU007.md), [NU141](cases/NU141.md)

## objection-dialogue — Each answer earns the next buyer question

**Use when:** Audiences with several connected objections or a context-specific suitability question.

**Appeal (hypotheses):** vicarious learning; objection resolution; reduced embarrassment

**Build it:** Ask the question raised by the previous answer; keep speaker knowledge and commercial roles distinct.

**Payoff and bridge:** Questions advance from explanation to selection and practical use.

**Avoid:** Source medical advice is unverified; do not create fake experts, witnesses, or endorsements.

**Read:** [U3](cases/U3.md), [S1](cases/S1.md), [H1](cases/H1.md), [P2](cases/P2.md), [NU129](cases/NU129.md), [NU132](cases/NU132.md)

## article-curiosity — An information promise earns the next click

**Use when:** Briefs whose conversion event is an article visit rather than immediate purchase.

**Appeal (hypotheses):** curiosity; low-commitment next action

**Build it:** Give recognition and a useful partial answer; state exactly what the article will explain.

**Payoff and bridge:** The bridge is to information and must match the actual destination.

**Avoid:** Do not promise an imminent in-video answer and withhold it, or assume clicks prove purchases.

**Read:** [Z3](cases/Z3.md), [B1](cases/B1.md)

## participatory-proof — Ask the viewer to inspect or compare

**Use when:** Product-aware buyers evaluating quality, appearance or value.

**Appeal (hypotheses):** participation; uncertainty reduction; concrete proof

**Build it:** Pose a visible comparison; show relevant details; explain the supported conclusion.

**Payoff and bridge:** Early product demonstration supplies its own relevance.

**Avoid:** Actual visuals and provenance must support the comparison; do not fake aged products or price anchors.

**Read:** [F4](cases/F4.md), [F2](cases/F2.md)

## one-purchase-many-uses — A narrow purchase occasion expands into daily usefulness

**Use when:** Buyers who understand the product but question versatility or ongoing value.

**Appeal (hypotheses):** future-use imagination; reduced choice burden; value

**Build it:** Start with a concrete occasion; show supported additional use cases and the features that enable them.

**Payoff and bridge:** Product-first or discovery bridge, with feature-to-use explanation.

**Avoid:** Do not invent capacity, material, airline compatibility, or customer experience.

**Read:** [F1](cases/F1.md), [F3](cases/F3.md), [F6](cases/F6.md), [F8](cases/F8.md)

## meaning-and-real-offer — A specific object or actual availability supplies a reason now

**Use when:** Warm audiences evaluating personal meaning, price or a genuine offer.

**Appeal (hypotheses):** identity expression; value anchoring; action friction reduction

**Build it:** Tie meaning to a specific design or use; state only the actual current offer and terms.

**Payoff and bridge:** Product/offer-first; evidence and terms resolve the remaining buying obstacle.

**Avoid:** Celebrity association, awards, restocks, scarcity and discounts in swipes are unverified historical claims.

**Read:** [F5](cases/F5.md), [F7](cases/F7.md), [U2](cases/U2.md)

## ordered-risk-explanation — An ordered sequence makes a developing problem legible

**Use when:** A brief that needs an accurate ordered explanation and has evidence for its stages.

**Appeal (hypotheses):** attention through sequence; perceived relevance of consequences

**Build it:** Use only verified stages or steps and explain the consequence of each without implying inevitable deterioration.

**Payoff and bridge:** Any solution recommendation needs independent support for its role; the source ad does not provide it.

**Avoid:** The source kidney-disease fear ladder is unverified and must not supply medical facts or treatment advice.

**Read:** [S2](cases/S2.md)

## characters-explain-jobs — Personified parts make feature roles memorable

**Use when:** Requests for explanatory dialogue or adjacent animated formats.

**Appeal (hypotheses):** concreteness; memory aid; question sequencing

**Build it:** Give each character one supported job and connect the jobs to the buyer question.

**Payoff and bridge:** A combined product must actually supply the stated jobs.

**Avoid:** An animated claim is still a claim; do not force this adjacent format into spoken UGC.

**Read:** [U1](cases/U1.md), [H1](cases/H1.md), [H2](cases/H2.md), [Z4](cases/Z4.md), [AU1](cases/AU1.md), [NU001](cases/NU001.md), [NU003](cases/NU003.md), [NU129](cases/NU129.md), [NU132](cases/NU132.md)

## price-concession-and-specification — Admit price, explain the choices behind it

**Use when:** Price-aware buyers who need a meaningful reason for the cost.

**Appeal (hypotheses):** perceived candor; value justification

**Build it:** Concede the real price, explain actual design trade-offs, and connect the chosen features to the buyer’s job.

**Payoff and bridge:** Product-first value explanation, with substantiated feature differences.

**Avoid:** Do not invent manufacturing costs, daily prices, superiority or clinical justification.

**Read:** [NU034](cases/NU034.md), [NU121](cases/NU121.md)

## skepticism-to-authenticity — Turn authenticity concerns into an inspection task

**Use when:** Buyers with a real seller-authenticity question.

**Appeal (hypotheses):** loss avoidance; purchase confidence

**Build it:** Show verifiable brand/seller identifiers and where to obtain the actual product.

**Payoff and bridge:** Official-channel CTA follows the demonstrated authenticity checks.

**Avoid:** Do not call competitors counterfeit or dismiss complaints as fake purchases without evidence.

**Read:** [NU012](cases/NU012.md), [NU038](cases/NU038.md)

## skeptic-aligned-explanation — Acknowledge category ad fatigue, then earn trust

**Use when:** Viewers skeptical of repeated category promises.

**Appeal (hypotheses):** skeptic alignment; perceived transparency

**Build it:** Acknowledge the specific concern, explain the relevant product distinction, and supply evidence.

**Payoff and bridge:** Recommendation follows an answer to the actual skepticism.

**Avoid:** Calling other ads misleading does not establish your claims or authorize invented expert authority.

**Read:** [NU069](cases/NU069.md)

## reason-why-offer — A real event explains a specific offer

**Use when:** Warm buyers who need current terms and a reason to act.

**Appeal (hypotheses):** deal salience; action friction reduction

**Build it:** Explain a verified stock, seasonal, or pricing event; state the actual offer and next step.

**Payoff and bridge:** Offer-first CTA with accurate terms.

**Avoid:** Do not fabricate warehouse incidents, inventory counts, deadlines, scarcity or visible-link stock tests.

**Read:** [NU117](cases/NU117.md), [NU134](cases/NU134.md)

## conceded-social-fantasy — Qualify a social aspiration and return to concrete use

**Use when:** Viewers whose practical use connects to a valued identity or activity.

**Appeal (hypotheses):** identity expression; perceived candor

**Build it:** Use an identifiable hypothetical aspiration or real experience, qualify it honestly, and explain supported everyday value.

**Payoff and bridge:** Connect price and product function to ordinary participation.

**Avoid:** A concession does not validate the remaining promise; do not invent partner dialogue or customer outcomes.

**Read:** [NU046](cases/NU046.md)

## historical-restoration — Explain a real advance and a remaining gap

**Use when:** A brief with a relevant, sourced history that genuinely changes product understanding.

**Appeal (hypotheses):** narrative coherence; causal reframing

**Build it:** Acknowledge what improved, explain a supported remaining limitation, and derive the needed action.

**Payoff and bridge:** Position the product as a supported response to the gap.

**Avoid:** Do not manufacture an ancestral golden age, medical history, universal treatment damage, or a false villain.

**Read:** [NU009](cases/NU009.md)
