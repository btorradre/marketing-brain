# Choose a structure by the question the viewer needs answered

These are flexible argument shapes. Use the parts needed by the brief. Do not add a credential, clinical discovery, trauma, founder experience, failure story, offer, or result just to fill a position in a structure.

## Expert or informed educator

**Best fit:** the viewer has a real question and needs a clear explanation or buying criteria.

Question or recognizable problem → explanation of the relevant gap → feature/ingredient jobs → supported selection criteria → recommendation → evidence or expectations → next step.

Keep the credentials brief and accurate if supplied. An informed product demonstrator can explain a nonclinical function without pretending to be a clinician. The value is the explanation, not the appearance of authority. A recommendation remains commercial copy when that is its actual context.

**Common failure:** impressive terminology replaces the causal explanation, followed by a sudden product pitch.

**Repair:** finish the specific explanation, name the requirement it creates, then explain the product's fit. Nivara and Lanural supplied this pattern in the study.

## Customer discovery

**Best fit:** the important uncertainty is whether a new approach could be meaningfully different from prior attempts.

Specific lived difficulty → relevant failed attempts → discovery with a real basis → explanation in the customer's own terms → reason for choosing this product → supported experience → return to the original activity.

A customer can teach what they learned. Preserve the difference between personal experience, something they were told, and an independently established claim. Use only actual supplied experience for an apparently real testimonial. If no testimonial is available, use a demonstrator or plainly hypothetical scenario.

**Common failure:** a long pain story postpones the answer, then the narrator suddenly speaks like a technical authority.

**Repair:** retain the scene that changes the stakes and the explanation that changes the decision. Remove repeated suffering and unsupported expertise. Lanural and Zafira supplied distinct short and long examples.

## Interview or third-party exchange

**Best fit:** the viewer has several natural follow-up objections.

One question about the problem → answer → question about the unfamiliar explanation → answer → relevant audience objection → selection criteria → product → expectations or next action.

Each question should arise from the preceding answer. Give the speakers distinct knowledge and motives; avoid two voices delivering alternate halves of the same monologue. Do not invent a real person's endorsement or witnessed results.

**Common failure:** questions are decorative prompts such as “Tell me more” and do not advance the decision.

**Repair:** ask the question the explanation leaves unresolved. Nuora's interview-style example demonstrates this progression.

## Founder story

**Best fit:** the viewer needs a reason to trust this seller and understand why this product exists.

Real reason for building → specific unmet requirement → formulation or design decisions → relevant evidence → product → practical next step.

The founder's financial interest is part of the situation. Do not write them as a neutral reviewer. Founder motivation does not prove effectiveness. avaRoot's sister-founder story is a structural reference, not a verified history to reuse.

## Product-aware review, offer, or demonstration

**Best fit:** the product or category is already relevant; the remaining question concerns value, function, comparison, or the offer.

Product or concrete question → useful demonstration, explanation, or surprising qualification → relevant evidence → actual offer/action.

Naming the product in the opening is appropriate. A reverse-warning hook needs an honest payoff; do not manufacture a false accusation or complaint. A deadline requires an actual deadline. Resilia's product-first review and Elvera's early reveal disprove a universal late-brand rule.

## Article bridge

**Best fit:** the requested conversion is to consume an explanation on the destination page.

Recognizable situation → useful partial answer or clear unresolved question → what the article explains → reason that information matters → article action.

Deliver the payoff promised inside the ad. If the answer belongs on the article, make that destination clear rather than promising an answer in the next few seconds and withholding it. Do not force a complete product VSL into an information-seeking ad. Zafira and Blyme supplied examples with this different conversion job.

## Bridges that make product entry relevant

| Bridge | Required setup | How to make it follow | Failure to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Checklist match | Specific, supported requirements | Explain how the product meets the requirements already taught. | Inventing exclusivity or reciting unrelated features. |
| Complete option | Several relevant component jobs | Show how the product combines those jobs conveniently. | Adding ingredients solely to make the list longer. |
| Discovery | A credible search or decision in the supplied story | Make the product the next event in that decision. | A miraculous discovery with no basis or unexplained trust. |
| Early reveal | An unresolved question about a known product or use | Name it, then answer the question through function or evidence. | Assuming product visibility eliminates the need for explanation. |
| Article bridge | A clear need for the next page's information | Sell the value of that information and identify the destination. | Pretending an incomplete purchase argument is a finished sales VSL. |

## Match explanation length to awareness

- **Cold/problem-aware:** establish recognition and the causal links needed to understand the solution category. Explain unfamiliar vocabulary once.
- **Solution-aware:** address the unresolved distinction between options. Do not repeat an entire category lesson if the brief establishes the viewer already knows it.
- **Product-aware:** resolve a specific buying obstacle through demonstration, proof, use, comparison, or a real offer.

Treat awareness as a brief-specific hypothesis, not a reason to stereotype the audience. A familiar diagnosis does not imply familiarity with an ingredient or mechanism. When the script covers several levels, sequence the missing premises rather than restarting the story for each.

## Controlled variations

If the user requests variants, identify what each tests: opening situation, speaker role, mechanism explanation, proof, bridge, or offer. Preserve the relevant facts. Hook-only variations should still connect grammatically and logically to the shared body. If a new hook changes the premise, revise the connecting passage instead of attaching an incompatible body.

Do not infer a brand's internal testing process from similar ads. The research observed repeated texts and related executions; it did not reveal experiment design or attribution.
