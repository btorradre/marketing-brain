# DR UGC Brief

Turns one angle plus its hook into a complete UGC creator brief that a creator who has never heard of the brand can shoot without a single follow-up question. Use when someone says "write a creator brief," "brief this for UGC," "brief for the creator," "what do we send the creator," "turn this angle into a shoot brief," or has an approved angle and hook ready for production.

A bad brief produces bad footage no matter how good the creator is. These briefs are not shot lists — they are creative strategies written in plain language, and every instruction in them exists to protect the angle from being diluted in production.

## Operating rules

1. **The brief serves the angle, not the creator.** Every instruction exists because without it the angle degrades on set.
2. **Specificity prevents mistakes.** Write every instruction as if you cannot be reached to clarify it, because you cannot.
3. **The hook is non-negotiable.** The creator has freedom in the body. The opening three seconds are delivered as written. Say this explicitly in the brief.
4. **Tone comes from awareness level.** A solution-aware customer is sceptical. A problem-aware customer is frustrated. The creator's energy has to match — explain which one they're playing.
5. **Show, don't tell.** Instead of "seem relaxed," write "film in your kitchen like you're telling a friend something you just figured out."
6. **No file paths, no internal jargon.** No internal IDs, no internal tool/system names, no awareness-level vocabulary in the creator-facing document. Those live in an internal header only, never in the part sent to the creator.

## How to use this

1. **Load everything the brief depends on.** Read the source angle and its chosen hook. If either doesn't exist yet, write the hook first rather than improvising it inside this brief. Load product-truth documentation — mandatory: no brief may claim a spec, material, dimension, or capability that isn't actually true of the product. If any dimension or spec is genuinely unknown for this product, no brief may state a size for it. Load the voice-of-customer research for the phrases that must appear verbatim.

2. **Write the internal header first (never sent to the creator):** the angle name, awareness level, target persona, the golden nugget, and where the hook came from.

3. **Then write the creator-facing document:**

   **Overview (3 sentences maximum).** What this ad is trying to do and who it speaks to.

   **The hook (non-negotiable).** The exact opening line, word for word. Visual direction for the first 2-3 seconds: setting, framing, body language. What the creator must NOT do in the opening. On top of funnel, the opening never leads on the product — if the first frame is a product beauty shot, the brief is wrong.

   **The body.** Key points in order, written as talking points rather than a full script. The emotional journey: what the creator's energy is at each stage. Which customer phrases must appear verbatim. **Demo beats** — every claim gets a paired on-screen demonstration, written as a two-column list so nothing ships as a bare assertion:

   | Claim she makes | What the camera shows while she says it |
   |---|---|
   | it holds a 16 inch laptop | the laptop sliding in, lid closing over it |
   | the material softens instead of scuffing | thumb pressed into the material, releasing |

   What objections to address, and how.

   **The close.** How the ad ends. Specific CTA language.

   **Production notes.** Recommended setting and why it serves this angle. Wardrobe and styling direction. What to avoid. One creator throughout, same person in every shot — if this is an AI-generated production, lock the creator as the same reference image/identity in every generated shot.

   **What success looks like.** One paragraph describing the finished ad if the brief was executed correctly — this is what any reviewer grades the footage against.

4. **Run the gates** (below) and fix violations before sending.

5. **Write and hand off.** Save the finished brief as a standalone document. If the team tracks concepts before production starts, log this one (product, concept name, angle, one-sentence thesis, format, type) and note the returned tracking ID back on the angle record.

6. **Close with a two-line self-audit** confirming the six-month test and the swap test both pass, then name the intended production method (AI-generated UGC, real creator shoot, multi-shot production, or a reference-ad replication).

## Rules & standards

| Gate | Kill condition |
|---|---|
| **Demo beat** | Any claim in the body with no paired visual. |
| **Creator voice** | Any "our," "we," or "my brand." The creator says "they're," "their," "this brand." |
| **Product truth** | Any spec, dimension, material, colorway, or capability not actually true of the product. |
| **Voice tells** | Em dash, "not X it's Y," parallel repetition stacks, in any line the creator actually speaks. |
| **Swap test** | The script would read identically for a competitor's product. |
| **Six-month test** | The concept dies with the season or moment. |
| **Clean brief** | Any internal ID, internal tool/system name, or internal vocabulary in the creator-facing section. |
