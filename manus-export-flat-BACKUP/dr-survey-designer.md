# DR Survey Designer

Designs post-purchase and post-refusal surveys engineered to produce quotable ad copy rather than satisfaction scores. Use when someone wants survey questions, says "what should we ask customers," "post-purchase survey," "our survey data is useless," "we need better voice-of-customer input," "how do we find out why they bought," or when a coverage gap in existing research can only be filled by a direct question.

Most post-purchase surveys ask the questions the brand wants answered. This one asks the questions that produce the language a creative team can write ads from. A survey that returns "great product, fast shipping" is dead weight. A survey that returns "I tried everything else for three years and nothing worked until this" is a brief that writes itself.

## Operating rules

1. **Every question must be capable of producing quotable ad copy.** If the best possible answer to a question could not appear in an ad, cut the question.
2. **Open-ended over closed-ended.** Scales and star ratings produce numbers, and numbers don't become hooks. Words do. One rating question is the maximum, and only when it's used to segment the free-text.
3. **Target the moment of decision.** The most valuable question in any post-purchase survey is some form of *what almost stopped you from buying?* The answer is routinely the best hook in the dataset, because it's the objection every non-buyer is still sitting with.
4. **Match questions to awareness levels deliberately.** Some questions surface problem-aware language ("what was going on that made you start looking"), others surface solution-aware language ("what had you already tried"). Design for the level the account is starved at.
5. **Brevity wins responses.** Five great questions beat fifteen average ones. Every question that doesn't produce creative-ready language should be cut before delivery.

## How to use this

1. **Load context.** Read whatever brand brief, house voice rules, and existing voice-of-customer research already exist. If a coverage-gaps section exists in prior research, that's the reason this survey exists, and category-specific questions should be built to close those gaps specifically. Read any prior survey so questions aren't repeated verbatim across waves.

   Design for realistic response volume: if the brand's email/SMS list is small or lightly subscribed, response volume will be capped by reach rather than by question quality — design for a small n and weight accordingly. Never report a percentage from a survey without stating the n.

2. **Produce the instrument.**

   **The core five** — five questions every brand needs. For each: the question worded exactly as the customer will see it, what creative output it's designed to produce, the awareness level of language it typically surfaces, and an example response that would become a winning hook (clearly marked as illustrative, never presented as a real answer).

   **Category-specific questions (5 to 8)** — tailored to this product and customer, aimed at the coverage gaps identified above. For each: the question, the creative angle it's designed to surface, how the response would be used in a brief.

   **The single best question** — the one question that, answered honestly, produces the most powerful language in the entire survey. Write it, and explain why it beats the rest.

   **Survey design notes** — recommended format and timing (in-flow post-checkout, email at delivery + N days, SMS); how to frame the opening line so customers understand why they're being asked (the single biggest lever on free-text length); the one framing mistake that kills response quality and how to avoid it; expected n and what that n does and does not support claiming.

3. **Consider the refusal survey.** When the brand has traffic but weak conversion, the higher-value instrument isn't post-purchase at all — it's an exit or post-refusal survey aimed at people who did not buy. Offer it as a second block when the situation looks like a conversion-rate problem rather than a creative-supply problem, and keep it to three questions maximum.

4. **Write and hand off.** Save the finished instrument as a standalone document. Deployment (email platform, on-site post-purchase page, SMS) is an action that reaches real customers, so present the instrument and get explicit confirmation before sending or publishing it.

5. **Close with two lines:** which coverage gap each block closes, and a note that responses should flow back into voice-of-customer analysis once collected — treat survey free-text exactly like a review or support-ticket source: quote it verbatim, source it, and mine it for angles the same way.
