# The ten context vectors

A core angle is a problem stated at the widest resolution the customer still recognises as hers. A micro-angle is that same problem narrowed onto one cohort. The vectors below are the ten axes a person's life varies along, and crossing an angle with a vector is what generates cohorts systematically instead of by inspiration.

They are deliberately category-free. Nothing here is about bags, supplements, or software; it is about the ways one problem shows up differently in different lives. That is why the same ten work for any niche.

**Run them in the order given.** The first four are the highest-yield in almost every category, because they carry the most emotional charge and the most specific language.

---

## 1. failure-moment

*The scene where the problem cost her something. The story she still tells.*

**Mining question:** where does the research describe a specific moment rather than a general state? Look for a time, a place, and a witness.

**Grep the corpus for:** `the time I`, `in front of`, `I had to`, `ended up`, `everyone saw`, `I was so embarrassed`, `never again`

| Category | Core angle | Failure-moment micro-angle |
|---|---|---|
| Weekender bag | Bag sags and reads as a gym bag | The gate-check: handed over the bag at the jet bridge because it wouldn't fit, arrived without a suit |
| Gut supplement | Bloat that won't budge | Left a friend's wedding at eight because the dress stopped closing by the toasts |
| Blackout blinds | Room never gets properly dark | The 5am summer wake-up before the day that mattered |

**Why it is first:** the highest-charge language in any corpus lives here, and a moment is inherently visual, which means the brief writes itself.

**Anti-pattern:** a moment nobody witnessed and nothing was lost in. That is a state, not a moment.

---

## 2. incumbent

*What she uses now, and the exact way it fails.*

**Mining question:** what did she try before, and what specifically broke? The failed-solution pass of the corpus is this vector in raw form.

**Grep for:** `I tried`, `switched from`, `used to use`, `gave up on`, `returned`, `lasted`, `fell apart`, `doesn't work`

| Category | Incumbent micro-angle |
|---|---|
| Weekender bag | The rolling carry-on for a two-night trip: overkill, but the only thing that holds its shape |
| Gut supplement | The probiotic that did nothing for six months at $45 a month |
| Blackout blinds | The blackout curtain that leaks light down both sides |

**Why it matters:** these become the strongest solution-aware ads in the account, because she has already accepted the problem and only needs a reason this one is different. Law 6 still binds: intelligence about the incumbent never becomes a named competitor comparison in copy.

**Anti-pattern:** "she uses nothing" is not an incumbent. Everyone has a workaround, even if the workaround is enduring it.

---

## 3. identity

*The role or job that reshapes the problem.*

**Mining question:** who announces themselves in the research? People self-label constantly, and the label usually arrives attached to a constraint.

**Grep for:** `as a`, `I'm a`, `nurse`, `teacher`, `mom of`, `contractor`, `student`, `retired`, `shift`, `WFH`

| Category | Identity micro-angle |
|---|---|
| Weekender bag | Nurse packing scrubs, shoes and lunch for three twelves, needs a wipe-down surface |
| Gut supplement | Teacher who cannot leave a classroom of thirty to use a bathroom |
| Blackout blinds | Night-shift worker sleeping while the street outside is at full volume |

**Anti-pattern:** an identity that changes nothing about the problem is decoration. "Dog owners" only earns a record if owning a dog changes what breaks.

---

## 4. occasion

*The specific event the product gets used in.*

**Mining question:** when does she reach for this, and what is at stake in that particular event?

**Grep for:** `for my`, `wedding`, `trip`, `interview`, `holiday`, `moving`, `hosting`, `first day`

| Category | Occasion micro-angle |
|---|---|
| Weekender bag | Client-facing work travel: gate to lobby in an hour, no time to change |
| Gut supplement | The two weeks before a beach holiday, after everything else was already planned |
| Blackout blinds | Setting up the nursery before the baby arrives |

**Anti-pattern:** an occasion so broad it is the category ("travel"). Occasion means one event with stakes, not a use case.

---

## 5. constraint

*The rule, limit, or shortage she has to obey.*

**Mining question:** what is she not allowed to do, cannot afford, cannot fit, or cannot physically manage?

**Grep for:** `won't fit`, `can't afford`, `not allowed`, `rental`, `small`, `tiny`, `budget`, `HOA`, `landlord`, `arthritis`

| Category | Constraint micro-angle |
|---|---|
| Weekender bag | Budget-airline sizer: personal-item-only travel with a strict measured frame |
| Gut supplement | Cannot swallow capsules, has been quietly avoiding every supplement for years |
| Blackout blinds | Rental where nothing can be drilled into the wall |

**Why it converts:** a constraint disqualifies most of the market's solutions for her, so the ad's job shrinks to proving one thing.

**Anti-pattern:** a preference dressed as a constraint. "Prefers neutral colours" is taste; "the landlord forbids drilling" is a constraint.

---

## 6. life-stage

*The transition that changed the problem.*

**Mining question:** what recently changed in her life that made a tolerable problem intolerable?

**Grep for:** `since I had`, `after my`, `just moved`, `new job`, `retired`, `divorce`, `postpartum`, `menopause`, `empty nest`, `first apartment`

| Category | Life-stage micro-angle |
|---|---|
| Weekender bag | First trip after the baby: packing for two people in a body that is not back yet |
| Gut supplement | Perimenopause, when the thing that worked for fifteen years stopped working |
| Blackout blinds | Newborn at home, where thirty minutes of extra sleep is the entire negotiation |

**Why it converts:** a transition creates urgency with a natural deadline and no discount required. Confirm the transition is real in the research, though: this vector is the easiest one to fabricate plausibly.

**Anti-pattern:** a life stage as a demographic band. "Women in their fifties" is not a transition. "Six weeks after the last kid moved out" is.

---

## 7. frequency

*Heavy user versus occasional user. Usually two entirely different problems.*

**Mining question:** how often does she hit this, and does the daily version differ in kind from the annual version?

**Grep for:** `every week`, `twice a year`, `daily`, `every morning`, `once in a while`, `all the time`

| Category | Frequency micro-angle |
|---|---|
| Weekender bag | The weekly flyer: out Monday, back Thursday, the bag lives half its life in an overhead bin |
| Gut supplement | The person for whom this is every single day, not a flare-up |
| Blackout blinds | Guest room used four nights a year versus the bedroom used every night |

**Why it matters:** the heavy user's problem is durability, ritual, and time; the occasional user's is dread and preparation. Same product, opposite copy.

**Anti-pattern:** stating frequency without changing the problem. If the ad reads the same, it is not a micro-angle.

---

## 8. relational

*Who else the problem touches.*

**Mining question:** who else in her life sees, suffers, judges, or benefits? The problem seen through their eyes is often a different angle entirely.

**Grep for:** `my husband`, `my wife`, `my kids`, `my mom`, `my coworkers`, `my clients`, `my partner`, `they said`

| Category | Relational micro-angle |
|---|---|
| Weekender bag | Buying it as a gift, and the fear of getting the size or the colour wrong |
| Gut supplement | The partner who has heard about it for two years and has stopped asking |
| Blackout blinds | The toddler whose 5am wake-up sets the whole household's day |

**Why it matters:** it opens the buyer-versus-user split, which is a whole second market for the same product, and gift buyers convert on completely different copy.

**Anti-pattern:** mentioning another person without their stake changing anything.

---

## 9. environment

*Climate, geography, home, commute.*

**Mining question:** where does she live and move, and does that make the problem worse in a way she names?

**Grep for:** `humidity`, `winter`, `rain`, `city`, `apartment`, `commute`, `walk to`, `subway`, `hard water`, `altitude`

| Category | Environment micro-angle |
|---|---|
| Weekender bag | Walking commuter: the bag is carried fifteen blocks, not wheeled thirty feet |
| Gut supplement | Travel weeks where water, food and timing all change at once |
| Blackout blinds | North-facing room in a northern summer, light at 4:30am |

**Anti-pattern:** geography that does not change the mechanics. A city name is not an environment; what the city does to the problem is.

---

## 10. objection

*The blocker itself, run as the angle.*

**Mining question:** what stops her buying, and is that hesitation itself a problem she would recognise as hers?

**Grep for:** `burned before`, `scam`, `too expensive`, `does it actually`, `skeptical`, `wasted money`, `hard to return`, `worth it?`

| Category | Objection micro-angle |
|---|---|
| Weekender bag | Has already bought two that failed, and does not trust another product photo |
| Gut supplement | Has spent hundreds on supplements that did nothing and cannot justify another |
| Blackout blinds | Measured wrong once, ate the cost, will not order online again |

**Why it works:** the sceptic cohort is large, cheap to reach because nobody writes to them, and converts on proof rather than promise. Every claim in an objection micro-angle needs a demo beat (Law 7).

**Anti-pattern:** a price objection with no proof to answer it. That is not an angle, it is a discount waiting to happen.

---

## Stacking

Two vectors crossed give an L3 record: `identity × frequency` = "nurse working three twelves". `occasion × constraint` = "work travel under a budget-airline sizer".

Stop at two. Three vectors describes a person you met once, and the self-ID line stops finding anybody.

## Coverage, honestly

The matrix is angles by vectors, and a full grid is not the goal. A cell is legitimately empty when the vector genuinely does not vary for the category, or when the research shows no such cohort. It is illegitimately empty when the sweep stopped early.

Say which is which when presenting. An empty cell you have ruled out is a finding; an empty cell you never looked at is a gap.
