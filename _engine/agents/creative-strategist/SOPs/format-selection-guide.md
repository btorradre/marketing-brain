# Format Selection Guide

## Overview

Format selection is a strategic decision, not a stylistic one. The format you choose determines:
- **Information density**: How much mechanism detail can you reveal?
- **Proof architecture**: What types of evidence work best?
- **Speed of persuasion**: How fast does the reader need to move toward conviction?
- **Funnel position**: Where in the customer journey does this piece live?
- **Avatar mindset**: What state of awareness does this format suit?

This guide helps you choose the right format for your brief, based on your avatar's awareness level, the mechanism complexity, the villain you're targeting, and the funnel architecture.

## Decision Tree: Choosing Your Format

Start at the top and work down until you land on a format.

### Question 1: What is your avatar's awareness level?

**Unaware or barely Problem-Aware?**
→ Use **Hook** or **Listicle** or **Native Image Brief**
- Reason: Unaware avatars need to be stopped in their scroll. Short, disruptive formats win.
- These formats don't require mechanism explanation; they ask simple recognition questions.
- Example hook: "You think you're just tired. But most people experiencing what you're experiencing have a specific metabolic issue."

**Problem-Aware or Solution-Aware?**
→ Consider **Long-Form (500-750w)**, **Video (60s)**, **Listicle**
- Reason: These avatars know they have a problem and are looking for solutions. They want some mechanism explanation but don't need deep proof yet.
- Example long-form: "Everyone says sleep more or exercise more. But if you have [specific symptom], neither of those fixes the actual problem."

**Solution-Aware, Product-Aware, or Most Aware?**
→ Consider **Long-Form (1000-2000w)**, **Video (3+ min)**, **Advertorial**
- Reason: These avatars need deep mechanism explanation and substantial proof. They're comparing options. You need space to build credibility and show differentiation.
- Example long-form: "You've tried everything. But you're missing the piece that actually resets your metabolism. Here's why..."

---

### Question 2: How complex is your mechanism?

**Simple mechanism** (One idea, easy to explain)
- Example: "The problem is nutrient deficiency. The solution is nutrient replacement."
- Best formats: **Hook**, **Listicle**, **Video (60s)**, **Static Ad**, **Native Image Brief**
- These formats can explain simple mechanisms clearly without space constraints

**Moderate mechanism** (2-3 component system, some explanation required)
- Example: "Fatigue is caused by (1) substrate depletion, (2) circadian dysregulation, (3) dopamine deficiency. You need to address all three."
- Best formats: **Long-Form (500-750w)**, **Video (60s-90s)**, **Advertorial**
- These formats have enough space to explain multi-part mechanisms without getting lost

**Complex mechanism** (4+ components, requires deep explanation, proof of each component)
- Example: "Metabolic reset requires addressing substrate depletion via [specific compounds], circadian rhythm via [specific behaviors], dopamine system via [specific mechanism], AND gut dysbiosis via [specific mechanism]. Here's why each matters and how they interact."
- Best formats: **Long-Form (1000-2000w)**, **Video (3+ min / VSL)**, **Advertorial**
- These formats have space for detailed mechanism explanation and multi-layered proof

---

### Question 3: What villain are you targeting?

**External Villain** (Industry, system, competitor, culture)
- Focus: "The lies you've been told"
- Best formats: **Advertorial** (editorial credibility), **Long-Form (750-2000w)** (space to expose the lie), **Video (60-90s)** (narration can be accusatory)
- Why: Tearing down an external villain requires showing the false narrative AND the true narrative. This takes space.
- Example: Advertorial titled "The Energy Industry Has Lied To You: Here's What Actually Works"

**Internal Villain** (Self-blame, shame, character beliefs)
- Focus: "You're not broken; the system is"
- Best formats: **Video (60-90s)** (emotional impact), **Long-Form (500-750w)** (personal story), **Native Image Brief** (relatability), **Listicle** (signs of false belief)
- Why: Internal villains respond to empathy and relatability. Shorter, more personal formats work better.
- Example: Long-form: "You think you're lazy. You're not. Here's what's actually happening..."

**Philosophical Villain** (Fatalism, permanence, "just how it is")
- Focus: "This is fixable"
- Best formats: **Long-Form (500-750w)** (mechanism proof), **Video (3+ min / VSL)** (before-after proof), **Advertorial** (social proof from others)
- Why: Philosophical villains require proof that change is possible. Before-after stories and mechanism evidence are strongest.
- Example: Long-form with testimonials: "Three women share how they moved from 'this is permanent' to 'I have my energy back'"

---

### Question 4: What is your funnel architecture?

**2-Piece Funnel: Lead Ad → Product Page (no bridge)**
- Lead ad must do ALL the work: Problem, villain, mechanism teaser, credibility, trust, CTA
- Best lead format: **Long-Form (750-1000w)**, **Video (90s-2min)**
- Why: Lead ad is the only chance to build trust. Needs substance.
- The product page should assume the reader understands the problem and mechanism (you taught them in lead ad)

**3-Piece Funnel: Lead → Bridge → Product Page**
- Lead ad: Problem + villain + curiosity (lighter on mechanism detail)
  - Best format: **Hook**, **Video (60s)**, **Long-Form (500-750w)**
- Bridge: Mechanism + proof (mechanism detail, testimonials, social proof)
  - Best format: **Long-Form (1000w)**, **Advertorial**, **Video (3min)**, **Video (VSL)**
- Product page: Objection handling, differentiation, conversion
  - Usually separate from Creative Strategist domain

**Extended Funnel: Lead → Bridge 1 → Bridge 2 → Product Page**
- Lead: Problem + curiosity (minimal detail)
  - Format: **Hook**, **Video (60s)**, **Short long-form (500w)**
- Bridge 1: Mechanism explanation (first component)
  - Format: **Long-Form (750-1000w)**, **Video (2-3min)**
- Bridge 2: Proof + objection handling + urgency (testimonials, comparisons)
  - Format: **Long-Form (1000w)**, **Advertorial**, **Video (VSL)**

**The Exit-State / Entry-State Rule:**
- After your piece, where should the reader be emotionally/informationally?
- Where does the next piece need them to be?
- These MUST match, or there's a funnel fracture

Example:
- Lead ad exit-state: "My fatigue might actually be fixable"
- Bridge ad entry-state: Requires belief that fatigue is fixable, plus curiosity about mechanism
- Match? YES → No fracture, proceed

## Format-Specific Guidance

### Hook (1-2 sentences, 15-20 minutes)

**What it is:** A single compelling opening line (or two-part hook) designed to stop the scroll and create curiosity or disbelief.

**When to use:**
- Early-stage avatars (any awareness level)
- A/B testing angles (you can create 5 hook variations and test which resonates)
- Teaser copy for video ads
- Email subject lines or Facebook ad text pre-testing
- Listicle headlines

**Awareness level it suits:** Any. Hooks work at every stage because they're not making mechanism claims; they're creating questions.

**Mechanism it suits:** Any mechanism can be hooked. The hook is a teaser, not an explanation.

**Villain it suits:** Any. Hooks work best when they're villain-focused ("The [villain] doesn't want you to know...") but can work with any villain type.

**Proof architecture:** None. Hooks don't prove anything. They promise. Proof comes later.

**Example hooks:**
- Disbelief hook: "Everyone thinks fatigue means you need more energy. But it actually means your metabolism is broken."
- Curiosity hook: "There are three metabolic signals your body sends when fatigue is actually fixable. Most people miss all three."
- Permission hook: "You have permission to optimize your energy the same way you optimize your fitness. Here's how."
- Specificity hook: "A 30-day metabolic reset protocol is restoring energy in 87% of women who stick with it."

**Production time:** 15-20 minutes (generate 5-7 variations, pick the strongest)

**Key success factors:**
- Opens with problem the reader recognizes
- Creates curiosity or disbelief (makes reader want to know more)
- Doesn't explain mechanism (that comes later)
- Matches the temperature/posture of the full piece it will headline

### Long-Form Copy (500-2000 words, 45-120 minutes)

**What it is:** An article-length piece of persuasive writing, designed for self-contained reading (Facebook primary text, sales page, email sequence, blog post).

**When to use:**
- Main conversion copy (lead ads, bridge ads, product page copy)
- When mechanism is moderate to complex and needs explanation
- When you have strong proof (testimonials, data, case studies) to support mechanism
- When avatar needs to be moved through belief shift progression
- Awareness level: Solution-Aware, Product-Aware (best results)

**Mechanism it suits:** Moderate to very complex. Long-form has space to explain multi-part mechanisms, show how each part works, and provide proof for each component.

**Villain it suits:** Any, but best with internal and external villains (which require narrative space to deconstruct).

**Proof architecture:**
- Problem statement + social proof
- Villain identification + logic for why old belief is wrong
- Mechanism explanation with specificity
- Testimonial or case study proof
- Before-after promise
- CTA

**Structure** (from [[/long form copy/skills/long-form-copy/]]):
1. **Hook (first 2-3 sentences)**: Problem recognition or disbelief
2. **Problem exposition (next 3-4 paragraphs)**: Establish relevance, social proof for problem
3. **Villain reveal (next 2-3 paragraphs)**: Here's what's actually causing this
4. **Avatar self-recognition (1-2 paragraphs)**: Does this apply to you? Signs/symptoms.
5. **Mechanism introduction (next 2-3 paragraphs)**: There's another way. Here's how it works.
6. **Mechanism deepening (2-4 paragraphs)**: Specific details, component breakdown, scientific basis
7. **Product introduction (1-2 paragraphs)**: If peripheral or central integration, introduce here
8. **Social proof / validation (2-3 paragraphs)**: Testimonials, data, expert endorsement
9. **Objection handling (1-2 paragraphs)**: "But what about...?"
10. **Future-state visualization (1-2 paragraphs)**: What becomes possible when this belief shifts?
11. **Close + CTA (final paragraph)**: What's the next step?

**Word count targets:**
- 500-750 words: Quick reads, lighter mechanism, lead ad format
- 750-1000 words: Standard length, moderate mechanism, bridge ad format
- 1000-1500 words: Deep dives, complex mechanism, bridge/product page format
- 1500-2000 words: VSL-equivalent depth, extensive proof, complex mechanism

**Temperature guidance:**
- Hot temperature: Short sentences (8-12 words). Vivid verbs. Rapid idea progression. More paragraphs, fewer words per paragraph.
- Warm temperature: Medium sentences (14-18 words). Conversational tone. Story-forward. Balanced paragraph length.
- Cool temperature: Longer sentences (18-24 words). Technical language acceptable. Logic-forward. Denser paragraphs.

**Production time:**
- 500-750 words: 45-60 min
- 750-1000 words: 60-75 min
- 1000+ words: 75-120 min

**Success factors:**
- Hook is compelling (reader wants to keep reading)
- Problem is established with specificity (reader sees themselves in the problem)
- Villain is clearly identified (reader understands what belief to abandon)
- Mechanism is explained step-by-step (reader understands the "why" not just the "what")
- Proof is credible (testimonials, data, logic)
- Objections are addressed
- CTA is clear and matches awareness level

### Video Script (60s - 180s, 30-90 minutes)

**What it is:** Screenplay-format script for a video ad, including visual directions, voiceover, on-screen text, and timing.

**When to use:**
- Video ad campaigns (YouTube, Facebook, TikTok, Instagram)
- Lead ads (hook + problem + curiosity)
- Bridge ads (mechanism explanation, proof)
- Product-focused ads (feature + benefit)
- Awareness levels: Any, but best for Problem-Aware and Solution-Aware (they watch videos to find answers)

**Mechanism it suits:** Simple to moderate (video is fast-moving; complex mechanisms get lost). Best for mechanisms that can be shown visually.

**Villain it suits:** Any, but best with external villain (video can show the villain's deception) or philosophical villain (show before-after transformation).

**Proof architecture:** Visual proof, testimonial quote, data super, before-after footage.

**Format options:**

**60-second video:**
- 0-3 sec: Visual hook (compelling image, text, or action)
- 3-10 sec: Problem statement (VO)
- 10-15 sec: Villain or mechanism tease (VO)
- 15-40 sec: Solution explanation or demonstration (VO + visuals)
- 40-50 sec: Social proof (testimonial or data super)
- 50-60 sec: CTA (strong close, clear next step)

**90-second video:**
- Adds 30 seconds to mechanism explanation or proof
- Can show more detailed mechanism or multiple testimonials
- Better for moderate-complexity mechanisms

**3-minute video:**
- Full mechanism explanation with proof
- Can include expert validation
- Can show before-after progression
- Best for Product-Aware or Most Aware avatars

**VSL (Video Sales Letter) — 10-30+ minutes:**
- Extended form of video sales page
- Full belief shift journey
- Deep mechanism explanation
- Extensive social proof
- Multiple close attempts
- Best for high-value products or complex purchasing decisions

**Voiceover considerations:**
- **For hot temperature**: Fast pacing, urgent tone, direct language
- **For warm temperature**: Conversational, story-forward, empathetic
- **For cool temperature**: Authoritative, evidence-focused, technical acceptable

**Production time:**
- 60 sec: 30-45 min
- 90 sec: 40-55 min
- 3-5 min: 60-90 min
- VSL: 90-180+ min

**Key success factors:**
- First 3 seconds are visually stopping (scrolling user stops)
- Problem is stated clearly and fast
- Mechanism is shown, not just told (visual demonstration)
- Proof is credible (real testimonial, real data)
- CTA is clear and easy to follow
- Pacing matches temperature (hot = fast cuts, warm = longer holds, cool = information density)

### Advertorial (1000-1500 words, 45-75 minutes)

**What it is:** Article-formatted persuasive writing that appears editorial in nature but is designed to sell.

**When to use:**
- Bridge ads (positioned as "thought piece," not salesy)
- Product-Aware avatars who are skeptical of direct advertising
- When tearing down external villain (editorial position adds credibility)
- When mechanism is complex (editorial format allows deep explanation)
- Awareness level: Solution-Aware, Product-Aware (best results)

**Mechanism it suits:** Moderate to complex. Editorial format allows space to explain multi-component mechanisms.

**Villain it suits:** Excellent for external villain (industry deception, competitive lies). Also works for philosophical villain (showing how change is possible).

**Proof architecture:** Editorial credibility (byline, sourced quotes, expert validation, data presentation)

**Advertorial frameworks** (choose one):

1. **Article Frame**: "Here's the story of a discovery"
   - Headline: Investigative or narrative
   - Byline: Author credentials
   - Opening: Story hook or surprising fact
   - Body: Evidence building to insight
   - Conclusion: Mechanism explanation + solution mention

2. **Investigative Frame**: "Here's what nobody's telling you"
   - Headline: Exposé angle
   - Opening: The lie
   - Body: Evidence of the lie, who benefits from the lie
   - Turning point: The truth
   - Conclusion: Solution that aligns with truth

3. **How-To Frame**: "Here's how to [achieve desired outcome]"
   - Headline: Promise of result
   - Introduction: Why this matters
   - Steps: Sequential explanation of mechanism
   - Proof: Before-after or success story
   - Conclusion: Solution for easier implementation

4. **Interview Frame**: "Expert explains [topic]"
   - Opening: Introduction of expert
   - Q&A pairs: Mechanism explained through questions
   - Proof: Expert credentials + quote validation
   - Conclusion: Recommended action

5. **Narrative Frame**: "One woman's journey from [problem] to [solution]"
   - Character introduction: Relatable protagonist
   - The struggle: Problem established
   - The turning point: Mechanism discovery
   - The transformation: Proof through before-after
   - The lesson: Mechanism generalized

6. **Contrarian Frame**: "Why [common belief] is wrong"
   - Headline: Contrarian premise
   - Opening: Common belief stated
   - Body: Evidence that belief is false
   - Turning point: Alternative explanation
   - Conclusion: Solution aligned with alternative

**Production time:** 45-75 min

**Key success factors:**
- Reads like editorial, not advertisement (use sourced quotes, expert voice, data presented as journalism)
- Credibility is primary (byline, expert validation, logical flow)
- Mechanism is explained clearly but positioned as discovery, not sales pitch
- Proof is substantial (testimonials, data, expert endorsement)
- Product mention is late (builds credibility before introducing product)
- CTA is soft (discovery/education focused, not hard sell)

### Listicle (400-600 words, 40-50 minutes)

**What it is:** Numbered or bulleted list format ("5 Reasons," "7 Signs," "10 Myths") with list items that resonate emotionally and create category recognition.

**When to use:**
- Lead ads (highly shareable, stops scroll)
- Early-stage awareness (helps reader self-identify into a category)
- Social proof (list items create sense of "everyone has this problem")
- Quick reads (no deep mechanism explanation required)
- Awareness level: Any (listicles work at every awareness level)

**Mechanism it suits:** Simple or implicit (mechanism is embedded in list items, not explained directly).

**Villain it suits:** Best for internal villain (list items are symptoms of self-blame, signs reader shouldn't blame themselves) or philosophical villain (list items showing permanence is a myth).

**List item formula:**
- Headline: Specific, resonant claim (5-10 words)
- Body: 1-2 sentences of explanation or proof
- Why it matters: Tie to larger pattern

**Example listicle:**
```
5 Signs Your Fatigue Is Metabolic (Not Character Flaw)

#1: You crash hard in the afternoon even when you've slept well
Why it matters: If sleep alone fixed it, you wouldn't crash. Afternoon crashes point to metabolic dysregulation, specifically the inability to sustain energy production through circadian rhythm. This is not laziness; this is physics.

#2: You feel energized by stimulants but crash harder after
Why it matters: True metabolic fatigue responds to stimulants (caffeine, sugar) but the crash is proportional to the boost. This pattern indicates your body is borrowing from future energy reserves. Actual energy supply is depleted.

#3: You perform better under pressure/stress
Why it matters: Stress hormones (adrenaline, cortisol) provide temporary energy. If stress temporarily fixes your fatigue, your baseline metabolic energy is too low. Your body only works when it's forced to by external pressure.

...etc.
```

**Production time:** 40-50 min

**Key success factors:**
- List items are specific (not generic)
- Each item resonates (reader thinks "yes, that's me")
- Items are distinct from each other (no repetition)
- Items progress logically (building toward a category recognition)
- Conclusion ties back to mechanism or solution (why this pattern exists)
- Tone matches temperature (urgent vs. patient)

### Static Ad Concept (20-40 words, 20-30 minutes)

**What it is:** A visual concept + 30-word supporting copy designed to stop scroll on feed platforms (Facebook, Instagram).

**When to use:**
- A/B testing multiple angles/villains (quick to produce)
- Lead ads with strong visual differentiation
- Early-stage awareness (visual must stop scroll)
- Awareness level: Any (visual stops before awareness level matters)

**Mechanism it suits:** Minimal or implied (mechanism detail comes in long-form, not in static ad).

**Proof architecture:** Visual proof (image that shows the problem or solution)

**Structure:**
1. **Visual concept description** (5-10 sentences)
   - What's in the image? What's the mood? What emotion does it evoke?
   - Specific composition, color, subject details

2. **Copy** (30 words max)
   - Hook (opens with problem or disbelief)
   - One claim (mechanism teaser or villain identification)
   - Soft CTA (learn more, discover, see how)

**Example static concept:**
```
VISUAL: Woman sitting at desk in late afternoon sunlight, head in hands, coffee cup (half-full) beside laptop. She looks exhausted but not asleep. Office visible but muted. Feeling is "I should be able to do this." Color palette: cool, desaturated.

COPY: You think you're just tired. But afternoon crashes point to something specific. What if your energy problem is actually metabolic — and fixable? [LEARN MORE]
```

**Production time:** 20-30 min

**Key success factors:**
- Visual is distinctive (stands out in feed)
- Visual tells a story (relatability, not just aesthetics)
- Copy is specific (not generic energy messaging)
- CTA is clear and soft (learn more, discover, etc.)
- Visual + copy are congruent (same villain, same tone)

### Native Image Brief (50-100 words caption, 15-25 minutes)

**What it is:** A detailed brief for a native image concept (image post on Facebook/Instagram with accompanying caption), designed for organic sharing or retargeting.

**When to use:**
- Native ads (appear as regular posts, not ads)
- Relatability-first messaging (image resonates before copy)
- Early-stage awareness (viral potential)
- Awareness level: Unaware, Problem-Aware (best for self-recognition)

**Mechanism it suits:** Simple or emotional (mechanism detail comes after image resonates).

**Proof architecture:** Visual proof of problem (image shows the emotion/situation) + caption that validates the problem and teases solution.

**Structure:**
1. **Image concept** (4-8 sentences)
   - What's shown? What's the emotion? What's the viewer thinking when they see this?
   - Specificity: exact scenario, mood, visual details

2. **Caption** (50-100 words)
   - Hook: Problem recognition or validation
   - One insight: Small mechanism hint or villain identification
   - Soft CTA: Share if you relate, tag a friend, etc.

**Example native image brief:**
```
IMAGE: Mother sitting at kitchen table in early morning light, empty coffee cup in front of her, kids' breakfast items around her. She's looking at the coffee cup like "again?" Not angry, just resigned. Lighting is soft, realistic, morning home setting.

CAPTION: If you're staring at your third cup of coffee by 10am and *still* feeling like you're moving through mud, it's not laziness. Most people think they need stronger coffee or more willpower. But if afternoon crashes are your pattern, it points to something specific: your metabolism isn't sustaining energy production. You're not broken. Your system just needs resetting. Tag someone who needs to know this.
```

**Production time:** 15-25 min

**Key success factors:**
- Image is specific (not stock, not generic)
- Image shows realistic scenario (reader sees themselves)
- Caption validates the problem (makes reader feel seen)
- Caption hints at mechanism without over-explaining
- CTA is organic (share, tag, relate) not pushy

## Format Selection by Funnel Architecture

### 2-Piece Funnel (Lead → Product Page)
**Lead ad format options:**
- Long-form (750-1000w): Highest conversion if mechanism is clear + proof is strong
- Video (90s-2min): High engagement, good for visual mechanisms
- Combination: Hook + Long-form in Facebook Ads primary text (hook in headline, long-form in body)

**Why only these formats?**
- Lead ad is doing all the work: Problem → villain → mechanism → trust → CTA
- Must be substantial enough to convince without bridge education
- Short formats (hooks, listicles) insufficient to build necessary trust

---

### 3-Piece Funnel (Lead → Bridge → Product Page)
**Lead ad format options:**
- Hook (curiosity-focused)
- Video (60s, problem + mechanism tease)
- Listicle (relatability + social proof)
- Long-form (500-750w, shorter than 2-piece funnel)

**Bridge ad format options:**
- Long-form (1000-1500w, mechanism deep dive + proof)
- Advertorial (editorial credibility)
- Video (3+ minutes, detailed mechanism + testimonials)
- VSL (extended explanation + multiple closes)

**Product page handled separately** (usually by conversion team, not Creative Strategist)

---

### Extended Funnel (Lead → Bridge 1 → Bridge 2 → Product Page)
**Lead ad:** Hook or 60s video (curiosity, no mechanism)

**Bridge 1:**
- Long-form (750-1000w) or Video (2-3 min)
- Mechanism explanation (first component)
- Emerging proof

**Bridge 2:**
- Long-form (1000-1500w) or Advertorial or VSL
- Complete mechanism + extensive proof
- Objection handling
- Urgency/scarcity

## Quick Decision Framework

**Use this table to quick-select format:**

| I need to... | Use this format |
|------------|----------------|
| Stop scroll immediately, test angle | Hook or Static Ad |
| Test angle with early-stage avatar | Listicle or Native Image |
| Educate moderate-awareness avatar on mechanism | Long-form (500-750w) or Video (60s) |
| Deep-dive on complex mechanism | Long-form (1000w+) or Advertorial or Video (3+min) |
| Build editorial credibility while selling | Advertorial |
| Show before-after transformation | Video (3+ min) or Testimonial-heavy long-form |
| Fast production cycle, multiple angles | Hooks, Listicles, Static Concepts |
| High conversion, single angle | Long-form (1000w+) with strong proof |
| Viral/organic sharing potential | Listicle, Native Image, Relatable Long-form |

## Common Format Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Choosing long-form for simple mechanism
- Problem: You write 1500 words to explain a 500-word mechanism
- Result: Pacing feels slow, reader loses interest
- Fix: Choose listicle, video (60s), or hook if mechanism is simple

### Mistake 2: Choosing hook when mechanism is complex
- Problem: Mechanism requires explanation, hook can't provide it
- Result: Reader gets curious but then confused; no conversion
- Fix: Add long-form or advertorial after hook to explain mechanism

### Mistake 3: Choosing advertorial for early-stage avatar
- Problem: Advertorial assumes sophistication the avatar doesn't have
- Result: Credibility play falls flat; audience isn't ready for editorial positioning
- Fix: Use long-form or listicle for early-stage; advertorial for solution-aware or product-aware

### Mistake 4: Choosing format that doesn't match funnel position
- Problem: Lead ad is 2000-word essay (too much for cold audience)
- Result: Read-through is low; audience not ready for this depth
- Fix: Match format to funnel position (lead = light, bridge = medium, product page = deep)

### Mistake 5: Choosing format that doesn't match villain
- Problem: Internal villain (self-blame) gets academic, cool-temperature format
- Result: Mechanism explanation lacks empathy; reader doesn't feel understood
- Fix: Match villain to temperature/narrator (internal villain = warm, empathetic; external villain = investigative, angry)
