# Self-Assessment SOP: Copy Chief Grading Framework

## Overview

Every piece of creative must be graded before filing. Copy Chief grading is your quality gate. It ensures nothing below 6.5 makes it into campaigns, and nothing below 7.5 gets volume deployment.

This SOP walks you through the assessment process: reading the piece fresh, scoring the 5 dimensions, applying anti-inflation rules, checking the Kill List, and making the production/archive decision.

**Assessment time per piece:** 10-15 minutes

---

## The 5-Dimension Scoring Framework

Copy Chief evaluation uses 5 core dimensions. Each is scored 0-10. The average of the 5 scores is your final Copy Chief score.

### Dimension 1: Belief-Shift Architecture (BSA)

**Definition:** Does the piece move the reader from an old belief to a new belief? Is the shift clear, compelling, and inevitable given the information presented?

**What you're evaluating:**
- Is there a clear "old belief" the reader holds at the start?
- Is there a "new belief" the reader should hold at the end?
- Does the piece build a logical case for why the old belief is wrong?
- Does the piece build a compelling case for why the new belief is true?
- Would a reader say "I used to think X, but now I think Y"?

**Scoring guide:**

**9-10 (Exceptional belief shift):**
- Old belief is crystal clear and emotionally resonant
- New belief is equally clear and emotionally compelling
- Proof for new belief is layered (logical + emotional + social)
- Reader has no choice but to shift their belief
- Example: Reader enters thinking "I'm lazy and undisciplined" and exits thinking "My metabolism is broken and fixable"

**7-8 (Strong belief shift):**
- Old belief is clear
- New belief is clear
- Proof for new belief is strong
- Reader is likely to shift their belief
- Some readers might resist, but evidence is compelling

**5-6 (Moderate belief shift):**
- Old belief is somewhat clear
- New belief is somewhat clear
- Proof exists but feels incomplete
- Reader might shift their belief, but it's not inevitable
- Some objections remain unanswered

**3-4 (Weak belief shift):**
- Old belief is vague
- New belief is vague or missing
- Proof is minimal or unconvincing
- Reader is unlikely to shift their belief
- Significant objections unaddressed

**0-2 (No belief shift):**
- No clear old belief identified
- No clear new belief stated
- No proof for any belief
- Reader leaves confused, not convinced
- Piece is unfocused

**Questions to ask yourself:**
- What belief does the reader enter with?
- What belief should they exit with?
- Is the gap between those two beliefs addressed?
- Is the proof for the new belief stronger than the old belief?
- Would I shift my own belief if I didn't already know the answer?

---

### Dimension 2: Dual-Track Narrative (DTN)

**Definition:** Does the piece work on both the intellectual level (mechanism, logic, reason) AND the emotional level (story, feeling, humanity)? Do both tracks support each other?

**What you're evaluating:**
- Does the piece have a logical, rational argument? (Mechanism, proof, data)
- Does the piece have an emotional, human story? (Character, struggle, feeling)
- Do logic and emotion support the same conclusion?
- Is there rhythm between intellectual moments and emotional moments?
- Would a skeptic find the logic compelling? Would an emotional person find the story compelling?

**Scoring guide:**

**9-10 (Perfect dual-track):**
- Logic track: Mechanism is crystal clear, each step follows necessarily from the previous, proof is robust
- Emotion track: Story is vivid, character is relatable, struggle is real, feeling is earned
- Both tracks lead to the same conclusion
- Reader is convinced both intellectually and emotionally
- Neither track overshadows the other; they're balanced

**7-8 (Strong dual-track):**
- Logic track: Mechanism is clear, proof is strong, some steps could be clearer
- Emotion track: Story is relatable, character is engaging, feeling is present
- Both tracks generally support the same conclusion
- Reader is convinced on both levels, though one level might be slightly stronger

**5-6 (Moderate dual-track):**
- Logic track: Mechanism exists but feels incomplete or unconvincing
- Emotion track: Story exists but feels shallow or template-like
- Tracks mostly support the same conclusion, but some friction exists
- Reader is partially convinced on both levels

**3-4 (Weak dual-track):**
- One track is strong, the other is weak
- Example: Strong logic but no emotion (reads academic, cold)
- Example: Strong emotion but weak logic (reads like hype, unconvincing)
- Tracks might even contradict each other
- Reader is convinced on one level but skeptical on the other

**0-2 (No dual-track):**
- Only one track exists, or neither track is present
- Logic is absent (pure hype)
- Emotion is absent (pure mechanism dump)
- Tracks contradict each other significantly

**Questions to ask yourself:**
- What's the logical argument here? (Mechanism, cause, effect)
- What's the emotional story here? (Character, struggle, transformation)
- Do they support each other or work at cross-purposes?
- Would a data-driven person be convinced? Would an intuitive person be convinced?
- Is there flow between intellectual and emotional moments, or does it feel disjointed?

---

### Dimension 3: Pacing Law Compliance (PLC)

**Definition:** Does the piece follow the 16 pacing laws from [[/long form copy/skills/long-form-copy/]]? Is the rhythm natural, the proof placed strategically, the mechanism revealed at the right moment?

**What you're evaluating:**
- Is specificity density consistent? (Specific language early, more specificity as trust builds)
- Is vulnerability placed at the right moment? (Early enough to be human, not so early reader doesn't trust you yet)
- Is proof strategically timed? (Problem proof before mechanism, mechanism proof before product, objection proof before CTA)
- Does the mechanism reveal happen at the right moment? (Not too early—reader isn't ready. Not too late—reader lost interest.)
- Is there rhythm between short punchy ideas and longer explanations?
- Does the pace match the temperature? (Hot = fast, warm = medium, cool = steady)
- Are there moments of pause (where reader can digest) and momentum (where reader is pulled forward)?

**Scoring guide:**

**9-10 (Exceptional pacing):**
- All 16 pacing laws are honored
- Specificity density builds naturally
- Proof is placed at exactly the right moment to support each claim
- Mechanism reveals happen with perfect timing
- Rhythm matches temperature
- Reader never feels rushed or bored
- Pace feels inevitable, not forced

**7-8 (Strong pacing):**
- Most pacing laws are honored
- Specificity density mostly builds naturally
- Proof is mostly well-timed
- Mechanism reveals are well-placed
- Rhythm generally matches temperature
- Occasional moments feel slightly off-pace

**5-6 (Moderate pacing):**
- Some pacing laws are honored, some are violated
- Specificity density is sometimes inconsistent
- Proof placement could be better
- Mechanism reveal might happen too early or too late
- Rhythm doesn't always match temperature
- Reader might feel slightly rushed or bored at certain points

**3-4 (Weak pacing):**
- Many pacing laws are violated
- Specificity density is inconsistent
- Proof is poorly timed
- Mechanism reveals at wrong moments
- Rhythm doesn't match temperature
- Reader feels significantly rushed or bored

**0-2 (No pacing coherence):**
- Pacing laws are ignored
- Specificity is random
- Proof appears randomly
- Mechanism is dumped without structure
- No rhythm at all

**Questions to ask yourself:**
- Does the specificity density build naturally? (Vague → specific → very specific)
- When is vulnerability revealed? Does it feel natural for the timing?
- When is proof introduced? Does it support what came before?
- When is mechanism explained? Is it early enough to build curiosity, late enough to build trust?
- Does the pace feel rushed, natural, or slow?
- Do short and long sentences create rhythm, or does it feel random?
- Would I want to keep reading at each point in the piece?

---

### Dimension 4: Voice & Authenticity (VA)

**Definition:** Does the piece sound like a real human being? Does the voice match the narrator choice from the brief? Do word choices match the avatar's actual language?

**What you're evaluating:**
- Does the voice sound like a real person or like an AI/generic marketing voice?
- Does the narrator voice match the brief? (If brief says "insider," does it sound like an insider?)
- Are word choices specific to the avatar's vernacular? (Would this avatar actually use these words?)
- Is there texture to the writing? (Specific details, surprising word choices, humor, authenticity?)
- Are there any marketing clichés that make the piece feel "salesy"? ("Don't miss out," "Limited time," "Act now," etc.)
- Are there any AI tells? (Flowery prose that no human would write, forced rhymes, over-explanation)
- Is the voice consistent throughout, or does it shift?

**Scoring guide:**

**9-10 (Exceptional authenticity):**
- Voice is unmistakably human, specific, and believable
- Narrator voice perfectly matches the brief
- Every word choice feels natural to the avatar's vernacular
- Texture is high: specific details, surprising moments, humor
- Zero marketing clichés
- Zero AI tells
- Voice is consistent and compelling throughout
- Reader believes they're talking to a real person

**7-8 (Strong authenticity):**
- Voice feels human and specific
- Narrator voice mostly matches the brief
- Most word choices feel natural to avatar's vernacular
- Good texture; some specific details and surprising moments
- Minimal marketing clichés (1-2 present)
- No obvious AI tells
- Voice is mostly consistent
- Reader mostly believes they're talking to a real person

**5-6 (Moderate authenticity):**
- Voice feels somewhat human but with occasional generic moments
- Narrator voice is mostly present
- Some word choices feel natural; some feel off
- Moderate texture; mostly generic details
- Some marketing clichés present
- Occasional AI tells (flowery prose, over-explanation)
- Voice mostly consistent with occasional shifts

**3-4 (Weak authenticity):**
- Voice feels somewhat generic or AI-like
- Narrator voice is vague or inconsistent
- Many word choices feel off
- Low texture; generic details
- Multiple marketing clichés present
- Multiple AI tells present
- Voice inconsistency is noticeable

**0-2 (No authenticity):**
- Voice sounds completely AI or generic
- No clear narrator voice
- Word choices are all wrong for the avatar
- No texture; all generic
- Filled with marketing clichés
- Obvious AI tells throughout
- Voice is all over the place

**Questions to ask yourself:**
- If I didn't know this was marketing copy, would I believe a human wrote this?
- Does the voice match the brief's narrator choice?
- Would the avatar actually speak like this?
- Are there any moments that feel "marketing-y" or "AI-like"?
- Are there specific, surprising, human details? Or is it all generic?
- Is the voice consistent? Or does it shift mid-piece?
- What would I change to make this feel more human and less marketery?

---

### Dimension 5: Structural Engineering (SE)

**Definition:** Is the piece well-architected? Do the beats flow naturally? Are transitions smooth? Is the conclusion earned by the evidence presented?

**What you're evaluating:**
- Does the piece follow a logical structure? (Problem → villain → mechanism → proof → CTA)
- Do transitions between ideas feel natural or forced?
- Are all beats from the skill file structure present?
- Is the conclusion (CTA) earned by the evidence, or does it feel like a sudden ask?
- Is there a clear arc (beginning, middle, end)?
- Are any sections out of order or misplaced?
- Is any critical element missing?
- Is anything extraneous or distracting?

**Scoring guide:**

**9-10 (Exceptional structure):**
- Architecture follows skill file perfectly
- Every beat is present and in the right order
- Transitions are smooth and natural
- Each section supports the next
- Conclusion is inevitable given the evidence
- Nothing is extraneous
- Nothing critical is missing
- Piece feels like a complete, coherent whole

**7-8 (Strong structure):**
- Architecture mostly follows skill file
- Most beats are present and in the right order
- Most transitions are smooth
- Mostly flows logically
- Conclusion feels earned
- Minimal extraneous content
- No critical missing elements

**5-6 (Moderate structure):**
- Architecture partially follows skill file
- Some beats are missing or out of order
- Some transitions feel forced
- Generally flows but with rough spots
- Conclusion feels somewhat earned
- Some extraneous content
- One or two minor elements missing

**3-4 (Weak structure):**
- Architecture loosely follows skill file
- Multiple beats are missing or out of order
- Many transitions feel forced
- Significant rough spots in flow
- Conclusion feels somewhat unearned
- Significant extraneous content
- One or more important elements missing

**0-2 (Broken structure):**
- Architecture doesn't follow skill file
- Many beats are missing or completely out of order
- Most transitions are forced
- Doesn't flow coherently
- Conclusion feels unearned or non-existent
- Lots of extraneous content
- Critical elements missing
- Piece feels chaotic or incomplete

**Questions to ask yourself:**
- Does this piece follow the skill file structure?
- Are all the required beats present?
- Does each paragraph/section flow naturally from the previous one?
- If I read this aloud, does it feel coherent?
- Could anything be cut without losing meaning?
- Is anything essential missing?
- Does the ending feel like it was always inevitable, or does it feel like a surprise?

## Anti-Inflation Rules

**This is critical:** Default starting score is 5. You must earn every point above 5.

### Why Anti-Inflation?
Piece quality naturally regresses to "fine." If you don't enforce anti-inflation rules, everything scores 6.5-7.5, and your scoring becomes meaningless. High scores must be rare and earned.

### The Rules

**Rule 1: Default Starting Score is 5**
- Every dimension starts at 5 (average)
- You add to this score only when the piece demonstrably exceeds average
- You subtract only for clear failures

**Rule 2: Half-Point Increments Only**
- Scores must be X.0 or X.5
- No X.3 or X.7 (forces decision-making)
- Final score is average of 5 dimensions, can result in X.1-X.9

**Rule 3: No Single Dimension Above 8**
- 9-10 scores are reserved for the top 1% of creative
- Most strong pieces max out at 8
- This prevents inflation from one exceptionally strong dimension

**Rule 4: Score Represents the Entire Piece, Not Just One Dimension**
- Even if one dimension scores 8.5, your overall final score can't be 8.0+ if other dimensions are weak
- Overall score is the average, not the best dimension
- Example: Belief-Shift is 8, but Voice is 5, Pacing is 5.5, Dual-Track is 6, Structure is 6 → Average is 6.1

**Rule 5: Justify Every Half-Point Above 5**
- For each dimension:
  - If you score it 5.5, write one sentence justifying the +0.5
  - If you score it 6, write one sentence justifying the +1.0
  - If you score it 7 or higher, write 2-3 sentences justifying the higher score
- This forces specificity and prevents drift

**Rule 6: Market-Level Calibration**
- 5 = Market average (what most marketing copy scores)
- 6 = Above average (noticeably better than typical)
- 6.5 = Production-quality (safe to deploy)
- 7 = Very good (clear differentiation)
- 7.5 = Scaling-quality (approved for volume)
- 8+ = Exceptional (rare)

**Calibration examples:**
- Your piece has 9/10 belief-shift architecture but 5/10 voice
- Average = 6.3
- This is above 6.5? No. Average is 6.3. Score it 6.3.
- Why? Because the weak voice is dragging down the exceptional belief-shift

## The Kill List: Instant Deductions

Certain violations are automatic score-cappers. If a piece commits these sins, cap the maximum score at 7.0 or lower, even if other dimensions are strong.

### Kill List Items

**1. Obvious AI Language**
- Flowery prose that no human would write
- Over-explanation of simple concepts
- Forced alliteration or rhyme
- List structures that scream "AI wrote this"
- Examples: "Delve into the depths," "synergistic solutions," "a tapestry of benefits"
- **If present: Cap score at 7.0**

**2. Transparent Marketing Tells**
- "Limited time"
- "Don't miss out"
- "Act now before..."
- "This won't last long"
- "Exclusive offer"
- Fake urgency that violates trust
- **If present: Cap score at 7.0**

**3. Naked Asks (CTA without trust setup)**
- CTA appears without sufficient belief-shift work
- Reader is asked to buy something they don't yet understand or believe in
- No proof before the ask
- No permission established
- Example: Piece explains fatigue problem, then immediately "Buy Lunessa now"
- **If present: Cap score at 7.0**

**4. Mechanism Vagueness**
- Mechanism is unclear or hand-wavy
- Reader doesn't understand *why* the solution works, just that it does
- Claims are made without explanation
- Example: "Lunessa energizes your body" (how? why? what specifically?)
- **If present: Cap score at 7.0**

**5. Villain Absence**
- Piece doesn't clearly identify what belief must shift
- Reader doesn't know what false belief you're demolishing
- No clear antagonist (external, internal, or philosophical)
- **If present: Cap score at 7.0**

**6. Approval-Seeking Language**
- "If this resonates with you..."
- "If you're like me..."
- "I don't know if this applies to you, but..."
- "You might relate to this..."
- Weak posture that undermines authority
- **If present: Cap score at 6.5**

**7. Failed Funnel Congruence**
- If piece is part of a multi-piece funnel, and it doesn't prepare reader for the next piece
- Or doesn't assume the learning from the previous piece
- Exit-state / Entry-state mismatch
- **If present: Cap score at 6.5**

**8. Logical Fallacies or False Claims**
- Piece makes claims that are contradicted by its own evidence
- Or makes claims it doesn't support
- Example: Mechanism claims to address three factors, but only explains two
- **If present: Cap score at 6.0**

### How to Apply Kill List

**During assessment:**
1. Read through piece once
2. Check for each Kill List item
3. If you find 0 items: No cap, score normally
4. If you find 1 item: Cap score at 7.0 maximum
5. If you find 2+ items: Cap score at 6.5 maximum

**Document in assessment notes:** "Kill List: PASSED" or "Kill List: FAILED — [item name] present"

## The Assessment Process: Step-by-Step

### Phase 1: Fresh Read (5 minutes)

1. **Step away from the piece for 5-10 minutes** after writing and polishing
   - This gives you mental distance
   - You'll read it with fresher eyes
   - You won't be in "writer mode" anymore

2. **Read the piece aloud, slowly**
   - Listen for rhythm, tone, flow
   - Note any moments that feel off
   - Mark any obvious problems

3. **Read it a second time silently**
   - Follow the argument
   - Check the logic
   - Notice the voice

### Phase 2: Dimension Scoring (10 minutes)

1. **Score Belief-Shift Architecture**
   - Write the old belief the reader enters with
   - Write the new belief they should exit with
   - Score how clearly the piece makes this shift
   - Write justification (1-2 sentences)

2. **Score Dual-Track Narrative**
   - Identify the logical argument
   - Identify the emotional story
   - Score how well both work and support each other
   - Write justification

3. **Score Pacing Law Compliance**
   - Check specificity density progression
   - Check proof placement
   - Check mechanism reveal timing
   - Check rhythm match to temperature
   - Write justification

4. **Score Voice & Authenticity**
   - Does it sound human?
   - Does it match narrator choice from brief?
   - Does it use avatar's language?
   - Any marketing tells or AI language?
   - Write justification

5. **Score Structural Engineering**
   - Check against skill file beats
   - Check transitions
   - Check if anything is missing or extraneous
   - Write justification

### Phase 3: Kill List Check (2 minutes)

Review the 8 Kill List items. Check each one.

**If no items present:** Write "Kill List: PASSED"

**If 1+ items present:** Document which items, cap score accordingly

### Phase 4: Calculate Final Score (1 minute)

```
Belief-Shift Architecture: ___
Dual-Track Narrative: ___
Pacing Law Compliance: ___
Voice & Authenticity: ___
Structural Engineering: ___
---
AVERAGE: ___ (this is your final Copy Chief score)
Kill List check: PASSED | FAILED: [item]
Adjusted cap (if Kill List failed): ___
```

### Phase 5: Production Decision (1 minute)

- **6.5-7.4**: Production-ready (safe to use in campaigns, test before scaling)
- **7.5+**: Scaling-ready (approved for volume deployment)
- **Below 6.5**: Archive in `/rejected/` (do not use in campaigns)

## Common Assessment Mistakes & Corrections

### Mistake 1: Scoring High on One Dimension Inflates Overall Score
**Problem:**
- Belief-Shift is 8.5 (exceptional)
- But Voice is 5 (generic)
- You score the piece 7.5 because "the belief-shift is so strong"

**Fix:**
- Calculate average: (8.5 + 5 + 5.5 + 5 + 6) / 5 = 6.0
- That's your score, not 7.5
- Overall quality is limited by the weakest dimensions

### Mistake 2: Scoring High Because "It's Better Than What I Expected"
**Problem:**
- You expected a mediocre piece
- The actual piece is above-average
- You score it 7.0 because it exceeded expectations

**Fix:**
- Expectations don't matter
- Score against the 5-dimension rubric
- Above-average expectation = above-average scoring, but still calibrate to the rubric
- If the piece is truly 7.0 per the dimensions, fine. But don't inflate because of expectations.

### Mistake 3: Not Applying Kill List
**Problem:**
- Piece has "Don't miss out" and naked ask (CTA without proof)
- You score it 7.2 overall
- You don't apply the 7.0 cap

**Fix:**
- Kill List violations are automatic caps
- 1 item → cap at 7.0
- 2+ items → cap at 6.5
- Apply the cap always

### Mistake 4: Scoring High on Voice Without Checking for Marketing Tells
**Problem:**
- Piece sounds natural and human
- But it's filled with "Act now!" "Limited time!" "Don't miss out!"
- You score Voice at 7.5 because it sounds natural

**Fix:**
- Marketing tells are part of Voice evaluation
- Authentic voice doesn't use transparent marketing language
- Presence of marketing tells should lower Voice score
- And trigger Kill List cap

### Mistake 5: Scoring Without Justification
**Problem:**
- You score Belief-Shift at 7.5
- You don't write why
- Later you can't remember how you justified that score
- You lose consistency in calibration

**Fix:**
- Write justification for every score above 5
- Even a one-sentence justification helps
- This also forces you to think critically instead of gut-scoring

## Calibration Over Time

After 10-15 assessments, you'll develop calibration intuition. But recalibrate every month:

- **Review your scoring patterns**: Are you averaging 6.8? Probably inflating. (Average should be closer to 6.0-6.3 across all pieces.)
- **Re-read old pieces you scored 7.5+**: Do they still feel that strong? Or do they feel weaker now?
- **Compare pieces**: Is the 7.1 piece noticeably weaker than the 7.4 piece? Or are they similarly strong?
- **Adjust if needed**: Reset your calibration to match reality

## Ready to Assess?

Use the template above. Score the 5 dimensions. Check the Kill List. Calculate average. Make production decision. File with frontmatter.

**No piece goes into campaigns without a score. No score goes unfiled. No score goes unexplained.**
