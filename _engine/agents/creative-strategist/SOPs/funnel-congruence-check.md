# Funnel Congruence Check SOP

## Overview

When you write multiple pieces designed to work together in a funnel, they must fit together coherently. A fracture between pieces breaks trust, loses readers, and kills conversions.

This SOP ensures that your multi-piece creative sets work together, not against each other.

**Congruence check time:** 5-15 minutes per funnel (not per piece)

---

## What Is Funnel Congruence?

Funnel congruence means that each piece in the funnel prepares the reader for the next piece. The Exit-State of Piece A becomes the Entry-State of Piece B.

**Exit-State**: The emotional, informational, and trust state the reader is in after reading Piece A.

**Entry-State**: The emotional, informational, and trust state Piece B requires the reader to be in for it to work.

### Example: 3-Piece Funnel (Lead Ad → Bridge → Product Page)

**Lead Ad Exit-State**: "My fatigue might be fixable. It's not just something I have to accept."

**Bridge Entry-State**: Requires belief that fatigue is fixable, and curiosity about *how* it's fixable.

**Match?** YES → No fracture

**Bridge Exit-State**: "My fatigue is caused by a specific metabolic dysfunction. There's a protocol that addresses it."

**Product Page Entry-State**: Requires understanding of mechanism and readiness to learn about the solution.

**Match?** YES → No fracture

---

## The Exit-State / Entry-State Test

This is the primary congruence test. Apply it to every transition in your funnel.

### How to Apply the Test

**Step 1: Define Piece A Exit-State**

Read your first piece. Ask yourself:
- What does the reader believe after reading this?
- What emotions are they feeling?
- What information do they have?
- What questions are they curious about?
- How much do they trust you?
- Are they ready for the next message?

**Write the Exit-State as 2-3 sentences:**
- Example: "The reader believes their fatigue has a fixable cause. They're curious about what that cause is. They partially trust you because you positioned yourself as knowledgeable."

**Step 2: Define Piece B Entry-State**

Read your second piece. Ask yourself:
- What does this piece assume the reader already believes?
- What information does it assume they have?
- What trust level does it require?
- What emotional state does it need them to be in?

**Write the Entry-State as 2-3 sentences:**
- Example: "This piece assumes the reader believes fatigue is caused by something specific (not just 'life'). It requires moderate trust. It assumes they want to understand the cause before learning about the solution."

**Step 3: Compare**

Does Exit-State match Entry-State?
- YES → No fracture, proceed
- PARTIAL → Minor fracture, consider revision
- NO → Major fracture, must revise

### Example Matches & Fractures

**Example 1: Match**
```
Piece A (Lead Ad) Exit-State:
"My afternoon crashes are a real problem, not just something I have to accept.
I'm curious if there's an explanation."

Piece B (Bridge) Entry-State:
"Requires reader to believe the problem is real and be curious about the cause."

Match? YES → No fracture
```

**Example 2: Partial Match**
```
Piece A (Lead Ad) Exit-State:
"My fatigue is probably caused by stress and poor sleep.
I should try harder with sleep hygiene."

Piece B (Bridge) Entry-State:
"Requires reader to understand that sleep alone won't fix metabolic dysfunction.
Mechanism is more complex than just 'sleep better.'"

Match? PARTIAL FRACTURE →
Piece A points reader toward sleep-focused solution, but Piece B says sleep alone won't work.
Reader feels misled. Need to adjust either Piece A or Piece B.
```

**Example 3: No Match**
```
Piece A (Lead Ad) Exit-State:
"There are lots of energy supplements out there, and Lunessa might be worth trying."

Piece B (Bridge) Exit-State:
"Requires reader to understand that Lunessa is NOT just another supplement,
but a specifically designed metabolic reset tool."

Match? NO FRACTURE →
Piece A positioned product as generic supplement. Piece B positions it as unique solution.
Reader feels bait-and-switched. Must revise Piece A to properly set up Piece B positioning.
```

## The 5 Handoff Checks

Beyond Exit-State / Entry-State, use these 5 checks to audit handoffs between pieces.

### Handoff Check 1: Voice Continuity

**Question**: Does the narrator voice stay consistent between pieces?

**What to check:**
- Narrator personality: Is it the same narrator? Or a different person?
- Posture: Does the voice feel urgent in Piece A but patient in Piece B? (Or vice versa?)
- Temperature: Does it switch from hot to cool, or stay consistent?
- Relationship to reader: Do they address the reader the same way?

**Example fracture:**
```
Piece A (Lead Ad): Insider narrator, angry posture, warm temperature
"The diet industry has lied to you for decades about energy. Here's what's actually happening."

Piece B (Bridge): Expert narrator, patient posture, cool temperature
"Research shows that metabolic dysfunction follows these specific patterns: [scientific explanation]"

Fracture? YES →
Narrator switched (insider → expert). Posture switched (angry → patient). Temperature switched (warm → cool).
Reader feels like they're being handed off to a different person. Trust fractures.

Fix: Either keep Piece B as the same insider (but slowing down), or adjust Piece A to introduce the expert narrator.
```

**Good continuity:**
```
Piece A (Lead Ad): Insider narrator, angry-turning-hopeful posture, warm temperature
"The diet industry lied to you, but here's what I discovered..."

Piece B (Bridge): Same insider narrator, hopeful posture, warm temperature (slightly cooler)
"Here's the specific mechanism I learned about, and here's how I used it to..."

Continuity? YES →
Same narrator, posture evolves naturally (anger → hope → education), temperature stays warm but deepens.
Reader feels like they're continuing a conversation.
```

### Handoff Check 2: Mechanism Language Match

**Question**: Do the two pieces use the same language for the mechanism?

**What to check:**
- Mechanism components: Are they named the same way?
- Terminology: Do you call it "metabolic reset" in Piece A and "metabolic re-anchoring" in Piece B? (Confusing)
- Specificity: Are you equally specific about mechanism in both pieces, or does one jump to vague language?

**Example fracture:**
```
Piece A (Lead Ad):
"Your fatigue is caused by three things: energy substrate depletion, circadian dysregulation, and dopamine deficiency."

Piece B (Bridge):
"The mechanism involves your body's energy production being broken."

Fracture? YES →
Piece A sets up three specific components. Piece B regresses to vague language.
Reader thinks "Wait, what happened to the specific explanation you promised?"

Fix: Keep Piece B specific. Reference the same three components.
```

**Good language match:**
```
Piece A (Lead Ad):
"Your fatigue is caused by three things: energy substrate depletion, circadian dysregulation, and dopamine deficiency."

Piece B (Bridge):
"We already know your fatigue comes from three components. Let's dive into each one:
First, energy substrate depletion happens because [explanation]..."

Match? YES →
Same terminology, same specificity level, Piece B builds on Piece A's foundation.
```

### Handoff Check 3: Emotional Temperature Progression

**Question**: Does the emotional temperature progress naturally from Piece A to Piece B?

**What to check:**
- Is there a logical reason for temperature change?
- If temperature shifts (hot → warm or warm → cool), is it intentional and earned?
- Or does temperature shift randomly (reader notices inconsistency)?

**Example fracture:**
```
Piece A (Lead Ad): Hot temperature (fast, urgent, vivid)
"Your energy is broken. Not your willpower. Your metabolism. And there's one thing that fixes it."

Piece B (Bridge): Hot temperature (fast, urgent, vivid)
"Stop wasting time on sleep optimization. Stop believing the hype about coffee.
Your metabolism is broken and here's the only way to fix it."

Fracture? Maybe →
Both pieces are hot temperature. But Piece A ends with hope ("there's one thing that fixes it").
Piece B restarts urgency with "stop wasting time" (like reader has been wasting time for months).
Reader feels nagged, not guided forward.

Fix: Piece B should be warm temperature (you've convinced them the problem exists; now educate them gently).
```

**Good temperature progression:**
```
Piece A (Lead Ad): Hot temperature
"Your afternoon crashes point to something specific. It's not laziness. It's metabolic."

Piece B (Bridge): Warm temperature
"Now that you know your fatigue is metabolic, let me explain exactly how it works.
Here's the three-part mechanism..."

Progression? YES →
Piece A creates urgency (something specific is wrong). Piece B shifts to education (here's what's wrong, explained).
Natural progression from hot → warm.
```

### Handoff Check 4: Awareness Level Progression

**Question**: Does the reader's awareness level progress naturally from Piece A to Piece B?

**What to check:**
- Does Piece B assume the reader knows something they weren't taught in Piece A?
- Or does Piece B re-teach something Piece A already covered?
- Is there a logical progression of awareness?

**Awareness levels:** Unaware → Problem-Aware → Solution-Aware → Product-Aware → Most Aware

**Example fracture:**
```
Piece A (Lead Ad) Exit-State: Reader is Problem-Aware
"Your fatigue is real. It's not laziness. It's something specific."

Piece B (Bridge) Entry-State: Product-Aware required
"Here's why Lunessa's unique ingredient blend is different from every other energy supplement..."

Fracture? YES →
Piece A only got reader to "problem is real." Piece B assumes "reader understands our product differentiation."
Reader skips multiple awareness stages. They're not ready for product comparison.

Fix: Piece B should first be Solution-Aware (explain mechanism), then Product-Aware (introduce Lunessa as the mechanism solution).
```

**Good awareness progression:**
```
Piece A (Lead Ad):
Problem-Aware → Solution-Aware
"Your fatigue is metabolic. Solutions exist for metabolic fatigue."

Piece B (Bridge):
Solution-Aware → Product-Aware
"Here's how metabolic reset works. Lunessa addresses the substrate + dopamine components of that reset."

Progression? YES →
Natural awareness progression. Each piece prepares the reader for the next awareness level.
```

### Handoff Check 5: Trust Transfer

**Question**: Is trust built in Piece A maintained and escalated in Piece B?

**What to check:**
- Does Piece A establish credibility/authority in a way that carries to Piece B?
- Does Piece B immediately re-establish trust, or does it assume trust from Piece A?
- Does Piece B do anything that breaks trust earned in Piece A?

**Example fracture:**
```
Piece A (Lead Ad):
Insider narrator ("I solved this problem myself 2 years ago")
Proof: "Here's what I learned through my own experience"
Trust level: Moderate (personal experience credible)

Piece B (Bridge):
Expert narrator ("According to research and clinical studies")
Proof: "Here's what peer-reviewed science says"
Trust shift: Abrupt (from personal → scientific)

Fracture? PARTIAL →
Different proof structures (personal vs. scientific).
Reader thinks "Wait, are we trusting personal experience or science?"
The narrator switch makes it feel like a different person is talking.

Fix: Either keep Piece B as the same insider (but referencing research),
or introduce the expert narrator in Piece A to prepare for handoff.
```

**Good trust transfer:**
```
Piece A (Lead Ad):
Insider narrator with scientific foundation
"I solved this problem, and here's what the science says about why it works"
Trust level: Moderate-High (personal + scientific)

Piece B (Bridge):
Same insider narrator, deepening the science
"As I mentioned, here's a deeper dive into the scientific mechanism"
Trust level: High (consistent narrator + escalating evidence)

Transfer? YES →
Trust is maintained and escalated. Reader feels like the same credible person is taking them deeper.
```

## Common Funnel Fractures & How to Fix Them

### Fracture 1: Villain Switch Mid-Funnel

**What happens:**
- Lead Ad targets internal villain (self-blame)
- Bridge Ad targets external villain (industry deception)
- Reader feels whiplash (first blamed themselves, now blaming the industry)

**Fix:**
- Choose one primary villain per funnel
- Secondary villains can be introduced, but primary villain should stay consistent
- Example: Primary villain is internal (self-blame), secondary villain is external (industry pressure that caused the self-blame)

### Fracture 2: Mechanism Complexity Jump

**What happens:**
- Lead Ad explains mechanism simply: "Your metabolism is broken"
- Bridge Ad dives into biochemistry: "ATP synthesis is inhibited due to substrate depletion and mitochondrial dysregulation"
- Reader lost halfway through Bridge

**Fix:**
- Bridge should gradually increase complexity, not jump
- Lead: "Your metabolism is broken"
- Bridge (step 1): "Here's what 'broken metabolism' means"
- Bridge (step 2): "Here's the specific biological processes involved"
- Bridge (step 3): "Here's the biochemical explanation"

### Fracture 3: Trust Shift from Personal to Scientific

**What happens:**
- Lead Ad uses personal testimony: "I fixed my fatigue by [mechanism]"
- Bridge Ad uses only data: "Studies show that [mechanism]"
- Reader trusted the person, not sure they trust the data

**Fix:**
- Lead Ad should combine personal + scientific: "I fixed my fatigue by [mechanism], which aligns with research showing [findings]"
- Bridge Ad continues both: "Here's deeper science on what I discovered"
- Trust is maintained because both sources remain consistent

### Fracture 4: Awareness Level Gap

**What happens:**
- Lead Ad: Problem-Aware (fatigue is real, not my fault)
- Bridge Ad: Product-Aware (here's why Lunessa > other supplements)
- Reader skips Solution-Aware entirely (doesn't understand *what kind* of solution exists)

**Fix:**
- Fill the gaps with intermediate awareness
- Lead Ad: Problem-Aware
- Bridge Ad Part 1: Solution-Aware (mechanism, category of solution)
- Bridge Ad Part 2: Product-Aware (why Lunessa, differentiation)

### Fracture 5: Posture/Tone Shift

**What happens:**
- Lead Ad: Urgent, angry, you've been lied to
- Bridge Ad: Patient, educational, here's what science says
- Reader feels like urgent problem became calm lecture

**Fix:**
- If you start urgent, maintain urgent (or deliberately shift in intentional way)
- Lead Ad: Angry + urgent
- Bridge Ad: Angry (at the lie) + educational (here's the truth)
- Keep the anger/posture consistent even as you add new information

## Multi-Piece Funnel Audits (3+ Pieces)

If you're writing a 3+ piece funnel, audit ALL transitions:

```
Lead Ad → Bridge 1 [Check congruence]
Bridge 1 → Bridge 2 [Check congruence]
Bridge 2 → Product Page [Check congruence, if you're writing product page]
```

Each transition uses the same 5 Handoff Checks.

---

## When to Skip Congruence Check

You only need to do the full congruence check if:
1. You're writing 2+ pieces in the same funnel, AND
2. The pieces are meant to work together sequentially

**Skip if:**
- You're writing isolated hooks/ads (not part of a funnel)
- You're writing single long-form pieces
- You're writing pieces for different products/avatars

**Still do a light version if:**
- You're writing multiple formats (hook + long-form + video) even if they're standalone
- Different formats should feel like they're from the same campaign, not random pieces
- At minimum, check voice continuity and mechanism language match

---

## Congruence Before Filing

**Before you file a piece that's part of a funnel:**

1. Run the full congruence audit
2. If congruence issues found, revise the piece
3. Re-audit after revision
4. Only file once congruence passes

**If you find a congruence issue AFTER filing:**
1. Move the piece to `/revisions/` folder
2. Note the specific congruence issue
3. Revise and re-audit
4. Re-file with updated date

---

## Ready to Audit?

**If you're writing multi-piece funnels, always run this check before filing.**

**Use the audit template. Check all 5 handoff dimensions. Identify and fix any fractures. Then file.**
