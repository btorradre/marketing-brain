# Brief Creation SOP

## Overview

A creative brief is your specification document. It tells you (and anyone reading it later) exactly what you're about to write, why you're writing it, and what it's supposed to accomplish. A well-formed brief prevents mid-draft direction changes, false starts, and low scores from "unfocused writing."

This SOP walks you through brief creation from raw intelligence to filed, approval-ready brief.

**Typical brief creation time:** 10-20 minutes per brief (faster if you've already designed the concept during the creative sprint pre-production)

## What Is a Creative Brief?

A brief answers these 7 core questions:

1. **Desire**: What does the avatar want? (The outcome they're chasing)
2. **Angle**: What belief are we targeting to shift? (The insight we're exploiting)
3. **Concept**: Who's telling the story, and how? (Narrator, posture, temperature, product integration)
4. **Mechanism**: The "why" — why does this solution work? (The structural logic)
5. **Avatar Sophistication**: How aware are they? (Schwartz stage, awareness level)
6. **Villain Architecture**: What belief needs to die for success? (What are we tearing down?)
7. **Close**: What's the ask? (Soft CTA, hard ask, trial, etc.)

A brief is **not** a creative concept. It's not the ad itself. It's the strategic specification that the ad will execute against.

## Pre-Brief Preparation: Pulling from Market Analyst Intelligence

Before you write your brief, you need raw material. The [[/agents/market-analyst/intel-drops/]] folder contains this material. Here's how to extract it:

### Step 1: Read Latest Intel-Drop
Navigate to [[/agents/market-analyst/intel-drops/]] and open the most recent file (newest date).

Extract these sections:

**Unsaturated Angles:**
- Market Analyst identifies angles that haven't been explored yet (or are underexplored)
- Copy these to your working notes
- These are HIGH-PRIORITY angles for your brief (less competition in the conversation)

**Villain Architecture:**
- Which external villains are active? (Industry, product category, competitor claims)
- Which internal villains are active? (Self-blame, shame, identity beliefs)
- Which philosophical villains are active? (Fatalism, "this is just how it is")
- Choose ONE villain per brief. You can't demolish all three in a single ad.

**Avatar Language Shifts:**
- Market Analyst studies the exact words your avatar uses when discussing the problem
- Example: "I'm not actually tired, I'm just... numb" is a language shift that changes how you should frame the messaging
- Use this language in your brief and copy

**Desire Evolution:**
- Market Analyst may identify desire shifts (what the avatar wants is changing)
- Example: Desire shifted from "have more energy for my kids" to "be functional at work without crashing"
- Ensure your brief targets current desire, not old desire

**Competitive Positioning:**
- What are competitors doing? What angles are they using?
- Your brief should exploit gaps (angles competitors aren't using)

### Step 2: Cross-Reference Against Existing Creative
Search the vault for existing creative on your target product. Document:
- What angles are already covered? (Avoid repetition)
- What mechanisms have been used? (Process? Ingredient? Permission?)
- What villains have been targeted? (Diversify your villain selection)
- What formats exist? (Long-form? Video? Advertorial?)

**This prevents creating a duplicate brief.**

### Step 3: Identify Your Angle Opportunity
Combine intel-drop + vault inventory:
- Intel says "Permission angle is unsaturated"
- Vault search shows you've never written a Permission angle for this product
- **Your angle opportunity**: Permission angle for [Product] to [Specific Avatar Segment]

## The Brief Template Structure

Use [[creative-brief-template.md]] as your working document. Here's what each section means and how to fill it:

### 1. Desire Statement

**What does the avatar want?**

This is not what the product does. It's what the customer wants to happen in their life.

**Examples (not for your brief, just to show structure):**
- "I want to have energy to play with my kids without crashing at 3pm"
- "I want to feel confident that my body can handle a full day without exhaustion"
- "I want to be functional at work without relying on 5 cups of coffee"

**How to source this:**
- Market Analyst intel-drops (they research avatar desires)
- Your own customer interviews (if available)
- Reference library ([[/long form copy/references/]] has 100+ real FB ads with stated desires)

**Why it matters:**
- Your copy will frame the product as the bridge between "current state" and "desired outcome"
- If you get desire wrong, everything downstream fails
- The desire determines which avatar segment you're speaking to (tired moms vs. office workers vs. athletes all have different desires even if they're technically tired)

**Write it as a clear, specific, one-sentence statement:**
- ✓ "I want to have sustained energy throughout my workday without the 3pm crash"
- ✗ "I want more energy" (too vague)

---

### 2. Angle

**What belief are we targeting?**

An angle is the specific belief we're exploiting to make the desire feel possible/credible again.

**Examples (not for your brief):**
- **Fatigue Spiral Angle**: "Most tired people think tiredness is a character flaw, but it's actually a metabolic dysfunction that's fixable"
- **Permission Angle**: "You've been taught that your exhaustion is normal and unchangeable, but you have permission to optimize your energy like you optimize your fitness"
- **Metabolism Angle**: "People think the solution is to push harder, but the real solution is to fix the underlying metabolic dysfunction that's causing the crash"

**How to find your angle:**
1. **Start with Market Analyst intel** — they flag unsaturated angles
2. **Map to villain architecture** — which belief are we un-believing?
3. **Reference vault gaps** — what angles haven't we touched?
4. **Cross-check competitor references** — what angles are competitors using that we haven't tried?

**Structure an angle statement like this:**
- "The [Angle Name] Angle: Most [Avatar Type] believe [old belief], but the truth is [new belief/insight]. This angle positions [Product] as the solution that makes [new belief] possible."

**Example:**
- "The Metabolic Reset Angle: Most chronically tired women believe their fatigue is a character flaw or permanent condition. But the truth is that modern eating patterns and stress cycles have created a metabolic dysfunction that's specifically fixable through [mechanism]. This angle positions Lunessa as the metabolic reset tool that makes recovery possible."

**Why it matters:**
- The angle is your hook idea + the belief shift your copy will engineer
- Wrong angle = entire copy misses the target
- Right angle = entire copy becomes coherent and compelling

---

### 3. Concept

**Who's telling this story, and how?**

A concept specifies the narrator choice, posture, temperature, and product integration position. This is your creative direction.

**Components:**

**Narrator Choice** (Who is the voice?)
- **Guide**: Expert/authority figure teaching the avatar (outside expert, coach, doctor)
- **Insider**: Someone already in the club, who already solved it, sharing the secret (peer, success story)
- **Protagonist**: The avatar themselves, telling their own story (first-person, you = the hero)
- **Villain Confessor**: The villain admitting their deception (industry confessing the scam, internal doubt admitting it's wrong)
- **Other**: Third-party observer (journalist, researcher, spouse, friend)

**Narrator details:**
- Guide: "A functional medicine practitioner who specializes in energy metabolism"
- Insider: "A woman who recovered from the fatigue spiral 2 years ago and is now a high-energy entrepreneur"
- Protagonist: "The tired mom, speaking in first-person, revealing her journey"

**Posture** (What's the relationship to the reader?)
- **Urgent**: Time is running out. The problem is acute. Speed matters.
- **Patient**: We have time. Let's solve this right.
- **Angry**: Furious at the villain, protective toward the reader.
- **Joyful**: Excited about the possibility of change.
- **Conspiratorial**: "We know something others don't. This is secret."
- **Educational**: Neutral, evidence-based, teaching mode.

**Examples:**
- Urgent + Insider: "I didn't realize I was running out of time until my body shut down completely. That's why [mechanism]."
- Patient + Angry: "The diet industry has been lying to you for decades. But here's the actual truth, and here's why it matters."
- Joyful + Insider: "When I finally understood [mechanism], everything changed. And now I want to help you see it too."

**Temperature** (Emotional intensity and pacing speed)
- **Hot**: Fast, urgent, high-energy, visceral, short sentences, immediate action
- **Warm**: Personal, relatable, medium pace, human, conversational
- **Cool**: Intellectual, analytical, evidence-heavy, slow reveal, data-forward

**Examples:**
- Hot temperature: Short sentences. One idea per sentence. Rapid pacing. Exclamation marks. Vivid verbs.
  - "Your energy is broken. Not your willpower. Not your character. Your metabolism. And there's one thing that fixes it."
- Warm temperature: Medium-length sentences. Story structure. Conversational tone. Natural rhythm.
  - "When I finally understood that my fatigue was metabolic, everything clicked. I wasn't lazy. My body was just broken in a specific, fixable way."
- Cool temperature: Longer sentences. Logic-forward. Evidence-heavy. Technical language welcome.
  - "The fatigue cascade that most chronically tired individuals experience is rooted in three specific metabolic dysfunctions: substrate availability, circadian rhythm dysregulation, and dopamine-dependent motivation depletion."

**Product Integration Position** (How central is the product?)
- **Invisible**: Product isn't mentioned until the very end, or not at all. Focus is entirely on mechanism and belief shift.
- **Peripheral**: Product mentioned 1-2 times. It's one piece of the solution, not the hero.
- **Supporting**: Product introduced mid-way. It's clearly part of the mechanism explanation.
- **Central**: Product is the hero. The entire copy is about the product and why it's special.

**Concept Write-Up Example:**
```
Narrator: Insider (woman who solved the fatigue spiral 18 months ago; now a fitness coach with a thriving business)
Posture: Angry (at the fitness industry for perpetuating the "just push harder" myth) + Joyful (about the possibility of change)
Temperature: Warm (personal story, human pacing, not rushed)
Product Integration: Peripheral (Lunessa is introduced mid-way as one component of a multi-part reset; not the focus, but essential)
```

### 4. Mechanism

**The "why" — why does this solution work?**

A mechanism is the structural logic that explains how the desired outcome becomes possible. It's not what the product is, it's how it functions.

**NOT a mechanism:**
- "Lunessa has ingredients X, Y, Z" (that's a product spec, not a mechanism)
- "It gives you energy" (that's a benefit claim, not a mechanism)

**IS a mechanism:**
- "The chronic fatigue cycle is driven by three simultaneous breakdowns: depleted energy substrate, circadian dysregulation, and dopamine depletion. A multi-phase reset that addresses all three simultaneously (Phase 1: substrate replenishment, Phase 2: circadian re-anchoring, Phase 3: motivation neurochemistry repair) is required for lasting change. Lunessa specifically targets the substrate + neurochemistry components, which, when combined with sleep rhythm optimization, creates recovery."

**Examples of strong mechanisms:**
- **Process mechanism**: "The fatigue spiral has three phases: [Phase 1 description], [Phase 2 description], [Phase 3 description]. Breaking the spiral requires interrupting the cycle at [specific phase], which is what [mechanism/product] does."
- **Ingredient mechanism**: "The problem is [specific biochemical dysfunction]. The solution requires [specific compound or compound combination] because [biochemical explanation]. [Product] delivers [compounds] in [specific format/dosage/timing] which produces [specific result]."
- **Permission mechanism**: "You've been taught [false belief] and that's kept you trapped. The truth is [corrected belief], and once you understand that, [desired outcome] becomes possible."
- **Relationship mechanism**: "The missing link isn't effort or willpower. It's [specific relationship dynamic, knowledge, or external support]. Once you have [mechanism], transformation is inevitable."
- **Positioning mechanism**: "[Product category] has been positioned as [common positioning], but the actual differentiation is [true positioning]. When you understand [true positioning], the right choice is obvious."

**How to develop mechanism:**
1. **Start with the problem**: What's actually causing the avatar's pain?
2. **Identify the broken system**: Is it metabolic? Psychological? Informational? Relational?
3. **Design the fix**: What needs to happen to restore that system?
4. **Name the mechanism**: Can you describe it in 2-3 sentences?

**For your brief, write mechanism as a specific 3-5 sentence explanation:**
- What is actually broken?
- What is the broken system / cycle?
- What specifically fixes it?
- What is the avatar's role in the fix?
- How does the product fit into the fix?

**Example mechanism brief:**
```
Mechanism: Chronic fatigue in high-performing women is driven by three simultaneous metabolic dysfunctions: (1) energy substrate depletion from sustained stress + inadequate nutritional support, (2) circadian rhythm desynchronization from erratic schedules, and (3) dopamine depletion leading to motivation collapse. A complete recovery requires addressing all three simultaneously. The substrate + neurochemistry components can be reset through [mechanism], but this only works if paired with circadian re-anchoring (consistent sleep schedule). Lunessa targets the substrate + dopamine components of this equation, providing the nutritional and neurochemical reset that makes recovery possible when paired with sleep optimization.
```

### 5. Avatar Awareness Level & Sophistication Stage

**How aware is the avatar?**

This determines the entire tone, proof strategy, and mechanism reveal timing of your copy.

**Awareness Level** ([[DR-SOP-Obsidian.md]] — Schwartz stages, adapted):

- **Unaware**: Avatar doesn't know they have a problem. They're functioning, but suboptimally. They don't see their fatigue as a "problem" yet; it's just normal.
  - Copy strategy: First establish that a problem exists. Lead with problem recognition, not solution.
  - Example: "You think this is just what life with kids looks like. But most women aren't actually this tired."

- **Problem-Aware**: Avatar knows they have fatigue. They experience it, resent it, but don't know the cause or solution categories.
  - Copy strategy: Problem + villains + mechanism options.
  - Example: "Everyone tells you to just sleep more or exercise more. But that's not actually fixing the root cause."

- **Solution-Aware**: Avatar knows solutions exist (sleep optimization, supplements, etc.) but doesn't know which is right or effective.
  - Copy strategy: Compare your solution to others. Position as better/different.
  - Example: "You've tried everything: more coffee, better sleep, workouts, multivitamins. But you're missing the one thing that actually fixes it..."

- **Product-Aware**: Avatar knows your product exists but is skeptical, comparing to alternatives, or thinks it's not for them.
  - Copy strategy: Overcome objections. Provide proof. Show uniqueness.
  - Example: "You've heard of Lunessa but think it's just another energy supplement. It's not. Here's why..."

- **Most Aware**: Avatar is a past customer or deeply familiar with the product. They're either a repeat customer or they're deciding between your product and a specific competitor.
  - Copy strategy: Retention, upgrade, or detailed competitive positioning.
  - Example: "You've tried Lunessa before. Here's why one more round will change everything."

**Sophistication Stage** ([[DR-SOP-Obsidian.md]] — Schwartz stages):

- **Stage 1**: Unaware the product exists. Doesn't know change is possible.
- **Stage 2**: Aware a solution exists but highly skeptical, resistant, or thinks it's not for them.
- **Stage 3**: Aware and exploring. Open to trying. Needs some convincing but movable.
- **Stage 4**: Ready to buy. Just needs the right offer and push.
- **Stage 5**: Already bought or completely committed. Repeat customer or evangelist.

**Your brief should specify both:**
```
Awareness Level: Problem-Aware (knows fatigue is a problem, not aware that metabolic reset is the solution)
Sophistication Stage: 2 (aware energy products exist, highly skeptical they work, thinks they've tried everything)
```

### 6. Villain Architecture

**What belief needs to die?**

This section identifies the specific false belief or mindset you're going to demolish in your copy.

**Three villain categories:**

**External Villain** (Industry, system, product category, market dynamics, competitor)
- The diet industry has lied to you about energy
- The supplement industry pushes stimulants instead of real solutions
- Your employer's culture demands you push through fatigue
- Modern food supply doesn't contain the nutrients your body needs

**Internal Villain** (Self-blame, shame, identity, character-based guilt)
- "I'm just not disciplined enough"
- "I'm lazy"
- "I'm broken"
- "I'm not cut out for this level of responsibility"
- "My fatigue is my fault"

**Philosophical Villain** (Fatalism, permanence, inevitability, "just how things are")
- "This is just what getting older feels like"
- "Chronic fatigue is permanent"
- "Nobody actually has sustained energy; they're just faking it"
- "My genetics means I'll always be tired"

**For your brief, specify:**
1. **Primary villain type**: External | Internal | Philosophical
2. **Specific villain description**: "The belief that chronic fatigue is permanent and unfixable"
3. **How the brief demolishes it**: "By presenting mechanism evidence that fatigue is metabolic (not permanent) and showing specific recovery examples"

**Example villain architecture for brief:**
```
Primary Villain: Internal (self-blame for low energy; belief that "I'm just lazy and not disciplined enough")
Specific Belief: "My fatigue is a character flaw. If I were smarter/stronger/more disciplined, I wouldn't be tired."
How We Demolish It:
  - Establish that fatigue has specific metabolic causes (substrate depletion, circadian dysregulation, dopamine deficiency)
  - These are not character flaws; they're system failures
  - System failures have system solutions (not willpower solutions)
  - Proof: Women with identical "discipline" levels have different fatigue based on metabolic status
```

### 7. Product Integration & Close

**How explicitly does the product show up? What's the ask?**

**Product Integration Positioning** (See [[DR-SOP-Obsidian.md]] — Product Integration Spectrum):

- **Invisible** (0% mention until CTA): Problem + mechanism + belief shift is the entire focus. Product mentioned only in final CTA or link.
  - Use when: Villain is primary focus; product is secondary benefit
  - Example: Advertorial about "The Fatigue Industry Scam" that ends with "Learn more about metabolic reset solutions at [URL]"

- **Peripheral** (15-25% explicit): Product mentioned 1-2 times mid-way. Clearly one component of larger solution.
  - Use when: Mechanism involves multiple components; product is one piece
  - Example: "Sleep optimization + nutrients + circadian re-anchoring = recovery. Lunessa handles the nutrient component."

- **Supporting** (35-50% explicit): Product introduced halfway through, integrated into mechanism explanation.
  - Use when: Product IS the mechanism or a central piece of it
  - Example: "The specific combination of [compounds in Lunessa] addresses the substrate depletion + dopamine components of the fatigue equation."

- **Central** (60%+ explicit): Product is the primary focus. Entire copy is about the product and why it's uniquely effective.
  - Use when: Product differentiation is the core insight
  - Example: "Lunessa is designed specifically for metabolic reset, which is why it works differently than every other energy product."

**Close Type** (What's the call-to-action?):

- **Soft ask / Curiosity CTA**: "Learn more" | "Discover" | "See what this is about" | "Click to understand"
  - Use when: Avatar is early-stage aware or resistant. You're trying to get them to your sales page, not commit yet.
  - Example: "Click here to understand the real cause of your fatigue"

- **Trial CTA**: "Try it" | "Test it for yourself" | "See if this works for you"
  - Use when: Avatar is ready to try but wants permission to test
  - Example: "Try the 30-day metabolic reset and see if it changes your energy"

- **Soft commitment CTA**: "Get started" | "Take the first step" | "Begin your reset"
  - Use when: Avatar is fairly convinced and just needs direction
  - Example: "Get Lunessa and start your metabolic reset today"

- **Hard ask CTA**: "Buy now" | "Order your supply" | "Lock in this price"
  - Use when: Avatar is highly convinced or you're running a limited-time offer
  - Example: "Order Lunessa now — this pricing expires tonight"

**For your brief, specify:**
```
Product Integration Position: Peripheral (mentioned 2-3 times, clearly one part of multi-component solution)
Close Type: Soft ask (avatar is problem-aware but needs to understand mechanism first; we're directing them to sales page for full education)
Specific CTA: "Click below to discover the three-phase metabolic reset protocol that's changing women's energy (it's not what you think)"
```

### 8. Funnel Context

**Where does this piece sit?**

If writing for a funnel, specify the funnel position and the handoff requirement.

**Funnel positions:**
- **Lead ad**: First touchpoint. Must break through the noise, establish problem relevance, create curiosity.
- **Bridge / PSA**: Second piece. Assumes problem awareness. Introduces mechanism. Builds trust. Sets up offer.
- **Product page**: Final piece. Assumes mechanism awareness. Focused on differentiation and conversion.
- **Email sequence**: Ongoing. Builds relationship and social proof.
- **Viral content**: Standalone, shared for value. May not be part of formal funnel.

**Handoff specification:**
- What belief/state should reader be in after THIS piece? (Exit-State)
- What belief/state does the NEXT piece require them to be in? (Entry-State)
- **Must these match?** Yes. If not, there's a funnel fracture.

**Example for brief:**
```
Funnel Context: Lead ad in 3-piece funnel
Exit-State (what belief should reader have after this piece?): "My fatigue might actually be fixable. It's not a character flaw; it's metabolic."
Next Piece Entry-State (what does bridge ad require?): Avatar must believe fatigue is fixable and caused by metabolism (not willpower)
Handoff Status: MATCH — No fracture. Bridge can assume this foundation and move to mechanism details.
```

## Brief Approval Checklist

Before you file a brief and start writing, complete this checklist:

- [ ] **Desire is specific**: Not "more energy" but "sustained energy throughout my workday without 3pm crashes"
- [ ] **Angle is unsaturated**: Check [[/agents/market-analyst/intel-drops/]] and vault inventory. Is this angle explored before?
- [ ] **Angle is distinct**: Anti-mimicry audit passed. This angle's villain/mechanism/narrative is different from recent work.
- [ ] **Concept is fully designed**: Narrator, posture, temperature, product integration all specified
- [ ] **Mechanism is clear**: Could a copywriter read this and understand exactly how the solution works? (Not just "what it does," but "why it works")
- [ ] **Avatar sophistication is accurate**: Awareness level and Schwartz stage are realistic for the target segment
- [ ] **Villain is specific**: Not "people are skeptical" but "women blame themselves for fatigue and believe it's a character flaw"
- [ ] **Product integration makes sense**: Invisible/peripheral/supporting/central choice is justified by the mechanism and angle
- [ ] **Close aligns with awareness**: Hard asks only for highly aware avatars; soft asks for early-stage
- [ ] **Funnel handoff works**: If multi-piece, does exit-state match next entry-state?

If any checkbox is incomplete or uncertain, revise the brief before proceeding to writing.

## The Filed Brief

Once approved, save your brief with this format:

**File naming:** `YYYY-MM-DD_[PRODUCT]_[ANGLE]_[FORMAT]_brief.md`

Example: `2026-03-22_Lunessa_MetabolismAngle_LongForm_brief.md`

**Frontmatter:**
```yaml
---
product: "Lunessa"
angle: "Metabolism Shutdown Angle"
format: "long-form"
awareness_level: "Problem-Aware"
sophistication_stage: "2"
target_avatar: "High-performing women, 28-45, office jobs, experience chronic fatigue, blame themselves"
author: "Creative Strategist"
date_created: "2026-03-22"
status: "approved"
---
```

**Body sections:**
1. Desire Statement
2. Angle & Insight
3. Concept (Narrator, Posture, Temperature, Product Integration)
4. Mechanism
5. Avatar Sophistication
6. Villain Architecture
7. Product Integration & Close
8. Funnel Context (if applicable)

## Common Brief Mistakes & Fixes

### Mistake 1: Mechanism is too vague
**Bad:** "Lunessa helps with energy by providing the nutrients your body needs"
**Good:** "Chronic fatigue is caused by three simultaneous metabolic failures: substrate depletion (body has run out of the compounds it needs to generate ATP), circadian dysregulation (sleep-wake cycle is misaligned), and dopamine system depletion (motivation neurochemistry is broken). A complete reset requires addressing all three. Lunessa targets the substrate + dopamine components via [specific compound list]. This works only when paired with circadian re-anchoring (consistent sleep times)."

### Mistake 2: Angle is generic
**Bad:** "The energy angle" or "The solution angle"
**Good:** "The Metabolic Reset Angle: Targets the belief that fatigue is a permanent condition or character flaw. Reframes as a specific, fixable metabolic dysfunction with a clear recovery protocol."

### Mistake 3: Villain is not specific enough
**Bad:** "People don't believe fatigue is fixable"
**Good:** "Women internalize the blame for their fatigue. The cultural narrative is 'if you're tired, you're not trying hard enough / don't love your kids enough / are lazy.' This internal villain keeps women from seeking solutions because they believe the problem is them, not their metabolism."

### Mistake 4: Product integration doesn't match the mechanism
**Bad:**
- Mechanism: "Three simultaneous factors cause fatigue; solution requires addressing all three"
- Product integration: "Central — entire copy is about Lunessa"
- Problem: If mechanism is multi-part and Lunessa is only one part, it should be "Supporting" not "Central"

**Good:**
- Mechanism: "Fatigue is caused by [specific mechanism]"
- Product integration: "Peripheral — one component of full reset, but the critical component"

### Mistake 5: Close doesn't match awareness level
**Bad:**
- Awareness level: "Problem-Aware (barely aware fatigue is solvable)"
- Close: "Hard ask — Buy now"
- Problem: Avatar isn't ready for a hard ask. They're still skeptical.

**Good:**
- Awareness level: "Problem-Aware"
- Close: "Soft ask — Discover the mechanism"

## Brief to Creative Workflow

Once brief is approved:

1. **File the brief** in `/creative-briefs/` folder
2. **Load relevant skill file** (long-form, video, advertorial, etc.)
3. **Generate hook first** using the brief's angle + villain + mechanism insights
4. **Draft full piece** following the skill file structure
5. **Reference brief throughout** to stay on track
6. **Self-assess** and file with reference to brief in frontmatter

The brief is your north star during the entire production phase. If you get lost during writing, re-read the brief.
