## Cross-ad pattern observations

### 1. Both ads use the "medical establishment failed me/my family" frame
- Cleantra: "Doctors just prescribe milk thistle without testing drainage"
- Berbino: "Doctors keep prescribing MiraLAX without testing for overgrowth"

This is a strong angle in the gut/liver niche in 2026. Users are increasingly skeptical of pharma-default treatment protocols. Both ads position the natural compound stack as "what the research actually shows" vs "what doctors default to."

### 2. Both use specific, verifiable scientific authority
- Cleantra cites ALT/AST values and European hepatology tradition
- Berbino cites Cedars-Sinai, Dr. Pimentel, and the 2014 Chedid study

**Takeaway:** The gut/liver niche in mid-2026 has evolved past vague "detox" claims. Readers want named researchers, named institutions, named studies. The bar for proof has risen.

### 3. Both pick unusual avatars within their niche
- Cleantra: drinking men 40s (counter-positioned against the feminine wellness default in liver copy)
- Berbino: MiraLAX-dependent women (a specific, self-identifying sub-segment within the constipation market)

**Takeaway:** Picking a tight avatar and pressure-testing the hook against that avatar's exact self-identification keeps resonance high even at long word counts.

### 4. Mechanism-first, product-last architecture
- Cleantra reveals product at ~65% mark
- Berbino reveals product at ~70% mark (likely — truncated before full reveal)

Both are Tier 3-4 integration. The mechanism and avatar are carrying the weight of the first two-thirds of the ad. Product is presented as the answer to a puzzle the reader has been led through.

## Top insight (single most actionable takeaway for the brain)

**The "named organism" mechanism is now the gold standard in gut health copy.**

Berbino doesn't say "bad bacteria" or even "SIBO" — it says *Methanobrevibacter smithii*. Cleantra doesn't say "toxins" — it says *hepatic lymphatic drainage*. When the mechanism is so specific it can be Googled, verified, and cross-referenced, the reader's skepticism drops because the claim is falsifiable. Any brand still running vague "gut bacteria imbalance" copy in mid-2026 is leaving conversion on the table.

The next ads we write in this niche should name organisms, name compounds, name studies. Specificity is the new proof.
