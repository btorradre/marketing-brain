# Desire → Angle → Concept Matrix — Complete System

This skill generates the strategic creative architecture that sits between research and copywriting. It takes consumer intelligence (pain themes, language, awareness levels) and produces a fully differentiated matrix of desire angles, sharp angles, and specific concepts — the raw material that every copy skill draws from.

The matrix answers three questions in sequence:
1. **What does the avatar FEEL?** (Desire Angle)
2. **What specific incision point gets their attention?** (Angle)
3. **Who tells the story and how?** (Concept)

Read this entire file before generating. The definitions, quality rules, and anti-mimicry checks are non-negotiable.

---

## 0. WHEN TO USE THIS SKILL

This skill covers any task where the goal is to generate strategic creative options before writing copy:

- **Full matrix build** — Generating a complete desire-angle-concept matrix for a product or avatar. This is the default.
- **Angle expansion** — Taking an existing desire angle and generating new angles and concepts beneath it. Use when current ads are fatiguing and you need fresh cuts on the same pain.
- **Concept variation** — Taking a proven angle and generating new concept vehicles to deliver it. Use when an angle is winning but the creative format is stale.
- **Competitive gap mapping** — Analyzing what angles and concepts competitors are using, then generating the ones they're NOT. Use alongside competitive brand analysis from avatar-research.
- **Sub-avatar matrix** — Building a targeted matrix for a sub-segment of an existing avatar. Narrower desire set, sharper angles.

## 1. CRITICAL DEFINITIONS

These definitions are the foundation. Every failure in creative strategy traces back to confusing these three levels. They are NOT interchangeable.

### DESIRE ANGLE

The raw, felt problem the avatar is experiencing. This is the entry door — the symptom, frustration, or condition that makes them stop scrolling. Desire angles are broad categories of suffering within the product's problem space.

A desire angle names WHAT HURTS. It's the category of pain, not a specific cut.

Examples for a GLP-1 digestive support product: constipation, sulfur burps, nausea, bloating, food fear.

### ANGLE

The specific emotional, logical, or visual incision point within a desire. An angle is NOT a category — it is a sharp, specific cut. It names a fear, a scene, a moment, or a mechanism the reader has never seen articulated in an ad before. Multiple angles branch off each desire angle.

**The test for a good angle: Can you SEE it? Can you FEEL it? Does it name something the reader has experienced but never seen written down?** If not, it's a category pretending to be an angle.

Good angles name scenes, moments, relationships, or mechanisms:
- "My husband started sleeping in the guest room" — relationship erosion caused by sulfur shame
- "Bathroom hostage" — the fear of being trapped in the bathroom for 45 minutes and missing your own life
- "Shot day dread" — the specific anticipatory anxiety that builds every 7 days before injection

Bad angles name categories or topics:
- "Emotional transformation" — no edge, cuts nothing
- "Health benefits of digestive support" — a topic, not an angle
- "Feeling better" — a desired outcome, not an incision point

**The sharpness test:** After reading the angle name and one-sentence description, a copywriter should be able to write the first 3 lines of the ad without needing more information. If the angle requires further clarification to become a hook, it's too vague. Sharpen it or kill it.

### CONCEPT

The delivery vehicle that carries the angle to the reader. The concept is the frame, the narrator, the format — the WHO and HOW of the storytelling. The same angle can ride inside completely different concepts and become completely different ads.

**The test for a good concept: Could two different copywriters take this concept description and write the same TYPE of piece, even if the words were completely different?** If the concept is too vague to constrain the format, it's not a concept.

Good concepts define a specific narrator, format, and relationship:
- "A gastroenterologist's off-the-record explanation to a patient" — specific narrator, specific relationship, specific framing
- "A husband's letter to his wife that he never sent" — specific format, specific emotional container
- "An investigative thread by a health journalist who took GLP-1s herself" — specific narrator with built-in credibility and personal stakes

Bad concepts name tones or modes:
- "Educational content" — a tone, not a concept
- "Emotional story" — a mode, not a concept
- "Informative article" — a format category, not a concept

### THE HIERARCHY

```
DESIRE ANGLE (what hurts)
  └── ANGLE (the specific cut — scene, moment, fear, mechanism)
        └── CONCEPT (who tells it, how, and what's their relationship to reader + product)
```

Every level must be sharp enough to constrain the level below it. A vague desire angle produces vague angles. A vague angle produces vague concepts. Sharpen from the top.

## 2. INPUT REQUIREMENTS

The matrix requires specific information about the product and avatar. If avatar-research output exists, pull from it. If not, gather these inputs before generating:

**Product:**
- Product name
- What it does mechanistically (one sentence — not marketing language)
- The belief shift the copy must create (what must they believe by the end?)

**Avatar:**
- Primary avatar description (age, gender, situation, awareness level)
- Core problem space (the broad category of problems)
- Key emotional states (3-5 dominant emotions — e.g., shame, fear, exhaustion)
- Failed solutions they've tried (what has this person already tried that didn't work?)

**From Avatar Research (if available):**
- Pain themes from Section 1 (these become candidate desire angles)
- Consumer language from Section 2 (this grounds the "what this feels like" voice)
- Peak pain situations from Section 3 (these become candidate angles)
- Awareness level from Section 5 (this determines how sophisticated the angles need to be)
- Dominant community narrative from Section 6 (the angles must work with or against this)

When avatar research exists, the desire angles should map directly to the pain themes. The matrix doesn't invent new pain — it sharpens and structures the pain the research already surfaced.

## 3. GENERATION RULES

### Desire Angle Rules

Generate a MINIMUM of 6 desire angles. Go beyond the obvious symptoms. The desire set must include:

1. **Symptom-level desires** (2-3) — The physical experiences: pain, discomfort, bodily dysfunction. These are the most obvious and the most competitive.

2. **Emotional/social consequence desires** (1-2) — What the symptoms DO to their life: relationship strain, social withdrawal, identity erosion. These are less competitive and often more powerful.

3. **Meta-frustration desires** (1) — Frustration with the search itself: exhaustion from failed solutions, anger at conflicting advice, distrust of the entire product category.

4. **Identity-level desires** (1-2) — Who they're BECOMING because of this problem: "I used to be someone who..." These are the deepest and hardest to write but produce the strongest response.

5. **Decision-point desires** (1) — The specific crossroads: considering quitting treatment, restarting, changing doses, giving up entirely.

### Angle Rules

Generate 3-5 angles per desire angle. Each angle must:

- **Name a specific scene, moment, fear, or mechanism** — not a category.
- **Use a fundamentally different emotional mechanism than every other angle under the same desire.** If Angle 1 uses shame, Angle 2 cannot use shame — even in a different scene. Force variety: fear, loss, mechanism ignorance, identity threat, social consequence, medical gaslighting, future projection, relationship erosion, etc.
- **Be specific enough to write a hook from.** The sharpness test applies to every angle.
- **Connect to real consumer language** from the avatar research when available. The best angles use phrases the avatar already uses — they just sharpen them into an incision point.

### Concept Rules

Generate 3 concepts per angle. Each concept must:

- **Define the RELATIONSHIP between narrator and reader.** Not "a doctor explains" but "a doctor who was told by her hospital not to recommend supplements admits what she actually tells her own patients." The relationship creates the tension that carries the copy.

- **Vary across all dimensions:**
  - Narrator identity (spouse, journalist, pharmacist, researcher, the avatar themselves at different life stages, support group moderator, skeptic who changed mind, etc.)
  - Format (story, letter, confession, exposé, clinical explanation, overheard conversation, social media post, journal entry, investigative thread, etc.)
  - Emotional temperature (calm authority, raw vulnerability, contained anger, reluctant honesty, enthusiastic discovery, quiet resignation, etc.)
  - Narrator's relationship to the product (discovered it, was skeptical, recommended it to someone else, was the person it was recommended to, studied it professionally, etc.)

- **No narrator type can appear more than once across the ENTIRE matrix.** If "gastroenterologist" appears anywhere as a narrator, no other concept in the entire matrix can use a gastroenterologist. This forces genuine creative variety.

### Mechanism Alignment Rule

Every angle's implied mechanism must align with what the avatar already believes about their body or situation. If the angle implies a mechanism that contradicts their existing beliefs, it will trigger defensive rejection instead of a belief shift. When avatar research includes a dominant community narrative, check each angle against it.

## 4. OUTPUT FORMAT

### Per Desire Angle:

```
DESIRE ANGLE: [Name]
What this feels like to the avatar: [One sentence in their voice — what they'd type 
into a Reddit post at 2am. Must sound raw, not crafted.]

ANGLE 1: [Vivid Name — 2-5 words that instantly evoke the scene or feeling]
  The cut: [One sentence — what specific pain, fear, scene, or realization 
  does this angle exploit?]
  Why it stops the scroll: [One sentence — why has this person never seen 
  this articulated before?]
  Emotional mechanism: [One word — shame, fear, loss, identity threat, etc.]
  
  Concept A: [Specific narrator + format + framing + relationship to reader 
  and product]
  Concept B: [Completely different from A across all dimensions]
  Concept C: [Completely different from A and B across all dimensions]

ANGLE 2: [Same structure]
ANGLE 3: [Same structure]
```

### After the Full Matrix — Anti-Mimicry Verification Table:

| Dimension | Unique Count | Duplicates? |
|---|---|---|
| Narrator types used | X | Yes/No |
| Emotional mechanisms per desire | X | Yes/No |
| Format types (story, letter, exposé, etc.) | X | Yes/No |
| Narrator-product relationships | X | Yes/No |
| Emotional temperatures | X | Yes/No |

If any duplicates exist, revise until the matrix is fully differentiated. Do not deliver a matrix with duplicates — the entire point is forced creative variety.

## 5. QUALITY GATES

Before delivering the matrix, run these checks:

1. **Angle Sharpness Test:** Read each angle name and one-sentence cut. Can you write the first 3 lines of the ad from just that? If any angle needs further clarification to become a hook, it's too vague. Sharpen or replace.

2. **Concept Specificity Test:** Read each concept description. Could two different copywriters take it and write the same TYPE of piece? If the concept is too vague to constrain the format, it's not a concept. Add specificity or replace.

3. **Emotional Mechanism Diversity:** No two angles under the same desire can share an emotional mechanism. Check each desire's angle set.

4. **Narrator Uniqueness:** No narrator type appears more than once across the entire matrix. Check the full narrator inventory.

5. **Consumer Voice Authenticity:** Every "what this feels like" sentence must sound like a real person typed it — not a copywriter. If it sounds polished, rewrite it raw.

6. **Desire Angle Coverage:** Verify the desire set includes symptom-level, emotional/social, meta-frustration, identity-level, and decision-point desires. If any category is missing, add it.

7. **Mechanism Alignment:** Every angle's implied mechanism aligns with the avatar's existing beliefs. Flag any angle that would trigger defensive rejection.

## 6. SCOPE VARIATIONS

**Full Matrix (default):** 6+ desire angles, 3-5 angles each, 3 concepts each. This is the complete creative architecture for a product launch or major creative refresh.

**Angle Expansion:** Start with 1-2 existing desire angles and generate 5-8 new angles each with 3 concepts. Use when current ads are fatiguing on a winning desire.

**Concept Variation:** Start with 1-3 proven angles and generate 5-7 new concepts for each. Use when an angle is winning but creative formats are stale.

**Quick Matrix:** 3-4 desire angles, 2-3 angles each, 2 concepts each. Use for rapid ideation or when testing a new sub-avatar before committing to full build.

**Competitive Gap Matrix:** Analyze competitor angles first (from their ads, landing pages, and community reactions), then generate only the angles and concepts they're NOT using. The output includes a "competitor coverage map" showing which desires and angles are saturated vs. open.

When the user's request implies a scope variation, confirm which variation before generating.

## 7. PIPELINE INTEGRATION

This skill sits between avatar-research and the copy skills:

```
avatar-research → desire-angle-concept → hook-generation → long-form-copy / 
                                                            advertorial / 
                                                            video-ad-scripts / 
                                                            listicle-builder
```

**Input from avatar-research:**
- Pain themes → become desire angle candidates
- Consumer language → grounds the avatar voice in each desire
- Peak pain situations → become angle candidates
- Awareness level → determines angle sophistication
- Community narrative → mechanism alignment check

**Output to copy skills:**
- The selected angle + concept pairing feeds directly into hook-generation as the starting constraint
- The "what this feels like" voice carries into long-form-copy as the narrator's emotional register
- The concept's narrator identity and format become the structural skeleton for the copy skill to build on
- The belief shift stays constant across all angles and concepts — only the entry point and vehicle change

When handing off to a copy skill, specify: the desire angle, the selected angle, the selected concept, and the belief shift. That's everything the copy skill needs to begin.
