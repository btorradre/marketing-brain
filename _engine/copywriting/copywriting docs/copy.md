## The Pattern Across Both

The consistent issue is  **mid-piece bloat between mechanism education and solution search.**  In the Motilli ad, the transition from "I understood why nothing worked" to "I started searching" is immediate. In both CoraVita pieces, the narrator pauses to reflect on family members, the broader system, or the emotional weight of the discovery. These reflections are well-written but they kill forward momentum at the exact point where the reader should feel urgency to find the answer.

The rule from the Motilli piece: once the mechanism is understood, the narrator acts. Reflection belongs in the front-load or the close, not in the middle.

Second pattern: the Motilli ad's failed-solution dismissals are specific and metaphorical. Both CoraVita pieces use generic category descriptions. Naming real products (or at least specific product types with vivid one-line metaphors for why they fail) would sharpen these sections considerably.
