My Doctor Keeps Pushing Statins. I Keep Refusing. A Cardiologist Finally Explained Why I Might Be Right—And What To Try Instead.

The three-pathway mechanism most doctors never learned

Heart Health Insider | January 2026 | 5 min read

Every six months, the same conversation.

My doctor pulls up my lipid panel. Sighs. Looks at me over her glasses.

“Your LDL is 234. We really need to talk about statins.”

And every six months, I say the same thing.

“I’ve seen what they do to people. I’m not ready.”

She writes something in my chart. Probably “non-compliant.” Probably “refused treatment again.”

I’m not stupid. I know my numbers are dangerous. I know my father died of a heart attack at 57. I know I’m playing with fire.

But I’ve also watched my mother shuffle around her house like a zombie for the past eight years. Brain fog so thick she forgets my kids’ names. Muscle pain so bad she can’t climb stairs. She’s 67 and moves like she’s 90.

“It’s the Lipitor,” she tells me. “But my doctor says the side effects are worth it.”

Worth it to whom?

I refuse to believe my only options are:

Feel like my mother feels for the next thirty years.

Or die like my father died.

There has to be a third path.

The Conversation That Changed Everything

I was at a dinner party last month. Ended up sitting next to a woman who turned out to be a cardiologist.

I don’t know why I told her. Maybe the wine. Maybe I was tired of carrying it alone.

“My doctor wants me on statins,” I said. “My LDL is 234. But I’ve watched what they do to people. I can’t make myself fill the prescription.”

I expected the lecture. The statistics about heart attacks. The “benefits outweigh the risks” speech I’d heard a dozen times.

Instead, she set down her fork.

“What if I told you your hesitation isn’t entirely wrong?”

I stared at her.

“Statins work,” she said. “For some people, they’re the right choice. But they only solve one part of a three-part problem. And for a lot of patients, the trade-off isn’t worth it—especially when there’s another option most doctors don’t know about.”

The Three-Pathway Problem

She explained it like this:

High cholesterol isn’t just about your liver making too much LDL. It’s about three things going wrong at the same time.

1

Production

Your liver overproduces cholesterol. Statins address this.

2

Clearance

Your body can’t pull LDL out of your bloodstream efficiently. So it just floats there, accumulating. Statins don’t address this.

3

Oxidation

The LDL stuck in your blood starts to oxidize—like metal rusting. Oxidized LDL is what actually sticks to artery walls and forms plaque. That’s what causes heart attacks. Statins don’t address this either.

“So statins fix one thing out of three?” I asked.

“Exactly. They block production. That’s it. The other two problems keep getting worse—just slower.”

“Then why does everyone act like statins are the only answer?”

She shrugged. “Because that’s what we were taught in medical school. Block production, lower the number, problem solved. The nuance got lost.”

Why Statins Make People Feel Terrible

“But here’s the part that matters to you,” she said. “The pathway statins block? It doesn’t just make cholesterol. It also makes CoQ10—the molecule every cell in your body needs for energy.”

I thought about my mother. The exhaustion. The brain fog. The way she describes feeling “empty” all the time.

“So when you take a statin, you’re not just blocking cholesterol production. You’re blocking your cells’ ability to make energy. That’s not a side effect. That’s the mechanism.”

“The muscle pain?”

“CoQ10 depletion.”

“The brain fog?”

“CoQ10 depletion.”

“The fatigue?”

“CoQ10 depletion.”

I felt sick. My mother had been complaining about these things for years. Her doctor kept telling her it was “just aging.”

“Your mother isn’t weak,” the cardiologist said, like she was reading my mind. “Her cells are starving.”

“So what do I do?” I asked. “My LDL is 234. I can’t just ignore it.”

“You don’t have to ignore it. You have to address all three pathways—not just production.”

She told me about a formulation she’d been recommending to patients who couldn’t tolerate statins. Or patients like me, who refused to start them in the first place.

“Three pathways,” she said. “Production. Clearance. Oxidation. All addressed. Without draining your CoQ10 in the process.”

Why I’d Never Heard of This

“I’ve looked into red yeast rice before,” I told her. “Everything I read said it was basically a weak statin. Same mechanism, same problems.”
