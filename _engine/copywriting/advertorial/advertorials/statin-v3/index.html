The Lie Your Lab Work Tells You

Here’s what your doctor doesn’t explain when she says your LDL is “controlled”:

Statins lower the number. That’s what they’re designed to do. They block your liver from producing new cholesterol.

But lowering production isn’t the same as stopping plaque formation.

Dr. Chen puts it bluntly: “I’ve seen hundreds of patients with ‘perfect’ cholesterol numbers who had significant plaque buildup. Their LDL was managed. Their arteries were still clogging. They felt nothing until something catastrophic happened.”

Why?

Because high cholesterol isn’t one problem. It’s three.

1

Production

Your liver makes too much cholesterol. Statins address this.

2

Clearance

Your body can’t pull LDL out of your bloodstream efficiently. It just floats there, accumulating. Statins don’t address this.

3

Oxidation

The LDL stuck in your blood starts to oxidize—like rust forming on metal. Oxidized LDL is what actually sticks to artery walls and forms plaque. That’s what causes heart attacks. Statins don’t address this either.

One pathway out of three.

Your statin lowers your LDL number. But the LDL already in your bloodstream? It’s still there. Still oxidizing. Still forming plaque.

Silently. For years. Sometimes decades.

Until it isn’t silent anymore.
