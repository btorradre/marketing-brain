What No One Tells You About Sciatica

[Image: Anatomical diagram showing L4-L5 disc herniation and nerve compression]

When a disc herniates at L4-L5, your body does something intelligent. It clamps the muscles around the injury. Protective spasm. Ancient survival mechanism. Your spine is trying to stabilize itself.

But those muscles squeeze so hard they crush their own blood vessels. No blood flow means no oxygen. No oxygen means the muscle physically cannot release.

It stays locked. Around your nerve. Like a fist that forgot how to open.

That locked muscle is the source of your sciatica. The shooting pain down your leg. The electric shocks. The numbness. The burning.

All of it comes from a muscle strangling your nerve because it doesn't have the one compound it needs to release.

That compound is magnesium.

Which means the muscle clamped around your nerve has been starving for the one compound it needs to release. For months. Sometimes years.

That's not a disease. That's a deficiency. And not a single treatment on the standard medical path addresses it.
