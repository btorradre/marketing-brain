## Advertorial Frameworks: The 6 Core Approaches

These are the six frameworks the SKILL actually uses. They're orientations (a direction the piece leans), not rigid boxes — they can blend, with one center of gravity. The framework is selected off the upstream ad's narrator + the Camacho product-category axis (see the SKILL's pre-flight).

### 1. Authority Expert
**A credentialed figure (doctor, researcher, PT, pharmacist) independently confirms the mechanism and adds a credibility category the peer-voiced ad couldn't.**
- Best for: innovation/gadget products; emotional or peer-narrator upstream ads
- Hook: credential + tension + inciting incident in the first 50 words
- The authority REVEALS, never lectures

### 2. Crisis Narrative
**One person's dramatic story (crisis → discovery → transformation) drives the whole piece.**
- Best for: improvement products; failed-solutions graveyard angles
- Hook: a dramatic opening moment, mechanism learned through the story
- The reader witnesses a breakthrough rather than being sold

### 3. System Exposé / Whistleblower
**A former insider or system survivor reveals what the industry/standard-of-care hides — a different path that arrives at the ad's same conclusion.**
- Best for: audiences who distrust authority; analytical narrators
- Hook: revelation + personal cost; lived experience + insider access

### 4. Expert Comparison / Review
**An editorial voice evaluates multiple options against criteria and reveals a winner.**
- Best for: audiences overwhelmed by options ("which one?" not "should I?")
- Hook: objective-seeming evaluation; the product emerges as the criteria-meeting winner

### 5. Listicle / Discovery
**Numbered revelations that build the case until the product is the logical conclusion.**
- Best for: research-mode audiences who want to feel informed, not sold
- Hook: promised utility; each item independently valuable
- Note: overlaps the standalone Listicle skill — use that for a 1-step hard-close listicle

### 6. Suppression / Conspiracy
**The product/science has been buried or attacked by the establishment; it exists despite resistance.**
- Best for: audiences who already distrust the system
- Hook: hidden truth + an economic motive (why the system suppresses it)
- Urgency tied to narrative conflict ("forced to remove by midnight")
