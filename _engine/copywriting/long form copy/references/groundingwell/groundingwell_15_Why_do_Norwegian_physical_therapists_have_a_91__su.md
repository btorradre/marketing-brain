Why do Norwegian physical therapists have a 91% success rate with neck pain? 🇳🇴

It's not because they have better doctors.

And it's not because people in Norway somehow have healthier necks.

It's because they treat the neck in a way most clinics never do.

But before I explain what they're doing differently I need to tell you what happened to me.

Because if your neck feels like concrete every morning, your shoulders are always tight, and by mid afternoon the ache starts crawling up into your skull…

you'll probably recognize this story immediately.

It didn't start with some dramatic injury.

No car accident. No fall. No moment where I thought oh no I've hurt my neck.

It started the way a lot of these things do. Quietly enough that I almost ignored it.

I remember one Tuesday morning in late October.

It was still dark outside. I reached over to turn off my alarm and the second I lifted my head from the pillow I felt it.

Not sharp pain.

Just this strange heavy stiffness running from the base of my skull down into the tops of my shoulders.

The kind where you instinctively tilt your head a little to one side and wait for it to release. Except it doesn't.

I sat up slowly, rolled my shoulders, turned my head left then right.

Still there.

I remember standing in the bathroom brushing my teeth staring at myself in the mirror and thinking

I must have slept weird.

That was the whole explanation I gave it.

Slept weird.

The next morning it happened again.

Then again on Friday.

By the following week I noticed something that made me uneasy.

It wasn't just that my neck felt stiff when I woke up.

It was that it stayed stiff.

I'd make coffee, unload the dishwasher, answer a few emails and an hour later it would still feel like the muscles around my neck were wrapped too tight.

By the time I sat down at my desk I could already feel that pulling sensation spreading across my shoulders.

And around 2 or 3 in the afternoon like clockwork the pressure would start building at the base of my neck.

Not enough to stop me from functioning.

But enough that I was constantly aware of it.

Enough that I started rubbing the back of my neck without even realizing it.

Then came the mornings when it felt worse.

One Saturday I woke up and tried to glance at the clock on my nightstand.

Just a small movement.

But the second I turned my head it felt like something in my neck had locked up.

Not excruciating.

Just tight. Restricted. Wrong.

That's when I started paying attention.

Because stiffness is one thing.

But when your body starts compensating for something before your brain even admits there's a problem… that's when you know it's becoming real.

At first I kept trying to outsmart it.

Hot shower in the morning.

Heating pad over my shoulders while answering emails.

A few stretches I found on YouTube.

I even bought one of those neck support pillows that promises to "align the spine naturally."

I booked a massage and for about an hour afterward my neck actually felt loose. Almost normal. But by the next afternoon the tightness had crept right back like it had never left.

For a while every new thing gave me a small burst of hope.

Maybe this is what was missing.

Maybe it's my posture.

Maybe I just need to stretch more.

But the pattern never changed.

Wake up stiff.

Loosen up slightly.

Sit at my desk.

Feel the tension build.

Finish the day with that same dull ache sitting at the base of my neck.

And by then I'd already spent more than I wanted to admit between the massage appointments, the chiropractor visits, the ergonomic pillow, the heating pad. All of it chasing relief that never lasted more than a day.

Driving became annoying.

Checking my blind spot meant rotating my entire upper body.

Sitting through dinner became uncomfortable.

Even reading in bed started irritating it.

I'd prop myself up with pillows, adjust positions, lie on my side then on my back… and still feel like my neck couldn't fully relax.

One evening my husband asked me something from the kitchen.

I turned to answer him and caught myself wincing.

He noticed immediately.

"Your neck again?" he said.

I laughed it off.

But later that night I caught myself in the bathroom mirror and had this strange thought.

When did this become "my neck again"?

When did it turn from a random annoyance into something that followed me through the entire day?

The part no one tells you about chronic neck tension is that it doesn't just hurt.

It starts quietly changing your routine.

Especially for women in their 40s and 50s when hormones start shifting and sleep becomes lighter.

You start planning around it.

Long drive? Think about the neck.

Long meeting? Think about the neck.

Wake up stiff? Wonder what kind of day it's going to be.

And if you're already in perimenopause or menopause where sleep can already be fragile… the nighttime tension makes it even worse.

Some nights I'd wake up at like 2 AM and instantly feel the tightness in my neck.

Before I even opened my eyes.

I'd shift my pillow. Roll my shoulders. Try a different position.

Then just lie there half awake waiting for my body to settle down again.

Eventually I brought it up to my doctor.

Then another.

Then another.

And the conversation always sounded roughly the same.

"Your scans look normal."

"Your bloodwork is fine."

"Probably posture."

"Maybe stress."

"Try stretching."

At one point I remember sitting in my car after an appointment just staring out the windshield.

Not because I'd gotten bad news.

Because I'd gotten no news at all.

And yet every morning my neck was telling me something very different.

That disconnect really messes with you.

Because when your body feels bad every day but every test says everything is "normal" you start questioning yourself.

Am I exaggerating this?

Is this just aging?

Is this just stress?

But then the next morning I'd wake up with that same locked stiffness and I knew I wasn't imagining it.

So I started searching online.

Eventually I found forums where other women my age were describing the exact same symptoms.

Morning stiffness.

Tension headaches.

Shoulders that felt permanently tight.

One woman wrote

"I wake up every morning with a stiff neck that takes hours to loosen up."

Another said

"By the end of the day my neck feels like a rock."

Reading those felt strangely comforting.

Because for the first time I realized I wasn't dealing with something random.

Eventually I stumbled across an explanation from a physical therapist about neck pain during menopause.

He explained that hormonal changes can increase muscle stiffness and inflammation while modern posture from phones and laptops and screens slowly compresses the cervical spine.

Over time the muscles tighten to compensate.

The joints compress.

The tension builds.

Suddenly the pattern made sense.

This wasn't just stress.

And it wasn't just posture.

It was compression building slowly over time.

And that's when I understood why everything I'd tried had only helped temporarily.

The heating pad warmed the surface but didn't release the compression underneath. The stretches loosened things for a few minutes but couldn't hold. The massage relaxed the muscles but didn't address the joints being squeezed together. The support pillow held my neck in place but didn't actively unwind anything.

Each thing was only treating one piece of a problem that needed several things working together.

That's when I read about what some physical therapy clinics in Norway were doing differently.

Instead of using one therapy at a time they combined several together.

Heat.

Massage.

Traction.

Proper neck support.

Almost like helping the neck unwind from several directions at once.

A few nights later I was deep in a discussion thread and someone mentioned something I hadn't seen before.

A small device designed around that exact same therapy combination that Norwegian clinics were using.

Not a regular pillow.

More like a small curved support for the neck that applied gentle warmth and stretching at the same time.

It was called the Callixe traction therapy pillow.

At first I almost ignored it.

Because by that point I'd already seen dozens of gadgets promising relief.

But the concept itself made me curious.

So I ordered one.

When it arrived it looked surprisingly simple.

Just a small curved pillow with a control button.

No complicated straps.

No setup.

The fabric felt softer than I expected too. That dense velvety cushion material that feels almost suspiciously comfortable.

You know those expensive massage chairs at the airport that cost like $3 a minute? That kind of feel.

I've never actually had a fancy spa massage.

But if I had to imagine what a "premium massage pillow" might feel like this was probably it.

The first time I used it was on a quiet Sunday afternoon.

I lay back thinking ok let's see what this thing actually does.

At first it just felt like comfortable neck support.

Then warmth slowly spread across the back of my neck.

A few minutes later a gentle vibration started.

And then something I wasn't expecting. This slow gradual stretch through my neck. Not pulling or forcing. Just this quiet opening like the pillow was gently creating space where everything had been compressed all day.

You know that feeling right after a really good chiropractic adjustment where your neck suddenly feels taller and lighter like a pressure valve just released? It was like that. Except softer. And I was lying on my couch instead of paying $80 in someone's office.

And the muscles at the base of my skull, the ones that felt like tight ropes all day, they started loosening.

Little by little.

Like slowly untangling a knot.

What surprised me most wasn't some dramatic change.

It was how relaxed my neck felt afterward.

Like the tension had softened.

Almost like my neck had been clenched for months and was finally remembering how to let go.

Over the next week I used it in the evenings when the tension built up.

And something subtle began changing.

Mornings felt lighter.

The stiffness didn't linger as long.

The headaches appeared less often.

One evening I realized I had sat through an entire dinner without rolling my shoulders or rubbing my neck once.

That hadn't happened in months.

The biggest difference came in the mornings.

Instead of waking up with that locked feeling I started waking up comfortable again.

It took my husband to point out the sleep thing.

We were having coffee on a Saturday and he said "You didn't get up last night."

I looked at him.

"You usually toss around 2 or 3 AM. Shift your pillow. Roll over a bunch. Last few weeks you've just been… sleeping."

I hadn't even noticed. But he was right. No more lying there at 2 AM waiting for my neck to settle. No more waking up just to rearrange myself. Just sleep. The kind I used to take for granted.

Like your body suddenly remembered something it used to know how to do.

But the moment it really hit me was completely ordinary.

I was backing out of the driveway to run a quick errand.

Normally when I reverse the car I turn my whole upper body because turning my neck too quickly used to trigger that tight pulling feeling.

But that morning I didn't think about it.

I just glanced over my shoulder.

Naturally.

The way you do without even noticing.

It wasn't until I was halfway down the street that it hit me.

I had turned my head fully to check the blind spot and there was no stiffness. No hesitation. No little wince of pain that I had gotten used to.

Just a normal movement.

It sounds like such a small thing.

But when something as simple as turning your head has been uncomfortable for months the first time it happens effortlessly again feels surprisingly emotional.
