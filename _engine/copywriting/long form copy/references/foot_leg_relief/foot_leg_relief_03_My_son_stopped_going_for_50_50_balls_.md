My son stopped going for 50/50 balls.

That’s when I knew something was really wrong.

Jake used to be fearless. The kid who’d slide in puddles and charge straight into a crowd of defenders.

Now he was pulling back from plays he used to love.

“Why didn’t you go for that ball?” his coach asked after practice.

Jake just shrugged. But I could see it in his eyes.

He was doing what kids in pain learn to do: asking himself if it was worth it before every play.

Most of the time, the answer was no.

Watching my confident kid turn into someone who second guessed everything was heartbreaking.

It wasn’t just soccer. He started walking with a little limp he didn’t even notice anymore.

He’d let the bus go by rather than sprint for it.

He avoided anything that might hurt.

His teammates noticed too.

“Come on, Jake!” one of them yelled after he backed off another tackle.

I saw his shoulders drop. That wasn’t just tiredness. That was defeat.

That night I found him unlacing his cleats like an old man.

“My heel’s fine, Mom,” he said. But we both knew it wasn’t.

The pain wasn’t just in his foot anymore. It had gotten into his head.

Every move was careful. Every play was hesitation.

I’d been focused on fixing the pain with ice, stretching, and fancy shoe inserts.

But I missed how the pain was changing his confidence.

He wasn’t just hurting. He was scared.

Scared that sprinting meant another sharp stab.

Scared that one wrong move would make it worse.

Scared his body couldn’t be trusted.

I started reading about how kids deal with pain.

One line stuck with me: pain can actually train the brain to be afraid of movement.

Even when the pain eases up, the fear can stay.

That explained everything. Why Jake played different even on good days. Why his confidence disappeared even when he wasn’t limping.

Resting wasn’t helping. If anything, it was teaching him to avoid movement even more.

What kids like Jake need isn’t just relief, it’s the chance to move again without being scared it’ll hurt.

That’s when I found research on compression sleeves made for kids with growth plate injuries.

They weren’t about keeping kids on the bench. They were about giving support so they could move confidently while they healed.

I ordered one right away.

When Jake put it on before his next game, I didn’t say much. I just watched.

Fifteen minutes in, he went for a ball he would’ve pulled out of for months.

Not only did he go for it, he won it!

And then he looked over at me with this huge grin.

The kind of look that said, I’m back.

Over the next few weeks, the difference was night and day.

No limping. No hesitation. No second-guessing every step.

The sleeve didn’t just help his heel.. it gave him the confidence to trust his body again.

I just wish I’d known sooner.

No kid should lose their confidence because of something that can be helped this simply.
