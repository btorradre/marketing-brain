But somewhere around menopause, things changed. The cellulite got worse every single year, and she slowly stopped doing the things she loved because she couldn't stop thinking about it.

She tried everything over the years. Creams from every price point. A round of those lymphatic massage treatments that cost us $1,200 and did nothing. I finally decided to figure this out myself.

And what I found explained why she'd been losing this battle for over a decade. It's called a "velcro effect," and it's why the dimpling keeps getting deeper year after year no matter what you do.

Worse, those compression leggings she lived in were restricting what little circulation she had left. Making the hooks tighter. Making the cellulite worse every single day she wore them.

No cream was going to fix that. No massage. No amount of walking or eating right. The fat was physically locked in place by her own skin, and nothing she'd tried had ever addressed that.

"Just try it for a month," I said. "If it doesn't work, I'll stop. I promise."

She used it every night without ever mentioning it. I didn't ask.

"It's smoother," she said quietly. Like she didn't want to jinx it.
