## Archetype Examples

### Archetype 1: Clean List

**Arrae Bloat** — Pure numbered list, 5 items. Each item: benefit headline → 2-3 sentences of supporting copy → CTA button. Sticky discount bar. No opening narrative. Items: (1) Works in under 1 hour, (2) Clinically proven 86% reduction, (3) Targets face puffiness, (4) Over 1M women trust it, (5) Clinically proven for IBS. Close: "1,000,000+ Have Made the Switch — Why Haven't You?"

**Beeya Wellness** — 5 reasons, clean format. Each item: benefit headline → 1-2 sentence explanation. Minimal copy per item. Product card with pricing and "Add to Cart" integrated mid-page. Doctor endorsements section. Trust badges row.

**Fungies Cordyceps** — 5 benefits, very clean. Each item: benefit headline → paragraph of supporting copy → "Get Your Gummies" CTA. Testimonial section at bottom. Final offer with pricing and discount.

**Longevity Store** — 7 reasons, editorial-style with byline ("By Jane M."). Comparison table at top (vs. competitors). Each item is short (2-3 sentences). Clean, informational tone.

**NOKT** — 5 reasons, clean list with video embeds. Opening line establishes the promise. Each item: benefit → mechanism explanation → how NOKT solves it. Bonus "science" section after the list. Founder letter section. Customer reviews at bottom.

### Archetype 2: Narrative-Framed List

**BB Company Provitalize (HE & SI)** — Opens with doctor-patient dialogue scene (300+ words). Establishes the mechanism (Hip Endotoxemia / SI Joint Dysfunction) through conversation. "Here's What Most Doctors Get Wrong" bridge section. THEN transitions to "5 Ways Provitalize Helps." Each list item is substantial (200+ words) with ingredient deep-dives, clinical studies, and embedded testimonials. Heavy social proof throughout. Multi-bottle pricing at close. FAQ section. Trust badges.

**Rejuveen KeraLush** — "The 5 Hidden Reasons Women Over 40 Experience Sudden Shedding." Opens with personal narrative (shower drain, widening part, crying in bathroom). Establishes "Cortisol Shedding" as the mechanism. Then transitions to "5 Ways KeraLush Stops Cortisol Shedding." Each item is a deep-dive into a specific cortisol → hair loss pathway with the product's ingredient fix. Very long, very detailed.

**GetSom Sleep** — "5 Reasons You're Still Awake at 2AM." Opening paragraph validates the reader's frustration (melatonin isn't working) and introduces the three-part framework (nutrition + relaxation + sleep signal). Each list item addresses a specific failure of current solutions and how Som Sleep fixes it. Educational tone.

**GLP-1 SOS** — "4 Reasons 55,000+ GLP-1 Users Swear By GLP-1 SOS." Brief opening that speaks directly to GLP-1 medication users. Each item addresses a specific side effect (constipation, sulfur burps, brain fog, supplement juggling) and how the product handles it. Very avatar-specific.

**Nuora Gummies** — 7 reasons. Opens with emotional validation ("Millions of women silently struggle..."). Bold statement: "If your pH is off, no amount of creams, washes, or pills will fix it." Then numbered list with frank, conversational tone. Video embeds throughout.

**Switch Strips** — 7 reasons. Positioned as "why sublingual strips beat pills." Each item combines mechanism education with product differentiation. Includes market trend data ("$66 billion wellness trend"). Founder letter close.

## Social Proof Techniques

### Inline Testimonials (within list items)
Provitalize embeds 2-3 testimonials per list item with age, credentials, and specific results: "Sammie L., 55-64: 'I was suffering with constant pain in my left hip...Not only did the pain STOP, I lost about 15-20 pounds in three months.'"

- Doctor quotes with full credentials: "As a Nurse Practitioner for 22 years..." (Provitalize)
- Doctor recommendation badges: "Dr. Grace Chang, ND, Hormone Expert" (Beeya)

## CTA and Close Patterns

### Funnel-conditional close (shared rule across the copy library)

The listicle is a **1-step hard-close destination** — the same role a PDP plays. The upstream ad or post sends here to convert, so the listicle carries the full hard apparatus: named product, guarantee/risk reversal, scarcity/urgency, pricing tiers, trust-badge rows, and a direct CTA. Exit the reader at **Most-Aware**. This is the opposite of the advertorial's soft CTA bridge.

The shared rule across all copy skills: **sending to a cart (listicle/PDP) → hard close; sending to an article (advertorial) → soft close.** "Soft" elsewhere in the library means low push intensity, not brand concealment — but the listicle is never the soft end of that spectrum. If you find yourself wanting a soft, no-apparatus close here, the piece probably wants to be an advertorial, not a listicle.

## Tone Registers

### Clinical/Authority (Provitalize, GLP-1 SOS, Begin Health)
Studies cited by institution name. Ingredient names with strain IDs. Specific percentages. Professional testimonials with credentials. Structured, calm, data-driven.

### Casual/Frank (Nuora, Arrae, GetSom)
"You know the drill." "Let's be real." "No more 'food baby' belly." Testimonials use everyday language. Emoji used sparingly. Short sentences. Direct address.

### Editorial/Journalistic (Longevity Store, Switch Strips)
Byline with author name and date. "X-Minute Read" tag. Comparison tables. Source citations with footnote numbers. Reads like a curated recommendation article.

### Enthusiastic/Discovery (Fungies, Auri, Tropical Oasis)
"Experience the power of..." "Say goodbye to..." Exclamation marks (used judiciously). Bright, optimistic tone. Focus on transformation and possibility.

## List Counts

From the reference library:
- **3 items**: Rare. Used for very focused, single-benefit products.
- **4 items**: GLP-1 SOS. Good for products solving a tight cluster of related problems.
- **5 items**: Most common. Arrae, Beeya, Provitalize (both), GetSom, Nuora (5-reason version), HealthyCell, Tropical Oasis, PWRRR, Rejuveen. The sweet spot for most supplement listicles.
- **7 items**: Nuora (7-shocking version), Begin Health, Longevity Store, Switch Strips, Kovaria. Good for products with broad benefit profiles or when overcoming heavy skepticism.
- **10 items**: Auri. Used for broad-spectrum mushroom supplement with many distinct benefits. Risk of dilution — only works if each item is genuinely distinct.

**Guidance**: Default to 5 unless the product warrants more. Every item above 5 must earn its place by addressing a distinct concern the reader has.

## Structural Observations

### What the best listicles have in common:
1. **CTA frequency** — A buy button appears at least every 2 list items, often after every single item.
2. **Testimonial integration** — Social proof is woven INTO the list, not bolted on at the end.
3. **Scannable structure** — Bold subheadlines, short paragraphs, visual proof elements. The reader can skim the subheadlines alone and get the pitch.
4. **Congruence with the ad** — The first thing the reader sees confirms what the ad promised. No jarring tone shifts or topic changes.
5. **One product, one problem** — No listicle tries to sell multiple products or address multiple unrelated problems.
6. **Specificity** — Numbers everywhere. "86% reduction." "43.1% less hair loss." "27% cortisol reduction." "$1.97/day." Specific beats vague every time.

### What weak listicles do:
1. **Generic benefit claims** without proof ("Supports overall wellness" — says nothing).
2. **Walls of text** per list item with no proof elements or testimonials to break them up.
3. **No CTA until the very bottom** — loses everyone who was convinced at item #3.
4. **Tone mismatch** with the ads driving traffic.
5. **Filler items** that don't address a real concern (padding to reach 7 or 10).
6. **Ingredient dumps** without explaining why the reader should care about each one.
