## WHY SHE BELIEVES IT WORKS BEFORE THE BRAND IS NAMED

**The core trick: by 2:09 the ad has never asked her to believe anything about a product.
It has only asked her to believe things about her own body and her own lab report.**

The chain, in order — note how late the first unverifiable claim arrives:

1. **0:04 — Agreement first.** "losing hair, exhausted, can barely think straight." She already
   believes this. Zero resistance spent.
2. **0:11 — Agreement again.** "her doctor looked at her ferritin, called it normal, sent her
   home." She verifies from memory. Two agreements banked before any ask.
3. **0:15 — First new belief is a DEFINITION, not a claim.** "That range was designed to tell you
   whether you're in danger of dying from anemia." Definitions are cheap to accept — her
   experience cannot contradict one.
4. **0:30 — She does the math herself.** Given 12/15/22 and then 70, the viewer computes her own
   deficit. **The ad never states a conclusion about her.** Self-generated conclusions are
   unfalsifiable to the person who generated them.
5. **0:40 — Indifferent enemy.** "The medical system just hasn't caught up." Lag, not conspiracy.
   She never has to defend her doctor, so she never argues back.
6. **0:43 — Social proof as practice volume.** "every single week," "two, three, four doctor
   visits," "put on antidepressants." Not testimonials — caseload. Only an authority can do this.
7. **1:26 — Absolution.** "It's not them. It's the iron." Seven words. The emotional pivot.
8. **1:29 — Mechanism arrives as a SPEC FOR THE PROBLEM, brand-free.** "What actually needs to
   happen is a three-step process." Steps 1/2/3 describe a *body*, not a *bottle*. She agrees to
   the acceptance criteria before she knows what meets them.
9. **2:09 — The product is revealed as the only thing matching criteria she already accepted.**

**Steal this:** write the mechanism as a spec sheet for the problem, get agreement on the spec,
then reveal that one product matches it. The brand never has to make a performance claim — it
makes a *matching* claim, which is far cheaper to believe.

## OBJECTION HANDLING — there is no objection section

Every objection is killed before it can be raised, and each one is killed BY THE MECHANISM
rather than by counter-argument. All of them collapse into the same single idea (absorption →
conversion → storage), which is why the ad needs no rebuttal block.

| Objection | Killed at | How |
|---|---|---|
| "My doctor said I'm fine" | 0:15–0:22 | Redefines what the test measures. The *range* is wrong, not the doctor |
| "If this were real my doctor would know" | 0:40 | System lag, not conspiracy |
| "So I'll just buy iron" | 1:07–1:12 | Ferrous sulfate absorbs 15% — kills the cheap substitute BEFORE naming the product |
| "Iron wrecks my stomach" | 1:12 | Reframed as *evidence for the thesis*: the side effect is the unabsorbed 85% |
| "I already tried iron, nothing happened" | 1:19 → 2:01 | Planted as her lived experience, explained 82s later by missing cofactors. Her failure becomes proof |
| "It's my fault, I wasn't consistent" | 1:26 | "It's not them. It's the iron." |
| "Why not any cheap supplement?" | 2:16 | "Most just have iron and maybe some vitamin C. They're still only doing step one" |
| "How long?" | 2:24 | "four to eight weeks" — modest, specific, installs the LTV window |
| "Too good to be true" | throughout | Every number is small: 15%, 4x, 4-5 points, 4-8 weeks. Zero big round claims |

## COPYWRITING STYLE

- **Expert testimony, not testimonial.** First person, but the "I" is a practitioner ("Patients
  come to me"). She can make mechanism claims a customer can't, and she never needs a
  before/after or a personal transformation arc. This is why the ad fits in 2:40 — no story act.
- **Stance in the first five words.** Not a question, not a promise, not a curiosity tease — a
  *position*, plus personal risk ("I'm done being quiet about it").
- **Second person throughout.** 13 uses of you/your in 509 words. Addressed to one woman.
- **Numbers are symptom numbers, never money numbers.** 12, 15, 22, 70, 15%, 4-5 points, 4-8
  weeks, third time. (Brooks: cost is NOT the pain point — the pain is that it doesn't work.)
- **Sentence length is a weapon.** Median 10 words. The ONE 35-word sentence is the victim
  picture at 0:04 — comma-stacked, relentless, one breath. The shortest are the pivots:
  *"That's all it was ever meant to do." · "It's not them. It's the iron." · "That's it."*
  **Long for pictures, short for turns, never long for explanation.**
- **Emotion by scene, never by adjective.** No "devastating," no "heartbreaking." Instead: a
  doctor *laughed*; half the hair she used to have; she left that office in tears. Three facts,
  8 seconds, done.
- **Zero throat-clearing.** Every "here's" carries payload ("here's the big problem," "here's
  what nobody explains"). No "so," "anyway," "the thing is" buying time.
- **Transitions are logical connectors that advance the argument** — "So a woman can be…",
  "And here's…", "And step three…". None of them are narrative glue.
- **One idea per sentence. Each idea stated exactly once.** Zero repetition in 509 words.
- **Flat, low-pressure close.** "I'll link Ferrovital below, and I hope this helps." No urgency,
  no discount, no scarcity — hard selling would break the authority frame.

## PORTABLE RULES FOR MOTILLI

1. **500-520 words. 2:40. Hard cap.** Write to word count; runtime = words ÷ 190 × 60.
2. Mechanism as a **numbered spec for the problem**, brand-free, agreed to before the reveal.
3. **Counting loop** ("a three-step process") to span the mechanism section.
4. Plant a symptom early, explain it 60-90s later. One long-span loop per ad.
5. Kill the cheap substitute BEFORE naming the product.
6. Absolution line, under 10 words, immediately before the mechanism.
7. Numbers are symptom numbers. Never money.
8. Every idea stated exactly once.
9. Flat close. No urgency stack under an authority frame.
