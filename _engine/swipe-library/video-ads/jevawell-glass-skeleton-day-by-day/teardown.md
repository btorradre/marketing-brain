## THE VISUAL SYSTEM — "the glass skeleton"

One recurring character carries all 118 seconds:

- A **translucent glass-skinned skeleton woman** — clear glossy shell over clean white bone, with
  the stomach, small intestine and colon visible inside as translucent wet tissue. Two large round
  human eyes in the skull sockets. No hair, no nose. Reads as a museum anatomy model, not horror.
- **Bright, clean, clinical environments:** white square-tiled bathroom, white kitchen, bedroom,
  café, living room. Soft even daylight, shallow depth of field, pale neutral palette.
- **Colour code:** warm amber/gold = working · dull grey-brown = stalled · soft teal = calm/settled ·
  red X = failed solution.
- **Four shot kinds, never blended:** an organ isolated on white · endoscopic macro inside the tract ·
  the figure full/half body in a room · real named competitor products.
- **Captions:** ONE word per card, heavy geometric sans, white, bold, thick black shadow, centred at
  chest height, ~0.35s per card. First card sits in a red box.
- **Signature frames:** the glass colon alone on white packed with stool · the skeleton on a toilet ·
  gold gummies with particle trails travelling the gut · a green vapour burp at a café table ·
  a pile of real named laxatives with the skeleton throwing its hands up · a wide room of skeletons
  behind "50,000+" · a dull-vs-glowing before/after split to close.

**Why it works:** the invisible is made visible without a diagram. No presenter to distrust, no
"I", pure second person. And it is cheap to scale — one approved character seeds every shot.

## THE AWARENESS CEILING — and how to invert it

This ad is **solution-aware and cannot be run cold.** The hook names the product in its first
sentence, and the whole ad is a product-results timeline. It only works on someone who already
believes a gummy could fix this.

**The inversion (used for MOT-VID-011):** keep the engine, reverse its direction.

| | Jevawell | Problem-aware port |
|---|---|---|
| Ladder | 14 days of what the gummy DOES | 5 days of what the shot does when nothing is aimed at it |
| Hook | names the product | names the problem — "this is what is going on inside your gut right now" |
| Emotional lever | promise | curiosity, then recognition, then self-blame |
| Belief flip | a missing molecule (acetate) | **placement** — everything she swallowed landed six feet below the problem |
| Product enters | 0:10 | 1:23 of 2:06 |
| Proof | 50,000 + 60-day | real page-verified figures + 90-day |

Same character, same caption cadence, same runtime band, same shot grammar. Only the direction of
the ladder and the position of the reveal change. **The day-marker device is format-neutral — it
carries results at solution-aware and failure at problem-aware equally well.**

## WHAT TO STEAL

1. **One character seeds the whole ad.** Approve the base frame, then everything derives from it.
2. **Time markers as caption anchors.** "Hour six." "Day three." Free structure, free pacing.
3. **The A/B on one camera.** Show the healthy state, then break it in the identical framing. Two
   shots teach a mechanism that a paragraph cannot.
4. **Repetition of frame as argument.** Three identical colon framings in a row for laxative/fiber/
   magnesium — the sameness IS the placement proof.
5. **Life moment paired to every anatomical beat.** The anatomy earns belief; the life moment earns
   identification. Neither works alone.
6. **Real trade dress in the failed-solutions pile.** Named boxes, legible. The proof is the label.
7. **Social proof as a picture,** not a caption — a room full of the same character.

## WHAT NOT TO STEAL

- Naming the product in the hook (caps the ad at solution-aware traffic).
- "Acetate" and the colon-flooding mechanism — that is their molecule and it argues the bottom.
  Motilli's spine is TOP-NOT-BOTTOM.
- Round social-proof numbers you cannot verify on your own page.
