## Design DNA

Healthtime is running **multiple distinct visual archetypes simultaneously** — this is a more sophisticated test matrix than Harmony Diet.

- **Color palette:** Mostly clean editorial-magazine — soft yellows, creams, navy text, occasional warm orange for the doctor-authority cards. Very different feel from card to card on purpose.
- **Typography:** Magazine-headline sans-serif. Bold, sentence-fragment claims. Subhead "Read full article" framing it as editorial content rather than ad.
- **Photo style:** Mix of stock-feeling clinical imagery (woman in white tank top measuring waist), professional female-doctor headshots, and bold-text-only cards.
- **Layout signature:** Each variant carries a small "READ FULL ARTICLE" or "Read this 5-minute article" pill — positioning the click as **editorial-content discovery, not a product purchase**. Classic advertorial-front-end pattern.
- **Overall vibe:** **Authority-driven advertorial entry point**. Looks like a Women's Health or Healthline article, not a supplement ad.

## Notes / Why This Is Interesting

1. **Multiple distinct visual archetypes inside ONE creative test** — this is a more advanced testing posture than running 3 color variants of the same layout. Suggests Healthtime has scale media spend and is actively hunting a winning archetype.
2. **"READ FULL ARTICLE" pill** = the dominant native-advertorial signal in this niche right now. Borrow it.
3. The body copy is a **first-person testimonial that name-drops an endocrinologist** — Healthtime stacks two authority sources (doctor + lived experience) in the same copy block. Strong belief-shift architecture.
4. All variants are showing **"Low impression count"** flags as of capture — could mean these are fresh-launched and haven't scaled yet, OR could mean these creatives are tail-end and being phased out. Worth re-checking in the next rotation.
