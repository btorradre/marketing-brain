# Funnel Advisory — PDP vs. Advertorial Decision Framework

This skill governs the strategic decision of WHERE to send paid traffic after the ad click. It answers one question: does this offer need an advertorial bridge, or can the ad do all the selling and send traffic straight to a product detail page?

This is an architecture decision, not a copy decision. Get the architecture wrong and even perfect copy won't convert. Get it right and mediocre copy has a chance.

Read this entire file before making a funnel recommendation. The factors are interdependent.

---

## The Core Principle

The funnel architecture is determined by ONE question:

**Can the ad copy complete ALL five belief shifts before the click?**

The five belief shifts (in sequence):
1. "My suffering is real" — emotional clearing
2. "My current approach is wrong" — trust vacuum
3. "The problem is in [root cause]" — mechanism education
4. "The solution needs A + B + C" — solution criteria / UMS
5. "This actually works" — product discovery, results, close

If the ad can land all five inside the primary text (even if it takes 3,000–5,000+ words), the reader arrives at the PDP as a fully converted believer. The PDP just needs to confirm the product, show social proof, display pricing, and provide checkout. Go straight to PDP.

If the ad can land beliefs 1–2 (maybe 3) but the mechanism is too complex or unfamiliar to fully resolve inside a Facebook ad, the reader arrives needing more education. She's emotionally invested but not yet convinced. She needs an advertorial to complete beliefs 3–5. Use an advertorial.

---

## The Five Decision Factors

### Factor 1: Mechanism Complexity

This is the single most important factor.

**Simple mechanism = PDP viable.**
A simple mechanism has: one root cause, one causal chain, one villain, one solution formula. The entire education can be delivered through a narrator's discovery journey inside a story without the narrator needing to suddenly become a scientist.

Example: Vitamin D deficiency → calcium can't reach bones → calcium deposits in arteries → take D3 + K2 + Magnesium + MCT together. One chain. One formula. A scared wife can relay what the doctor told her and the reader gets it.

Example: Nitric oxide depletion → blood vessels constrict → blood pressure rises → beetroot nitrates restore nitric oxide → vessels relax. One cause. One pathway. One ingredient class.

**Complex mechanism = Advertorial needed.**
A complex mechanism has: multiple interacting causes, a multi-part solution protocol, unfamiliar biological concepts, or a causal chain that requires sustained clinical-depth explanation to be believed.

Example: Bowel loses nitric oxide signaling → peristalsis fails → sublingual L-arginine bypasses gut to restore signal. The concept of "nitric oxide signaling in the bowel" is so unfamiliar to the avatar that it needs editorial-depth framing. The sublingual delivery route needs explanation (why drops under the tongue for a bowel problem?). A Facebook ad can open the loop but can't close it.

Example: Gallbladder removal → continuous bile drip → enzyme cascade failure + gut lining damage → need complete enzyme spectrum + targeted probiotic strains + proper sequencing protocol. Three-part solution with specific strain names and a sequencing protocol. This CANNOT be taught inside a story. It needs a structured editorial page.

**The test:** Can a narrator who is NOT a medical professional explain the full mechanism naturally through their own discovery journey, in language a friend would use? If yes, the mechanism fits inside an ad. If the narrator would need to suddenly shift into clinical language or teach concepts the avatar has never encountered, the mechanism needs an advertorial.

### Factor 2: Education Burden

How much does the avatar need to learn before she can believe the product will work?

**Low education burden = PDP viable.**
The avatar already has a rough mental model of the problem. She knows Vitamin D matters. She knows blood pressure is about blood vessels. The ad just needs to add the MISSING PIECE — the specific insight that makes the product the logical answer (e.g., "taking D alone is dangerous because calcium goes to arteries without K2 to direct it").

**High education burden = Advertorial needed.**
The avatar has NO mental model for the root cause. Nobody has ever told her about nitric oxide signaling in the bowel. Nobody has ever explained what gallbladder removal actually did to her digestive coordination. The education isn't adding a missing piece — it's building an entirely new framework from scratch. That takes editorial depth and authority that a Facebook ad format can't provide.

**The test:** Has the avatar likely encountered the root cause concept before — even vaguely — in a doctor's office, a magazine article, or a health forum? If yes, you're adding a piece to an existing puzzle (low burden, PDP viable). If no, you're building the puzzle from nothing (high burden, advertorial needed).

### Factor 3: Product Form Factor Familiarity

Does the product LOOK like what the avatar expects a solution to look like?

**Familiar form factor = PDP viable.**
A capsule or softgel for a health problem. A cream for skin. A powder for nutrition. The avatar's brain pattern-matches the product to its category without friction. She doesn't need an advertorial to explain WHY this product form could work.

**Unfamiliar form factor = Advertorial needed.**
Sublingual drops for constipation. A nasal spray for anxiety. A patch for blood sugar. When the product form doesn't match the avatar's expectation for the problem category, she needs explanation for why THIS delivery method works for THIS problem. That explanation needs more space than a PDP provides.

**The test:** If you told the avatar "there's a [product type] for [problem]," would she immediately think "that makes sense" or would she think "how would that work?" If the latter, you need an advertorial to bridge the form factor gap.

### Factor 4: Avatar's Trust Deficit

How many times has the avatar been failed by solutions, and how deep is her skepticism?

**Low trust deficit = PDP viable.**
The avatar hasn't tried many solutions yet. She's relatively early in her journey. She's open to trying something new because she hasn't been burned repeatedly. A well-crafted ad with social proof and a strong story can earn her trust.

**High trust deficit = Advertorial needed.**
The avatar has been through 5 doctors, 12 supplements, 3 prescription medications, 2 elimination diets, and a specialist who contradicted everything the previous specialists said. Her default state is "nothing works for me." Overcoming years of medical failure requires sustained, authoritative content that builds trust through depth — the kind of trust that an editorial page with a journalist byline, clinical citations, and structured mechanism education can provide. A Facebook ad, no matter how long, still LOOKS like an ad. An advertorial LOOKS like journalism. The format itself carries trust signals that supplement the content.

**The test:** How many failed solutions has the typical avatar tried? If 0–2, PDP is viable. If 3+, seriously consider an advertorial. If 5+, an advertorial is almost certainly required.

### Factor 5: Solution Component Count

How many things does the product need to do simultaneously?

**Single-action solution = PDP viable.**
The product does one thing (e.g., delivers Vitamin D correctly with the right cofactors). The ad explains why that one thing matters, and the PDP confirms the product does it.

**Multi-action solution = Advertorial needed.**
The product needs to do 2–3 things simultaneously (e.g., complete enzyme spectrum + targeted probiotics + proper sequencing). Each component needs to be justified against the mechanism. The avatar needs to understand WHY all components are necessary and WHY they must work together. This sequential education is the advertorial's job.

**The test:** Can you describe what the product does in one sentence without using "and"? If yes, PDP viable. If you need "and" or a list, you likely need an advertorial.

## The Decision Matrix

Score each factor 1 (PDP-favorable) or 3 (advertorial-favorable):

| Factor | 1 (PDP) | 2 (Borderline) | 3 (Advertorial) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mechanism complexity | One cause, one chain | One cause, longer chain | Multiple causes or multi-part protocol |
| Education burden | Adding a piece to existing knowledge | Introducing one new concept | Building entirely new framework |
| Form factor familiarity | Expected format (capsule, cream) | Slightly unusual | Counter-intuitive delivery method |
| Trust deficit | 0-2 failed solutions | 3-4 failed solutions | 5+ failed solutions, deep medical skepticism |
| Solution components | Does one thing | Does two things | Does three+ things simultaneously |

**Score 5-8:** Go straight to PDP. The ad can do all the work.
**Score 9-11:** Borderline. Test both. If the ad copy is exceptional (3,000+ words, cinematic narrative, complete belief-shift sequence), PDP may work. If you can't fit the full mechanism inside a story, use an advertorial.
**Score 12-15:** Use an advertorial. The education burden is too high for ad copy alone.

## PDP-Direct Funnel Requirements

If you're going straight to PDP, the AD COPY must:

1. **Run 3,000–5,000+ words** — This is non-negotiable. You're doing the advertorial's job inside the ad. Short ads cannot shift all five beliefs.

2. **Complete all five belief shifts** — Emotional clearing (55-65% of copy), trust vacuum, mechanism education, solution criteria, product discovery + results + close.

3. **Use Narrative Trojan Horse hooks** — Because you're sending to PDP (not an editorial page), the hook needs to pull in UNAWARE traffic through story/drama/curiosity, not health signals. The reader needs to be fully sold before she realizes she's reading a supplement ad.

4. **Deliver mechanism through narrator discovery** — The narrator learns the mechanism from a doctor, specialist, researcher, or through personal research. She relays it in conversational language. The reader learns alongside her.

5. **Introduce the product through authority or organic discovery** — A doctor writes it on a piece of paper. A patient recommends it. The narrator finds it after exhaustive research. The product enters as the resolution of the story, not as a pitch.

The PDP itself needs: product confirmation (matches what the ad described), pricing/offer, social proof (reviews, ratings), guarantee, and checkout. No further education. No more selling. Just confirmation and conversion.

---

## Advertorial Funnel Requirements

If you're using an advertorial, the AD COPY must:

1. **Build massive emotional investment** — The ad's job is to make the reader CARE enough to click. Emotional clearing, identity recognition, rock-bottom moments.

2. **Open the mechanism loop without closing it** — The ad should hint at WHY everything else failed and tease that there's a root cause nobody has explained. But don't fully resolve the mechanism — that's the advertorial's job.

3. **Create a natural bridge to editorial content** — The click should feel like "I need to learn more" not "I need to buy something." The reader should arrive at the advertorial expecting INFORMATION, not a sales page.

4. **Use hooks at problem-aware to solution-aware levels** — Unlike PDP-direct (which needs unaware Trojan Horse hooks), advertorial funnels can use hooks that reference the condition directly, because the reader needs enough baseline awareness to seek out the education.

The ADVERTORIAL must: complete the mechanism education at editorial depth, build the solution criteria (UMS), introduce the product against those criteria, deliver social proof, handle objections, and close with pricing/guarantee/CTA. Use the advertorial skill for writing this.

## Common Mistakes

**Mistake 1: Using an advertorial when the mechanism is simple enough for the ad.**
This adds unnecessary friction. Every click is a leak point. If the ad can do all the work, sending traffic to an advertorial before the PDP just loses people who were already ready to buy.

**Mistake 2: Going straight to PDP when the mechanism needs editorial depth.**
The reader arrives at the PDP still confused about WHY the product works. The PDP's social proof and pricing can't overcome a mechanism education gap. She bounces.

**Mistake 3: Writing short ads and sending to PDP.**
A 500-word ad cannot shift all five beliefs. If you're going PDP-direct, the ad needs to be a complete sales vehicle (3,000–5,000+ words). Short ads need advertorials.

**Mistake 4: Using problem-aware hooks for PDP-direct funnels.**
If you're sending to PDP, the ad needs to do ALL the work — including pulling in unaware traffic. Problem-aware hooks pre-filter the audience down to people already looking for a solution, which works fine for advertorial funnels (the advertorial completes the sell). But for PDP-direct, you need maximum top-of-funnel volume, which means Trojan Horse hooks that pull in unaware traffic.

**Mistake 5: Ignoring the trust deficit.**
A supplement for an avatar who's tried 8 failed solutions needs an advertorial even if the mechanism is simple. The advertorial's editorial format carries trust signals (journalist byline, structured layout, clinical citations) that a Facebook ad and a PDP cannot replicate.

## Real-World Examples

**True Nutra (Vitamin D3 + K2) — PDP Direct**
Score: 6 (PDP). Simple mechanism (D deficiency → calcium misrouting). Low education burden (everyone knows Vitamin D). Familiar form factor (softgel). Low-moderate trust deficit. Single-action solution.

**Rosabella (Beetroot for Blood Pressure) — PDP Direct**
Score: 7 (PDP). Simple mechanism (nitric oxide depletion → vessel constriction). Low-moderate education burden. Familiar form factor (capsule). Moderate trust deficit (medication skepticism, but product is natural alternative not replacement for years of failed supplements). Single-action solution.

**Nutrition Therapy (Bowel Support Drops for IBS-C) — Advertorial**
Score: 13 (Advertorial). Complex mechanism (nitric oxide signaling in bowel). High education burden (avatar has never heard of this). Unfamiliar form factor (sublingual drops for constipation). Very high trust deficit (years of gastroenterologists, laxatives, elimination diets). Single-action but unfamiliar delivery.

**ClearDose (Enzymes + Probiotics for Post-Gallbladder) — Advertorial**
Score: 14 (Advertorial). Complex mechanism (bile coordination failure → enzyme cascade → gut lining damage). Maximum education burden (no doctor has ever explained this in 50 years). Familiar form factor (capsule — this is the one factor in its favor). Very high trust deficit (decades of "learn to live with it"). Multi-component solution (enzymes + probiotics + sequencing).
