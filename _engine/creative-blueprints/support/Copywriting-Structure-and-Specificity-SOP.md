# Copywriting structure and specificity

Source: explicit user instruction in the Lunessa podcast conversation, September 15, 2026. Applies across workspace copywriting. This SOP records the requested structure and distinguishes practical interpretation from the user's exact wording.

## Required order — user's labels

1. Hook
2. Problem
3. Brief problem
4. Agitation
5. Solution
6. Dismissal
7. UMP: the real root cause and why past solutions didn't work
8. UMS: what really works
9. The solution intro
10. Some future pacing using social proof

UMP means the problem mechanism; UMS means the solution mechanism. Finish explaining the problem before introducing how the proposed solution addresses it. A mechanism described as unique or a root cause requires evidence; the framework does not establish uniqueness or causation.

## Working interpretation for execution

These distinctions are an editorial interpretation, not additional quoted user instructions:

- Problem identifies the customer's immediate situation; brief problem supplies concise context without repeating the same point.
- Agitation shows the specific cost in the person's life. Keep its length appropriate to the brief; the current Lunessa request calls for brief agitation.
- The first solution beat raises the familiar route or category the customer would consider. Dismissal voices a credible objection or explains a supported limitation, rather than attacking every alternative.
- UMP answers why the customer's difficulty persists. UMS explains how the proposed approach addresses the demonstrated gap.
- The later solution intro names the actual product and connects verified features to the requirements already explained. Do not disclose the entire product mechanism prematurely in beat five.
- Future pacing depicts the desired life after the problem is addressed. Relevant genuine social proof can support this vision; a hypothetical scene is not a real customer's outcome.

Apply the functions at the scale of the deliverable; a short asset may carry multiple functions in one sentence or visual. Do not manufacture ten paragraphs for every headline. Preserve current brand priorities and concept-specific directions while using this sequence; fashion benefits should remain aspirational rather than defaulting to exaggerated distress.

## Customer voice and life specificity

Use customer vocabulary, ordinary sentence rhythm, specific frustrations and recognizable routines. Here “accent” means the avatar's way of speaking and seeing the problem, not an invented national accent or caricature. Research/VOC informs language; it does not license a narrator to claim experiences they did not have.

Ask of every benefit: what could the person actually do, say or notice? Prefer an observable moment over “more energy,” “better results,” “changed my life” or a bare numerical outcome. Choose moments grounded in this avatar. Family time, getting out of bed and returning to an activity are the user's examples, not a compulsory scenario library.

The user illustrates a gradual arc: no noticeable change in week one, some energy and getting out of bed in week two, time with a spouse by week four. Preserve this preference for credible progression where the actual record supports it. Do not fabricate an initial failure to make the later success persuasive. Do not present the example as Lunessa evidence.

## Proof must be specific and traceable

Before citing a study, read the actual source. Capture:

`year / study identity → population → exact preparation or formulation → dose → duration → comparator → endpoint → result → material limits → relevance to our finished product`

Use the most relevant details in spoken copy; keep the complete provenance in the claim record. An ingredient study does not become a Lunessa trial. A safety observation under specified conditions does not mean no side effects, safety for everyone or superiority to medication. Red yeast rice studies concern particular preparations given to particular participant populations; these are different facts.

The user's “a 2019 study…” is an illustration of research specificity, not a supplied verified finding. Do not fill in a year, sample, preparation, outcome or dose from memory to make a claim sound authoritative.

Testimonials must have an actual source and preserve the customer's meaning. A podcast researcher can report documented customer experiences with attribution, but must not acquire a fabricated first-person patient history. A proposed outcome scene or conditional future pacing must remain distinguishable from actual social proof.

## Review and handoff

Check order, brief agitation where requested, customer vocabulary, observable desired outcomes, distinct UMP/UMS/product introduction, and source support for every concrete result. Record missing proof explicitly. Recount words and update provisional timing and the existing editing plan whenever the selected script changes. User-requested script-first work ends with copy; it does not trigger unsolicited storyboard/media generation.
