# Animated silent microstory

Read the [shared contract](../creative-diversity-router/references/production-contract.md) and [R06 evidence](../../research/R06/analysis.md). The approximately 15-second source uses desire → purchase → secret collection reveal → another request. It has no spoken script or observed editorial captions.

## Write the action script

State the premise and payoff in plain language, then write what a viewer can see: who wants what, how another person responds, what action changes the situation and what new information resolves the story. Every beat must remain understandable without narration. Build a hook from immediately legible desire or action; for fashion make the object and styling coveted.

Design the reveal backward. Decide exactly what remains hidden, what gesture exposes it and how much time the viewer needs to recognize it. R06's long hallway/closet action earns its collection reveal; cutting away when the door begins to open would discard the joke. After the reveal, the bag-placement shot confirms the new purchase joins the collection; the last phone request repeats the desire in a new context.

Avoid reusing this particular joke as a claim about customers or relationships. Write original situations appropriate to the brand and avatar. The format can end emotionally as well as comically. A sales CTA is optional target direction, absent from the source.

## Visual planning

- Define stable characters and their relationship, exact wardrobe by scene, product/prop identity, carry side, room geography and expression at each action.
- Establish the object before a reaction to it; maintain eyelines so the viewer knows what the character sees.
- Use scale to reveal information: a two-shot for a request, closer action for a transaction, wider spatial reveal for hidden abundance, reaction close-up for payoff. Choose only the scales the actual story needs.
- Let actions complete before cutting. Specify the first and last visible state of doors, hands, bag, phone or other props across shots.
- Keep covering images in the same animation world. Do not insert unrelated live-action B-roll simply to increase cuts.

## Edit and sound

Use a hard cut to move to the next causal step; a brief overlap can compress time when spatial continuity is clear. The source has short overlaps around frames 44–49 and 298–300, plus two hard cuts. These are observed windows, not named presets to imitate. Do not spread dissolves across the whole film.

Build an original sound cue sheet from actions: transaction, door, reveal and reaction may merit accents. Let silence or a held sound protect anticipation. Model-assisted source notes suggest playful music and comic accents, but exact sounds/sync require listening verification. Avoid a continuous wall of effects.

Generate and inspect first frames before motion; follow the actual production/board workflow in the contract. Inspect all transitional frames for identity, age, wardrobe and body drift. R06 visibly transforms its protagonist during the hallway sequence: this is a defect, not a desired device. Also check object count, clasp/handle design, reflections, hand contacts and legible phone content.

Deliver the action/editing plan, reveal timing, continuity sheet and sound intentions. QA once without sound for story clarity, then with sound for timing and payoff. Do not add dialogue to repair an unclear visual premise unless the user requests a format change.
