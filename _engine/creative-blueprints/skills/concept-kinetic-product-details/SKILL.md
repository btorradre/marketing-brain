# Kinetic product-detail sequence

Read the [shared contract](../creative-diversity-router/references/production-contract.md) and [R13 audit](../../research/R13/analysis.md). The COS source cycles ten still compositions of one bag, holding each about 0.27–0.33s. This is observed rapid image replacement; the capture method and beat-perfect synchronization are not established.

## Construct a visual inventory

Resolve exact product/color/size and approved references. List the specific shapes, material surfaces, openings, fasteners, compartments and carry attachments the viewer should notice. Choose distinct images that reveal those things. One enlarged crop cannot supply the same information as a new physical angle or action.

Plan a legible movement of attention: dramatic open form → signature detail → interior → construction angle → functional feature → recognizable whole. Change the sequence to suit the actual product; a shirt or shoe needs different details from a bag. Keep background, lighting and color coherent enough that quick replacements still belong to the same object.

## Why each image changes

- A whole/open view establishes the object's structure before macros fragment it.
- Surface and hardware close-ups make a tactile or signature detail visible; they do not independently prove material composition.
- An interior or underside answers a different visual question from another front crop.
- A strap/attachment view explains carrying construction; preserve the real connection and proportions.
- A resolved full silhouette reconnects the details to a desirable finished product.

Record that job beside every proposed image. Reject a second composition if it adds no new information or aesthetic contrast. The source repeats the same ten-view cycle many times. Under the user's variety instruction, do not copy those repeats to pad a target. Prefer a purposeful single sequence; a loop requires an explicit reason in the target brief, not a claim that repeated frames are new scenes.

## Rhythm and delivery

Use direct cuts between stills; do not generate unnecessary motion or morphs. Very fast changes can create energy, but target hold lengths must let the intended feature register. Give more time to unfamiliar geometry or any text. Avoid placing a detailed offer over a three-tenths-of-a-second hold.

Choose a musical phrase and test the actual cut rhythm by listening after assembly. Source model analysis indicates electronic music and no speech; exact tempo, phase and mix are unverified. A sparse end hold or seamless visual return can resolve the sequence if the brief calls for it. No CTA card is observed in the source.

Produce images with GPT Image 2 from approved product references, then use Resolve for the sequence under the shared contract. Inspect hardware count, closures, internal layout, stitching, straps and surface color across every image. Do not treat generated macro views as physical measurement or documentary proof.

Deliver a compact editing plan with feature inventory, distinct composition list, rationale and recognition time for each, background/light system, music intention and ending. QA that the sequence communicates one accurate product and does not become rapid arbitrary texture flashes or visually repetitive filler.
