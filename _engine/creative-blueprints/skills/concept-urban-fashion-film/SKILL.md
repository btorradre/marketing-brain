# Urban fashion editorial film

Read the [shared contract](../creative-diversity-router/references/production-contract.md) and [R10 evidence](../../research/R10/analysis.md). The approximately 20.8s Sézane reference has no spoken sales script, captions or CTA card. Its visual story is a desirable collection in a coherent city world.

## Visual argument

Define the collection's styling promise, palette, silhouettes and relationship to place. Cast and style to make that promise visible. A location supports the clothing through light, geometry and lived context; it should not become generic travel footage that overwhelms the product.

Build whole look → meaningful detail → worn movement → another look, varying the exact order to preserve surprise. R10 alternates coats, accessories, portraits and walking figures. Polka dots, denim, trench shapes, bags and city architecture provide distinction without abandoning the overall world.

For every shot state what new styling information it adds: proportion, drape in movement, bag-to-outfit relationship, texture, face/attitude or a contrasting silhouette. A close-up belongs because it reveals something not readable in the wide. Return to a full figure after details so viewers can imagine the complete look.

## Movement and transitions

Start with an arresting relevant gesture or composition. Source begins with a very short blurred city movement, then enters fashion; a target may lead directly with a striking look if that communicates faster. Treat this as a choice, not an obligatory template.

Use real subject actions such as walking, a bag lift, coat adjustment or turn. Direct camera movement in relation to them: track alongside to show drape, approach for attitude, tilt for accessory relationship. Do not add identical slow pushes to every shot.

Short city pans at approximately 0–0.24s, 6.8–7s and 12.4–12.72s punctuate R10. They are visible blurred camera intervals, not proof of a named transition preset. Place a target whip/pan only where direction, scene change or rhythm benefits from it; preserve a clear incoming look. Distinguish in-shot motion, blur and hard cuts.

## Music, ending and QA

Choose a licensed/original track whose phrases suit the desired attitude. Vary shot holds by recognition and musical structure, then verify the actual edit by listening. The source audio analysis suggests a driving percussive bed; exact beat synchronization is unverified. Do not infer a chase narrative from energetic music.

Keep typography sparse or absent when the brief seeks pure editorial feeling. Any brand/CTA addition is explicit target direction. End on a resolved look, movement or brand hold appropriate to delivery rather than an unfinished gesture.

Deliver a visual-beat editing plan with look identities, action, location/light, shot scale, cut/transition purpose, music phrase intention and ending. For generated production, maintain each model/outfit/product through its scene and inspect gait, hands, bag straps, fabric motion and physical scale. Audit all looks for distinct compositions and actions. The film sells an imagined way of dressing; it is not a material test or documented testimonial.

For concept planning or an editing handoff, read the matching pair before mapping the target script. Keep observed reference calibration separate from proposed target timings. The blueprint defines the beat sequence; the profile defines coverage, cuts, transition exceptions, B-roll styles/placement, movement, captions, audio and ending.
