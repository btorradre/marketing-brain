# AI UGC greenscreen product story

Read the [blueprint](references/ai-ugc-greenscreen-story-blueprint.md), [editing profile](references/ai-ugc-greenscreen-story-editing-profile.md), [workspace evidence](../../research/W01/analysis.md) and [production contract](../creative-diversity-router/references/production-contract.md). This family keeps one conversational creator as an orientation point while the background supplies the product argument. The guide, background, captions and voice are separate tracks.

## Choose the right format

Use this family when the presenter comments on a sequence of concrete images: desirable looks, a selection principle, an item, its relevant details and a next action. The speaker need not stand in the depicted room or pretend to own the product. A synthetic guide is not a real customer testimonial.

For a sequence of premium/alternative comparisons and diagrams that teaches a service, use [the comparison guide](../concept-ai-greenscreen-guide/SKILL.md). For narration with no visible guide, use [product voiceover](../concept-product-voiceover/SKILL.md). Do not convert this family into a full-screen talking head interrupted by unrelated stock clips.

## Set the argument and reveal rules

Choose the applicable mode before timing shots:

| Mode | Communication sequence | Reveal rule |
|---|---|---|
| Product discovery / aspirational reference | Coveted look → relevant selection criteria → target reveal → visible reasons to want it → styled outcome → action | A delayed target reveal is allowed when explicitly part of the concept. A reference bag, celebrity photo or comparison image cannot imply target ownership or endorsement. |
| Styling adviser | Useful styling promise → identify the item → distinct complete looks → exact detail/action → styling principle → lived payoff → action | An early named reveal can support multiple styling examples. Keep different wearers across independent looks when the brief calls for them; preserve a wearer inside her matching detail shot. |
| Opinion / selection reframe | Provocative fashion opinion → specific familiar tension → category alternative and its limitation → better criteria → named product → outfit consequence → imagined use → action | The product may appear before its spoken name, as in Vivienne. Visual presence and named introduction are separate fields. |

For new or revised copy apply the current copywriting SOP through the production contract, including UMP before UMS. A fashion selection rationale can be ordinary shape/material/styling logic; do not invent a medical mechanism, personal purchase history or social proof to fill the structure. Keep approved scripts exact. These visual modes organize the presentation; they do not authorize changing approved copy order.

Record `first_target_visible_cue`, `first_target_named_cue`, `comparison_asset_identity`, `offer_evidence` and `closing_destination`. Do not inherit a universal six-second reveal, four-image hook or mandatory sale screen from older concepts.

## Map the background to the exact thought

Save the target editing plan first using the [shared template](../creative-diversity-router/assets/concept-editing-plan-template.md). Give each beat its exact words, asset or gap, visible action, style ID, entry/exit, duration, cut reason and scene reason. Keep these choices explicit:

- Show the whole styled look when the line is about an outfit. A city name does not automatically earn skyline footage; the clothes and carry action usually do the work.
- Cut to a distinct material, hardware or matching-accessory view when the specific detail becomes the subject. Preserve the model, sleeve and setting when the detail continues the same example.
- Return to a meaningful whole view or advance to a new example when interpretation resumes. The keyed guide can remain visible throughout; “return” need not mean a full-screen presenter.
- Use a new setting/action for future pacing and a fresh composition for the recap. Do not replay prior shots or count a crop as a new scene.
- Give a real, current page enough unobstructed space at the destination cue. Hide the guide if necessary, preserving the continuous voice. A clean product-and-action ending is also valid.

Fashion desire leads. The opening must reward attention with immediately readable styling or relevant action; exaggerated distress is not this family's default. Phone-like light and framing, generated imagery, real customer media and actual phone capture are different properties. Preserve their provenance.

## Keep the presenter and edit coherent

Read [layout, voice and timing handoff](references/presenter-layout-and-audio.md) for production or revision. Start with one consistent corner guide, a protected product region and a caption region. Check their collision throughout the sequence. Change corner on a meaningful scene boundary only when the new composition requires it; do not bounce the presenter between sides for novelty.

Background cuts normally follow a new idea, detail or completed action. Presenter continuity does not require a new talking take at every background cut. No compulsory dissolve, zoom, beat-sync or B-roll percentage applies. A still can hold; a useful stepped crop stays on the background track.

The named ElevenLabs **Woman Over 40** voice in the source projects was an exact library selection, not an instruction to generate any mature-sounding voice. Resolve an explicitly named voice to its actual provider identity before production. The source projects' HeyGen presenter use was explicitly authorized for those projects; it does not automatically grant a model exception for every new task. Google Omni remains the standing video route unless the user's applicable authorization specifies otherwise.

When tightening an existing ad, preserve approved words and playback speed; synchronize every cut in the presenter, narration and captions. Do not reuse old cue times after changing the audio. Verify the actual revised export, not only a source avatar or a proxy review.

Deliver the selected mode, blueprint/profile, concrete visual/edit rationale and requested artifact. This skill request alone does not authorize a new video, storyboard, provider job or campaign launch.
