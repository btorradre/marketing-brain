## Coverage and layer structure

Use a full-frame background track plus one independent keyed guide, exact phrase captions, optional specific label/CTA and continuous narration. The background is often the principal visual evidence, not a generic insert covering a talking-head scene. Keep the guide’s identity/wardrobe stable; independent fashion examples may intentionally feature different people. Lock separate visual and spoken product-reveal cues. A no-guide destination ending is allowed when the selected mode needs legibility.

## Cut rules and protected holds

Cut the background on a new idea, item, feature scale, useful action completion or destination cue. Keep expressive guide delivery continuous across picture changes. Protect product recognition, full-outfit inspection, matching-detail comprehension and the completed pickup/turn. Do not cut on every caption phrase. Quiet-gap joins inside one background scene are not new scenes. Inspect adjoining source/target frames for every meaningful boundary; use actual aligned speech rather than estimating a cut from a script word count.

## Transitions and handles

Direct background cuts are the baseline established by these examples. The presenter and captions do not dissolve with the background. A planned background-only detail step crop is a framing event. Do not add whooshes, whip pans, flashes, morphs or dissolves at every clause. If another reference warrants a transition, name its affected layers, cue, handles and reason. Layout changes happen at a meaning/scene boundary with a clear subject to uncover; avoid arbitrary presenter travel.

## B-roll entry, exit and return

Choose the next background by what the line makes inspectable: complete look for styling, distinct material/detail for a descriptor, matching accessory for a relationship, fresh everyday scene for imagined use, real destination for action. Give every insert a start cue, finish cue and return/next-view decision. Same presenter is intentional continuity; repeated backgrounds, recolors, mirrors and crops are not independent variety. Keep stills as stills where useful. Use the defined mode’s coherent treatment across the entire ad instead of making every section polished commercial footage or every hook distressed phone footage.

## Camera, reframing and subject action

Separate genuine captured/generated subject action from post transforms. A step, cuff adjustment, bag lift or chair pull supplies useful motion; allow its result to appear. Use a restrained background step crop only when the next phrase names a smaller detail and resolution supports it. Keep the guide and captions fixed during that crop. Do not animate every still or repeat a short action loop to fill narration. Presenter size must be assessed from visible silhouette at the actual aspect ratio; trial roughly 25–35% picture height, then adjust to product/face/hand clearance. Exact source pan/tilt/zoom values are not transferable.

## Captions and graphic events

Align exact approved captions to the selected final voice. Use readable short semantic groups, with natural line breaks and sufficient contrast; approximately 3–6 words can be a trial, not a segmentation law. Negation must remain understandable across phrase changes. Protect face, hands, key product details, full-look shoes and current platform UI. Keep captions, city/item labels and CTA as separate events. The source white sans-serif/outline treatment can guide a target, but no exact font/size match is inferred from thumbnails. Refresh page/price/stock text from actual evidence.

## Voice, music and sound design

Select the exact requested provider voice before avatar production. The historical Woman Over 40 choice meant the named ElevenLabs voice, not an age prompt. Keep identity/settings/source hash recorded; no automatic cloning. Use current authorized presenter route; HeyGen in W01 was explicitly requested for those projects and is not a blanket exception to Google Omni. Prefer finalized, conservatively tightened narration before a new audio-driven avatar when supported. For existing matched audio/video, apply the same ordered keep/remove map and re-align captions, background cues and CTA. Preserve words, pronunciation and selected speed; do not double-apply 1.1×. Mute unwanted generated B-roll audio; select no music or a licensed low bed deliberately. Review actual lip sync and intelligibility, disclosing if audio QA is only model-assisted.

## Image finish and time treatment

Keep coherent natural skin/material color and a clean keyed edge on both light and dark plates. Match backgrounds to the chosen phone-like or natural-fashion treatment without adding shake/grain as authenticity proof. No forced slow motion, beauty filter or perpetual smile. Distinct scene actions and product construction must survive source-to-export review. Respect source duration at differing fps; converting frame cadence must not retime voice. Preserve native provider files, selected revisions and original exports. A static guide or unverified render is a work in progress.

## Reveal, CTA and ending

Enter the ending while the invitation is spoken. Use either a real current page or an accurate product-plus-action composition; no mandatory discount or scarcity. Clear the guide if she covers relevant page content, as in Vivienne; retain her if the product/action are readable, as in Eleanor. A short 0.2–0.8s post-speech hold is a proposed trial, not a source-wide rule. Extend for comprehension, not padding. Resolve the reveal policy from the brief; old-money target delay does not override a product-visible Vivienne-style hook.

## Shorter, longer and timing conflicts

At short duration use fewer distinct arguments/examples and lighter caption load within authorized copy scope; preserve exact locked words. At longer duration add genuinely different styled examples, actions or relevant evidence, not replayed photos or stretched short clips. Long city descriptions can hold a meaningful look or introduce a distinct feature view at its exact cue. Do not force the 24.6s Nuamore runtime, the 70.667s Eleanor timing or the 35.9167s Vivienne picture length onto a new script. Preserve product/reveal constraints when changing mode. Surface duration conflicts instead of silently speeding speech.

## Reject and verify

Reject wrong library voice, a generic maturity substitute, unsupported provider exceptions, a static guide described as speaking, independent audio/presenter cuts, captions left on the pre-trim timeline, arbitrary side switching, guide/product overlap, unreadable grids/PDP, repeated inserts disguised as crops, unsupported product details, invented customer proof and expired offer claims. Inspect the selected first frames and finished export; verify all source/target joins, matte, full product visibility, whole-ad variety and exact ending. Model QA can miss a corner shift; source frames and actual receipts resolve contradictions. No performance claim follows from creative resemblance.
