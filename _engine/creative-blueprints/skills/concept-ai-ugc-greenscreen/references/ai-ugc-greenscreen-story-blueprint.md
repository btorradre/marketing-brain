## Concept and script architecture

One conversational creator remains as a small keyed guide while changing background images make a product argument visible. This family combines the Nuamore-style visual language with documented Eleanor travel and Vivienne quiet-luxury revisions. Its continuity comes from the guide and narration; its variety comes from genuinely distinct backgrounds, actions and viewing scales.

Choose discovery, styling adviser or opinion/reframe mode in the family skill. For new copy apply Hook → Problem → Brief problem → Agitation → Solution/category → Dismissal → UMP → UMS → Solution intro → Future pacing using supported social proof, with the current SOP and evidence limits. Fashion emphasizes an attractive outfit and useful selection criteria; unsupported mechanisms or testimony are never invented. Preserve exact approved narration. The slots below are visual jobs: merge, repeat or move them only to follow the authorized script. An approved styling guide may identify its product before the examples; an opinion ad may show the product from frame one and name it later. Record visible reveal and spoken reveal independently.

## Keep and vary

**Defining grammar:** One deliberate guide identity and voice; separate editable picture/presenter/caption/audio tracks; background changes tied to meaning or action; imagery shows the specific styling/detail being discussed; varied B-roll with controlled product identity; aspiration before utility in fashion; current truthful destination/offer.

**Adaptable:** Single-action versus optional grid hook; early versus delayed target appearance; phone-like versus more polished natural fashion coverage; presenter corner/size; intentional presenter exit for a readable close; number of looks and duration of each; still/motion mix; optional detail crop; product-and-action or authentic page ending. Voice/provider/casting are resolved from the actual brief, not copied automatically from W01.

## Beat blueprint

Cues are semantic placeholders. Replace them with exact approved words/actions in the target plan. Dwell ranges are proposed seconds for the named shot/module, not source measurements or a total-runtime promise.

| Slot / communication job | Visible action and style | Placement; enter → leave / return | Proposed dwell; handoff | Why this scene and edit belong |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| G01 — Hook: establish immediate desire or a compelling styling proposition | A wearer already stepping with a striking complete look, or a legible grid of genuinely distinct approved style examples. Keep a clean region for the guide. **G-HOOK** | First frame with opening phrase. Leave when the proposition is understood and the next thought changes the viewing job. A delayed-reveal grid excludes the target until its designated cue. | Proposed 2–5s per opening visual; an approved longer hook may need additional distinct evidence rather than a static overfilled grid. Direct background cut; guide/voice continue. | The viewer can see the desired look immediately, making the spoken hook concrete. A grid signals range only when each outfit/product remains readable; action impact comes from desirability, not unrelated distress. |
| G02 — Problem/brief problem/agitation: make the fashion tension recognizable | A distinct dressing or outfit scene showing the relevant decision: a cuff adjustment, a knit beside jeans, or an actual authorized style comparison. **G-LOOK** | Enter on the specific familiar tension; stay through the visible action. Advance on the category alternative or guiding criterion, keeping the same narrator. | Proposed 2–5s per distinct action; protect the gesture and readable silhouette. Direct cut. | The tension stays connected to getting dressed and expressing taste. It does not turn an aspirational argument into exaggerated failure imagery or imply a fabricated customer history. |
| G03 — Solution category and dismissal: show the route and the remaining gap | A new considered wardrobe arrangement or dressing detail that illustrates the category choice while the guide explains its limitation. **G-RECAP** | Enter when the alternative becomes specific; exit on the phrase introducing better selection criteria. Omit as a separate picture if the approved short script combines these thoughts. | Proposed 2–5s; can share one useful continuous picture with the adjacent thought. No compulsory extra shot at a sentence boundary. | A concrete wardrobe example lets the viewer understand what is being considered. The creator supplies the interpretation; the image is not presented as proof that every alternative fails. |
| G04 — UMP to UMS: explain the relevant selection logic | A precise shape, texture or material relationship visible at the scale the line needs; compare silhouette to an outfit only when the copy calls for it. **G-DETAIL** | Enter on the exact criterion such as shape or texture. Hold through the relationship, not merely the noun. Move to product introduction when the spoken logic reaches it. | Proposed 2–4s per independently useful view; extend for comprehension. Direct scale change; optional background-only step crop if the source has detail. | The visual makes an ordinary fashion criterion inspectable. Do not invent a hidden scientific cause or use one generic macro for every claimed quality. |
| G05 — Named product introduction | An unobstructed whole-product view, or a deliberate hand touch showing the same selected item and color. **G-PRODUCT** | Enter on the exact product-name cue; this slot may move earlier for an approved styling guide. The target may already have appeared if its visual reveal is unrestricted. | Proposed 2–4s including enough recognition time. Direct cut, with stable guide and continuous voice. | A clean whole view binds the spoken name to the object before a chain of details or styling examples. This is not permission to introduce the target early in a delayed-reveal concept. |
| G06 — Feature: show the specific visible reason to want it | New close compositions of the actual named material, edge, hardware or handle action; maintain garment/person continuity if tied to one look. **G-DETAIL** | Enter on the feature phrase. Exit after that feature is inspectable and before the script resumes a whole-outfit consequence. Return to a relevant whole look, not automatically a full-screen face. | Proposed 1.5–4s per distinct feature; longer if action requires. Direct cuts; no decorative macro morph. | The close view supplies visible support for the specific descriptor. Unsupported interior, capacity or construction demonstrations remain asset/evidence gaps. |
| G07 — Styling demonstration: connect details to a complete look | A distinct full or three-quarter outfit with the product visibly carried; for a multi-look guide use different subjects/settings where required, then a matching accessory detail if spoken. **G-LOOK** | Enter on the outfit, city or use-context cue. A detail insert starts at its exact phrase and rejoins the complete look or next distinct example. Keep the guide continuous. | Proposed 3–7s per useful look; longer approved descriptions can use a separate relevant angle/action. Finish a pickup, turn or carry before leaving it. | The viewer sees what the product does for an outfit. Named locations organize examples; unrelated skyline B-roll would hide the styling the speaker is explaining. |
| G08 — Synthesis or reassurance: explain the common principle | Stable guide over a fresh quiet wardrobe/product composition, leaving room for a meaningful expression or single pointing gesture. **G-PRES** | Enter when the guide interprets the examples; hold across the connected thought. Leave on the concrete future-use moment. Full-screen presenter is optional only if deliberately planned. | Proposed 2–4s or the actual interpretive phrase. Direct background reset; no forced punch-in or hand-wave transition. | The guide links the distinct examples into a usable decision. A calm background prevents new visual information from competing with the summary; fresh arrangement avoids replaying prior B-roll. |
| G09 — Future pacing: show the desired life moment | A new location and action: arriving at lunch, adjusting a coat at a train door, or joining an occasion while wearing the styled product. **G-LIFE** | Enter on the exact imagined-use or supported experience phrase. Hold long enough to understand the setting and complete the action; exit on the actual invitation. | Proposed 3–5s, adjusted to action/voice. Direct cut, no mandatory dreamy dissolve or slow motion. | A specific event lets the viewer imagine enjoying the look. Label hypothetical scenes as such in the plan; synthetic people and reactions are not customer proof. |
| G10 — CTA and clean ending | Actual current product page with readable identity and terms, or a fresh accurate product composition plus one valid action. **G-PAGE** | Enter during the spoken action/destination. Hide or reposition the guide only to expose essential page content; the voice continues. Hold the result of any real scroll. | Proposed 3–6s including spoken CTA, with a trial 0.2–0.8s after the last word if already readable. Extend for actual reading need. Direct cut/end; no invented sale UI. | The viewer connects the item to the next action. Showing the destination during speech reduces unnecessary silent ending time while preserving comprehension. |

## Required asset roles

One guide identity/reference and separate clean presenter source; exact approved narration and provider voice selection; distinct hook and every covering scene/insert; first-frame image for each planned motion scene; selected product/color/revision references; current destination capture or truthful closing composition; separate caption/CTA layers; source/generation provenance; a single final alignment and optional keep/remove map. When storyboarding is requested, attach all selected first assets to the correct Cut Room cards and preserve separate reference evidence.

## Instantiate and hand off

Map every approved line or silent action to these slots; assign actual selected asset IDs or explicit gaps. Resolve the entry/exit/return cues and all separate picture, overlay, caption and audio events. Save the target editing plan before production; apply the shared contract for Cut Room and current model/editor rules.
