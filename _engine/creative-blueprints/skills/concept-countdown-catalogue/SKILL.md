# Countdown catalogue

Source: armful-of-bags hook, then ten individual black bags in 8.94s. There are ten product-change cuts and no separate end card. A spoken countdown is model-assisted evidence; visible ranking numbers are absent after the heading.

## Selection and visual script

Define the collection's actual basis: personal favorites, selected silhouettes, current colors or a supported ranking. Do not call items best-selling or rank by quality without evidence. An aesthetic selection can be presented honestly as favorites.

Open with immediately desirable range: abundance, a strong styling arrangement or the most striking silhouette. Establish what the viewer will see and how many items there are. Then show each actual item once, with enough time to distinguish shape, material and proportions. Choose an order that creates useful contrast: large/small, structured/slouchy, woven/smooth, long/short handle. Nearby similar products need a deliberately visible difference.

Maintain stable presenter, background, lighting and camera distance when it improves comparison. This continuity is purposeful; it does not justify repeating the same item or treating a crop as a new catalogue entry. Change the actual product/pose or informative detail. Preserve exact product design, color and scale relationships through approved references.

## Timing and edit

R08 gives the introduction about 1.47s, then each product roughly 0.67–0.83s. Use those only as observed calibration. Complex products, text labels or unfamiliar categories need more recognition time. A title, count and product image should not compete with a long explanation.

Cut directly at product change with a small clear presentation gesture. Synchronize the target spoken number, if used, to the real item transition after final audio alignment. Do not copy approximate model timestamps. A sound-only countdown can provide progression while the picture remains visually clean. Onscreen numbers are an explicit target choice, not a source observation.

Use music to sustain momentum; verify count intelligibility and actual beat alignment in the target. Avoid unnecessary wipes, zooms or repeated whooshes that make product comparisons harder. Decide whether the ending is the strongest item, a complete set, a short truthful CTA or a loop; do not append a long sales section by habit.

Deliver a compact editing plan listing item identity, distinct visual trait, order rationale, presentation gesture, duration, voice/count cue and ending. QA the exact item count, no accidental duplicates, correct voice order, readable silhouettes and honest selection criteria. No amount of rhythmic precision proves the products are ranked objectively or that the format improves conversion.
