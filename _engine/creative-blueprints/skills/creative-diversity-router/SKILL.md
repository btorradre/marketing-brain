## Selection workflow

1. Read the brief, avatar/VOC, approved copy, brand memory and actual asset inventory. State the desired viewer change in one sentence. Identify whether the request is research, planning, script revision, storyboard or production; do only the authorized scope.
2. Pick a primary family by that job. Read its source audit before borrowing a specific technique. A microphone does not automatically mean a podcast; off-camera narration does not prove AI; repeated stills do not prove stop-motion capture.
3. For a requested concept set, record each concept's speaker relationship, argument structure, visual world, picture-to-word relationship, pace curve, proof type and reveal/ending. Flag any pair that differs only in hook wording, clothing, color or voice. Change a substantive dimension and explain why it serves the brief. This is an editorial diversity check, not a performance score.
4. Choose at most the relevant specialist combination. A greenscreen explanation may have a product VO section, but define where and why the format changes. Do not mix every family into one ad or use the same B-roll formula across the set.
5. Save the editing plan before asset production. For each beat choose among literal demonstration, physical mechanism, comparison, sensory metaphor, testimony, reaction, future pacing and mood. State what the image can and cannot establish. Preserve deliberate presenter holds.
6. Record a testable hypothesis separately from observation: for example, “a material/action opening may increase qualified attention.” Measure hook rate and downstream results from actual campaign data; these references contain no verified performance evidence.

## Keep these distinctions

- **Script is not always speech.** A silent joke has setup/action/payoff; a craft film has material/process/ownership; a mood montage has associative progression. Analyze each as it exists.
- **New target copy follows the current copy SOP.** Preserve UMP before UMS and evidence limits; do not force invented roots, testimony or scientific language into a fashion film. See the contract for scope handling.
- **Visual change is not always a cut.** R11 follows accessories through continuous camera motion; R05 replaces overlays over a stable speaker; R07 cuts to listeners whose reactions carry meaning.
- **Pace is section-specific.** R03 slows for carry and brand; R06 protects the closet reveal; R02 resets to full speaker for reasons; R13 runs a rapid still sequence. Never flatten them into a universal two-second cut rule.
- **Learn from defects.** Reject R06 identity drift, R04 mismatched expression/late overlay and repetitive catalogue scenes, and similar repeated poses in R09. Source observation is not automatic permission to reproduce a weakness.

Deliver the chosen family, distinctiveness rationale, relevant source cues, evidence gaps and next authorized artifact. For analysis-only requests, do not generate assets or imply a Cut Room storyboard has been delivered.
