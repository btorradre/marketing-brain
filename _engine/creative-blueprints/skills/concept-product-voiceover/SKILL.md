# Product voiceover demonstration

Read the [shared contract](../creative-diversity-router/references/production-contract.md), [R09 creator-style audit](../../research/R09/analysis.md) and [R14 concise value audit](../../research/R14/analysis.md). These establish voiceover format, not synthetic voice provenance. Use broll-ai-voiceover-vsl for broader narrated coverage where relevant.

## Select the argument variant

**Creator endorsement:** R09 moves from claimed compliments → named garment → texture → better everyday styling → occasions → personal fit reference → desired identity → scarcity/availability. Use authentic wearer facts and testimony; do not assign a generated narrator personal experiences they did not have. Source compliments, body measurements and sellout history are not transferable claims.

**Concise product/value:** R14 contrasts comfortable feel with a polished look, names the fabric, demonstrates fit/styling, presents a price comparison and closes with action and brand cards. Its 17.58s duration leaves little room for digression. Verify material, price and comparison basis for the target.

For new target copy, follow the current copy SOP and evidence limits through the contract. Keep approved narration exact on visual revisions. Do not force a short approved format to contain fabricated UMP, UMS or social proof. Fashion aspiration leads the outcome; fit and function help explain why the look is desirable.

## Map every clause to an action

| Line function | Visible action and reason |
|---|---|
| Desired appearance | Show the finished silhouette immediately or complete a short dressing reveal; viewers see the outcome. |
| Named product | Present the actual item clearly enough to identify it. |
| Texture/material | Use a distinct close view, fold or hand movement to expose the visible surface. A macro cannot prove composition. |
| Fit/shape | Use a readable front, side or back view appropriate to the claim. Show the actual approved wearer/size context. |
| Styling versatility | Change the meaningful outfit or occasion, rather than changing crop on the same pose. R14 changes shorts/trousers. |
| Social response/availability | Treat words as claims requiring evidence; modeling footage does not prove compliments or stock status. |
| CTA | Keep the purchasable product identifiable; use a truthful readable offer/action if requested. |

Plan different subject details and actions across the entire ad. R09 uses frequent chest/sleeve touches and near-identical balcony compositions; borrow its texture-to-look progression while improving variety. A continuous voice bridges distinct useful actions, not arbitrary filler.

## Voice and edit

Choose recorded voice or authorized synthetic narration explicitly. Write/direct natural customer language, clear emphasis and breath, not dense technical exposition. No visible speaker needs lip sync when the voice is intentionally offscreen, but do not falsely imply the pictured wearer gave the testimonial.

Align cuts after final voice. Cut on a new semantic action or useful phase of dressing; let the narration continue across picture cuts. R09 has many sub-second modeling shots, whereas R14 gives longer recognition and two roughly 1.5s ending cards. Neither rhythm is universal. Favor direct cuts unless a transition explains a real time/state change.

R09 has no added captions; R14 uses restrained white serif phrases. Pick the target approach deliberately, check contrast over light garments and keep captions away from informative fabric/fit details. Music supports the spoken argument; assess the actual mix and intelligibility rather than copying unverified source settings.

Deliver exact narration, claim ledger, action-per-clause editing plan, first asset selections and target alignment when authorized. QA product geometry, wardrobe continuity, meaningful shot differences, word/caption sync, current offer and a complete readable finish.
