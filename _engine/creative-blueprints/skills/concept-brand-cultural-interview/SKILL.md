# Brand cultural interview

Read the [shared contract](../creative-diversity-router/references/production-contract.md) and [R07 evidence](../../research/R07/analysis.md). The source pairs two people discussing Paris recommendations and personal style; it does not turn every named place into an insert.

## Conversation architecture

Start from a brand-relevant cultural premise: a neighborhood, craft, shared interest or way of living. Choose speakers with actual perspectives. Write a compact question ladder from accessible recommendations toward a more personal belief or experience. In R07, place recommendations lead to a longer conversation about gray hair and confidence. This progression earns attention to the people beyond their information.

Keep authentic corrections, disagreement, laughter and small verbal detours that reveal the relationship. Do not rewrite actual answers into brand praise. A topic heading or question is an orientation device, not proof of expertise. Verify factual recommendations when the target presents them as current guidance.

## Visual and editing decisions

- Establish both people together. A shared frame makes the relationship and street context legible; costume consistency can provide understated brand presence.
- Change location or background at a genuine chapter boundary. R07's turquoise doorway marks a new topic; this is a structural reset, not arbitrary wallpaper.
- Choose face, two-shot or listener by the emotional function of the line. A listener can be the right picture while the other person continues speaking. Preserve eyelines and do not mistake that for a sync error.
- Keep a meaningful statement in a longer two-shot if shared attention matters. R07 includes a roughly 9.8-second hold during the personal hair discussion, despite faster exchanges elsewhere.
- Add B-roll only when the viewer needs visual information the conversation cannot supply. A restaurant name alone does not require stock food footage; reaction and banter may be the stronger subject.

Use simple cuts and conversational reframes. Distinguish visibly changed crop from proven digital zoom; source lens and scale settings are unknown. Put persistent topic labels in one zone and changing speech captions in another. Check that neither obscures expressions or hands/microphones. In source, uppercase white text identifies topic and speech, with independent timing.

## Voice, ending and QA

Direct curiosity and a listening relationship, not recited ad copy. Preserve breath and reaction time around humor or vulnerability. Prioritize dialogue over music and retain coherent ambience between locations. Specific source mix settings remain unverified.

End when the shared thought resolves. A brand tag or CTA may be appropriate for the target brief, but document it as an addition; R07 ends conversationally without a sales card. Generated adaptations require consistent speaker identity, mic positions, lip sync and natural listener behavior, and cannot be framed as real unsolicited endorsement.

Deliver a topic map, actual answer selections or clearly staged script, speaker/reaction coverage map, location chapter plan and reasons for each hold or cut. QA that the people remain the focus, dialogue meaning is intact, inserts have a communication job and brand affinity has not been mislabeled as verified conversion performance.

## Generalized blueprint and editing profile

For concept planning or an editing handoff, read the matching pair before mapping the target script. Keep observed reference calibration separate from proposed target timings. The blueprint defines the beat sequence; the profile defines coverage, cuts, transition exceptions, B-roll styles/placement, movement, captions, audio and ending.
