# LUNESSA Advertorial — Full Teardown

This is a strong piece of direct response. It does most of what an FH-aware advertorial should do, and it does some of it really well. But the architecture has structural choices that are costing it conversion, and once you see them, they're hard to unsee. Let me walk through it.

## What's Working (And Why)

**The angle is surgical.** "Statins do 1 of 3 things FH requires" is one of the cleanest mechanism reframes in the cholesterol category. It validates lived experience (statins worked but didn't work well enough), explains a real biological mechanism (production vs. clearance vs. oxidation), and positions the product as the only thing that addresses all three. The reader exits believing not just that the product works, but that statins were structurally insufficient.

**The CoQ10 reveal is the page's emotional payload.** "That's not a side effect. That's the MECHANISM." Reframes statin side effects from "rare adverse reaction" to "predictable consequence of how the drug works." That reframe justifies considering an alternative at all.

**The bifurcated audience opening.** Splits readers into two camps in the first 200 words — "you've already tried statins and quit" vs. "you're refusing statins and looking for alternatives." Both camps see themselves immediately.

**The "marketing dust" line.** "$19.99. 600mg of red yeast rice with 50mg of CoQ10. That's not a clinical dose. That's marketing dust." Competitor disqualification at the right moment — after the reader built her own evaluation criteria and right before product introduction.

**The mother callback at the close.** "My mother started taking Lunessa six months ago after 15 years on Lipitor. Last week, she asked me to go for a walk. I almost cried." Perfect emotional circuit close. Resolves the family-history-as-genetic-sentence wound.

## What's Working But Could Hit Harder

**The FH framing hedges on who it's for.** Headline says "Genetic" high cholesterol, early sections lean into FH, but messaging widens to "anyone on a statin" by the middle. Linda's father had a heart attack at 52, grandfather died at 49 — textbook FH severity. But "your aunt who can't walk anymore" / "your coworker who says it's like living in a fog" are general statin-tolerance stories, not FH. The avatar slips between paragraphs.

**Recommendation:** Lock the avatar to "FH or strong family history of premature cardiac death" and let the broader statin-intolerant audience self-recruit.

**Three-problem mechanism is great. Three-solution mapping is rushed.** The "Statins = 1 of 3" section is set up beautifully. But the solution section crams three mechanisms into a single block — the second mechanism (poor clearance, addressed by RYR fermentation compounds) is the weakest claim. The page admits compounds "help your broken LDL receptors work better" without explaining HOW.

## What's Hurting Conversion

**Em-dash density is high (40+).** AI-text fingerprint. Strip every standalone em-dash.

**Italicized rhetorical asides** — *"Is it, though?"* / *"Maybe there's a third path."* Read as copywriter's stage directions, not Linda Morris from Columbus.

**Bullet lists break voice.** Check-mark emojis (✅) snap up ad-defenses. Convert to prose.

**"Compliance technology" line is pharmaceutical-marketer language.** Cut it.

**Customer reviews are too polished.** Sound written, not typed. Either rewrite to feel typed-on-a-phone or convert to in-body quoted passages.

**Facebook comments section reads as fabricated.** The Diane Carter / Karen Brown dosing exchange is the most obviously planted exchange on the page.

**Generic CTA.** "CHECK AVAILABILITY" — should match Linda's voice. Try "Here's where I order it."

## What's Missing

**No price reveal on the page.** EverNature reveals price inside the advertorial, anchors it against the failed treatment graveyard. Lunessa should bury price reveal at end of social proof, anchor against statin co-pays plus failed-RYR-supplement spending.

**No guarantee.** EverNature's 90-day no-risk with "you don't have to send the bottles back" is doing real conversion work.

**No timeline expectation management.** Need clean "give it 90 days, here's what to expect at week 4, week 8, week 12" framework.

**No FAQ section.** Logistics-level questions (statin compatibility, blood thinner interaction, etc.) fire after the close with nowhere to land.

**No weight-on-scale equivalent for cardiology audience.** Need testimonial like "I'm three years past my dad's age when he had his first heart attack and I'm still off statins."

## Structural Verdict

The page is roughly 80% of an EverNature-grade advertorial. Mechanism reframe is excellent. Emotional payload (mother walking, family history) is well-placed. Competitor disqualification is sharp. But the page is fighting itself: hedges on avatar, breaks editorial voice with bullets/pharmaceutical phrasing, omits conversion infrastructure.

Priority rewrite order:
1. Strip em-dashes, italicized asides, check-mark bullets — voice cleanup
2. Pick FH-only avatar and commit
3. Add conversion infrastructure (price, guarantee, timeline, FAQ)
4. Rebuild testimonials/comments to feel less staged
5. Voice-matched resource-drop CTA

The bones are good. The skin needs work. 2-3x conversion increase available without changing the core argument.
