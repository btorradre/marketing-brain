## How the competitor (Nutrition Therapy) achieves simpler, more conversational language

Five mechanical choices, not vocabulary size:

1. **Shorter sentences at emotional beats. Longer sentences for thinking.** Emotional beats land in 4-9 words; reflection stretches to 13-14 words. Rhythm carries the reader.

2. **More interior monologue, less narration of events.** "I just stared at her." "That word stayed with me. Signal." The narrator's mind on the page.

3. **Visual/kitchen-table scene details as anchors, not props.** Six specific objects in 2,000 words; nothing decorative.

4. **One core analogy commits to the whole job.** Factory floor, power cut. That's it. Then plain prose. The user's version stacks three competing images (highway, fermentation tank, six feet upstream — which is actually river language, not highway language).

5. **Conjunction-led sentence openers.** "But," "So," "And," "Because" at sentence-start mimics speech. Nutrition Therapy uses ~1.5–2x more per 1,000 words than the user's version.

## Bottlenecks in user's version

**Bottleneck 1:** Mechanism section is front-loaded and teacherly. Highway → fermentation tank → six feet upstream → three ingredients all stacked back-to-back. ~400 uninterrupted lecture words. Fix: kill fermentation tank, replace "six feet upstream" with on-ramp/off-ramp language to reinforce the ONE metaphor.

**Bottleneck 2:** Three-ingredient sequence reads like a feature list. Compare: Claire sells ONE criterion (sublingual L-arginine delivery method) and lets it carry. User's Sarah teaches three ingredients (apigenin, chlorophyllin, soluble fiber), each gets surface treatment. Fix: lean into apigenin/prokinetic as the single criterion.

**Bottleneck 3:** "That word stayed with me" beats are missing. No interior-monologue moment after the highway analogy lands. Add: "On-ramp. I kept repeating it in my head. For eight months I'd been pouring fiber into an off-ramp that wasn't even the problem."

**Bottleneck 4:** Diane (sister) is thinner than the generational equivalent. Nutrition Therapy uses the narrator's OWN MOTHER — housebound, world shrinking, four walls. Generational stakes. User's Diane is lateral cautionary. Replace Diane with a generational frame (mom/aunt/grandmother whose body failed in her 60s, narrator now seeing the same in her 50s).

**Bottleneck 5:** Mild AI-pattern violations — "No cleanouts. No fiber feeding... No more treating..." (parallel-no-stack), "Stop following / Start supporting" (stop/start parallel close — also in competitor, watch).

## One-line summary
Competitor reads simpler because (a) one analogy not three, (b) more conjunction openers and interior monologue, (c) shorter emotional sentences vs longer reflective ones, (d) generational stakes vs lateral cautionary tale.

Priority unlocks: kill fermentation tank + upstream imagery (highway only); add 2-3 interior-monologue beats; replace Diane with generational inheritance.
