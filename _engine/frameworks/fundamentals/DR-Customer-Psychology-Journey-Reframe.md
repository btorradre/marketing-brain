# Customer Psychology, Journey & The Reframe

**Purpose:** The missing layer that sits *underneath* awareness, sophistication, and the 8-beat structure. Frameworks tell you *what* to do. Customer psychology tells you *why* it works. An ad executed at a high level — like the hair-loss ad broken down in the Looms — is a perfect blend of framework AND psychology.

**Source intelligence:** Brooks's Loom commentary + follow-up note on customer psychology, journey, visual congruence, and the reframe.

---

## The Core Principle

Frameworks without psychology produce technically correct ads that don't convert. Psychology without frameworks produces emotional ads that don't belief-shift. **Winning ads blend both.**

Ask at every moment: *what journey am I taking this person on?*

---

## The Journey: Nightmare → Dream

Every direct response ad is a **transport vehicle**. You pick up the viewer in their nightmare state and drop them off in their dream state. Every beat is a mile marker on that journey.

| State | What It Is | Where It Shows Up In The Ad |
|---|---|---|
| **Nightmare State** | The specific, visceral, daily reality of the problem | Problem Agitation beat |
| **False-Solution Landscape** | The stuff they've already tried and trusted, now exposed as broken | Solution Dismissal beat |
| **Moment of Reframe** | The instant the old mental model shatters | UMP beat |
| **Hope Installation** | The new mental model that promises a way out | UMS beat |
| **Vehicle Appearance** | The product arriving as the obvious carrier of that new model | Solution Introduction |
| **Future Self** | The viewer seeing themselves already transformed | Future-Basing in Objection Handling |
| **Dream State** | The identity/life they become | Close |

If the viewer cannot *feel* each of those states, the ad is flat. The job is emotional transport, not information transfer.

---

## Hook Mechanic: After-State → Before-State Juxtaposition

The hook in the hair-loss ad works because it doesn't open in the nightmare state. It opens in the **after-state** first, then juxtaposes backward:

> *"You should have seen her face at my follow-up appointment." (after-state)*
> *"Let me back up. Three years ago I noticed my part getting wider..." (before-state)*

**Why this works psychologically:**

1. The after-state line is a **curiosity loop** — what happened at the appointment? The viewer *must* keep watching.
2. The jump backward creates **narrative tension** — we know the ending is good, now we want to see how she got there.
3. It frames the viewer as being on the *path* the speaker already walked — the speaker is a guide who made it out.

**Operational rule:** a strong hook often starts at the *after*, then rewinds. The viewer rides the whole journey with a destination already implied.

## The Reframe Is The Entire Ad

The single most powerful moment in any DR ad is the **reframe** — the instant an old belief dies and a new one installs.

In the Loom example:

> **Old belief:** "It's stress."
> **Reframe:** "It's not stress — it's silica deficiency."
> **New belief:** "I have a specific, named, fixable deficiency."

That one line does more work than the other seven minutes of the ad combined. Everything before the reframe exists to *set it up*. Everything after exists to *cash it in*.

### What Makes a Reframe Powerful

- **It names the old frame explicitly** — you have to kill the old belief by name.
- **It replaces it with something specific** — vague reframes don't stick ("it's something deeper" fails; "it's silica" works).
- **It's novel to this awareness level** — the viewer cannot already believe the new frame, or it's not a reframe.
- **It creates relief + urgency simultaneously** — "I finally understand" + "I need to act."

### How To Build A Reframe

1. List the top 3 things the avatar currently believes is causing their problem.
2. Kill each one by name in Solution Dismissal.
3. Install the UMP as the real, specific, named cause.
4. Make it land in ONE sentence. Not a paragraph.

**The reframe is the UMP. The UMP is the reframe.** They are the same beat, viewed from two angles — strategy and psychology.

## Visual-Verbal Congruence

The script carries the belief shift. The visuals carry the *emotion* of the belief shift. When they diverge, the ad feels off even if the copy is perfect.

### The Congruence Rule

**Every visual frame must match the emotional state of the line being spoken.**

| Script Beat | Visual State |
|---|---|
| Hook | Close-up on face, subtle tension, after-state setting |
| Problem Agitation | Before-state footage, dim light, tight framing, hands on problem area |
| Solution Dismissal | B-roll of failed solutions (pill bottles, products, receipts), discarded/tossed |
| UMP | Direct-to-camera, controlled posture, calm authority — this is the reframe moment |
| UMS | Ingredient visual (not product yet), isolated, clean |
| Solution Introduction | First reveal of product, casual, held naturally |
| Objection Handling / Future-Basing | Life-with-solution footage, brighter light, wider framing, movement |
| Close | Direct-to-camera, warm, the dream-state identity visible |

**Diagnostic:** if you mute the ad and can still read the emotional journey, the visuals are congruent. If muted, it looks like a single mood the whole time, the visuals are failing the script.

## Customer Psychology Blended Into Each Principle

### Awareness Level × Psychology

Awareness is not just *information* — it's *emotional history*.

- **Problem Aware** — scared, confused, suspects something is wrong, hasn't been validated yet. Psychology need: *validation*.
- **Solution Aware** — frustrated, cynical, burned by failed attempts. Psychology need: *explanation*.
- **Product Aware** — skeptical, comparing, worried about being fooled again. Psychology need: *proof*.
- **Most Aware** — loyal or lapsed, identity-attached. Psychology need: *belonging*.

Match your tone to their emotional state, not just their information state.

### Sophistication × Psychology

Sophistication is not just *competition* — it's *trust exhaustion*.

- **Stage 1–2** — people still trust claims. Novelty works.
- **Stage 3** — people trust mechanisms, not claims. Explanation works.
- **Stage 4** — people trust stacks + justification. Combinatorial logic works.
- **Stage 5** — people trust identity + community. Belonging works.

The deeper the sophistication stage, the less you can sell the product and the more you must sell *who they become*.

## The Angle As Emotional Entry

The angle is not "what the ad is about." It's the **emotional door** the viewer walks through into the ad.

- Doctor Dismissal = the door of *being unheard*
- Failed Solutions = the door of *being cheated*
- Social Shame = the door of *being watched*
- Hidden Cause = the door of *being curious*
- Identity Reinforcement = the door of *belonging*

When you pick an angle, you're picking the emotion the viewer feels in the first three seconds. That emotion has to match the sub-avatar's current felt reality or they keep scrolling.

---

## The High-Level Execution Bar

An ad is operating at a high level when all of the following are true:

1. The hook juxtaposes after-state → before-state (or a comparable curiosity mechanic).
2. The problem is agitated with *specific, visceral, recognizable* details.
3. The reframe (UMP) kills the old belief by name and installs a new one in one sentence.
4. The UMS is a clean, specific mechanism paired to the UMP.
5. The product is introduced casually, as a discovery, not a pitch.
6. The future-basing paints the dream state in concrete weekly milestones.
7. The close returns to the angle and ends on identity.
8. The visuals track the emotional journey line-by-line.
9. The viewer finishes the ad *wanting the mechanism*, not being sold the product.
10. The whole thing feels like a guide walking them from nightmare to dream, not an ad selling a thing.

That is the bar.

---

## The One Question

At every moment of writing or reviewing an ad, ask:

**"What journey am I taking them on right now, and does the visual match the verbal?"**

If you can answer that with a specific emotional state, the ad is alive. If the answer is fuzzy, the ad is information and will not convert.
