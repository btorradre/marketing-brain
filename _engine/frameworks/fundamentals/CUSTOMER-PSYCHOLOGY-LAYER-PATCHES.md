```markdown
---

## II-B. THE CUSTOMER PSYCHOLOGY LAYER — NIGHTMARE STATE → DREAM STATE

Awareness levels and sophistication levels tell you WHERE the market is. They don't tell you who the reader is at 11pm when she's searching for this problem. Before writing, answer two questions the awareness grid doesn't answer:

**1. What does this reader's NIGHTMARE STATE actually look like?**
The nightmare state is not "has symptom X." It is the full lived texture of the problem — the moment she cried in her car, the dollar amount she's spent, the conversation that made her feel dismissed, the thing she stopped doing because of it, the identity erosion she won't say out loud, the way she talks to herself in the mirror. Nightmare state is a scene, not a symptom.

**2. What does her DREAM STATE actually look like?**
The dream state is not "no longer has symptom X." It is the specific restored life — the ponytail with weight again, the husband who notices, the mirror moment where she sees herself again, the validation from the person who dismissed her, the moment she helps someone else who's where she used to be. Dream state is also a scene.

**The copy's job is to carry the reader from the nightmare scene to the dream scene. The angle is the bridge.**

### The Nightmare → Reframe → Dream Architecture

Every long-form ad moves the reader through three emotional states:

1. **Nightmare State** — the reader's current lived experience, narrated in such precise detail that she feels KNOWN (not pitched to)
2. **Reframe Moment** — the angle does its work. The reader's mental model of the problem is replaced with a new one. ("It's not stress — it's silica deficiency.")
3. **Dream State** — the reader is shown a version of herself that lives on the other side of the reframe. Specific, scene-based, progressive.

**Awareness/sophistication levels modulate HOW this runs, not WHETHER it runs:**

- A **Problem-aware** reader needs maximum time in the nightmare state before the reframe — she has to feel known before she'll accept a new framework.
- A **Solution-aware** reader needs the reframe faster and the dream state richer — she's tired of the problem, not of hearing about it.
- A **Most-aware** reader needs the dream state extended with differentiation — she knows products in the category exist; she needs to see why THIS one delivers the dream state others failed to.

### The Angle Is the Reframe Bridge

The angle is the sentence that moves the reader from her current mental model (nightmare) to the new mental model the product answers (dream). One reframe example: *"It's not stress — it's silica deficiency."* That reframe flips everything:

- Before the reframe: "My hair is thinning because of stress. Nothing I try works."
- After the reframe: "My hair is thinning because I'm deficient in a mineral no one tests for. The solution is specific and findable."

The nightmare state doesn't change — her hair is still thinning. But the *meaning* of the nightmare state changed, and that's what opens the door to the dream state. **A product without a reframe angle can only offer marginal improvement on an existing mental model. A product with a reframe angle offers an exit from the mental model entirely.**

### Visual Correlation Law (applies even in written copy)

Every sentence in long-form copy should make the reader SEE something. Not because you're writing a video — because belief shifts happen through sensory-specific scenes, not abstractions.

- Weak: "I'd tried so many things nothing worked."
- Strong: "Biotin for eight months. Collagen peptides at $80 a month. Viviscal. Nutrafol at $79 a month for a full year. Over $4,000 on supplements that didn't work."

The second version is visual. The reader SEES the bathroom shelf. That's nightmare-state visualization in prose.

**Test every major beat:** Can the reader draw this moment? If not, the beat is operating on the wrong layer. Rewrite until the reader has a picture in her head for the nightmare scene, the reframe moment, and the dream-state arrival.

### Nightmare Deposits, Dream-State Withdrawals

Every nightmare-state detail you drop early must be paid back in dream-state form later. If you named the hairbrush wrapped in hair in Act 1, you owe the reader a hairbrush that comes out clean in Act 3. If you named the parking-lot cry in Act 1, you owe the reader that same parking lot with different posture later. If you named the $4,000 failed-attempt graveyard early, you owe the reader a $39 price reframe late.

**Unredeemed nightmare details = emotional debt.** The reader feels the story left her in the dark.

### The Pre-Writing Customer Psychology Audit

Before writing any long-form copy, write down:

1. **The nightmare scene** (one paragraph of specific lived detail — the moment, the objects, the self-talk, the cost)
2. **The reframe** (one sentence — the angle that replaces the reader's current mental model)
3. **The dream scene** (one paragraph of specific restored life — the moment, the objects, the new self-talk, the proof)
4. **The nightmare→dream callbacks** (3–5 specific details you'll deposit in nightmare state and withdraw in dream-state form)

If you can't write these four specifically, you don't yet know the customer well enough to write the copy. Go back to avatar-research or desire-angle-concept before continuing.

---
```

## PATCH 2 — video-ad-scripts/SKILL.md

**Where to insert:** Immediately before `## Belief-Shift Sequencing` (currently line 147). The new section sits between "Spoken Cadence Laws" and "Belief-Shift Sequencing".

```markdown
---

## The Customer Psychology Layer — Nightmare → Dream on the Visual Track

Video scripts run on two tracks simultaneously: **what's being SAID** and **what's being SHOWN**. If the visual track doesn't carry the viewer from nightmare state to dream state alongside the spoken track, the script is operating at half power.

Awareness level tells you where the viewer is in the market. It doesn't tell you what she's living. Before you write, answer:

1. **What is her NIGHTMARE STATE scene?** The exact moment of the problem — parking lot tears, 2 a.m. phone scrolling, mirror moments she avoids, the conversation that made her feel dismissed.
2. **What is her DREAM STATE scene?** The exact moment of restored life — the body evidence, the social validation, the identity return.
3. **What is the REFRAME (angle) that bridges them?** One sentence that replaces her current mental model with a new one.

The script moves her from nightmare to dream. The angle is the pivot. The visual track is the receipts.

### The Visual Correlation Law

Every spoken sentence in a video script must have a matching visual that either (a) deepens the nightmare state, (b) visualizes the reframe, or (c) delivers dream-state evidence. If a visual is decorative rather than correlated, it's burning attention.

- Audio: "I cried in my car for 20 minutes." → Visual: POV from driver's seat, slightly desaturated.
- Audio: "It's not stress — it's silica deficiency." → Visual: text overlay of the reframe landing on beat.
- Audio: "Week four, I saw baby hairs at my temples." → Visual: extreme close-up of temple, fingers pointing.

**Audio tells. Visuals prove.** A reframe that is only heard is a claim. A reframe that is also seen (text overlay + evidence shot + mechanism diagram) is a belief.

### The Nightmare→Dream Visual Callback System

Every nightmare-state visual is a deposit. Every dream-state visual is a withdrawal.

- Nightmare shot: hairbrush wrapped in hair → Dream shot: hairbrush with fewer hairs (same object, inverted meaning)
- Nightmare shot: parking-lot cry → Dream shot: parking-lot standing tall (same location, new posture)
- Nightmare shot: $4,000 bathroom shelf → Dream shot: one $39 bottle (same visual frame, different cost)
- Nightmare shot: dismissive doctor pushing papers aside → Dream shot: doctor taking notes, asking questions

**Rule:** Plan your visual callbacks BEFORE writing the script. Identify 3–5 nightmare-state objects, locations, or gestures. Plan their dream-state counterparts. Then write dialogue that lands on each of those shots.

### Dream-State Cold Open (The Hook Inversion)

The strongest video hooks don't open in the nightmare state. They open in the DREAM state, then juxtapose to the nightmare state, then carry the viewer back to dream state over the course of the ad.

Opening in dream state: *"When I showed my dermatologist the research, she actually rolled her eyes at me. You should have seen her face at my follow-up appointment."*

The viewer sees a confident avatar. She doesn't know why this person is confident yet — but she knows the person *made it through something*. That "made it through" energy is the hook. The nightmare-state re-entry comes at 00:09 ("Let me back up") and the viewer follows willingly, because she's been promised a destination.

**Weak hook pattern:** Open in the nightmare state ("I was losing my hair and I was desperate…"). The viewer has nothing to hope for yet. She has no reason to sit through the pain.

**Strong hook pattern:** Open in dream state → juxtapose to nightmare → earn the return to dream. This is the same structural law as the *hook-congruence* skill, applied to the full visual arc.

### Progressive Dream-State Beats

Dream state is delivered in small visual beats, not a single transformation montage. The viewer needs TIME to project herself into the dream state. One frame per time-marker:

- Week 1: one small body evidence beat
- Week 2: a second, slightly bigger beat
- Week 4: social validation beat (someone notices)
- Week 8: identity return beat (avatar sees herself)
- Post-product: authority validation beat (the dismissive authority now asks)

Each beat is its own shot. The viewer uses each shot to imagine her own week-by-week arc.

### Pre-Writing Customer Psychology Audit (for video)

Before writing a video script, document:

1. **Nightmare scene** — specific moment, objects, self-talk, cost
2. **Reframe** — one sentence that flips the mental model
3. **Dream scene** — specific moment, objects, new self-talk, proof
4. **Visual callback pairs** — 3–5 nightmare-state shots + their dream-state counterparts
5. **Progressive dream beats** — the time-marker sequence (Week 1, 2, 4, 8…) and what is visualized at each beat

If you can't specify these, the script will feel generic even if every line is well-written.

---
```

- [ ] Progressive dream-state time-marker beats mapped
- [ ] Every spoken sentence has a correlated visual (not decorative)

## PATCH 3 — ad-assessment/SKILL.md

**Where to insert:** As a new assessment dimension. Add as **Dimension 6** after `## Dimension 5: Structural Engineering (15 points)` (currently around line 264), and rebalance total to 110 points OR rescale dimension weights. Recommendation: keep current 100-point base, add Dimension 6 as a **+10 / -10 modifier**.

```markdown
---

## Dimension 6: Customer Psychology Layer (Modifier: +10 / -10)

This dimension modifies the base score. The copy is evaluated against whether it carries the reader from nightmare state through a reframe to a dream state, and whether nightmare-state details are redeemed in dream-state form.

### 6.1 Nightmare State Specificity (0–4 points)

**Test:** Is the reader's nightmare state rendered as a specific lived scene (moment, objects, self-talk, cost) rather than a symptom list?

- **0** = Nightmare described in generalities ("I was struggling," "nothing was working").
- **1** = One mildly specific nightmare detail; the rest is abstract.
- **2** = Nightmare has several specific details but no anchoring scene (no moment, no objects).
- **3** = At least one anchoring nightmare scene with objects and self-talk, plus supporting specific details. Minor abstractions remain.
- **4** = Nightmare is rendered as a full sensory scene the reader can draw. Specific moment, specific objects, specific internal dialogue, specific cost. The reader would screenshot this.

### 6.2 Reframe Clarity (0–3 points)

**Test:** Can you identify a single sentence (the angle) that flips the reader's mental model of the problem? Is it delivered as a genuine reframe, not a benefit claim?

- **0** = No identifiable reframe. Copy presents product as marginal improvement on existing mental model.
- **1** = A reframe is implied but never explicitly stated. Reader has to assemble it.
- **2** = Reframe is present but phrased as a benefit ("X works better than Y") rather than a mental-model flip.
- **3** = One sentence in the copy explicitly replaces the reader's current mental model with a new one. ("It's not X — it's Y.") The reframe is the pivot of the entire piece.

### 6.3 Dream State Specificity (0–3 points)

**Test:** Is the dream state rendered as specific restored-life scenes (body evidence, social validation, identity return) rather than as benefit claims?

- **0** = Dream state is claimed, not scened ("you'll feel amazing").
- **1** = One general dream-state claim; no scenes.
- **2** = Dream state includes some specific evidence but in a single burst, not progressive.
- **3** = Dream state is delivered through progressive, time-marked, scene-based beats. Each beat has objects, body evidence, and/or social validation.

### 6.4 Nightmare→Dream Callback Integrity (−10 modifier if failed; +0 if passed)

**Test:** Are the specific nightmare-state details (objects, locations, amounts, people) deposited early withdrawn later in dream-state form?

- **FAIL (−10 to total score):** Major nightmare details are deposited and never redeemed. The copy leaves the reader with unresolved emotional debt.
- **PASS (no modifier):** At least 3 nightmare-state details are redeemed in dream-state form (same object transformed, same location with new posture, price reframe against failed-attempt graveyard, etc.).

### Dimension 6 Calculation

- Sum sub-scores 6.1 + 6.2 + 6.3 = 0–10 points. Add to base score as a bonus.
- Apply 6.4 as −10 modifier if callback integrity fails.
- Maximum impact: +10 (strong psychology layer) to −10 (deposited nightmare, no redemption).

### Dimension 6 Hard Caps

- If there is no identifiable reframe sentence, overall score is capped at 7.0 regardless of other dimensions. A piece of copy without a reframe is selling on features, not shifting beliefs.
- If the nightmare state is rendered only in generalities (6.1 score of 0 or 1), overall score is capped at 7.5. Without a specific nightmare scene, the reader never feels known.
```

**Also add to the Assessment Commandments:**
```markdown
- **Identify the reframe before scoring.** If you cannot quote the one sentence that flips the reader's mental model, the copy has no angle — only benefit claims.
- **Check the callback ledger.** Write down every specific nightmare detail. Check that each one returns in dream-state form. Unreturned deposits = emotional debt = deduction.
- **Awareness level is not customer psychology.** A piece of copy that perfectly matches awareness level can still fail to render the nightmare scene, deliver a reframe, or scene the dream state. Score the psychology layer independently.
```

## What Was Added and Why

**Across all three skills, this patch installs three ideas:**

1. **Nightmare State → Reframe → Dream State** is now a core structural lens, alongside awareness/sophistication levels. It answers the customer-psychology question awareness levels don't: *who is this reader when she's alone with the problem, and where is she trying to go?*

2. **The angle is a reframe bridge**, not a benefit claim. The angle is what flips the reader's mental model of the problem. ("It's not stress — it's silica deficiency.") Without that flip, the product can only compete on features inside the reader's existing model.

3. **Nightmare deposits require dream-state withdrawals.** Every specific nightmare detail named early owes the reader a dream-state callback later. This is true in written copy (the reader replays the details), in video scripts (the visual callback system), and in assessment (the callback ledger).

These ideas are consistent with existing skill frameworks — they sit underneath the belief-shift architecture, alongside the pacing laws, and work through the narrator system. They add a customer-psychology diagnostic layer that makes the existing frameworks more executable.
