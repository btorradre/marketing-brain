# Direct Response Fundamentals — Loom 1 Breakdown

**Source:** Loom recording — "Direct Response Marketing Fundamentals" module
**Link:** https://www.loom.com/share/f4b372d5e1f94b80ba535ee9590432b2
**Purpose:** Captures the foundational mental model for how direct response works and how every principle connects to creative execution.

---

## Core Definition

Direct response is the discipline of leveraging what a customer *currently believes* — their trust, their assumptions, their existing framing — and then *shifting those beliefs* through an ad so they arrive at the buying decision on their own.

Every principle below serves that one job: belief shift.

---

## The Four Principles That Govern Every Ad

1. **Avatar** — who you are selling to
2. **Awareness Level** — how aware the avatar is of active solutions
3. **Market Sophistication** — how you differentiate in a crowded market
4. **Desire / Identification / Angle / Concept** — how you enter the avatar's mind

These four sit on top of each other. You cannot pick an angle until you know awareness. You cannot pick awareness until you know the avatar. You cannot pick a concept until the angle is locked. The order is the operating system.

---

## 1. Avatar

The avatar is the specific group you are selling to — narrow enough that one message lands.

Example used in the Loom: **GLP-1 users suffering from digestive side effects.** That is an avatar. "People who want to lose weight" is not.

Inside every avatar sit **sub-avatars** — smaller pockets with different entry points. Each sub-avatar gets its own angle.

---

## 2. Awareness Levels (The Funnel)

Awareness measures how much the avatar knows about active solutions. It maps directly onto a funnel from cold to warm.

| Level | Definition | Example (GLP-1 digestive avatar) |
|---|---|---|
| **Problem Aware (PA)** | Only aware of the problem. Has never tried a solution. | Just started GLP-1, starts feeling bloated/constipated, doesn't know what to do. |
| **Solution Aware (SA)** | Has tried other solutions in the category. | Has tried Miralax, stool softeners, fiber. |
| **Product Aware (PA)** | Has tried a product like ours. | Has tried a celery juice gummy before. |
| **Most Aware (MA)** | Has bought from us, maybe on subscription. | Existing customer. |

**Top of funnel is always the largest pool** (Brooks's example: 100M problem aware, 50M solution aware, 10M product aware). Fewer people exist at each deeper level because education has to happen to move them down.

### Awareness → Creative Rule

**Every ad takes the viewer from their current awareness level all the way to most aware.**

That is why problem-aware ads run long (4+ minutes in the Loom example). You start at the problem, agitate it, dismiss solutions, educate the mechanism, introduce the product. That is a longer belief-shift journey than a solution-aware ad that can skip the problem-agitation stage.

**Operational takeaway:** if you know the awareness level, you know the minimum length the ad needs to be.

---

## 3. Market Sophistication (5 Stages)

Sophistication measures what claims the market will *believe*. It is set by competitors, not by you.

| Stage | State of Market | What Works |
|---|---|---|
| **Stage 1** | First mover. No competitors. | Say anything. "Lose weight." Believed. |
| **Stage 2** | Early competitors. Claims escalate. | Add specificity. "Lose weight in 2 weeks." |
| **Stage 3** | All basic claims exhausted. | **Introduce a new mechanism.** (e.g. celery juice into a category of Miralax/stool softeners). |
| **Stage 4** | Mechanisms exhausted. | **Stack / combine mechanisms.** (e.g. celery juice + fiber + X). |
| **Stage 5** | Everything has been tried. | **Sell identity.** Who will you *become* by using this? (Nike running shoes.) |

Brooks's note: **most markets sit in Stage 3 or Stage 4.** That is why most winning ads are mechanism-led — you are introducing or stacking a new mechanism to differentiate.

### Sophistication → Creative Rule

Sophistication dictates *differentiation strategy*. Stage 3 ads need mechanism education. Stage 5 ads need identity / transformation selling. Get this wrong and the ad feels off even if the copy is good.

---

## 4. Desire, Identification, Angle, Concept

These are the four layers that translate avatar + awareness + sophistication into a scroll-stopping ad.

### Desire

- Cannot be created, only channeled. It already exists in the avatar.
- It is the **outcome they want**.
- Example: "the desire to not be constipated," "the desire to feel comfortable again."

### Identification

- Who the avatar **wants to become** as a result of getting the desire.
- Example (from not-constipated desire): "comfortable in social settings," "finally comfortable in front of their boyfriend."
- This is the transformation, the identity.

### Angle

- The **psychological point of entry**. What you *say* to make them care.
- The big idea behind the ad.
- Each angle targets one sub-avatar.
- Example angles from the Loom:
  - **Problem-education angle** — edgy/educational framing for a logical buyer.
  - **Failed-solutions angle** — "you've tried everything, here's why nothing worked."
  - **Social-shame angle** — appealing to one specific humiliating/anxious moment in their life.
  - **Doctor-dismissal angle** — "your doctor told you it's just stress, here's the real cause" (the example ad in the Loom).

### Concept

- **How you say** the angle. The creative format.
- Examples: UGC, AI UGC, authority/expert, founder story, testimonial, native image static.
- The concept wraps the angle in a deliverable format.

---

## The Chain of Decisions (Operating Order)

```
Avatar
  → Sub-avatar
    → Awareness Level  → determines ad length + education required
      → Market Sophistication  → determines differentiation strategy
        → Desire  → Identification
          → Angle (psychological point of entry for this sub-avatar)
            → Concept (format: UGC / static / authority / etc.)
              → Script / Creative
```

If an ad feels "off," walk backward up this chain. Nine times out of ten the break is at awareness or sophistication, not at the copy level.

---

## The Example Ad Walkthrough (Doctor Dismissal)

Brooks applies the framework to a real ad:

- **Avatar:** women with hair loss who've seen doctors and been dismissed.
- **Awareness:** **problem-aware** → hook opens with the problem state, no solution mentioned.
- **Sophistication:** **stage 3–4** → requires mechanism education, requires trust-building, hence the 4-minute length.
- **Angle:** **doctor dismissal** — "the doctor told her it's just stress" — that emotional point of entry is what makes the avatar lean in.
- **Core belief shift:** "it's not stress, here's the real root cause."

The length of the ad is not arbitrary. It's dictated by the awareness level (problem-aware = full journey) and the sophistication stage (3–4 = mechanism education required).

## What To Do With This File

Use this as the pre-write checklist before any creative brief:

1. Who is the sub-avatar? (one pocket, not the whole market)
2. What awareness level are we entering at?
3. What sophistication stage is the market in?
4. What is the desire? What is the identification?
5. What is the angle (psychological entry point)?
6. What is the concept (format wrapper)?

If any of those six are unclear, don't write yet. Go back to avatar research.
