## Slot 5 — Proof / Results (full cards)

How belief is earned after the product enters. You may stack 1-3 of these (unlike other slots), but lead with one primary. Pick in service of the avatar's dominant doubt.

### PR1. Progressive, Life-Measured Results
- **What:** results shown as returned LIFE, not metrics. "By week three I slept through the night" beats "reduced by 34%."
- **Use:** always viable; the default spine. Strongest for problem-aware emotional angles.
- **Directive:** measure in moments the avatar recognizes (a meal without fear, a walk without stopping). Progressive timeline, not instant miracle.

### PR2. Doctor / Authority Validation
- **What:** a credible third party confirms it works/is safe.
- **Use:** high-skepticism categories, safety-sensitive avatars (50+, medicated).
- **Directive:** one validation beat, woven into the story — not a credential dump.

### PR3. Second-Person Proof Loop
- **What:** turns to the reader: "if you've felt X, then you already know Y is possible for you."
- **Use:** mid-to-late body, to transfer the narrator's result onto the reader.
- **Directive:** optional but powerful for conversion. Don't overuse — one loop.

### PR4. Social-Proof Narrative
- **What:** others' results shown as story fragments, not a testimonial wall.
- **Use:** crowded categories; product-aware readers who want to see "people like me."
- **Directive:** specificity = credibility. Named, situational, sensory — never "5 stars, highly recommend."

**Selection by dominant doubt:**
- "Will it work for ME?" → PR1 + PR3
- "Is it safe / legit?" → PR2
- "Does it actually work for real people?" → PR4
- Skeptical + medicated 50+ → PR2 + PR1

**Law:** results pace progressively and are measured in life, not lab numbers. Proof rides inside the narrative.

## Slot 6 — CTA Type (full detail)

The CTA is decided by where the ad sends traffic, then dressed in a close style. Two picks: destination (drives the ASK) × close style (drives the EMOTION of the ask).

### A. Destination → the ask (funnel-aware)

The CTA must match the next step in the funnel — never ask for the sale the page isn't built to take.

| Destination | The ask | Example CTA language | Tone |
|---|---|---|---|
| Advertorial | keep reading / learn the cause | "Read the full breakdown of why this happens →" · "Here's the article that explains it →" | curiosity, soft |
| Listicle | see the list | "See all 7 reasons most people get this wrong →" | curiosity, enumerated |
| PDP / product page | buy / claim | "Claim yours while it's back in stock →" · "Try it for 90 days →" | direct, ownership |
| Presell / quiz | self-qualify | "Take the 60-second quiz to see if this is your issue →" | participation |
| VSL landing | watch / continue | "Watch how she did it →" · "Continue to the full method →" | momentum |

**Rule:** an advertorial/listicle CTA NEVER says "buy now" — it sends the reader deeper into the funnel ("read this to learn more"). Only PDP-bound copy asks for the purchase. Match the ask to the page.

### B. Close style → the emotion of the ask

- **CL1. Emotional Circuit** — resolves the open loop the lead opened. (default — pairs with any destination)
- **CL2. Identity Challenge** — "the kind of person who…"
- **CL3. Binary Choice** — two futures, pick one.
- **CL4. The Messenger** — "I'm just telling you what worked. Do with it what you will."
- **CL5. Quiet Exit** — understated, no hard push. (pairs with advertorial)
- **CL6. Doctor's Last Word** — authority closes. (pairs with Credential/Authority concepts)
- **CL7. Life Image** — paints the restored life and stops.
- **CL8. Permission Close** — gives the reader permission to want the fix.
- **CL9. Instruction Close** — tells them exactly what to do next. (pairs with PDP)

### Pairing by destination (recommended defaults)
- Advertorial → ask: "read more" · close: Quiet Exit or Emotional Circuit
- Listicle → ask: "see the list" · close: Emotional Circuit
- PDP → ask: "claim/try" · close: Instruction or Binary Choice
- Presell/quiz → ask: "take the quiz" · close: Permission or Identity Challenge
- VSL → ask: "watch/continue" · close: Emotional Circuit or Life Image

**Law:** no P.S. stacks, no detached CTA block. The close is the final beat of the SAME voice — the ask emerges from the story, it doesn't get bolted on.

## Slot 7 — Length / Ratio (full detail)

The pacing envelope. Length is EARNED, never padded. Pick a ratio and a target word band; hold to it while writing.

### Ratios
- **R1. Story-Dominant** — narrative carries; mechanism is light, woven into scenes. Best for problem-aware emotional angles, Transformation/Witness concepts.
- **R2. Mechanism-Dominant** — the "why it happens" is the spine; story frames it. Best for solution-aware, Investigative/Credential concepts, skeptical avatars.
- **R3. Balanced** — alternating story beats and mechanism beats. Default when unsure.

### Target bands (words)

| Band | Words | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| Short | 500–900 | product-aware/most-aware, PDP-bound, warm traffic |
| Standard | 900–1,800 | most ads; problem/solution-aware cold traffic |
| Long | 1,800–3,000 | high skepticism, heavy mechanism education |
| Ultra | 3,000+ | only when the belief shift genuinely requires it (deeply skeptical, high-ticket, or multi-villain teardown) |

### Defaults by awareness
- Unaware → R3 Balanced, Long (education-heavy)
- Problem-aware → R1 Story-Dominant, Standard
- Solution-aware → R2 Mechanism-Dominant, Standard–Long
- Product-aware → R3 Balanced, Short–Standard
- Most-aware → R1/R3, Short

**Law:** what scales with length is DEPTH of scene and mechanism, not number of points. Every sentence carries forward motion — no sentence exists to fill a word count.
