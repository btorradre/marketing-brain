# DR Market Intel

Two jobs, one discipline pointed in opposite directions: read an ad for the strategy underneath it, and turn that into an instruction for the next brief.

## Golden nugget doctrine

Before any verdict is produced, name the **golden nugget**: the single most emotionally loaded deep frame in the research — the real motive that makes buyers act — never the surface theme.

Topic is not motive. "Memory loss" is a topic; the frame underneath might be "I thought I was getting dementia just like my mother did, until I found this." A surface angle buys mild curiosity. A deep frame makes the reader feel caught. For every candidate finding, ask: is this the topic, or is this the motive? If it's the topic, dig one layer deeper. State the golden nugget in one explicit sentence before drafting. When analyzing a reference ad or competitor set instead of writing something new, state the nugget it's built on and whether it leads with it or buries it.

## Pick the mode

| Situation | Mode |
|---|---|
| Studying other brands, hunting white space, entering a category | **Competitor mode** |
| One of the brand's own ads is working and the learning needs capturing | **Winner mode** |
| The market is sophisticated and the account needs fresh, credible news to sell with | **New-information mode** |
| The product needs a "reason why" that restores hope in a burned-out category | **New-mechanism mode** |
| Both — a category sweep plus the brand's own top performer | Run competitor first, then winner. The category map tells you whether the winner is winning because of the angle or in spite of it. |

## Step 1 — Load brand context

Before running either protocol, read the brand's existing research, house voice and brand rules, and any prior swipe files or competitor/winner write-ups. Read the voice-of-customer research in particular — a category gap that no customer has ever expressed a desire for is not an opportunity. Check what has already been analyzed so the same ad isn't torn down twice.

## Step 2 — Run the chosen protocol

Both protocols expect real, cited ads pulled from an actual ad library, brand-tracking tool, or manual review of competitors' live accounts — never analyze an ad from a description of the category or from memory.

**Competitor protocol** — study the angle rather than the execution, treat 90+ days of running time as proof, and never let the output slide into a recommendation to copy anything. For the full section-by-section output spec (category angle map, category patterns, gap analysis, differentiation strategy), see `references/competitor-protocol.md`.

**Winner protocol** — run it while the ad is still spending, not after it dies. Separate the hook (the opening move) from the angle (the whole idea the ad is built around) — conflating them is why iteration fails. Every finding has to end as an instruction, not an observation. For the full section-by-section output spec (structural diagnosis, psychological mechanics, language analysis, transferable framework, iteration roadmap), see `references/winner-protocol.md`.

## Step 3 — New-information and new-mechanism modes

Run one of these when an awareness/sophistication read says the market has seen every standard promise: a Stage 3 market needs a unique mechanism, Stage 4 needs new discoveries, Stage 5 needs full repositioning.

**New-information mode.** Hunt genuinely new, credible material from the last 12-24 months that competitors haven't weaponized yet: peer-reviewed studies, regulatory changes, emerging use cases, demographic shifts. Deliver each finding as: source with publication and date → why it's new (the sophistication gap it closes) → how to use it ethically (educate, never fear-monger) → 3 curiosity-piquing hook samples.

**New-mechanism mode.** Identify 3-5 mechanisms genuinely present in the product — delivery method, an uncommon ingredient or process, a contrarian approach, an improved combination — that can be positioned as "the reason why" it works when everything else failed. The single job of a mechanism is to give the buyer new hope, not to sound sophisticated.

Hard rails on both modes:

- **No fabricated citations.** Every study, sample size, percentage, and expert must be real and verifiable before it ships. A finding that cannot be sourced is not a finding.
- **Mechanism is an ingredient inside a hook, never its own layer.** These modes feed existing angles with new hooks, or reveal a new problem worth its own angle. They do not create standalone "mechanism angles."
- **Mechanism claims must be true of the actual product.** No invented processes, no unverifiable origin or manufacturing stories.
- Record every finding under its own heading with source and date, and run it through the translation step below before it reaches a brief.

## Step 4 — Translate before handing off

This is the step that keeps competitor intelligence from leaking into competitor copy, and it's the step most likely to be skipped. Every gap and every finding leaves this process as a **positive claim built from the brand's own facts**, never as a comparison.

| Raw finding | Ships as |
|---|---|
| "Competitors all hide that the material frays" | The brand's own weave, hardware, and construction facts, stated plainly |
| "Nobody in the category shows the interior" | An ad built on the brand's own lining, pockets, and what actually fits |
| "Everyone is at product-aware, nobody is problem-aware" | A top-of-funnel brief written at the starved awareness level |

The mechanism is always the same: lead with a verdict, then stack the brand's own physical specifics. Those specifics become the criteria the buyer shops with, which is how a brand wins without ever naming a comparison.

Anything that cannot survive the translation still gets recorded — flagged as a competitor-comparison risk. It stays as intelligence and never becomes a brief.

## Step 5 — Write and hand off

Save competitor-mode output as a standalone market-gaps document. Save winner-mode output per asset, and update the source angle record (if one exists) with the asset's ID and the verdict — winner, flat, or loser.

Self-audit before presenting: every ad cited with an identifier and a date, every gap tested against real customer research and the swap test (below), no finding that reads as comparison copy, no invented running time or performance number.

Close with two lines: the single most defensible position found, and the recommended next step — usually turning the gaps into angle records ready to be hooked and briefed, or a full funnel-strategy rebuild if the finding is structural rather than about any one angle.

## Rules & standards

### The core gate, restated

Everything produced by this process is input. None of it is copy. Competitor intelligence may never become an ad that mentions, implies, or price-compares against another brand. Translate every gap into a positive claim built from the brand's own facts before it's used, and flag anything that can't survive that translation.

The one carve-out is a format that IS a comparison by design (a ranked roundup, "I tested five products"). Inside that format: no brand-specific product is denigrated by name, every competitor is treated as genuinely excellent and given a trade-off rather than a defect, and no competitor price is quoted unless it was verified on that brand's own site. No villain and no manufactured problem — the avatar isn't wounded, she's looking for a good product, and any line that would make a satisfied competitor customer feel stupid is the wrong line.

### House laws referenced throughout

- **No fabricated citations.** Every study, sample size, percentage, and doctor must be real and verifiable.
- **The swap test.** If a competitor's product could drop into the claim and it still reads perfectly, rewrite it onto the brand's own facts — the finding is selling the category, not the brand.
- **The six-month test.** No gap or angle whose relevance dies with a season or a moment.
- **Voice-tell check.** No em dashes, no "not X, it's Y," no parallel repetition stacks, on any line that ships as copy.
