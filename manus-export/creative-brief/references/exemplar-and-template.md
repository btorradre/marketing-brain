## Worked example — a full brief in this format

The following is a complete, real example, included as a reference for exactly how dense/sparse each section should be and what "good" looks like in practice.

# DCT 402 | The Drawer Full of Started Routines

**Sub-avatar:** Food-decision-fatigue mom with a short, selective eater who is comparing calcium chews and other height gummies.

**The One Angle:** Turn the bottle around and compare what the ingredients and daily directions actually say.

**Format:** Long-form AI mom UGC-style explainer with a label-led research journey, delayed product reveal, and handheld packshot.

**Facebook Page:** Laura Bennett.

**Landing Page:** Us vs Them PDP (HOT)
https://trynovexahealth.com/products/novexa-health™-5-in-1-growth-gummies-copy-1

**Inspiration:** https://fb.me/2ll4dNoal1N6zrt

---

## 1. Concept

### WHY

She has already dealt with supplement formats becoming part of the daily routine and sometimes turning into a battle. This audience also includes category shoppers who actively research and compare growth gummies. What wears her down is the mental energy of making food and supplement decisions over and over.

### WHAT

An AI-generated mom opens a drawer full of started routines and guides the viewer through the ingredients and daily directions worth checking. She never claims to be a customer. The comparison stays between generic height gummies and calcium chews, with no named competitors or doctor authority.

### HOW

Open on the crowded drawer, move through a phone-and-label research sequence, teach the calcium, D3, K2, osteocalcin, and zinc chain once, and withhold the first product reveal until 1:28. Frequent hard-cut B-roll keeps the visual rhythm moving before the final handheld packshot.

### Runtime

118 to 120 seconds for any selected hook and its matched bridge, followed by the shared body and CTA.

---

## 2. Ad Structure

### Hook 1 | 0:00-0:08

**Spoken:** "If you have a drawer full of gummies and chews you bought to help your short kid grow taller, turn the bottles around."

**Visual:** AI mom opens an overfilled kitchen drawer containing generic height-gummy pouches, calcium-chew tubs, and nutrition products. Every label is turned away, blurred, or unreadable. Small `AI-GENERATED PRESENTER` disclosure stays on screen.

**Spoken:** "When food is already a negotiation, every new powder or chew adds one more thing to remember. Put the labels on the table and start there."

**Visual:** Hard cuts between an untouched snack plate, a generic sticky gummy, a spoon beside a powder tub, and mom rubbing her forehead at the counter. Keep the child B-roll generic and non-testimonial.

### Hook 2 | 0:00-0:08

**Spoken:** "If you're comparing calcium chews and height gummies for a short, picky eater, start with the back label."

**Visual:** AI mom places three generic containers in a row on the counter. Labels remain unreadable. Small `AI-GENERATED PRESENTER` disclosure stays on screen.

### Bridge 2 | 0:08-0:17

**Spoken:** "Leave the promises on the front alone for a minute. Read the ingredients and the daily directions."

**Visual:** Quick cuts of a grocery list, breakfast leftovers, a weekly routine chart, and mom moving generic containers in and out of the drawer.

### Hook 3 | 0:00-0:08

**Spoken:** "If you're trying to help a short, picky eater grow taller, turn the bottle around before you choose one."

**Visual:** AI mom flips several generic bottles and pouches to their back panels. All competing labels stay too soft to read. Small `AI-GENERATED PRESENTER` disclosure stays on screen.

### Shared Body | 0:17-1:55

**0:17-0:31**

**Spoken:** "The useful comparison is the ingredient label and the daily directions."

**Visual:** Overhead shot of the kitchen table. Phone, notepad, and generic products are spread out. Mom writes four headings: calcium, D3, K2, zinc.

**0:31-0:48**

**Spoken:** "Calcium is the raw material, and D3 supports calcium absorption. K2 activates osteocalcin, which helps put calcium into bone. Zinc supports normal growth too."

**Visual:** Simple household-style animation: calcium enters frame, D3 points toward absorption, K2 switches on an osteocalcin icon, then zinc completes the four-part checklist. Keep the animation educational and non-medical.

**0:48-1:03**

**Spoken:** "That gives you something more useful to compare than the words on the front."

**Visual:** Mom turns generic height gummies and calcium chews around one by one. Show her checking only her handwritten ingredient list. No competing label can be read.

**Spoken:** "For a selective eater, I'd also check how much they have to take every day and whether it adds sugar."

**Spoken:** "Keep those ingredient notes beside you, then check the label and directions on the product itself."

**Visual:** Tight shot of the four-item checklist. Mom adds a final line: "How do we take it?" The product remains fully off screen.

Follow with: `MADE IN USA • cGMP-CERTIFIED • EVERY BATCH TESTED FOR PURITY AND POTENCY`. Keep `AI-GENERATED PRESENTER` visible.

**1:43-1:55**

**Spoken:** "Read the label, then decide whether it fits your family's routine."

**Visual:** Mom places the product in a tidy kitchen cabinet beside the routine chart. Add on-screen text: `DESIGNED TO SUPPORT HEALTHY GROWTH BY ADDRESSING NUTRITIONAL GAPS. DOES NOT OVERRIDE GENETICS OR GUARANTEE A SPECIFIC HEIGHT OUTCOME. RESULTS VARY.`

### CTA | 1:55-2:00

**Spoken:** "If the label makes sense for your family, they're offering 35% off with a 60-day money-back guarantee. The link's below."

## 3. Editing Notes

- Keep a small, unobtrusive `AI-GENERATED PRESENTER` disclosure visible throughout the AI mom's guidance.
- Use simple hard cuts and new B-roll every 1 to 4 seconds.
- Do not show the product or readable label before 1:28.
- All non-branded products must be generic and unbranded, with unreadable labels. Do not use any doctor or clinical imagery.
- Keep the child B-roll generic and non-testimonial. Do not show a height measurement, before-and-after comparison, or implied physical result.
- Verify the 35% offer and 60-day guarantee on the destination page again on launch day before export.
