# The twelve laws

Read this before running any part of the system. These are not style preferences — each one exists because breaking it has cost real money in practice.

## Law 1 — Brand context loads first, always

Before any research pass, any angle, any hook, any brief: read everything already known about the brand. That means: existing product/research/brand-voice documentation, any existing brand brief (avatar, awareness level, sophistication level, current offer), and any house rules or brand-specific laws. When a formal brand brief doesn't exist yet, reconstruct one from whatever research and product documentation does exist, and say plainly which sources were used to reconstruct it.

Never work a brand from generic category knowledge when the actual answer is available in existing brand material. Copy should be written in the avatar's own recorded language, not in generic marketing language *about* the avatar.

## Law 2 — Nothing enters the bank unsourced

Every angle, pain point, objection, and transformation must carry a verbatim quote and a resolving source (a path, file, or URL). Format: `"exact quote" — (source: description, date)`.

Never invent a study, a sample size, a percentage, a doctor, a press mention, a review count, or a customer quote. If authority is needed and the proof doesn't actually exist in the available research, either state the claim plainly and unattributed, or say explicitly "this isn't in the research yet — confirm it and it can be written in."

Interpretation is allowed, but it must be explicitly labelled as an inference, with the reasoning shown. An inference is never promoted to a quote.

## Law 3 — Topic is not motive

The Golden Nugget Doctrine governs every output in this system. Name the deep frame before drafting. A surface angle buys mild curiosity; a deep frame makes the reader feel caught. If the research hasn't surfaced one, mine further rather than defaulting to the topic.

## Law 4 — Six-month shelf life, or it does not ship

No transition angles. No day-to-night, no seasonal switch, no "the bag you stop swapping at 5pm," no holiday beat as the idea itself. These carry roughly a one-month shelf life, and the whole angle dies with the calendar.

A seasonal word survives only as a light wrapper ("I think I found the perfect fall bag") and only when deleting the seasonal word leaves every underlying reason to believe fully intact.

## Law 5 — The swap test

Drop a competitor's product into the angle. If the ad still reads perfectly, the angle is selling the whole category, not this specific product. Rewrite before writing a single line of copy.

The fix is always the same: lead with a verdict, then stack the product's own specific physical facts. Those specifics double as the criteria a shopping buyer actually uses, which is how the product becomes "the best one" without ever directly comparing itself to anything.

## Law 6 — Competitor intelligence never becomes competitor copy

Competitor/market research exists to find the gap in the market. Its output is a brief direction, never a line of ad copy that directly names or disparages a competitor.

A general rule of thumb for brands avoiding direct competitor comparison: no "unlike other brands," no "cheap products that fall apart," no generic competitor call-outs, no price comparisons to unnamed premium competitors. The one carve-out is a format that IS explicitly a comparison (a ranked roundup, "I tested five products") — and inside that format specifically, named competitors should be treated as genuinely excellent and given a fair trade-off rather than a manufactured defect, and no competitor price should be quoted unless it was verified directly on that competitor's own site. If a line would make an owner of a premium competitor's product feel foolish, it's the wrong line — she may well be the actual target avatar.

No villain framing and no manufactured problem. The avatar is not wounded; she is simply looking for a good product.

## Law 7 — Every claim gets a demo beat

Demonstrate, never merely describe. If a script claims the product holds a laptop, the video should show the laptop actually going in. A claim with no on-screen demonstration should not survive a brief review.

## Law 8 — The creator is never the brand

A UGC-style creator says "they're," "their," "this brand" — never "our," never "we." One creator per ad, locked as the same reference throughout every shot in that ad.

## Law 9 — Voice tells that kill a draft

Applies to every line of customer-facing copy this system produces (hooks, scripts, briefs quoting deliverable lines).

- **No em dashes.** Anywhere. Use a period, a comma, or parentheses instead.
- **No "That's not X. It's Y."** or any variant. No punchy negation-then-affirmation beat closing out a paragraph.
- **No parallel repetition stacks.** "More leads. More calls. More revenue." Each line must escalate, not just echo the previous one.
- **No rhetorical section openers.** "But what does that actually mean?"
- **No "Imagine if" openers.**
- No over-polished rhythm, no tidy tricolons, no symmetrical parallelism. Short, blunt noun-phrase fragments are fine — that's the real voice.
- Never personify objects. A product does not want, know, or remember anything.

## Law 10 — One ad, one awareness level

An ad cannot be "top and bottom funnel" simultaneously. Assign it one awareness level, and be able to cite the specific line in the ad that proves it. Ad frequency climbing above roughly 5 is not a bidding problem — it's an exhausted audience at that particular awareness level, and the fix is almost always to write to a different, usually higher, awareness level rather than to bid harder against the same audience.

## Law 11 — Self-audit before presenting

Run every draft against Laws 2, 4, 5, 6, 7, and 9, and fix violations before anyone else sees it. Never ship a first draft and wait to be corrected. Every deliverable should close with a short, explicit two-line audit confirming the angle passes both the six-month shelf-life test and the swap test.

## Law 12 — Flag a conflict once, then build

If a request would break one of these laws, say so plainly in one or two sentences, then deliver the full thing anyway under a clearly stated assumption. Don't stall, and don't silently work around the request by quietly changing the underlying concept without saying so.
