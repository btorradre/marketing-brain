## GOLDEN NUGGET DOCTRINE (mandatory, before any hook/angle/script/concept)

Before producing any hook, angle, script, concept, or audit verdict, name the **golden nugget**: the single most emotionally loaded deep frame in the research — the real motive that makes buyers act — never the surface theme.

- **Topic ≠ motive.** "Memory loss" is a topic (surface hook: "my memory feels as young as when I was 25"). The frame is: "I thought I was getting dementia just like my mum did, until I discovered this." Surface angles buy mild hope or curiosity; deep frames trigger identification so strong the reader feels caught.
- **The test:** for every candidate angle, ask — is this the topic, or is this the motive? If it's the topic, dig one layer deeper (weight loss → the stolen victory; a status-symbol bag → the fear of being seen trying too hard).
- **Where it goes:** the golden nugget leads — right at the top of the piece, as the hook. Never buried in the body.
- **Deliverable:** state the golden nugget in one explicit sentence before drafting. If analyzing a reference ad/funnel instead of writing, state the nugget it's built on and whether it leads with it. If the research hasn't surfaced one, mine reviews, voice-of-customer, and community discussion until it does — never default to a surface angle.

### Source material to mine before writing anything new

Before inventing a new hook or beat structure, mine whatever existing organic/UGC footage, static ad creative, and prior reference-ad breakdowns already exist for this product — pull the hook lines, beat structure, and pacing that have already proven to work rather than starting from a blank page.

### Avatar — the "Velantra Woman" ("Caroline", 40–48)

Coastal-affluent professional, $150K+ household income, Newport Beach / Hamptons / Nantucket energy. Owns the Birkin *silhouette* obsession but **rejects the logo, the price, and the artificial scarcity**. Buys quiet luxury on the "if you know, you know" principle — wants peer recognition, not stranger recognition. Discovers new brands via peer word-of-mouth and trusted micro-creators; sharing a find functions as an identity act ("look what I found" really means "look how good my taste is").

### Market sophistication & awareness defaults for this product

- **Sophistication: 4–5 out of 5** — the "Birkin alternative / dupe" claim is a crowded space. Don't lead with "luxury for less"; lead with a *mechanism* (how it's actually made) or a *new-identity* angle (a discovery story, a craftsmanship proof point, a specific-place anchor) instead.
- **Awareness:** problem-aware moving toward solution-aware for cold audiences; product-aware for retargeting audiences.
- **Core angle/wound:** "I want the recognizable shape without being the woman who paid a fortune for a logo" — taste-anxiety mixed with a fear of looking like new money trying too hard. The vindication being sold: the bag that earns a genuine "where did you get that?" without the markup behind it.

## Flow

1. **Load the context above** — never re-ask for product, colorways, avatar, or price band; it's all here.
2. **Confirm format.** Default: short-form UGC-style TikTok video (this product's proven material skews this way). Other options: a VSL-style landing-page video, a static-to-animated ad, or a talking-head format. Confirm with the user in one question rather than assuming.
3. **Mine any existing winning UGC or ad material for this product** before inventing new hooks — pull the actual proven hook lines and beat structures that already work.
4. **Build the concept using the product-tuned slot defaults below**, adjusting for whatever the user confirms or overrides.
5. **Write the finished concept as a brief** using the template below.
6. **Hand off for production** once the brief is approved — generate the actual assets using the velantra-boat-tote product-scale skill's verified reference photos and Locked Product Identity block.

### Product-tuned slot defaults (Boat Tote)

| Slot | Default | Why |
|------|---------|-----|
| Concept/vehicle | "The discovery" — a creator finds the Boatkin at a coastal pop-up or similar real-world moment | Matches this avatar's place-anchored, word-of-mouth discovery behavior |
| Lead/hook | Curiosity or authority-subversion (e.g. "everyone on the ferry has this bag, and it's not the luxury one") | At sophistication 4–5, a plain price-comparison claim is stale — a fresh frame is needed |
| Mechanism intro | Craftsmanship reveal — solid brass turn-lock, reinforced canvas, a real warranty | Earns the "quality you can actually feel" belief that justifies the price |
| Solution intro | Discovery-style introduction — the product enters as something she stumbled onto, not something being hard-sold | Avoids triggering her suspicion of being marketed to by "just another small brand" |
| Proof | Peer/UGC social proof, or a "where did you get that?" testimonial beat | Peer recommendation is her single strongest trust trigger |
| CTA | Soft, light scarcity ("colors sell out each season") pointing to the product page | Hard, aggressive scarcity reads as exactly the manufactured urgency she rejects |
| Length | Roughly 20–40 seconds for UGC-style, or 60–90 seconds for a VSL-style format | Matched to the channel |

## Rules for briefs handed to an outside editor

Any brief handed to someone outside the internal team must be fully self-contained — assume they have zero access to internal files, folders, or systems. Never reference a local file path, an internal drive location, or an internal filename in an editor-facing brief.

**Keep it short** — a handful of pages at most. It should carry only: the product-truth block, the script, the timeline, the generation prompts, and any brief-specific guardrails/QA notes. General "how we generate assets" process documentation belongs in one shared internal process reference, cited once — never re-explained inline inside every brief. Cut strategic reasoning (why this angle works, why this format was chosen) down to one or two lines — the editor executes the brief, they don't need the full strategic case.

Concretely:

1. **Product references → live product page links, not internal file paths.** Point the editor at the actual live product page, deep-linked to the exact colorway variant if the storefront supports it, and name the specific gallery photo to reference or save.
2. **Creator references → a sourcing method the editor can actually follow** (e.g. a specific reference-image search strategy plus an identity description per creator), not internal generation-pipeline instructions. If avatars are AI-generated from reference photos, never clone a real, identifiable person's actual face without consent — generate a distinct, non-identical likeness inspired by a reference, not a duplicate of a real individual.
3. **Reference ads → public links only** (an ad library URL, a public share link, or a platform URL) — never a downloaded local video file path. If an internal reference clip has no public URL, describe it inline beat-by-beat instead of pointing to a file.
4. **No companion files** — everything the editor needs (segment prompts, scripts, tables) should live directly inline in the brief document itself, not scattered across separate attachments.
5. **Generated B-roll must be photoreal, not CGI-looking.** If a generated frame reads as a 3D-rendered model rather than a photograph, it must be regenerated — no exceptions. Never ship the first generation attempt as final: generate multiple variants of every scene and select the one free of visible artifacts before it goes in the brief.

Internal reference material remains completely valid for actual internal generation work (image-to-image references, internal replication) — this rule only governs what ends up inside a brief handed to an outside collaborator.
