# DR Video Ads — the belief-shifting script engine

**Core thesis:** an ad is a belief-shifting machine wrapped in a retention machine. The retention layer (curiosity loops, emotional arcs, a withheld brand reveal) exists so the belief layer has time to work. The viewer converts because by the end of the ad she believes different things than she did at second one, and the ad watched her the whole way there. Sell outcomes, never products. One ad, one key emotional driver.

**Scope.** This is for unaware, problem-aware, and solution-aware traffic — where new buyers come from. Middle/bottom-of-funnel retargeting formats (apology ads, scam-warning ads, warehouse tours, authenticity ads) only work after ads like this have installed the belief system. They are a later layer, not this one.

## Golden Nugget Doctrine (mandatory, before anything else)

Before any hook, angle, script, or concept is produced, name the **golden nugget**: the single most emotionally loaded deep frame in the research — the real motive that makes buyers act, never the surface theme. "Memory loss" is a topic; "I thought I was getting dementia just like my mum did, until I discovered this" is the motive/frame. The test for any candidate: *is this the topic, or the motive?* If it's the topic, dig one layer deeper.

The golden nugget leads — it's what the hook voices and what the resolution pays off. State it in one explicit sentence before drafting anything. If the research hasn't surfaced one, keep mining voice-of-customer material until it does.

## House laws this skill runs on

This skill is part of a family that shares a common rulebook. Since it stands alone here, the specific rules it leans on hardest are inlined below — treat them as non-negotiable, not style preferences.

- **Nothing enters a script unsourced.** Never invent a study, sample size, percentage, doctor, press mention, or customer quote. State things plainly and unattributed when no real proof exists, or flag the gap. An interpretation is allowed but must be labeled `[INFERRED]` — it never gets promoted to a quote.
- **Topic is not motive.** The Golden Nugget Doctrine above governs every output.
- **Six-month shelf life, or it doesn't ship.** No transition angles, no day-to-night or seasonal switch as the idea itself. A seasonal word survives only as a wrapper, and only if deleting the season leaves every reason to believe intact.
- **The swap test.** Drop a competitor's product into the angle. If the ad still reads perfectly, it sells the category, not the brand — rewrite before writing a line. Lead with a verdict, then stack the brand's own physical facts.
- **Every claim gets a demo beat.** Demonstrate, never describe. A claim with no on-screen demonstration or mechanism attribution doesn't survive.
- **The creator is never the brand.** A UGC creator says "they're," "their," "this brand" — never "our," never "we." One creator per ad, locked as the same reference in every shot.
- **Voice tells that kill a draft.** No em dashes, anywhere. No "That's not X. It's Y." or any punchy negation-then-affirmation beat. No parallel repetition stacks ("More leads. More calls. More revenue." — each line must escalate, not echo). No rhetorical section openers ("But what does that actually mean?"). No "Imagine if" openers. No tidy tricolons or over-polished symmetrical rhythm — short noun-phrase fragments are fine, that's the real voice. Never personify objects; a product does not want, know, or remember.
- **One ad, one awareness level.** An ad cannot be "top and bottom funnel." Assign one level and be able to cite the hook that proves it.
- **Self-audit before presenting.** Run every draft against the checklist in `references/self-audit-checklist.md` before anyone else sees it. Don't ship a first draft and wait to be corrected.

## Angle vocabulary (locked terminology)

| Layer | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Avatar** | Who she is — one specific person in a specific situation |
| **Angle** | The specific problem, or the psychological reason she would buy |
| **Hook** | The claim, mechanism, or reframe that opens the ad and voices the angle |
| **Concept** | The narrative vehicle carrying the hook — story, demo, listicle, founder, street interview |

**An angle is a problem, not a claim.** "Menopause wrinkly skin" is the angle. "Progesterone is what makes your skin saggy" is a hook that argues *about* that angle. Writing the hook into the angle field is the single most common error.

**Mechanism** is not a layer — it's why the problem exists, an ingredient inside a hook that makes it believable. **Golden nugget** is not a layer either — it's the emotional motive underneath the angle, the depth requirement *on* the angle, voiced by the hook.

Two tests: *Angle test* — could you buy media against it, and would she recognize the problem as hers? *Hook test* — is it a sentence a person could actually say in the first two seconds?

## Load first, before writing anything

1. **The avatar/customer research** for this brand and product — reviews, interviews, forum mining, support tickets, whatever exists. If nothing exists, run that research first (mining raw customer language into sourced quotes is its own upstream step — see the companion voice-of-customer skill if the team has one). No script gets written from category knowledge; nightmare moments, objections, and language all come out of real research, verbatim.
2. **Any existing angle bank or angle records** for this avatar/product, so the angle chosen here is a known, sourced problem rather than a fresh guess.
3. **Any documented "how we actually built our best script" case study**, if one exists for this brand — it captures the real sequence (order of operations, belief architecture, loop map, trust hierarchy) as executed on an actual winning script, which is more useful than any abstract template.
4. **Two length/density benchmarks**, worth studying directly if available: a cold-skeptical-market script running ~1:32 / 274 words with a criterion line and one designated "killer" per failed solution, and a warm-market authority-narrator script running ~2:40 / 509 words with mechanism-as-spec structure. A third, longer 6-minute VSL is useful as a format-library reference, but long-form is the exception now, not the default — see the length law below.

## The pipeline — ten steps

Steps 1–4 produce durable assets reused across every ad for this avatar/product. Steps 5–10 are per-ad. **Never start at step 7 (the hook)** — a hook without a belief ledger under it is a guess.

```
DURABLE (once per avatar/product, reused forever)
 1. Avatar research    → avatar dossier
 2. Belief inventory    → beliefs-to-shift list, sourced from real customer language
 3. Angle               → the angle is the PROBLEM
 4. Mechanism doc        → the canonical belief system, built ONCE

PER AD
 5. Concept + format    → the narrative vehicle, chosen by awareness stage
 6. Belief ledger       → THE core artifact: which beliefs this ad shifts, how, where
 7. Hook                → opens the master loop, voices the golden nugget
 8. Bridge              → punch-in-the-gut: nightmare + knife-twist + loop
 9. Body                → the belief-shift chain
10. Close               → loops closed, brand revealed, offer with a reason-why
```

**Step 1 — Avatar research.** Use an existing dossier if one exists. Otherwise, mine real customer language first (reviews, forums, support threads, comments) — never write from category knowledge.

**Step 2 — Belief inventory.** Extract every belief standing between her and buying, each one sourced: identity beliefs ("this is just what happens at my age"), cause beliefs (what she thinks causes it — usually wrong), solution beliefs ("only X works," "I've tried everything"), trust beliefs ("doctors dismissed me," "ads lie"), self beliefs ("my body betrayed me," "I'm past fixing"). This list is durable — every ad for this avatar draws from it. Full detail and examples: `references/belief-ledger-and-mechanism-doc.md`.

**Step 3 — Angle.** Pull an existing sourced angle, or add one. The angle is the specific problem; the claim that argues about it is the hook, and comes later. State the golden nugget under the angle before moving on.

**Step 4 — Mechanism doc.** The single highest-leverage lesson from studying a top account: one canonical mechanism story feeds every ad, with vocabulary that never varies — in the studied account, the same causal chain appeared word-for-word identical across 1,244 ads. This doc is written ONCE per brand/product, not per script. It contains the causal chain, a mandatory sensory demo, a folk-language analogy set, why-others-fail logic, the category co-opt, real proof lines, seeded scarcity, and locked vocabulary. Full template: `references/belief-ledger-and-mechanism-doc.md`.

**Step 5 — Concept + format, chosen by awareness stage (one ad, one level):**

| Awareness | Formats | Length | Brand reveal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unaware / problem-aware | Story VSL (punch-in-the-gut, first person), discovery story | 2–6 min | Last 20% only |
| Problem-aware | Countdown listicle, reframe explainer | 45–90s | Late |
| Solution-aware | Mechanism timeline, speed explainer, ingredient personification, debate ad | 60–120s | Middle–late |

The reframe IS the belief shift in short formats. In short ads the curiosity loop is structural — the countdown or timeline itself creates the pull, so narrative loops aren't needed.

**Step 6 — Belief ledger, the core per-ad artifact.** Before a word of script, build a table: current belief (her words, sourced) → target belief → shift vehicle → where in script → loop that guards it → loop closed at. The shift vehicle is always a story moment, demo, or reframe — never an argument — and it must be **lived, not announced**: a stated beat moves nothing, the lived version (shown inside casual grammar) installs the belief. 3–6 rows for a VSL, 1–2 for a short. Full rules: `references/belief-ledger-and-mechanism-doc.md`.

**Step 7 — Hook.** Opens the ad's master loop and voices the golden nugget. Must be a pattern interrupt that collides with a ledger row, plus at least one of: shows the emotional payoff, triggers self-identification ("that's me" within two sentences, via specific behavior not demographics), or gestures at a new mechanism. Write the hook and the close as a pair — full detail in `references/hook-bridge-body-close.md`.

**Step 8 — Bridge (punch-in-the-gut).** Carries her from "you have my attention" to "I need to know the answer": nightmare-state micro-moments, the knife-twist (the peak-shame beat that indicts a false belief), failed solutions as a hope-embarrassment cycle, stakes/final straw, an indifferent enemy, a second loop plus the promise, and earning the reveal with one more agitation pass. Full structure: `references/hook-bridge-body-close.md`.

**Step 9 — Body.** The belief-shift chain: the mechanism arrives from a source that doesn't contradict any belief already shifted, a sensory demo comes first, the chain unfolds as a discovery narrative (question → failed candidates tested and killed → surprise answer → potency problem → process fix), every claim gets a mechanism attribution, objections die by testing rather than stating, arrival at the product is emotion first then logic, and a mandatory skeptic arc includes a "week one, nothing happens" beat before modest, attributed results. Full structure: `references/hook-bridge-body-close.md`.

**Step 10 — Close.** Why-I'm-sharing, cost of waiting in mechanism terms, brand reveal with the ritual, a scarcity payoff seeded earlier in the mechanism doc, a reason-why offer (never naked "50% off"), risk reversal denominated in the ad's own opening image, and a soft CTA. Full structure: `references/hook-bridge-body-close.md`.

## Length, density, and skepticism

Write to a **word count**, never a timecode. Natural delivery runs ~180–190 words per minute, so runtime is arithmetic: `words ÷ 190 × 60`. Every script states its target word count at the top and gets counted before it ships.

| Benchmark shape | Words | Runtime |
|---|---|---|
| Cold market, several novel ingredients | ~270–300 | ~1:35 |
| Warm market, authority narrator | ~500–520 | ~2:40 |
| A cautionary failed example | 1,251 | 6:35 |

Default targets: short mechanism ad 270–300 words, authority VSL 500–520 words. Anything past 700 words needs an explicit written reason ("the reference was long" doesn't count); past 900 words, stop and cut.

**Skepticism sets the SHAPE, never the length.** A colder market or a novel ingredient means more objections handled naturally inside the same short runtime — never a longer ad. The full skepticism-shape table, the four devices that carry this work (mechanism-as-spec, the mandatory criterion line, kill-by-failure-mode, one designated killer per failed solution), the ingredient phrasing formula, loop-cadence rules, and pacing/repetition rules all live in `references/length-density-language-laws.md` — read it before drafting any script for a cold or novel-ingredient market.

## Language

The prime rule: **literal and concrete, always.** No abstract language, no metaphor as a default register — every line names a thing a camera could film or a body could feel. The only sanctioned comparisons are the mechanism doc's mandatory sensory demo and its locked analogy set; no new simile enters a script without going into the mechanism doc first. Grade 4–6 comprehension, behavior never adjectives, numbers modest and specific. Full language laws and the "how trust is built" ranked list (density of lived detail, validating her effort, messenger congruence, testing not stating, modest numbers, skepticism transfer, never attacking what she loves): `references/length-density-language-laws.md`.

A natural-speech pass is mandatory for anything spoken, after the draft passes self-audit — a script that still sounds written is dead. Hand it to whatever voice/naturalization process the team uses before production.

## Self-audit before presenting

Run and report every check in `references/self-audit-checklist.md` — ledger, congruence, demo, proof, source-congruence, simplicity, literalness, truth-signal density, angle shelf-life/swap-test, length math, repetition, objection coverage, and (where relevant) the criterion-line placement. Do not ship a first draft and wait to be corrected.

## Handoffs

- **Hook variations** get spread into multiple opening-line options for testing.
- **Creator briefs** turn the finished script into shoot-ready instructions with no internal jargon, and enforce the demo-beat and creator-never-the-brand rules at the brief level.
- **A natural-speech pass** is mandatory before anything spoken goes to production — this strips written-sounding constructions the script draft still carries.
- **Production** follows a storyboard-first process where possible: keyframes get approved before full rendering, whether the format is a fast single-take read, a one-take 60–180s piece, a multi-shot creator video, or a replication of a reference ad.
- Agreed concepts should be logged in whatever creative-tracking system the team uses before production starts, so results are attributable back to the angle and hook that produced them.
