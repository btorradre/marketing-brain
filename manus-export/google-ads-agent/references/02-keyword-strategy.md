# Google Ads Keyword Strategy

## Match Types (2026 State)

### Broad Match
- Widest reach — ads show on related searches, synonyms, variations
- Google recommends as default when paired with Smart Bidding
- AI Max treats ALL keywords as broad match when enabled

### Phrase Match ("keyword")
- Ads show for searches including the meaning of your keyword
- Now intent-based — matches on meaning, not just word order
- Modified Broad Match (+keyword) is deprecated → treated as phrase

### Exact Match ([keyword])
- Most restrictive but NOT truly exact anymore
- Still shows for close variants, plurals, misspellings, reworded queries with same intent
- Best for: high-value terms, brand terms, precise control

**Key Trend**: Match types progressively loosened. Search themes and brand inclusions/exclusions are the future direction.

## Negative Keywords

### Critical Importance
- More important than ever due to AI-driven campaigns and broader matching
- Campaigns with 200+ negatives: 67% lower CPA, 89% better CTR
- 84% of advertisers use <50 negatives, missing 23-34% of irrelevant traffic

### Match Types for Negatives
- Negative Broad: blocks if ALL terms present (no synonyms/close variants)
- Negative Phrase: blocks if phrase appears in order
- Negative Exact: blocks exact query only
- 2025: Google now applies misspelling expansion to negatives

### Standard Negative Keyword Lists
- Universal: free, jobs, careers, DIY, tutorial, reviews, vs, comparison, salary, reddit, youtube, how to, what is
- Industry-specific lists
- Campaign-specific lists

### Limits
- Up to 10,000 negative keywords in PMax campaigns
- Use shared negative keyword lists for efficiency

## Quality Score (1-10)

### Components
1. **Expected CTR (39% weight)**: Prediction of click likelihood. Most important factor.
2. **Landing Page Experience (39% weight)**: Relevance, speed, mobile-friendliness, navigation, transparency
3. **Ad Relevance (22% weight)**: Match between ad copy and search intent

### Each Rated: Below Average, Average, Above Average

### Impact
- Higher QS = lower CPCs + better positions
- Ad Rank = Quality Score x Max Bid (simplified)
- 2026: QS is diagnostic — real-time auction signals go far beyond static QS

### Improvement Process
1. Export keyword-level QS data
2. Segment: 1-4 (poor), 5-6 (average), 7-10 (good)
3. Prioritize fixing QS 4-6 keywords with high spend
4. Address weakest component first

## Keyword Grouping Strategies

### SKAGs (Single Keyword Ad Groups)
- One keyword per ad group across match types
- **Status 2026**: Largely obsolete — close variants and RSAs eliminated precision advantage
- Still useful for: very high-value niche keywords

### STAGs (Single Theme Ad Groups) — RECOMMENDED
- 5-15 keywords sharing same search intent per ad group
- Better data collection for automated bidding
- Simpler management, higher impression volume
- Works best with Smart Bidding

### Hybrid
- STAGs for most keywords, SKAGs for handful of high-value terms

## Search Terms Report
- Shows actual queries that triggered your ads
- Review weekly minimum for first 3 months, then bi-weekly
- Mine for: new keyword ideas (high-converting terms) and negatives (irrelevant terms)
- Note: Google hides some low-volume queries (privacy thresholds)

- **Third-party**: SEMrush, Ahrefs, Moz, SpyFu, Ubersuggest
