## Golden nugget doctrine (mandatory, applies to every concept)

Before any hook, angle, script, concept, or audit verdict is produced, name the **golden nugget**: the single most emotionally loaded deep frame behind the product, the real motive that makes buyers act, never the surface theme.

- **Topic is not motive.** A topic is the surface subject; the frame is the real, often more vulnerable, motive underneath it. Surface angles buy mild hope or curiosity. Deep frames trigger identification strong enough that the reader feels caught.
- **The test:** for every candidate angle, ask — is this the topic, or is this the motive? If it's the topic, dig one layer deeper.
- **Where it goes:** the golden nugget leads. It's the hook, at the very top of the piece, never buried in the body.
- **Deliverable:** state the golden nugget in one explicit sentence before drafting anything. If analyzing a reference ad instead of writing one, state the nugget it's built on and whether it actually leads with it. If the research hasn't surfaced a real nugget yet, keep digging (reviews, voice-of-customer, forum language) rather than defaulting to a surface angle.

## Editor-facing brief rules (hard — no local file paths, ever)

Any brief handed to an outside editor must be fully self-contained — the editor has no access to internal files or tools. Never put an internal file path, vault directory, or local filename in an editor-facing brief.

**Brevity:** briefs are short — 5-6 pages max. They carry only: the product-truth block, the script, the timeline, the prompts, and brief-specific guardrails/QA. Cut strategic exposition (why the angle works, why this format) down to one or two lines — the editor executes, they don't need the reasoning. The general "how to generate creator and b-roll assets" process is documented once elsewhere in the brand's production process and should never be re-explained inside an individual brief.

1. **Product refs → live product links.** Point the editor at the live product page, deep-linked to the exact colorway variant, and name the gallery shot to save from the page.
2. **Creator refs → a real reference photo, turned into a generated identity via AI image generation.** Never clone a real person's actual face for an AI-generated creator reference. The brief itself only needs the source-image link and identity block per creator — never restate the generation process in the brief.
3. **Reference ads → public links** (an ad-library link, a share link, or a platform URL) — never a downloaded video file. An internal clip with no public URL gets an inline beat-by-beat description instead.
4. **No companion files** — everything the editor needs (segment prompts, scripts, tables) lives inline in the brief document itself.
5. **B-roll (generated scenes) → still images generated first, then animated.** Generate three variants of every scene and pick the one that isn't artifacted — never ship the first roll. **The 3D-model/CGI look is banned** — if a frame looks like a 3D render rather than photoreal, it gets regenerated, no exceptions. The brief itself only needs the finished keyframe/motion prompts per scene, never a restatement of the generation process.
