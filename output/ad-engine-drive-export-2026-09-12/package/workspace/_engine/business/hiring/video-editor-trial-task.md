## 1. What this task is

Here is how production actually works on this team. Every product has a content library: a folder of AI-generated b-roll clips that past ads were built from. A good clip generated for one ad is a good clip for the next one, so when we make a new ad we cut from the library first and only generate footage for beats the library cannot prove.

This trial is that job. You get a locked script, a reference ad, and the full b-roll library for our travel bag. You will not generate a single frame of footage. You will render the voiceover, scrub the library, choose the clip that proves each line, and cut a finished ad.

That makes this trial mostly a test of judgement, and that is deliberate. Tool knowledge is teachable. Knowing why a given shot belongs under a given line is what we are hiring for.

Read this whole document before you open the footage folder. There is a section near the end where you make the creative calls yourself, and it will change how you plan the edit.

## 2. What we are testing

**1. Claim to frame.** Every line of voiceover makes a specific claim. The clip underneath it has to be the proof of that exact claim, not a pretty shot that happens to be nearby. The library has around sixty clips and you need roughly a dozen. Most of the test is which ones you choose.

**2. Quality control.** The library is real, not sanitized for this trial. Some clips in it break our product rules, and AI footage develops flaws mid-clip that the first second does not show. Your job is to make sure nothing broken reaches the timeline. We know exactly which clips have problems. We are watching whether you catch them.

**3. Pacing.** A new visual every 1 to 2.5 seconds, no dead air, and a first two seconds that earns the third.

**4. Finish.** Clean burned text, sound design that feels native to the platform, exact spec compliance.

## 4. The product

**The Eleanor Weekender by Velantra.** A large structured travel bag, wider than tall, one generous size.

**Live product page:** https://velantrafashion.com/products/velantra-weekender
**Light Chocolate:** https://velantrafashion.com/products/velantra-weekender?variant=44165996544065
**Army Green:** https://velantrafashion.com/products/velantra-weekender?variant=44165996642369

### Product truth, non negotiable

Every clip you pull gets checked against this list before it touches the timeline.

- **One size only.** 18 x 14.5 x 7 inches. Two colourways: Light Chocolate and Army Green. $159.99.
- **Hardware is gold.** Never silver, never chrome, never white metal. Both front clasp plates are the same gold as each other.
- **No logo anywhere on the bag.** No embossed text, no lettering, no plate with a name on it.
- **No zipper anywhere on the bag.** No track, no teeth, no pull.
- **Carry is hand or forearm only.** The top handles are short. They physically cannot reach a shoulder. A shoulder carry or a long shoulder strap in any frame is an instant fail.
- **The flap is one single piece of leather** that folds all the way over the front. It never splits into pieces or tabs.
- **The two-tone split never moves.** The upper section of the bag body is a wide band of cognac leather all the way around, and everything below it is woven cream canvas. That split sits in exactly the same place whether the bag is open or closed. The handles anchor into the leather band, never into the canvas.
- **The bag is never opened, closed, fastened or unfastened on camera.** It changes state across a hard cut: one shot closed, the next shot already open. This is a standing production rule, not a trial restriction, and at least one clip in the library violates it.
- **Never state where the bag is made.** Not in the voiceover, not in on-screen text, not anywhere.
- **Never name or compare to another brand.** Not in text, not in the visuals.

This is the heart of the task. Each line makes one claim, and the column on the right is the proof that claim requires. We are not telling you which clip satisfies each row. Finding it is the job. Where a row needs two states or two subjects, expect to cut two clips against each other.

| Line | The claim | What the footage must prove |

| 1 | This specific bag exists and it is worth two seconds | A real bag in a real room, seen clearly enough to be recognised again later |

## 7. The footage library

You get a folder link with everything below. Confirm it opens before the clock starts.

### What is in it

- **`broll/`** is the pool you cut from. Roughly sixty clips, 6 to 15 seconds each, all AI-generated across several past shoots: hero shots, travel contexts, packing shots, detail macros, and a leather-workshop craft series showing the bag being made. Filenames carry a prefix naming the shoot they came from, so clips that share a prefix usually share a look and can be re-entered as one setup.
- **`broll/creator/`** holds clips with a creator's face on camera, tied to a specific spoken read. **Off limits for this ad.** The format is faceless voiceover, and a face silently mouthing someone else's words under your track reads as broken instantly.
- **`images/`** holds keyframe stills from the same shoots. A slow push or drift on a clean still is an acceptable finish here and sometimes the right call. Stills get checked against product truth exactly like clips.

### How to work it

1. **Scrub the whole library once before you cut anything.** Build your own shortlist against the claim-to-frame map. The clip that proves a line is sometimes from a shoot whose name suggests nothing of the sort.
2. **Watch every shortlisted clip at full length.** AI footage mutates mid-clip. Hardware grows, straps appear, flaps split, text engraves itself onto metal plates. A clip can be perfect for four seconds and dead at second five. Use the clean seconds and cut before the flaw, or reject the clip.
3. **Check every pick against section 4.** Some clips in the library fail it. Some have burned-in text making claims that are not in your script, and anything with burned-in source text is disqualified no matter how good it looks. Part of your deliverable is the list of clips you rejected and the rule each one broke.
4. **Trim, never stretch.** The clips are longer than your beats, which is the point. If a beat runs long, re-enter another clip from the same setup. Slowing footage down to fill time is visible and it is a fail.

## 8. The two calls you make yourself

Everything above hands you the answer. These two do not, and they are weighted heavily.

### 8a. Line 6 has no obvious clip

**"Packed full or nearly empty, it keeps its shape."**

No clip in the library is labelled for this line. Work out what would prove the claim, find it in the footage, and give us two or three sentences on why what you chose proves that specific claim. Think about what the claim actually is before you think about what looks nice. A bag keeping its shape is a comparison across two states, and one static pretty frame will not carry it.

### 8b. Cut a better opening

The hook we implied is safe: line 1 names the product over a clean hero shot. Give us an alternative first two seconds built from the library: a different opening visual, a rewritten line 1 to sit under it, and two sentences on why yours stops the scroll harder than ours. Deliver it as a real cut, not a description. It does not have to go in the final ad. Tell us which one you would run and why.

You are allowed to disagree with us. We would rather find that out now.

## 9. Edit spec

**Cadence.** New visual every 1 to 2.5 seconds. Around 24 to 28 cuts across the finished ad. Re-enter setups the way the reference does rather than letting any single shot sit.

**First two seconds.** The cut has to move inside the first second. Do not open on a static hold.

**Audio.**
- One continuous voiceover track for the whole script, rendered in ElevenLabs with the login we provide.
- A soft music bed sitting well under the voice, low enough that it never competes with her.
- Light ambient sound under the b-roll. Room tone, a bit of cabin noise if you use an aircraft shot, footsteps under a walking shot.

**On-screen text.** All of it added in your editor. None of it comes from the footage, and any text burned into a source clip disqualifies that clip.
- A persistent lower third across the entire runtime, black on a light background the way the reference does it: `THE ELEANOR WEEKENDER BY VELANTRA`
- A hook card over the first 2.5 seconds, five words or fewer. Use `ONE BAG. THREE DAYS.` or write your own and tell us why.
- A price and CTA card over line 9.
- Spelling is checked. A typo in a burned caption is an auto-fail.

**Captions.** Word-synced captions are optional. If you add them, they cannot fight the lower third. Show us your call.

**Export.** 1080 x 1920, H.264, MP4, AAC audio, 32 to 36 seconds.

**File name, exactly:** `VEL-TRIAL-02_<yourfirstname>_final.mp4`

## 10. Auto-fail

Any one of these ends the review. They are all things that would cost real money in production, which is why they are absolute.

1. A shipped frame showing the bag on a shoulder, or any long shoulder strap
2. A shipped frame with a zipper on the bag
3. A shipped frame with a logo, brand name, or embossed lettering on the bag
4. A shipped frame with silver, chrome or white hardware, or two front clasp plates that do not match each other
5. The bag being opened, closed or fastened on camera
6. The leather and canvas split moving, shrinking or disappearing between shots
7. Burned-in text from a source clip appearing in the finished ad
8. A claim on screen or in audio that is not in the script
9. A misspelling in any burned-in text
10. A face from the creator folder under your voiceover
11. Wrong file name or wrong export spec

Notice that most of this list is about what you let through, not what you make. That is the job.
