What My Nurse Friend Told Me
Over Coffee

Linda and I have been friends for almost 20 years.
She's a nurse at the hospital downtown. We meet for
coffee every couple of weeks to catch up.
I was complaining about my feet again. The burning.
The tingling. How I'd basically given up on those pills
because they made me feel like a zombie.
Linda set down her cup and looked at me.
"Margaret, have you ever tried compression for your
feet? Not the socks. Foot sleeves."
I rolled my eyes. Of course I'd tried compression
socks. Those tight things that go all the way up to
your knee? I nearly threw out my back trying to get
them on. And they made my feet so hot I wanted to
rip them off after an hour.
"No, no," she said, shaking her head. "Foot sleeves are
different. They just cover your foot and ankle. Open
toe. Way easier to deal with."
She leaned in like she was sharing a secret.
"Think about it. If your pain is in your feet, why do you
need compression all the way up your leg? All that
extra fabric just makes them harder to put on and
hotter to wear. Foot sleeves focus right where the
nerves are hurting. Your arch. Your heel. Your ankle."
That actually made sense.
"And the open toe part," she continued, "that's not a
shortcut. It's actually better for people like you. You
can check your toes whenever you want without
taking anything off. If you've got bunions or
hammertoes, they're not getting squeezed. And your
feet can breathe instead of getting all sweaty."
I nodded. I do have bunions. And those full
compression socks always made them ache worse.

Then Linda said something I wasn't expecting.
"But here's the real thing, Margaret. The most
important time to wear them is at night."
I must have made a face, because she laughed.
"I know, I know. You're thinking there's no way you
could sleep in compression. But hear me out."
She explained that your body does most of its healing
while you sleep. That's when your nerves can calm
down and repair. When blood flow can bring oxygen
and nutrients to the damaged areas. If you can wear
gentle compression at night, you're giving your feet 8
hours of recovery time.
"Eight hours," she said. "Every single night. That adds
up."

I shook my head. "Linda, I've tried wearing my
compression socks to bed. It was miserable. They
were so tight I woke up with marks on my legs. My

feet got hot and itchy. I felt worse in the morning, not
better."
She smiled and reached into her purse. She pulled out
a little notepad and wrote something down.
"That's exactly why I want you to look these up when
you get home. They're called Onecompress Bamboo
Foot Sleeves. They're different from anything you've
tried."
She handed me the paper.
"They're made of bamboo, so they're super soft. Way
softer than that synthetic stuff. And they use gentle
compression, not that tight medical grade squeeze. A
lot of my patients wear them to bed now. They
actually look forward to putting them on at night."
I looked at the notepad. She'd written the name and
underlined it twice.
"Just Google it when you get home," she said. "Read
the reviews. See what other people with neuropathy
are saying. I think you'll be surprised."
That night, I did exactly that. And what I found
changed everything.
