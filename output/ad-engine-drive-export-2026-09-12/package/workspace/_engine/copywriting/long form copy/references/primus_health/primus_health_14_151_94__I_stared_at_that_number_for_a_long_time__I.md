What I found was something I had never encountered in 30 years of training seriously and reading seriously about how the body works.

Endothelial dysfunction.

The endothelium is the inner lining of the blood vessels — a single layer of cells that lines every artery in the body. Its primary job is to produce nitric oxide — the molecule that signals the arterial smooth muscle to relax, keeping vessels flexible and blood pressure normal.

When you are young and healthy, the endothelium produces nitric oxide efficiently. Arteries stay elastic. Blood moves easily. Pressure stays low.

As you age — regardless of fitness level — the endothelium begins to lose function. Nitric oxide production declines. Arteries stiffen. Blood has to push harder to move through less flexible vessels. Pressure builds.

Here is what stopped me when I read this:

Exercise improves cardiovascular efficiency. It strengthens the heart, improves circulation, lowers resting heart rate, reduces inflammation. It does a remarkable number of things. But it does not directly repair endothelial function. It does not restore the endothelium's ability to produce sustained nitric oxide.

I had been training the cardiovascular system — the pump and the pipes — without addressing the lining of the pipes themselves.

My heart was strong. My arteries were stiffening. And no amount of zone 2 cardio was going to change that.

This is why fit men get elevated blood pressure and cannot explain it. Not because they are doing something wrong. Because they are doing everything right for the wrong problem.
