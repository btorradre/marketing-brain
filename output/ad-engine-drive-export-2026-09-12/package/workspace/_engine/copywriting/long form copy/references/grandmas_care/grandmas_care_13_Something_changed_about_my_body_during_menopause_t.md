Something changed about my body during menopause that nobody warned me about. It wasn't the hot flashes.

It wasn't the mood swings, the brain fog, or the nights I'd kick off every blanket and still wake up drenched.

Those I expected. Those were in the books, the blogs, the pamphlets my OBGYN handed me.

Nobody mentioned the smell.

This unfamiliar, stale, almost greasy odor that showed up one day and just… never left. Not sweat. Not regular BO. Something else entirely. Like old clothes left in a damp closet.

Except it was coming from me.

My neck. My chest. Under my arms. Places that never smelled like anything before menopause.

I remember standing in my bathroom one night, fresh out of the shower, towel still wrapped around me, and I could already smell it. I had literally just scrubbed every inch of my body. Hot water, exfoliating washcloth, an entire lather of my Dove body wash.

And it was already there.

That's when the shame really set in.

I stopped hugging my daughter as long. Started keeping distance from coworkers. I'd re-apply deodorant three, four times a day. Carry body spray in my purse like some kind of teenager.

I tried clinical-strength antiperspirant. Antibacterial soap. Charcoal soap. Tea tree oil. That crystal deodorant thing someone on Facebook swore changed her life.

Every single product promised to "eliminate body odor."

None of them touched this.

I even went back to my OBGYN. Told her everything. She checked my hormones, did bloodwork. Everything came back "within normal range."

She looked at me and said, "It's probably just a menopause thing."

A menopause thing. Like I was supposed to just accept it. Add it to the list between night sweats and brain fog and move on.

That's the part that broke me a little. Not the smell itself — the loneliness of it. This thing menopause was doing to my body that I couldn't control, that nobody seemed to have a real answer for. Just "it's hormonal" and a shrug.

I'd google it at 1 am. "Body odor during menopause." "Why do I smell different since menopause." "Menopausal body odor won't go away."

Every article said the same useless things. Shower more. Wear breathable fabrics. Try probiotics.

As if the problem was my hygiene.

I'm a clean person. I've always been a clean person. This wasn't a cleanliness issue. But everything out there treated it like one.

Then my husband and I went to Japan.

It was our 30th anniversary. We'd been planning it for two years. Kyoto, Tokyo, the whole trip. And honestly, I almost didn't go. The thought of being on a plane for 14 hours, sweating in a foreign country, worrying about this smell the entire time — I nearly told him I didn't want to do it anymore.

I'm glad I didn't.

Because something in Japan stopped me in my tracks.

We were at a ryokan — a traditional inn — in Kyoto. Shared bathing area, beautiful hot spring. And I noticed the older Japanese women around me. Women my age, older even. Seventies, eighties.

None of them had this smell.

Not masked with perfume. Not covered up. Just… absent. Clean skin. Fresh. Like it wasn't even a thing.

I thought maybe it was diet. Genetics. Something I couldn't replicate.

But then I saw what was in their bathing area. These small, amber-colored soap bars. Every single bathroom we visited during that trip — hotels, ryokans, even the Airbnb — had some version of the same soap. With a persimmon on the label.

I asked our host about it. She kind of laughed — not in a mean way, more like I'd asked why they use rice cookers. Like it was that obvious.

She said something like, "Oh, that's kakishibu. Persimmon soap. It's for kareishū — the smell that comes with age. Everyone uses it."

That was it. No sales pitch. No long explanation. Just — everyone uses it. Like it was the most normal thing in the world.

I nodded and smiled, but inside my brain was on fire. They had a word for this. They had a product for this. On every shelf. In every bathroom. And I'd been crying in mine for a year.

When I got home, I couldn't stop thinking about it. I started looking it up. And what I found made me furious.

There's this compound called 2-nonenal. Apparently scientists have known about this since 2001. Twenty-plus years. And somehow nobody thought to mention it to the millions of women going through menopause every year. Basically, as you go through menopause, your skin starts pushing out this oily chemical. It's a byproduct of fats breaking down differently in your skin as your hormones shift.

Here's the part that changed everything for me:

This stuff is oily. Like, actually oil-based. Which means water can't touch it. Regular soap can't dissolve it. Doesn't matter how expensive it is, doesn't matter how hard you scrub. You rinse, it stays. It just sits on your skin like a greasy film that no shower can cut through.

And here's what made my stomach drop:

It gets worse. Every year. Your body doesn't just start making this stuff during menopause and stop. It makes more of it as you get older. More every year. What I was smelling at 56 was going to be stronger at 58. And worse at 60.

That's why it felt like it was getting worse. Because it was. And it wasn't going to stop on its own.

Every failed product suddenly made sense. My soap wasn't built to handle this. No regular soap is.

But that persimmon stuff — the kakishibu our host mentioned like it was nothing — that works completely differently. It doesn't try to rinse the smell away. It grabs onto that oily nonenal and breaks it apart. Like how dish soap cuts through grease on a pan — except for your skin. It's the only natural thing that does this.

Japanese women have known this for generations. We just never got the memo.

I bought three bars before we flew home.

First morning back. I lathered up, let it sit on my skin for a couple minutes, rinsed off.

By midday I did what I always do — the secret sniff check. The one I'd been dreading since menopause began.

Nothing.

Not a masked smell. Not a different smell. Nothing. Clean skin. Just… me.

I actually laughed. Standing in my kitchen, sniffing my own wrist, laughing. Because for the first time in over a year I smelled like a person who just showered. At 3 in the afternoon.

By the end of the first week, I stopped carrying body spray. By the second week, I threw out the three deodorants I'd been layering since menopause started.

My skin wasn't dry either. Every "strong" soap I'd tried left me stripped and tight. This one didn't. My arms, my legs — softer than they'd been in years.

I hugged my daughter last Saturday. A real hug. Not the quick side-squeeze I'd been doing since all this began. And I didn't think about it once.

I've since told three friends. Two of them texted me within a week. Same reaction. Same disbelief.

That's what nobody tells you about menopause body odor. It's not really about the smell. It's about all the tiny ways you shrink your life around it. The hugs you cut short. The seats you avoid. The way you hold your arms a little tighter in a meeting.

And the longer you wait, the harder it gets. Not just emotionally — chemically. Your body is producing more of this compound right now than it did six months ago. And six months from now it'll be worse than today. The distance becomes habit. You stop reaching for people. And one day you realize you've been pulling away for months and nobody said anything because they didn't know how.

That's the part that scares me when I think back. Not the smell. How quickly I was disappearing from my own life.

If any of this sounds like your life right now — the shame, the confusion, the products that don't work, the doctor who shrugs and says "it's menopause" — you deserve to know what's actually happening. And that women on the other side of the world figured this out a long time ago.
