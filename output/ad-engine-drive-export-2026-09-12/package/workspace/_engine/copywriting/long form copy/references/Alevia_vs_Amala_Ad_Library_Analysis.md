## ALEVIA — COPY BREAKDOWN

### Ad 1: Dr. Michelle T. Carter (Authority-Educational) — ~900 ads

**Hook:** "The number one thing to lower your cholesterol is not fish oil, it's not oatmeal, and it's definitely not salmon. You're also probably waking up at 3am."

**Why it works:**
- Opens with a NEGATION that disrupts the reader's existing solution set. The reader who's been taking fish oil or eating oatmeal immediately feels called out.
- Stacks unexpected symptoms (3am waking, sugar cravings, hair thinning, joint pain) that the reader has but hasn't connected to cholesterol. This creates a "how does she know?" reaction that builds authority.
- No story. No character. The reader IS the character because the symptoms are HER symptoms.

**Structure:**
1. Negation hook (what it ISN'T) — ~50 words
2. Symptom stacking (the reader self-identifies) — ~80 words
3. Fear amplification ("most people over 40 tragically ignore these") — ~30 words
4. Mechanism education (free radicals → oxidized LDL → plaque) — ~200 words
5. The gap ("your LDL number doesn't tell you if it's damaged") — ~50 words
6. Solution introduction (Amla / emblicanins) — ~150 words
7. Clinical trial results — ~80 words
8. Statin contrast (forces vs. protects) — ~80 words
9. No side effects selling point — ~50 words
10. Product qualification framework ("most Amla products are garbage, here's what to look for") — ~150 words
11. Brand recommendation (Alevia) — ~30 words
12. CTA — ~20 words

**Total: ~1,000-1,200 words. Short for "long-form" copy.**

**What's different from narrative copy:**
- No personal introduction. The authority speaks AS an authority from line 1.
- No emotional clearing. Symptom recognition replaces emotional scenes. The reader doesn't need to feel understood — she needs to feel IDENTIFIED.
- No discovery arc. The mechanism is taught directly, not discovered through a character's research journey.
- No progressive results. No "Day 7... Day 14... Month 2" timeline. Just clinical trial data.
- No product stumbled upon. The authority explicitly recommends after qualifying.
- No emotional circuit to close. The copy ends on product qualification and CTA, not on a restored life moment.

**Why it scales:** The copy doesn't depend on narrative immersion. It works the same way whether the image is a surgeon in scrubs, a close-up of arteries, or a bottle of pills. That's why Alevia can test 900+ images against the same copy — the copy carries its own weight without needing visual storytelling support.

### Ad 2: Barber / Cholesterol Support Group (Narrative)

**Hook:** "I've been on Atorvastatin for 9 years. I've been a barber for 37. A husband for 33. A grandfather for 7. The drug gave me perfect cholesterol. Then it destroyed my hands."

**Why it works:**
- Personal introduction sequence: identity markers stacked quickly (barber, husband, grandfather, years of service). You know this person in one sentence.
- The paradox hook: the drug that was supposed to help destroyed the thing his identity depends on (his hands = his career as a barber).
- Vulnerability is immediate — a man admitting the thing that defines him was taken away.

**Structure:** Narrative-driven. Follows the standard belief-shift architecture with personal intro, emotional clearing, mechanism discovery, product encounter, results.

### Ad 3: Father / Widowmaker (Narrative - Short)

**Hook:** "My father took Atorvastatin for twelve years. Never missed a dose. His LDL stayed under 100 the entire time. His doctor called him a model patient. He died on the floor of his wood shop. Widowmaker heart attack. 71 years old."

**Why it works:**
- Death hook — the most powerful pattern interrupt in direct response.
- The paradox is devastating: perfect compliance, perfect numbers, dead anyway.
- Short sentences create a drumbeat pacing that mirrors the shock.
- Sets up the entire mechanism argument in 4 sentences without mentioning it: if perfect LDL numbers didn't prevent the heart attack, then LDL numbers aren't the right measure.

### What Amala Does Consistently:

**Scene-Level Specificity:** "1:47 AM," "Engine Company 14 in Scranton, Pennsylvania," "the floor of his wood shop." Every scene has a time, a place, and a sensory detail. Never summaries.

**Mechanism Delivery Through Story:** The oxidized LDL mechanism is always taught through a character's discovery — late-night research, a colleague's tip, a chart pattern recognized. Never delivered directly.

**Product as Afterthought:** PrimeCell H2 enters in the last 15-20% of the copy. Named once or twice. Referenced by description afterward. Never pitched.

**Binary Close:** Two paths. Status quo vs. narrator's choice. Phrased as a life decision, not a purchase decision.

## KEY DIFFERENCES — WHAT MATTERS FOR THE SKILL

### 1. Alevia Proves Authority Copy Works at Scale

The Dr. Carter copy is Alevia's most-deployed ad by a factor of 10x. It breaks narrative rules and still works because it operates on different levers: symptom recognition, authority credibility, and direct education. The skill must account for this architecture.

### 2. Amala Proves Narrative Diversity Matters

18+ unique angles means Amala is testing creative diversity, not image diversity. Each angle is a completely different emotional world with a different narrator, different stakes, and different entry point into the same mechanism. The skill's anti-mimicry rules are validated by Amala's approach.

### 3. Both Use the Same Mechanism Differently

Both brands sell through the oxidized LDL mechanism. But Alevia teaches it in ~200 words through direct explanation. Amala teaches it in ~800 words through a character's discovery arc. Same information, different delivery vehicles. The architecture determines the mechanism section's length and style — not the mechanism's complexity.

### 4. Hook Architecture Is Architecture-Dependent

Alevia's authority copy hooks through symptom negation + stacking ("not fish oil, not oatmeal"). Amala's narrative copy hooks through character paradox ("perfect numbers, dead anyway") or identity declaration ("I'm a Board-Certified Emergency Medicine Specialist"). The hook type must match the copy architecture.

### 5. Product Integration Is Architecture-Dependent

Alevia: Authority explicitly recommends after qualifying. Product appears at Position 4-5 with direct endorsement.
Amala: Narrator stumbles upon product through story. Product appears at Position 2-3 with reluctant discovery.

The skill's "product stumbled upon" rule (Law 10) only applies to narrative architecture. Authority copy uses direct recommendation — and that's not a flaw, it's the architecture working as designed.

### 6. Copy Length Is Architecture-Dependent

Alevia's authority copy: ~1,000-1,200 words. Efficient. Direct. Done.
Amala's narrative copy: ~2,500-3,500 words. Earned through emotional architecture.

Neither is "better." The architecture determines the natural length. Authority copy that runs 3,000 words is padding. Narrative copy that runs 1,000 words didn't earn its emotional stakes.
