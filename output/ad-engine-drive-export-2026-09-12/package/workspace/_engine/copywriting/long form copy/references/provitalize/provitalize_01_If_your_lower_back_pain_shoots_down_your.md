If your lower back pain shoots down your thigh but stops before the knee,
it's probably not sciatica.

Mine hit worst getting out of the car.
Here's what I learned from one of the world's top menopause experts:

========

We were getting out of the car at my daughter's house when my lower back seized up.
Not a pulled muscle.
Not a sudden twist.

Just a white-hot bolt of pain that shot through my lower back and buttock the moment I stood.

I grabbed the car door so hard my knuckles turned white.

My husband came around to help, smiling at first…
until he saw my face.

Because this wasn't a normal ache.

This was the kind of pain that shuts your body down mid-movement.

Sharp.
Stabbing.
Like someone drove a hot screwdriver straight into the base of my spine and twisted.

I laughed it off.
Of course I did.

"Just stiff from the drive," I said.

But inside, my stomach dropped.

Because this wasn't the first time.

========

For months, sitting had become a negotiation.

Twenty minutes in a chair and my lower back would throb like someone was pressing their thumb into the base of my spine.

I started dreading car rides.

Not because of traffic.

Because I knew that by the time we arrived, I'd have to sit there for an extra minute…
working up the courage to stand…
praying my back wouldn't lock up in front of everyone.

Climbing stairs felt like betrayal.

Every. Single. Step.

That stabbing pain in my buttock would shoot down my thigh — never quite reaching my knee, but close enough to make me grip the railing like my life depended on it.

I started avoiding my daughter's house.

She lives on the second floor.
