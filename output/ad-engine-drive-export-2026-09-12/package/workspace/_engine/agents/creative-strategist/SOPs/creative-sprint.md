# Creative Sprint SOP

## Overview
A creative sprint is a structured production cycle designed to generate 5-10 production-ready ads across formats while maintaining strategic coherence, thematic variety, and quality consistency. This SOP moves you from raw material to filed, Copy Chief-graded creative in a single focused session.

**Typical sprint duration:** 6-8 hours for 5-8 pieces
**Team size:** 1 Creative Strategist (you), reference to Market Analyst intel, access to skill files

## Pre-Production Checklist

Before writing your first word, complete this 10-point checklist. These gates prevent false starts and wasted drafts.

### 1. Market Intelligence Review
- [ ] Read the most recent [[/agents/market-analyst/intel-drops/]] file
- [ ] Extract and document:
  - Saturated angles (do not use)
  - Emerging angles (high priority)
  - Active villains (what beliefs need shifting?)
  - Recent avatar discoveries (updated pain points, language shifts)
  - Competitive positioning changes
- [ ] Cross-reference against existing creative in vault
  - Which angles are already covered?
  - Which mechanisms have been employed?
  - Where are the gaps?

### 2. Product & Avatar Definition
- [ ] Specify target product: Lunessa | Motilli | Velantra
- [ ] Specify target avatar:
  - Demographics: age, gender, geography, income
  - Psychographics: core pain, desired outcome, current belief, villain
  - Language markers: how do they talk about the problem?
  - Trust status: do they believe this is solvable? Do they believe this product solves it?
- [ ] Confirm awareness level: Unaware → Problem-Aware → Solution-Aware → Product-Aware → Most Aware
- [ ] Confirm sophistication stage (Schwartz): 1 (unaware), 2 (aware, resistant), 3 (aware, exploring), 4 (ready to try), 5 (committed)

### 3. Funnel Architecture Clarity
- [ ] Define funnel structure:
  - 2-piece funnel: Lead ad → Product page (no bridge)
  - 3-piece funnel: Lead ad → Bridge/PSA → Product page
  - Extended: Lead ad → Bridge #1 → Bridge #2 → Product page
- [ ] Identify which pieces you're writing in this sprint (all pieces? only lead ads?)
- [ ] Map the Exit-State → Entry-State for each handoff point
  - Exit-State: What emotional/logical state does Piece A leave the reader in?
  - Entry-State: What emotional/logical state does Piece B require them to be in?
  - **Must match or there's a fracture**

### 4. Angle Inventory & Selection
- [ ] Create working list of 4-8 potential angles:
  - Source 1: Market Analyst intel (unsaturated angles they've identified)
  - Source 2: Vault search (which angles haven't we used yet for this product?)
  - Source 3: Reference library ([[/long form copy/references/]], [[/video ads/references (transcripts)/]]) (what angles do competitors use that we haven't?)
  - Source 4: Villain architecture (what beliefs need shifting? What villain haven't we torn down yet?)
- [ ] For each angle, write one-sentence hypothesis:
  - Example: "The Fatigue Spiral Angle: Most tired moms blame themselves for low energy, but it's actually caused by [specific mechanism]. This angle tears down that internal villain."
- [ ] Rank by strategic value:
  - High-priority angles: unsaturated + aligned with current Market Analyst intel
  - Medium-priority: solid but already explored once
  - Low-priority: saturated or low relevance to current avatar
- [ ] **Select 3-5 angles for sprint** (more if you're producing volume)

### 5. Concept Design (Per Angle)
For each angle, design a distinct concept **before** writing. This prevents mid-draft direction changes.

- [ ] **Narrator choice**: Who is the voice?
  - Guide (expert, external, teaching)
  - Insider (peer, already won, sharing secret)
  - Protagonist (you, the customer, telling your story)
  - Other (villain confession, third-party testimonial)

- [ ] **Posture**: What's the relationship to the reader?
  - Urgent (time is running out)
  - Patient (we have time to solve this right)
  - Angry (at the villain, not the reader)
  - Joyful (celebrating the possibility)
  - Conspiratorial (we know something others don't)

- [ ] **Temperature**: Emotional intensity and pacing speed
  - Hot (fast-moving, high-energy, visceral)
  - Warm (personal, relatable, steady)
  - Cool (intellectual, analytical, evidence-heavy)

- [ ] **Relationship to product**: Where on the integration spectrum?
  - Invisible (product not mentioned until the very end, if at all)
  - Peripheral (mentioned once or twice, not the focus)
  - Supporting (product introduced mid-way, supports the mechanism)
  - Central (product is the hero, primary focus)

- [ ] **Villain assignment**: What belief must shift?
  - External villain: "It's the market's fault. It's the industry. It's the product-category."
  - Internal villain: "I'm not disciplined enough. I'm not smart enough. I'm broken."
  - Philosophical villain: "Nothing can really change. This is just how it is."
  - **Tear down one villain per piece.** (Don't try to demolish all three in a single ad.)

**Example concept brief:**
- Angle: Fatigue Spiral
- Narrator: Insider (mom who already solved it)
- Posture: Angry (at the belief system that blamed her)
- Temperature: Warm (personal, slow reveal)
- Product integration: Peripheral (introduced as one piece of the solution)
- Villain: Internal (self-blame for low energy; belief that "I'm just lazy")

### 6. Mechanism Clarity
- [ ] For each angle, articulate the **mechanism**: the "why" the solution works
  - Not what the product is, but how it functions structurally
  - Example mechanism: "It addresses the three root causes simultaneously: energy substrate availability, sustained release kinetics, and neurochemical optimization for dopamine-gated motivation."
  - This mechanism is the throughline of your copy. Everything else hangs on this.
- [ ] Cross-check mechanism against [[DR-SOP-Obsidian.md]] — Product Integration Spectrum
  - Does this mechanism require visibility of the product? Or is it more about the problem/solution architecture?
  - Adjust product integration position based on how central the mechanism is to the explanation

### 7. Format Selection & Batch Planning
- [ ] Review [[format-selection-guide.md]] for each angle
- [ ] For each angle, select primary format:
  - Long-form copy (500-2,000 words) → Facebook primary text, sales page, email
  - Video script (60 seconds, 90 seconds, 3 minutes, VSL) → YouTube, Facebook video, landing page hero
  - Advertorial → Article-style, credibility-first positioning
  - Listicle → Top X Reasons, X Myths, X Signs, etc.
  - Hook only → Testing opening lines, A/B testing angles quickly
  - Static ad concept → Visual + 30-word copy pair
  - Native image brief → Concept for image + supporting copy

- [ ] **Batch production strategy**: Create a production sequence
  - Example sequence for 7-piece sprint:
    1. Hook #1 (Angle A) — 15 min
    2. Hook #2 (Angle B) — 15 min
    3. Long-form (Angle A) — 75 min (using Hook #1)
    4. Long-form (Angle B) — 75 min (using Hook #2)
    5. Advertorial (Angle C) — 60 min
    6. Video script 60s (Angle D) — 45 min
    7. Listicle (Angle E) — 50 min
  - **Why this order?** Hooks are quick wins. They build momentum. Long-form takes longer, so batch them back-to-back. Shorter formats (advertorial, video, listicle) close the sprint, when mental fatigue is setting in but the work is smaller.

### 8. Anti-Mimicry Audit
- [ ] For each angle, run the **8-dimension anti-mimicry check** ([[DR-SOP-Obsidian.md]] — Anti-Mimicry System):
  1. **Villain assignment**: Is this a villain we've already torn down recently in the vault?
  2. **Mechanism category**: Is this a mechanism type (process, ingredient, relationship, positioning, permission, validation, simplification, amplification) we've already used?
  3. **Narrative structure**: Does the story path feel distinct from recent creative?
  4. **Hook opening**: Is the opening line different from recent hooks?
  5. **Temperature/posture**: Does the voice feel different from recent ads?
  6. **Avatar language**: Does this copy speak to this avatar differently than recent work?
  7. **Proof architecture**: Is the evidence structure (testimonial, data, logic, emotion) different?
  8. **Call-to-action framing**: Is the CTA approach distinct from recent pieces?

- [ ] **Fail threshold**: If 3+ dimensions fail, redesign the angle or concept. Do not proceed to writing with 3+ failures.
- [ ] **Document audit result** in your angle notes:
  - Example: "Angle A audit: PASS (4/8 matches with Angle X from 2 weeks ago, but villain is different, narrative is distinct, hook is new)"
  - Example: "Angle C audit: FAIL (3/8 failures: same villain as recent ad, similar mechanism type, identical narrative structure — redesign or archive)"

### 9. Skill File Preparation
- [ ] Load all relevant skill files for selected formats:
  - For long-form: [[/long form copy/skills/long-form-copy/]] (5 belief shifts, 16 pacing laws, 13 narrative beats)
  - For video: [[/video ads/skills/video-ad-scripts/]] (script templates, timing, stage directions)
  - For advertorial: [[/advertorial/copy skill files/]] (6 frameworks, confirm-and-escalate)
  - For listicle: [[/listicle/skill/]]
  - For hooks: [[hook-generation-v2]]
  - For statics: [[/statics/branded-static-ads/SKILL.md]]
  - For native images: [[/native images/skill/]]

- [ ] Read opening sections of each skill file (not full, just enough to remember the system)
- [ ] **Bookmark or highlight** key sections you'll reference during writing

### 10. Time Estimation & Schedule
- [ ] Estimate production time per piece:
  - Hook: 15-20 min
  - Long-form (500-750 words): 45-60 min
  - Long-form (1,000+ words): 75-120 min
  - Video script (60s): 30-45 min
  - Video script (3+ min): 60-90 min
  - Advertorial: 45-75 min
  - Listicle: 45-60 min
  - Static concept: 20-30 min
  - Native image brief: 15-25 min

- [ ] Add 50% time buffer (writing always takes longer)
- [ ] Add 20% time for self-assessment and revision
- [ ] Calculate total sprint time
- [ ] **Block focus time**: Minimum 2-hour uninterrupted blocks (shorter pieces), 3-4 hour blocks for full long-form

## Step-by-Step Sprint Workflow

### PHASE 1: Intelligence & Design (1.5 hours total)

#### Step 1: Market Intelligence (15 minutes)
```
Task: Read and extract from Market Analyst intel-drops
Output: Unsaturated angle list, villain map, avatar language shifts
```

1. Navigate to [[/agents/market-analyst/intel-drops/]]
2. Open most recent intel-drop file
3. Read "Unsaturated Angles" section — copy these to your working notes
4. Read "Villain Architecture" section — note active external, internal, philosophical villains
5. Read "Avatar Language Shifts" — note new pain points or language markers
6. Read "Competitive Positioning" — what are we not doing that competitors are?
7. **Create working document**: `SPRINT_[DATE]_NOTES.md` with extracted intel

#### Step 2: Vault Inventory (10 minutes)
```
Task: Identify what creative already exists for this product
Output: Gap map (what angles, mechanisms, formats have we NOT done?)
```

1. Search vault for creative on your target product (e.g., search for "Lunessa" in `/output/`)
2. List existing angles: "Fatigue Spiral, Permission, Metabolism, Social Proof"
3. List existing formats: "2 long-forms, 3 hooks, 1 video script"
4. List existing mechanisms: "Process (multi-step solution), Ingredient (key compounds), Validation (doctor endorsement)"
5. **Identify gaps**:
   - Angle gap: "We haven't used the Body Confidence angle yet"
   - Format gap: "No advertorials yet; only long-form and video"
   - Mechanism gap: "No relationship-based mechanism (e.g., trust/permission from trusted figure)"
   - Villain gap: "No philosophical villain copy (everything else targets internal or external)"

#### Step 3: Angle Selection (15 minutes)
```
Task: Pick 3-5 angles for the sprint
Output: Ranked angle list with one-sentence hypotheses
```

1. Combine intel list with gap map
2. Create angle candidates:
   - **From intel (unsaturated)**: "Metabolism Shutdown Angle" (Market Analyst flagged as emerging)
   - **From gap (untouched)**: "Body Confidence Angle" (vault search shows we haven't touched this)
   - **From reference library**: Search [[/long form copy/references/]] for competitor angles we haven't tried
3. For each angle, write hypothesis:
   - Example: "Metabolism Shutdown: Most people think Lunessa is for 'energy boosters' but it actually addresses metabolic dysfunction. This angle shifts belief from 'I need a stronger stimulant' to 'My metabolism is broken and needs repair.'"
4. Rank by strategic value:
   - **Tier 1** (highest priority): Unsaturated + fills gap + aligned with Market Analyst intent
   - **Tier 2** (solid): Fills gap but already somewhat explored elsewhere
   - **Tier 3** (bonus): Good backup if you finish early
5. **Select final angles for sprint**: Pick top 3-5 (depends on sprint duration and format mix)

#### Step 4: Concept Design (30 minutes)
```
Task: Design distinct concept for each angle
Output: Concept brief per angle (narrator, posture, temperature, villain, product integration)
```

For each selected angle, complete this concept worksheet:

**Angle: [Name]**
- Villain being torn down: [external | internal | philosophical] — [specific belief]
- Narrator choice: [Guide | Insider | Protagonist | Other] — [brief description]
- Posture: [Urgent | Patient | Angry | Joyful | Conspiratorial] — [why this posture?]
- Temperature: [Hot | Warm | Cool] — [pacing speed and emotional intensity]
- Product integration position: [Invisible | Peripheral | Supporting | Central]
- Avatar language hooks: [2-3 specific words/phrases from avatar's vocabulary]
- Mechanism category: [Process | Ingredient | Relationship | Positioning | Permission | Validation | Simplification | Amplification]
- Exit state (for funnel congruence): [What belief/emotion should reader have after this ad?]

**Example:**
```
Angle: Metabolism Shutdown
Villain: Internal (belief: "I'm broken and can't fix it")
Narrator: Insider (woman who already healed her metabolism, 2 years ago)
Posture: Angry (at the diet industry that caused the damage)
Temperature: Warm (slow, personal revelation)
Product integration: Supporting (introduced as one piece of a multi-part solution)
Avatar language: "Numb energy," "my body doesn't respond anymore," "I've tried everything"
Mechanism: Multi-step process (reset → rebuild → sustain) where Lunessa handles the biochemical repair phase
Exit state: "My metabolism isn't broken. It's just dormant. And there's a way to wake it up."
```

### PHASE 2: Brief Creation (30 minutes total)

#### Step 5: Write Creative Briefs
```
Task: Author one brief per angle/format combo
Output: Filled-out [[creative-brief-template.md]] files
```

For each angle selected:
1. Open [[brief-creation.md]] SOP
2. Use [[creative-brief-template.md]] to structure the brief
3. Fill in all required sections:
   - Desire statement (what does the avatar want?)
   - Angle (what belief are we targeting?)
   - Concept (narrator, posture, temperature, product integration)
   - Mechanism (the "why" it works)
   - Avatar awareness level + sophistication stage
   - Villain(s) to tear down
   - Product integration specifics (mention count? feature details? benefit focus?)
   - Close type (soft ask | trial ask | hard ask)
   - Funnel context (where does this piece sit?)
4. Save as `/creative-briefs/[DATE]_[PRODUCT]_[ANGLE]_[FORMAT]_brief.md`
5. **Review brief before proceeding** — Does it fully specify what you're about to write? Is anything ambiguous?

### PHASE 3: Production (Variable duration based on format)

#### Step 6: Hook Generation (Per Hook)
```
Task: Generate opening line(s) BEFORE drafting body copy
Output: Chosen hook + hook type classification
Time: 15-20 min per hook (includes iteration)
```

**Hook-first methodology:**
Your hook is the throughline of your entire piece. Everything after the hook is proof of the hook's promise. Write the hook first, alone, with full attention.

1. Open [[hook-generation-v2]]
2. Review the 4-part hook structure: Problem statement → Specificity → Disbelief → Resolution promise
3. Generate 5-7 hook variations using the hook framework:
   - **Disbelief hook**: "Everyone thinks [common belief], but [counter-evidence]"
   - **Curiosity hook**: "What if [unexpected premise]?"
   - **Specificity hook**: "[Exact result] in [exact timeframe] using [mechanism]"
   - **Counterintuitive hook**: "[Opposite of what they believe] is actually [truth]"
   - **Villain-focused hook**: "The [industry/belief/person] doesn't want you to know [truth]"
   - **Permission hook**: "You have permission to [what they thought was wrong/impossible]"
   - **Simplification hook**: "Everyone's been overcomplicating [problem]. The real solution is [simple mechanism]"

4. **Test each hook against your concept brief**:
   - Does it promise the right belief shift?
   - Does it align with your narrator and posture?
   - Does it create curiosity or urgency appropriate to your temperature?
5. **Choose winning hook** — one that makes you want to read the rest
6. **Classify hook type**: Disbelief? Curiosity? Specificity? Counterintuitive?
7. Document in your working notes: `Hook A (Curiosity): [your hook]`

#### Step 7: Full Copy Production (Per Format)
```
Task: Draft complete copy piece following skill file structure
Output: Complete first draft with full structure
Time: 30-120 min depending on format
```

**For Long-Form (500-2,000 words):**
1. Open [[/long form copy/skills/long-form-copy/]]
2. Reference the 13 narrative beats (setup, villain reveal, mechanism intro, etc.)
3. Reference the 16 pacing laws (specificity density, vulnerability reveal timing, proof placement, etc.)
4. Reference the 5 belief shifts framework (what belief needs to change, and when?)
5. Draft in this sequence:
   - **Hook + opening paragraph** (Promise the belief shift)
   - **Problem exposition** (Why does this belief exist? Social proof for the problem.)
   - **Villain reveal** (This is who/what's responsible for the bad belief)
   - **Avatar self-recognition** (Does this belief apply to you? Signs/symptoms)
   - **Mechanism introduction** (There's another way)
   - **Mechanism explanation** (Here's how it works, step by step)
   - **Product introduction** (If peripheral or central integration — introduce here)
   - **Proof & validation** (Testimonial, data, logic, or emotional resonance)
   - **Future-state visualization** (What's possible when this belief shifts?)
   - **Close & call-to-action** (What's the next step?)
6. **Aim for first draft completion** — don't edit mid-draft. Let it flow.
7. Save as `/working-drafts/[DATE]_[PRODUCT]_[ANGLE]_LongForm_DRAFT1.md`

**For Video Scripts (60-180 seconds):**
1. Open [[/video ads/skills/video-ad-scripts/]]
2. Reference script template for your target duration
3. Draft in this sequence:
   - **Hook (0-3 seconds)**: Visual hook + opening line
   - **Problem setup (3-10 sec)**: Show the pain, the frustration, the belief
   - **Villain reveal (10-15 sec)**: What's causing this?
   - **Solution intro (15-20 sec)**: There's another way
   - **Mechanism demo (20-45 sec)**: Show/explain how it works
   - **Product reveal (45-50 sec)**: Introduce the product (if not central, skip or minimize)
   - **Social proof (50-55 sec)**: Quick testimonial, stat, or validator
   - **Close & CTA (55-60 sec)**: What to do next + urgency
4. Include stage directions (VISUAL: [description], VOICEOVER: [dialogue], ONSCREEN TEXT: [text])
5. Save as `/working-drafts/[DATE]_[PRODUCT]_[ANGLE]_VideoScript60s_DRAFT1.md`

**For Advertorials:**
1. Open [[/advertorial/copy skill files/]]
2. Select one of the 6 frameworks (Article Frame, Investigative Frame, Interview Frame, Narrative Frame, How-To Frame, Contrarian Frame)
3. Draft using the framework structure specific to your choice
4. Reference the "confirm-and-escalate" pattern to guide reader from headline → byline → body → offer
5. Save as `/working-drafts/[DATE]_[PRODUCT]_[ANGLE]_Advertorial_DRAFT1.md`

**For Listicles:**
1. Open [[/listicle/skill/]]
2. Draft listicle title: "[X] Reasons You're [Problem]" or "[X] Signs of [Villain]" etc.
3. Write intro paragraph (establish problem, tease list)
4. Write 5-7 list items (each 2-3 sentences, specific and resonant)
5. Write conclusion (shift to mechanism/solution mention or CTA)
6. Save as `/working-drafts/[DATE]_[PRODUCT]_[ANGLE]_Listicle_DRAFT1.md`

**For Static Concepts:**
1. Open [[/statics/branded-static-ads/SKILL.md]]
2. Design image concept: What visual would stop the scroll? (Describe in detail: color, composition, subject, emotion)
3. Write supporting copy (30 words max): Hook + one claim
4. Save as `/working-drafts/[DATE]_[PRODUCT]_[ANGLE]_StaticConcept_DRAFT1.md`

**For Native Image Briefs:**
1. Open [[/native images/skill/]]
2. Write detailed image concept (3-5 sentences describing visual, composition, mood)
3. Write caption copy (50-100 words): Hook + claim + soft CTA
4. Save as `/working-drafts/[DATE]_[PRODUCT]_[ANGLE]_NativeImageBrief_DRAFT1.md`

#### Step 8: Revision & Polish (Per Piece)
```
Task: Read draft aloud, fix flow/rhythm, tighten language
Output: Polished second draft ready for assessment
Time: 15-25 min per piece
```

1. Read your draft aloud (slowly, paying attention to rhythm)
2. Fix obvious issues:
   - Repetitive words or phrases → find alternative language
   - Awkward sentence rhythms → rewrite for flow
   - Vague language → replace with specific details
   - Weak transitions → strengthen connective tissue
3. Check for compliance with skill file structure:
   - Long-form: All 13 beats present? All 5 belief shifts accounted for?
   - Video: Timing correct? Stage directions clear?
   - Advertorial: Framework structure intact?
4. **Do NOT rewrite conceptually** — you're polishing, not redesigning
5. Save as `/working-drafts/[DATE]_[PRODUCT]_[ANGLE]_[FORMAT]_DRAFT2.md`

### PHASE 4: Quality Assessment (15 minutes total per piece)

#### Step 9: Copy Chief Self-Assessment
```
Task: Grade piece using Copy Chief framework
Output: Score (0-10) with breakdown across 5 dimensions + Kill List check
Time: 10-15 min per piece
```

**Full assessment protocol in [[self-assessment.md]].** Here's the summary:

1. **Re-read your piece with fresh eyes** (step away for 5 minutes first if possible)
2. **Score the 5 dimensions** (each 0-10, then average for overall score):
   - **Belief-Shift Architecture**: Does the piece move the reader from old belief → new belief? Is the shift clear and compelling?
   - **Dual-Track Narrative**: Does the piece work on both intellectual level (mechanism/logic) AND emotional level (story/feeling)?
   - **Pacing Law Compliance**: Does the specificity density, proof placement, mechanism reveal timing, vulnerability placement feel natural? Is there momentum?
   - **Voice & Authenticity**: Does the narrator voice feel real? Do the word choices match the avatar's vernacular? Is there texture/humanity?
   - **Structural Engineering**: Is the piece well-architected? Do beats flow naturally? Does it avoid forced transitions?

3. **Apply anti-inflation rules**:
   - Default starting score: 5
   - You earn every half-point above 5 based on evidence
   - No single dimension should score above 8 (no piece is perfect)
   - Average score rarely exceeds 7.5 (very good is rare)

4. **Check Kill List** (instant deductions if present):
   - AI tells (overly flowery prose, non-human clichés, list structures that scream AI)
   - Marketing tells ("limited time," "don't miss out," transparent urgency hacks)
   - Mechanism vagueness (mechanism should be specific and clear, not hand-wavy)
   - Naked asks (CTA without trust setup)
   - Villain absence (piece doesn't clearly identify what belief must shift)
   - Approval-seeking language ("if this resonates," "if you're like me")
   - If 1+ Kill List item found: Cap score at 7.0 maximum

5. **Document scores**:
   ```
   Belief-Shift Architecture: 7
   Dual-Track Narrative: 7.5
   Pacing Law Compliance: 6.5
   Voice & Authenticity: 7.5
   Structural Engineering: 7
   ---
   AVERAGE SCORE: 7.1
   Kill List check: PASSED
   Status: Production-ready
   ```

6. **Make decision**:
   - **6.5-7.4**: Production-ready (deploy in campaigns, OK to test)
   - **7.5+**: Scaling-ready (approved for volume deployment)
   - **Below 6.5**: Archive in `/rejected/` with revision notes. Do not use.

7. **Save assessment** in frontmatter of final file (see Step 11)

### PHASE 5: Funnel Congruence Check (5 minutes, if applicable)

#### Step 10: Multi-Piece Funnel Audit
```
Task: Verify pieces work together in sequence (if writing funnel)
Output: Congruence verification + revision notes (if needed)
Time: 5-10 min per funnel (not per piece)
Applies: Only if sprint includes multiple pieces meant to funnel together
```

**This step applies ONLY if you're writing 2+ pieces that are meant to work together in a funnel.**

If you're just writing individual hooks or isolated long-form ads:
- **Skip this step** (or do a light version for your own reference)

If you're writing a 3-piece funnel (lead ad → bridge → product page):
- **Complete the full audit** ([[funnel-congruence-check.md]])
- Check Exit-State / Entry-State for each transition
- Verify voice continuity, mechanism language match, temperature consistency, awareness progression, trust transfer

### PHASE 7: Sprint Closure (15 minutes)

#### Step 12: Sprint Review & Documentation
```
Task: Summarize sprint results and capture learnings
Output: Sprint summary document
Time: 15 min
```

## Batch Production Guidelines: 5-10 Ads in One Sprint

### Variety Protocol (Prevent Repetition Fatigue)
When producing multiple pieces in one sprint, enforce variety to maintain freshness:

1. **Vary the angles**: Never write two pieces with the same angle
2. **Vary the villains**: If Angle A targets an internal villain, Angle B should target external or philosophical
3. **Vary the formats**: Don't write 5 long-form pieces in a row. Sequence them: Hook → Long-form → Advertorial → Video → Listicle → Hook → Long-form
4. **Vary the narrator**: If Angle A uses an insider narrator, Angle B should use guide or protagonist
5. **Vary the temperature**: Alternate between hot (fast, urgent) and warm (slow, patient) pacing
6. **Vary the avatar language**: Different segments of your avatar base may use different vocabulary. Each piece should speak to a distinct micro-segment.

### Production Sequence: 8-Piece Sample Sprint

This sequence is designed to maintain momentum and quality:

**Block 1 (2 hours): Hooks + Brief Creation**
1. Hook #1 (Angle A) — 20 min
2. Hook #2 (Angle B) — 20 min
3. Hook #3 (Angle C) — 20 min
4. Write 3 creative briefs (30 min, can batch)

**Block 2 (3.5 hours): Long-Form**
5. Long-form #1 (Angle A, using Hook #1) — 90 min
6. Long-form #2 (Angle B, using Hook #2) — 90 min

**Block 3 (2 hours): Diverse Formats**
7. Advertorial (Angle C, using Hook #3) — 60 min
8. Video script 60s (Angle D, new hook) — 45 min

**Block 4 (1 hour): Assessment & Filing**
- Self-assess all 8 pieces (10 min each) — 80 min
- File with frontmatter — 40 min

**Total sprint time: 8.5 hours → 8 production-ready pieces**

### Time Efficiency Hacks

**Parallel Processing:**
- While one long-form draft sits (cooling your brain), work on a different format
- Assessment doesn't require the context that writing does — batch assess multiple pieces together
- File while reviewing (open template + file simultaneously)

**Template Reuse:**
- First hook of the sprint takes longer (you're warming up). Subsequent hooks are faster.
- First long-form establishes your pacing rhythm. Subsequent long-forms follow similar structure, reducing decision-making.
- First advertorial of the sprint requires framework selection. If you use the same framework for multiple angles, subsequent advertorials are faster.

**Context Switching Minimization:**
- Write both hooks in a row (you're in "hook mind")
- Write both long-forms in a row (you're in "long-form mind")
- Assess all pieces after production is complete (full context on all work)
- File all pieces at end of sprint (one-time folder navigation setup)

## Common Failure Modes & Prevention

### Failure Mode 1: Vague Mechanism = Low Scores
**Problem:** You wrote a piece without clearly articulating the mechanism. The reader doesn't understand *why* the solution works.

**Prevention:**
- Before writing, complete this sentence: "This solution works because [specific mechanism, 1-2 sentences]."
- If you can't complete the sentence clearly, the brief is incomplete. Go back and fix it.
- During writing, the mechanism should be crystal clear by halfway through the piece.

### Failure Mode 2: Angle Repetition = Anti-Mimicry Failure
**Problem:** You designed an angle that's too similar to recent work. Audit failed on 3+ dimensions.

**Prevention:**
- Complete the 8-dimension anti-mimicry audit BEFORE you write.
- If you fail 3+ dimensions, redesign the angle/concept before proceeding.
- Reference [[DR-SOP-Obsidian.md]] — Anti-Mimicry System section

### Failure Mode 3: No Belief Shift = Functional Failure
**Problem:** The piece is well-written, but it doesn't move the reader from old belief → new belief. Copy Chief score reflects this.

**Prevention:**
- Before writing, identify the exact belief you're shifting: "From [old belief] to [new belief]"
- This should be visible in your brief and referenced during writing
- Half of the piece is proof the old belief was wrong. Half is promise of the new belief.

### Failure Mode 4: Cold Funnel Fractures = Handoff Failure
**Problem:** You wrote multiple pieces for a funnel, but they don't flow together. Piece 2's entry state doesn't match Piece 1's exit state.

**Prevention:**
- Complete the Exit-State / Entry-State test [[funnel-congruence-check.md]] before filing
- If you find a fracture, revise one of the pieces before filing
- Test the funnel by reading pieces in sequence.

### Failure Mode 5: No Urgency in Hot Temperature Angles
**Problem:** You intended an urgent/hot piece, but the pacing is slow. Copy Chief flags as incongruent with brief.

**Prevention:**
- During writing, use short sentences for hot temperature
- Use longer, contemplative sentences for warm temperature
- Read your draft aloud to feel the pacing
- If it doesn't feel hot, make short/urgent edits before assessment
