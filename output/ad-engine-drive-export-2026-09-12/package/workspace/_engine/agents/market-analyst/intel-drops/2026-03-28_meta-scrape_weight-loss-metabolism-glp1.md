## Synthesis & Recommendations

### Angles Worth Testing
- **"Third ovary" reframe (from Emily Watts/Maple Lane):** This is the most innovative mechanism concept in today's swipe. The idea that your body is deliberately building belly fat as an emergency estrogen factory completely reframes the problem. Could be adapted for any menopause-adjacent product. The emotional payload — "you're not broken, your body is trying to save you" — is extremely powerful.
- **Insulin resistance as hidden epidemic (from Eve Baker/Select Nutrients):** The "40% of women are insulin-resistant but their doctors never catch it" stat, combined with the "standard blood tests won't flag it until prediabetic" villain, creates a strong paranoia-loop. Less novel than the third ovary angle but potentially higher-converting because it's more tangible.
- **Social proof via encounter (Eve Baker #2):** The baseball game scene — seeing a transformed woman in real life rather than online — is a fresh social proof delivery vehicle. Most ads use "I saw a post" or "my doctor told me." The in-person encounter feels more credible.

### Mechanism Innovations Spotted
- **"Third ovary" (Maple Lane):** Belly fat as emergency estrogen production site. Body defends it against dieting because it needs the estrogen. This is the standout mechanism innovation of the session.
- **Estrobolome pathway (Maple Lane):** Gut bacteria directly control estrogen signaling. Specific probiotic strains restore the estrobolome → body stops building emergency fat. More sophisticated than the standard "gut health" pitch.
- **Ceylon cinnamon delivery system (Select Nutrients):** L-Carnitine as the delivery vehicle that gets compounds across cell membranes. Differentiation from cheap Cassia cinnamon (liver damage angle) is effective villain work against the supplement category itself.

### Market Signals
- **GLP-1 positioning is being co-opted by non-GLP-1 products.** Select Nutrients calls their cinnamon supplement "GLP-1 Burn" — trading on the GLP-1 agonist trend (Ozempic, Wegovy) without being a GLP-1 drug. This is a smart positioning play that will likely attract regulatory attention eventually. Worth monitoring.
- **Menopause belly is the dominant emotional wound in women's weight loss right now.** Every ad in today's results targeted women 40+ with unexplained belly fat. The market sophistication is Stage 3-4 — audiences have heard the problem story before and need increasingly specific mechanisms to stay engaged.
- **Probiotic vs. cinnamon — two competing mechanism lanes.** Maple Lane uses gut probiotics (estrobolome). Select Nutrients uses Ceylon cinnamon (insulin receptors). Both target the same avatar with different biological stories. This competitive dynamic will be worth tracking.
- **All new brands today launched March 28, 2026 (today).** Every ad had "Started running on Mar 28, 2026" and "Low impression count." These are brand new launches, not established winners. The copy quality (especially Emily Watts) suggests professional copywriters, but we should re-check in 2-3 weeks to see which survive.

1. **Study the "third ovary" mechanism from Emily Watts/Maple Lane.** This is the best mechanism reframe I've seen in the menopause niche. The concept can be adapted and the naming convention is worth cataloging for future mechanism development work.
2. **Monitor Eve Baker/Select Nutrients for scaling signals.** With 5+ active ads all launched today, this brand is testing aggressively. If these ads survive past 7 days with increasing impression counts, they've found product-market fit and the copy will be worth deeper analysis.

This is a market signal about how powerful the GLP-1/Ozempic brand recognition has become. Our products could test GLP-1-adjacent positioning without making drug claims.
