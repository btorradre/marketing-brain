# Compare competitor ad copy strategies — Session Summary

**Date:** 2026-03-26

**Transcript:** [[conversation-log/transcripts/2026-03-26_compare-competitor-ad-copy-strategies_e5373f5e]]

## What Happened
Scraped and analyzed all 4 active Alevia long-form Facebook ads from Meta Ad Library. Extracted full copy, measured word counts, and identified two distinct architectural styles. Used findings to add 4 new components to the long-form copy skill. Packaged and delivered the updated skill as a downloadable .skill file.

## Key Decisions Made
- Alevia runs TWO architectures: mechanism-dominant (~850 words, parasites/amla ad) and story-dominant (3,300-5,500 words, cholesterol ads)
- New mechanism-dominant ratio codified: 30-40% emotional / 60-70% mechanism (inverted from story-dominant default)
- "Then I found X" remains banned — Law 10-B uses hybrid approach where mechanism inevitability and narrator stumble converge
- Instruction Close (Type 9) is only valid when mechanism-dominant architecture earned it — cannot be used on story-dominant pieces

## Insights & Learnings
- Alevia's average copy length is ~17,000 characters / ~3,400 words — significantly longer than most competitors
- Their mechanism-dominant ad (parasites/amla) is dramatically shorter (~850 words) vs. story-dominant pieces (3,300-5,500 words), suggesting mechanism-dominant architecture naturally compresses
- Alevia runs both architectures simultaneously, meaning market response supports both formats
- Symptom stacking replaces extended emotional clearing in mechanism-dominant architecture
