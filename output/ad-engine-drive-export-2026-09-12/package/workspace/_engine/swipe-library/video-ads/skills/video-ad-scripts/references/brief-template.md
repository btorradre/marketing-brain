# Video Editor Brief Template

Use this template when generating a video editor brief. A brief translates a finished script into a producible video. It should be 2-3 pages max, written for a video editor who needs to know exactly what to create without ambiguity.

---

## Brief Structure

### Section 1: YOUR #1 JOB
One sentence describing the core task. Example: "Replicate the reference ad's style and pacing for [Brand]'s [product] script. Organic-style doctor talking head for TikTok/Meta — iPhone feel, slightly raw."

### Section 2: ASSETS TABLE

| Asset | Link / Location |
|-------|----------------|
| Script (approved copy) | [link or inline] |
| Reference Ad | [Meta Ad Library link or video file] |
| AI UGC Doctor / Speaker Footage | [link to footage] |
| Content Library (B-Roll + Product Shots) | [link to assets folder] |

### Section 3: EDITING RULES

These rules govern the visual rhythm and production style:

- **Rhythm**: Never more than ~15 seconds of speaker talking without a visual interrupt (overlay, b-roll, or animation). Organic and diagram visuals are roughly 50/50 with speaker footage
- **Overlays**: Float over speaker's lower chest/frame — NOT full-screen. Stock/lifestyle photos or diagram (infographics, charts)
- **Full-screen b-roll**: Speaker disappears completely. Used for visceral/emotional beats ONLY
- **Captions**: TikTok-style, large bold, center-screen. Green (#2E7D32) for product/solution keywords. White for general captions
- **Transitions**: Hard cuts only. No swipes, zooms, or fancy transitions
- **Diagrams**: Clean, simple, medically credible infographics. Not cluttered. Not cartoonish
- **Sound**: Clean audio on speaker. B-roll can use ASMR audio or go silent under voiceover

### Section 4: VISUAL LAYER SYSTEM

Based on analysis of top-performing ads, every video uses these 6 visual layers:

1. **Speaker-to-camera** — ~50% of ad. Speaker in frame (white coat for expert, casual for UGC, car interior for personal discovery). Warm home-office or natural setting
2. **Floating overlays** — Organic stock/lifestyle photos or diagram (infographics, charts) that float over the speaker's lower frame. NOT full-screen
3. **Full-screen b-roll** — Nature/macro footage for visceral beats. Speaker disappears. Used sparingly for emotional emphasis
4. **Full-screen 3D animations** — Medical CGI renders for mechanism explanation. Hair follicle shrinking, biofilm dissolving, gut lining, etc. Used during the cause-effect chain
5. **Credential props** — Phone/paper held up to camera. Screenshots of studies, Reddit threads, medical articles. Builds credibility
6. **Product sequence** — Website → two bottles → single bottle label rotation. Appears ONLY in the final 10-15% of the video

### Section 5: VISUAL INTERRUPT TIMING

Based on Gemini frame-by-frame analysis of 12 top-performing ads:

- **Average interrupt frequency**: One visual change every 4-5 seconds (12-15 interrupts per minute)
- **Speaker hold (trust build)**: 3-5 seconds uninterrupted when establishing credentials
- **B-roll cut**: 1-3 seconds per insert. Always tied to what speaker just said
- **Text overlay timing**: Appears 0.5s AFTER speaker says keyword, stays 1.5-2s, disappears 0.5s before next point
- **Product close-up**: 1-2 seconds. Appears after mechanism education, never before

### Section 6: B-ROLL FILES TO USE

Reference the Content Library for available clips. Editor should pick what fits each beat — they do NOT need to use all clips. Key categories:

- Medical/scientific (scalp close-ups, gut diagrams, hormone charts)
- Lifestyle/relatable (shower scenes, morning routine, pillow hair)
- Product (bottle angles, gummy close-ups, label details, packaging)
- Results (before/after timeline photos)

### Section 7: DELIVERABLES

1. Final video: MP4, 9:16, 1080×1920, H.264, 30fps minimum
2. Project file (CapCut / Premiere / DaVinci)
3. Diagram/overlay assets as separate PNG/PSD files
4. Brief rationale: 3-5 sentences on editing choices

### Section 8: EVALUATION CRITERIA

| Criteria | Weight |
|----------|--------|
| Reference fidelity (matches style of reference ad) | 30% |
| Pacing & visual interrupts (every 4-5s, no dead air) | 20% |
| Diagram/overlay quality (clean, credible, not cluttered) | 20% |
| B-roll integration (supports script, doesn't distract) | 15% |
| Platform nativeness (feels TikTok/Reels native, not produced) | 15% |

## Brief Generation Rules

When generating a brief from a script:

1. Match the script's format (Expert Educator / Personal Discovery / Third-Party) to the appropriate visual layer emphasis:
   - Expert Educator: Heavier on diagrams, 3D animations, credential props. Speaker in professional setting
   - Personal Discovery: Heavier on lifestyle b-roll, phone screenshots, casual setting. Car interior or bedroom common
   - Third-Party: Mixed. Both speakers may appear, or narrator with b-roll of the other person

2. Map every script beat to a visual layer. For each 15-second segment, note:
   - What the speaker is saying
   - What should be on screen
   - What text overlay (if any) reinforces the point

3. Identify the emotional climax of the script (usually the "everything clicked" moment or the discovery beat). This is where full-screen b-roll or the most impactful visual interrupt should land

4. Product shots appear ONLY after the mechanism is fully explained. Never before. Never during

5. Keep the brief to 2-3 pages. Editors don't read long briefs. Be specific and concise
