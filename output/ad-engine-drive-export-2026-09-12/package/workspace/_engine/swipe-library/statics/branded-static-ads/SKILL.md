# Branded Static Ad System — Complete Framework

This skill governs how to create high-converting branded static ad images for health supplement and wellness brands on Meta/Facebook. It is built from competitive analysis of 36,000+ ads across 7 top-performing brands (Auri Labs, Primal Viking, GleeFull, GLP-1 SOS, North Valley Health Clinic, Neurosmile, Primal Queen) and encodes the template architectures, persona-targeting logic, copy hierarchy, and visual design specifications that separate scroll-stopping statics from ignored ones.

This skill does NOT produce generic product photography. Every static it creates is engineered as a conversion tool — it stops the scroll, shifts a belief, and drives a click. The image IS the ad. The copy on the image does the selling. The primary text below is support.

Read this entire file before creating any static. The archetypes are interdependent with the persona system and copy architecture.

## I. THE 8 BRANDED STATIC ARCHETYPES

Every branded static must be built from one of these 8 proven archetypes. These are not design suggestions — they are conversion architectures validated by tens of millions of impressions. Each archetype leverages specific psychological triggers and visual hierarchies.

When given a product to create statics for, your first job is to select the 3-5 most appropriate archetypes for that product's category and target persona. Then execute each one according to its specifications below.

### Archetype 1: Ingredient-Benefit Callout

**What it is:** Split layout with bold claim headline on one side and product shot on the other. Numbered ingredient list with mini-benefit descriptions under each ingredient.

**Why it works:** It delivers mechanism education in a single glance. The reader understands WHY the product works without reading any ad copy. The visual hierarchy does the belief-shifting: headline catches attention, product anchors trust, ingredients provide proof.

**Visual spec:**
- Layout: 60/40 or 50/50 split. Text-heavy side on the left, product shot on the right.
- Headline: Bold, 2-4 words max in the provocative claim. Often uses quotes or asterisked words for compliance.
- Ingredient list: 3 ingredients maximum. Each has the ingredient name in bold + dosage in parentheses, then a one-line benefit description underneath.
- Product shot: Clean product image on solid or gradient background. Label readable.
- Color: Dark backgrounds (navy, forest green, black) work best. White or cream text. One accent color for the headline.
- Aspect ratio: 1:1 or 4:5.

**Best for:** Supplements with clear hero ingredients, products where mechanism education is the belief shift, brands positioning on ingredient quality/dosage.

**Example from field:** Auri Labs' "Rock-Hard Er*ctions Aren't FDA-Approved" — 89-day runner. Left side: headline + 3 numbered ingredients (Capsaicin 300mg, Beet Root Extract, CoQ10 + Hawthorn) with benefit lines. Right side: Cayenne Pepper bottle.

### Archetype 2: Bold Claim + Urgency Pricing

**What it is:** Large bold text claim dominating the top 40% of the image. Product image center. Crossed-out original price with prominent discounted price. Strong CTA button or urgency tag at bottom.

**Why it works:** The bold claim pre-qualifies the reader — only someone with the stated problem will stop scrolling. The pricing creates immediate purchase urgency. The crossed-out price anchors the perceived value. The combination of problem-acknowledgment + immediate deal collapses the decision timeline.

**Visual spec:**
- Layout: Vertical stack. Banner/claim at top, product center, pricing bottom.
- Top banner: Contrasting color bar (red, green, gold) with urgency text — SAY GOODBYE TO [PROBLEM], FLASH SALE, LIMITED TIME ONLY.
- Claim text: Large, bold, ALL CAPS or mixed case. Maximum 10 words. Must name the specific problem being solved.
- Product: Clean product shot, centered. Can include multiple bottles for bundle effect.
- Pricing: Original price with strikethrough in smaller text. New price in large, bold text. Often red or green. Below the product.
- CTA: SHOP NOW button or banner at very bottom.
- Color: Red/black, green/gold, or brand-colored backgrounds.

**Best for:** Flash sales, seasonal promotions, price-competitive products, retargeting audiences who have already seen educational content.

**Example from field:** Auri Labs' "SAY GOODBYE TO POOR CIRCULATION!" + $53.99 (before $89.99) + SHOP NOW — top-performing conversion static.

### Archetype 3: Authority Comparison

**What it is:** Two-column or two-section comparison where one recognized authority is positioned against a weaker or conventional alternative. The product is positioned as the authority-backed solution.

**Why it works:** It hijacks existing trust in medical/scientific authority and creates a knowledge gap the reader wants to close. The implied narrative: you are being given the inferior option, and here is what the experts actually use. This triggers both the authority bias and the information-gap curiosity drive simultaneously.

**Visual spec:**
- Layout: Two-column comparison or sequential revelation. Column A is the "inferior" approach. Column B is the "superior" approach.
- Column A header: Conventional authority (e.g., "American Doctors Prescribe Gabapentin")
- Column B header: Superior authority (e.g., "German Doctors Prescribe This")
- Body: Column A shows problems/limitations. Column B shows benefits/mechanisms.
- Product: Appears in Column B alongside the superior approach. Never in Column A.
- Typography: Column A in muted/grey tones. Column B in vibrant/confident colors.
- Trust signals: Research citations through narrator voice, clinical language, specific dosages.

**Best for:** Products competing against pharmaceutical options, products with international credibility angles, audiences who have been failed by conventional approaches.

**Example from field:** Neurosmile's "German Doctors Prescribe This. American Doctors Prescribe Gabapentin." — 83-day runner. Clean two-section layout with product integration in the "superior" side.

### Archetype 4: Competitor Comparison Chart

**What it is:** Three-column comparison table with 2 competitor brands showing red X marks for failures and the advertised product showing green checkmarks for benefits. Price displayed prominently with advertised product as the best value.

**Why it works:** Leverages anchoring bias. Positions the product as the rational choice across multiple dimensions simultaneously. Addresses the "why not the other one" objection before the reader has to voice it. The visual pattern of red-X, red-X, green-check creates an instant winner signal the brain processes in milliseconds.

**Visual spec:**
- Layout: 3-column table. Competitors on left and center. Your product on right (or highlighted center).
- Column headers: Competitor brand names with their logos or product images. Your brand last or center with highlight.
- Row items: 4-6 comparison criteria. Use checkmarks (green) and X marks (red). Criteria should be real differentiators, not invented ones.
- Price row: At bottom. Competitor prices shown at full price. Your price shown as a deal or lower.
- Highlight: Your product column gets a subtle background highlight, border, or "WINNER" badge.
- Color: Clean white/light background. Green for your product's column. Red for competitor failures. Professional, trustworthy design.

**Best for:** Products in crowded categories with clear differentiators, audiences in comparison-shopping mode, products with price advantages.

**Example from field:** GleeFull's comparison chart — Happy Mammoth ($70) vs Obvi ($50) vs GleeFull ($35) with checkmark/X grid. Top-performing conversion static.

### Archetype 5: Testimonial Card with Social Proof

**What it is:** Trial offer or urgency banner at top. Verified customer name with blue checkmark. Star rating. Quoted testimonial text in center. Urgency pricing at bottom with crossed-out original and red/green sale price.

**Why it works:** Combines three trust signals: social proof from the testimonial, authority from the verified badge, and scarcity from the pricing. The reader processes it as a peer recommendation rather than an ad. The format mimics review sites and app store ratings — formats the brain already trusts.

**Visual spec:**
- Layout: Vertical card format. Urgency banner top, testimonial center, pricing bottom.
- Top banner: "TRIAL OFFER", "WE'RE SELLING OUT AGAIN", or "VERIFIED PURCHASE" in accent color bar.
- Customer info: Name + "Verified Customer" with blue checkmark icon. Optional photo thumbnail.
- Star rating: 5 gold stars. Below customer name.
- Testimonial: 2-3 sentences max in quotation marks. Specific results, not vague praise. Must mention a timeframe and a tangible outcome.
- Pricing: Bottom section. Original price crossed out. Sale price in large bold. "TODAY ONLY" or "LIMITED TIME" tag.
- Color: White or light card on colored background. Professional, trustworthy.

**Best for:** Products with strong customer stories, retargeting audiences, price-sensitive buyers, products competing against subscription fatigue.

**Example from field:** Neurosmile's "TRIAL OFFER — WE'RE SELLING OUT AGAIN" with verified Amy K. testimonial and $79.95 → $17.97 pricing. 148-day runner.

### Archetype 6: Problem-State Text Overlay on UGC Image

**What it is:** Casual or UGC-style image of a real person (often showing a body part, bathroom mirror, kitchen scene) with bold text overlay stating the problem and outcome. The image looks organic. The text does the selling.

**Why it works:** The UGC-style image triggers native-feed pattern matching — the brain processes it as content, not an ad, which means it gets past the scroll filter. The bold text overlay then delivers a curiosity-gap claim that forces a conscious scroll-stop. The contrast between the casual image and the bold claim creates cognitive tension that demands resolution (clicking).

**Visual spec:**
- Image: Must look like an iPhone photo. Slightly imperfect lighting. Real setting (bathroom, kitchen, bedroom, gym). Can show a person from waist down, in a mirror, or a close-up of a body part relevant to the product's benefit.
- Text overlay: Bold, large, ALL CAPS or hand-written font. 5-12 words maximum. Positioned in the bottom third or centered. White or yellow text with dark shadow/outline for readability.
- No product shot: The image itself does NOT show the product. That comes in the ad copy below.
- No CTA button: This archetype relies on curiosity, not call-to-action. The click comes from wanting to know more.
- Aspect ratio: 1:1 or 4:5. Must look like it was posted organically.

**Best for:** Cold audiences, top-of-funnel awareness, provocative/edgy angles, products targeting shame-adjacent problems (weight, ED, skin, aging).

**Example from field:** GleeFull's "ESTROGEN BELLY GONE IN 14 DAYS (NO OZEMPIC - NO HRT)" overlaid on casual woman-in-kitchen image. Primal Viking's "SHE WON'T BE ABLE TO WALK FOR A WEEK OR YOUR MONEY BACK" on dark UGC-style image.

### Archetype 7: Clinical Dose / Benefit Grid

**What it is:** Clean product shot surrounded by clinical language (CLINICAL DOSE, RESEARCH-BACKED, THERAPEUTIC DOSE) and benefit icons or checkmark lists. Professional design with dark backgrounds. Strong CTA.

**Why it works:** Signals pharmaceutical-grade credibility without being a pharmaceutical ad. The clinical language activates a different trust pathway than lifestyle branding. Buyers who have tried everything — OTC, prescription, home remedies — respond to clinical authority because it suggests this product takes the problem as seriously as they do.

**Visual spec:**
- Layout: Product centered or left-aligned. Benefit icons/list on right or surrounding the product.
- Product shot: Premium photography. Dark or navy background. Product looks clinical, not lifestyle.
- Clinical language: "CLINICAL DOSE", "THERAPEUTIC STRENGTH", "RESEARCH-BACKED FORMULA" in clean typography.
- Benefit icons: 3-4 icons with one-line benefits. Use simple icons (checkmarks, shields, lightning bolts). Benefits must be specific and outcome-focused, not ingredient-focused.
- CTA: "ORDER NOW", "TRY RISK-FREE", or "START YOUR RECOVERY" in a contrasting button.
- Color: Navy/white, black/gold, dark green/cream. Must feel clinical, not casual.

**Best for:** Products targeting audiences who have been failed by conventional treatment, premium-priced products, products with clinical trial data or dosage differentiation.

**Example from field:** Neurosmile's "NERVE RELIEF — CLINICAL DOSE — ORDER NOW" static with product shot and benefit grid. Clean navy/teal design.

### Archetype 8: Counterfeit Warning / Brand Protection

**What it is:** WARNING banner with exclamation icon. Message about fake products on Amazon or other marketplaces. Explicit statement that the brand only sells on its own website. Directive to buy from the official site.

**Why it works:** Creates urgency by implying high demand (so popular it's being counterfeited). Drives direct-to-site purchases which improve margins. Positions the brand as premium and protective of quality. Pre-empts the Amazon search that would otherwise happen after seeing the ad. Also works as a retargeting creative for people who saw a previous ad and might search Amazon instead of clicking the ad link.

**Visual spec:**
- Layout: WARNING banner at top in red/yellow. Product images showing "real" vs "fake" or just the real product. Warning text in center. Brand-only purchase directive at bottom.
- WARNING text: Bold, red or yellow, with triangle/exclamation icon. "WARNING!" or "ATTENTION!" opener.
- Body: "FAKE [BRAND] [PRODUCT] ON AMAZON" — names the specific threat. "We don't sell on Amazon. If you see it there — IT'S A COUNTERFEIT."
- Directive: "Only buy from [official site]. Clinical grade formula. 120-day guarantee."
- Bottom: "DON'T GET SCAMMED!" in red text.
- Color: White background with red accents. Feels urgent, not designed.

**Best for:** Brands at scale that are being counterfeited or that want to prevent Amazon leakage, brands with strong direct-to-consumer strategies, retargeting audiences.

**Example from field:** Auri Labs' "WARNING! FAKE AURI LABS CAYENNE PEPPER SOFTGELS ON AMAZON" — 91-day runner. Clean white background with red warning elements.

## II. PERSONA-MATCHING SYSTEM

Every static targets a specific persona. Never create a static without first identifying which persona it serves. The persona determines the archetype selection, copy angle, emotional trigger, and visual style.

### The 6 Core Personas

**1. The Fading Alpha** (Men 40-65)
- Core pain: ED, low testosterone, beer belly, loss of vitality, partner disappointment
- Emotional driver: Fear of irrelevance; desire to reclaim masculinity and sexual confidence
- Responds to: Archetypes 1, 2, 6, 8. Authority language. Ingredient specificity. Before/after transformation promises.
- Micro-angles: The bedroom failure, the mirror shock, the wife's disappointment, the TRT alternative, the Viking/warrior identity, the "men over 40" tribal signal

**2. The Meno Warrior** (Women 35-60)
- Core pain: Menopause weight gain, hormonal chaos, estrogen belly, night sweats, identity loss
- Emotional driver: Frustration with body betrayal; want to feel like themselves again
- Responds to: Archetypes 2, 4, 5, 6. Competitor comparisons. Testimonials from women who "got their body back." Bold transformation claims.
- Micro-angles: The jeans-don't-fit, the night sweats, the Ozempic-isn't-the-answer, the estrogen belly specifically, the husband noticed

**3. The Nerve Sufferer** (50-75, any gender)
- Core pain: Neuropathy, diabetic nerve pain, dead feet, tingling, numbness, Gabapentin failure
- Emotional driver: Desperation; tried everything; ready for last resort; fears losing mobility
- Responds to: Archetypes 3, 5, 7. Clinical authority. German/European medicine angle. Specific timeline promises (14 days, 21 days).
- Micro-angles: The dead feet, the Gabapentin isn't working, the German doctors know better, the 14-day elimination, the can't-feel-my-toes

**4. The Ozempic Adjacent** (Women 30-55)
- Core pain: GLP-1 side effects, constipation, want weight loss without harsh pharmaceutical sides
- Emotional driver: Guilt about weight; want a solution without the social stigma of injectable weight loss drugs
- Responds to: Archetypes 2, 5, 6. Testimonials. "No Ozempic" positioning. Natural alternative framing.
- Micro-angles: The Ozempic constipation nightmare, the injection-free alternative, the natural GLP-1 activator, the "my doctor doesn't know about this"

**5. The Biohacker Wife** (Women 28-45)
- Core pain: Low energy, hormonal imbalance, brain fog, ancestral health curiosity
- Emotional driver: Identity tied to wellness optimization; rejects synthetic solutions; wants to feel "primal"
- Responds to: Archetypes 1, 4, 7. Ingredient education. Ancestral/whole-food framing. Premium positioning.
- Micro-angles: The beef organ revelation, the synthetic vitamin scam, the ancestral feminine health, the real food not isolates

**6. The Silent Sufferer** (45-70, any gender)
- Core pain: Blood pressure, cardiovascular concerns, circulation issues, fear of heart attack/stroke
- Emotional driver: Mortality awareness; fear of sudden cardiac event; keeping a secret from family
- Responds to: Archetypes 1, 3, 5, 8. Authority positioning. Ingredient-mechanism education. Urgency framing tied to health consequences.
- Micro-angles: The 5-year warning, the arterial plaque truth, the statin alternative, the bathroom floor at 3 AM, the check engine light

## III. COPY ARCHITECTURE FOR STATICS

The text on a branded static follows a strict 4-layer visual hierarchy. This hierarchy is not optional — it is the conversion architecture that determines whether the image stops the scroll and drives the click.

### Layer 1: Pattern Interrupt (Top 20% of image)
- Bold, high-contrast text
- Maximum 8 words
- Must create a curiosity gap OR state a provocative claim
- The reader should NOT be able to resolve the curiosity without clicking
- Examples: "ESTROGEN BELLY GONE IN 14 DAYS", "NO MORE DIABETIC ED", "German Doctors Prescribe This"

### Layer 2: Mechanism / Proof (Middle 40% of image)
- Ingredient callouts, benefit icons, comparison data, or testimonial quote
- This is where credibility is established
- Maximum 3 bullet points or comparison rows
- Each point must be SPECIFIC — dosages, timeframes, named outcomes
- Examples: "1. Capsaicin (300mg) — Dilates blood vessels to increase blood flow", "Pain-free feet / Better sleep / Restored sensation"

### Layer 3: Product Anchor (Middle-Bottom 20%)
- Product shot or product name
- Must be instantly recognizable even at thumbnail size
- This converts attention into brand recall
- The product should feel DISCOVERED, not pitched — it appears as the answer, not the ad

### Layer 4: CTA / Pricing (Bottom 20%)
- Price with crossed-out original, CTA button, or urgency tag
- Optional — not every archetype uses all 4 layers
- When present, must create immediate action impulse
- Examples: "$79.95 → $17.97 TODAY ONLY", "SHOP NOW", "ORDER NOW — LIMITED SUPPLY"

**Not every archetype uses all 4 layers.** Archetype 6 (Problem-State Text Overlay) uses only Layer 1. Archetype 8 (Counterfeit Warning) uses Layers 1 and 2 but replaces Layer 4 with a brand protection directive. Match the layers to the archetype.

## IV. PRODUCTION WORKFLOW

When asked to create branded statics for a product, follow this sequence:

### Step 1: Product Analysis
- What is the product? (supplement, device, topical, beverage)
- What is the hero ingredient or mechanism?
- What problem does it solve?
- What is the price point?
- What differentiates it from competitors?

### Step 2: Persona Selection
- Which 2-3 personas from Section II does this product serve?
- For each persona, which 2-3 micro-angles are most relevant?
- What emotional drivers will be activated?

### Step 3: Archetype Selection
- For each persona + micro-angle combination, which archetype best delivers the belief shift?
- Select 3-5 archetypes total for the initial batch
- Prioritize archetypes that match the persona's response patterns (listed in Section II)

### Step 4: Copy Generation
- For each archetype selected, write the text layers following Section III
- Layer 1 must be unique per static — no duplicate headlines across the batch
- Layer 2 must be factually grounded in the product's actual ingredients/benefits
- Layer 3 uses the actual product name and image reference
- Layer 4 uses the actual pricing if available

### Step 5: Gemini-Powered Competitor Static Analysis (Optional but Recommended)

Before generating new statics, analyze the best competitor statics from the vault using Gemini. This extracts the exact design DNA you need to replicate (or beat).

```python
from google import genai
import base64, os

GEMINI_KEY = '[REDACTED_SECRET]'
gemini_client = genai.Client(api_key=GEMINI_KEY)

STATIC_ANALYSIS_PROMPT = """You are analyzing a branded static ad image from a health/supplement brand on Meta/Facebook.

Perform a detailed structural analysis covering:

1. **ARCHETYPE CLASSIFICATION**: Which of these 8 archetypes does this static match?
   - Ingredient-Benefit Callout (split layout, ingredients + product)
   - Bold Claim + Urgency Pricing (big text + strikethrough price)
   - Authority Comparison (two-column superior vs inferior)
   - Competitor Comparison Chart (checkmark/X table)
   - Testimonial Card with Social Proof (quote + stars + product)
   - Problem-State Text Overlay on UGC Image
   - Clinical Dose / Benefit Grid
   - Counterfeit Warning / Brand Protection

2. **VISUAL HIERARCHY LAYERS**: Map the 4-layer copy architecture:
   - Layer 1 (Pattern Interrupt): What's the top headline/hook text? How many words? Font style?
   - Layer 2 (Mechanism/Proof): What credibility elements are shown? Ingredients? Benefits? Data?
   - Layer 3 (Product Anchor): Where is the product? How prominent? What angle/lighting?
   - Layer 4 (CTA/Pricing): Any pricing? CTA button? Urgency element?

3. **DESIGN DNA**:
   - Primary color palette (exact hex codes if possible, or closest named colors)
   - Background treatment (solid, gradient, image-based, textured)
   - Typography (font weight, size hierarchy, serif vs sans-serif, any hand-written elements)
   - Layout grid (columns, rows, spacing pattern)
   - Product photography style (flat lay, floating, on-surface, 3D render, lifestyle)
   - Overall design school (clinical/medical, fun/playful, premium/luxury, raw/urgent, editorial)

4. **COPY EXTRACTION**: Extract ALL text visible on the image, organized by layer position (top to bottom). Include exact wording, capitalization, and punctuation.

5. **CONVERSION MECHANICS**:
   - What specific belief shift does this static attempt?
   - What makes someone stop scrolling on this image?
   - What creates the click impulse?
   - What persona is this targeting?

6. **REPLICATION BRIEF**: If you were to recreate this static for a DIFFERENT brand/product, write the exact specifications:
   - Layout dimensions and grid
   - Color palette to use
   - Typography specs (weight, size, case, alignment for each layer)
   - Product shot requirements
   - Background treatment
   - Any graphic elements (icons, badges, dividers, etc.)

Be extremely specific — a designer should be able to recreate this layout from your description alone."""


def analyze_static_with_gemini(image_path):
    """Upload a static ad image to Gemini and get structural analysis."""
    print(f"Analyzing: {image_path}")

    uploaded = gemini_client.files.upload(file=image_path)

    response = gemini_client.models.generate_content(
        model='gemini-2.5-flash',
        contents=[uploaded, STATIC_ANALYSIS_PROMPT]
    )

    return response.text


def batch_analyze_brand_statics(brand_folder, max_images=10):
    """Analyze all statics from a brand folder and compile a design DNA report."""
    analyses = []

    image_files = [f for f in os.listdir(brand_folder)
                   if f.lower().endswith(('.jpg', '.jpeg', '.png', '.webp'))
                   and not f.startswith('.')]

    for img_file in image_files[:max_images]:
        img_path = os.path.join(brand_folder, img_file)
        analysis = analyze_static_with_gemini(img_path)
        analyses.append({
            'file': img_file,
            'analysis': analysis
        })
        print(f"  Analyzed: {img_file}")

    return analyses


def generate_design_dna_report(brand_name, analyses):
    """Take individual analyses and synthesize a brand design DNA report."""
    combined = "\n\n---\n\n".join([
        f"### {a['file']}\n{a['analysis']}" for a in analyses
    ])

    SYNTHESIS_PROMPT = f"""Based on these {len(analyses)} individual static ad analyses from {brand_name},
synthesize a BRAND DESIGN DNA REPORT covering:

1. **Dominant Archetypes**: Which archetypes does this brand use most? Rank by frequency.
2. **Color System**: The exact color palette used across all statics (primary, secondary, accent, background).
3. **Typography System**: Font choices, size hierarchy, weight patterns across all statics.
4. **Layout Patterns**: The 2-3 most common layout grids this brand uses.
5. **Product Photography Style**: How they consistently shoot/present the product.
6. **Copy Voice**: The tone, word choice, and headline patterns across all statics.
7. **Conversion Mechanics**: What belief shifts and persuasion patterns they consistently use.
8. **Template Extraction**: Describe 3 reusable templates derived from their strongest statics.
   For each template: exact layout, color palette, typography, and placeholder content zones.

Individual analyses:

{combined}

Be extremely specific. The output should be a production-ready brief that lets a designer replicate this brand's static ad system for a different product."""

    response = gemini_client.models.generate_content(
        model='gemini-2.5-flash',
        contents=[SYNTHESIS_PROMPT]
    )

    return response.text
```

**Usage pattern — analyze a competitor brand's statics:**

```python

# Save the DNA report to the brand's catalog
with open(f'{brand_folder}/design-dna-report.md', 'w') as f:

```

### Step 6: Image Generation with nano-banana CLI

Use the `nano-banana` CLI (Gemini 3.1 Flash Image Preview / Nano Banana 2) to produce the actual branded static ad images. The CLI handles sizing, aspect ratios, reference images, and cost tracking automatically — no Python boilerplate, no API client setup.

**Basic invocation:**

```bash
nano-banana "<prompt>" -a 1:1 -s 1K -d "/path/to/output" -o "filename"
```

**Flags for branded statics:**

| Flag | Value | Purpose |
|------|-------|---------|
| `-a` | `1:1` or `4:5` | Facebook feed ratio (use `9:16` for stories) |
| `-s` | `1K` (default) or `2K` | Output resolution |
| `-d` | `/statics/generated/{product_name}/` | Output directory |
| `-o` | `{product}_{archetype_code}_{n}` | Filename (no extension) |
| `-r` | product reference image path | Product/style reference (repeatable) |
| `--model` | `pro` | Upgrade from default flash for hero statics |

**Archetype filename codes:**

| Archetype | Code |
|-----------|------|
| Ingredient-Benefit Callout | `ingr` |
| Bold Claim + Urgency Pricing | `bold` |
| Authority Comparison | `auth` |
| Competitor Comparison Chart | `comp` |
| Testimonial Card with Social Proof | `test` |
| Problem-State Text Overlay | `ugc` |
| Clinical Dose / Benefit Grid | `clin` |
| Counterfeit Warning | `warn` |

**Prompt template** — concatenate archetype spec, persona, product info, copy layers, and (optionally) competitor DNA into one prompt string:

```
Generate a branded static ad image for Facebook/Meta.

ARCHETYPE: {archetype_name} — {one-line spec from Section I}
TARGET PERSONA: {persona_name}
PRODUCT: {product_name} — {short product description}

COPY LAYERS (text that MUST appear on the image, exactly as written):
- Layer 1 (top headline): {layer_1_text}
- Layer 2 (mechanism/proof): {layer_2_text}
- Layer 3 (product anchor): {product_name}
- Layer 4 (CTA/pricing): {layer_4_text}

{optional: REFERENCE DESIGN DNA — paste first ~2000 chars of competitor DNA report here}

DESIGN REQUIREMENTS:
- Professional branded ad look — not UGC, not casual
- Clear typography hierarchy: headline largest, benefit text smaller, CTA medium
- Prominent realistic product shot
- Solid or subtle gradient background — nothing busy
- All text readable at mobile sizes
- Premium, trustworthy color palette for a health/supplement brand
- No watermarks, AI artifacts, or placeholder text
- ALL specified copy layer text must appear on the image exactly as written
```

## V. VOLUME TESTING FRAMEWORK

Creating statics is not a one-time activity. It is a production system that feeds a testing framework.

### Launch Phase (Week 1-2)
- Create 5 statics per archetype x 3 micro-personas = 15 statics per product
- Run all 15 at low daily budget. Kill anything under 1% CTR after 48 hours.

### Scale Phase (Week 3-4)
- Take top 5 performers. Create 3 variations of each (different headline, same archetype).
- This identifies winner archetypes AND winner copy angles.

### Expansion Phase (Week 5+)
- Apply winning archetype + copy angle combinations to new micro-personas.
- Create 5-10 new statics per week targeting unexplored micro-angles.
- Never stop testing. The brands running 5,000+ ads are not making 5,000 unique concepts — they are testing micro-variations.

---

## VI. WHAT NOT TO DO

These are the patterns that fail. They are common and they are tempting. Avoid them.

- **Generic product photography with no text:** A product shot alone is not a static ad. It is a catalog image. Without the copy layers, there is no belief shift and no scroll-stop.
- **Too many ingredients or benefits:** More than 3 in a single static creates visual noise. The brain processes 3. It skips 7.
- **Vague claims:** "Supports healthy function" stops no one. "Eliminates diabetic dead feet in 14 days" stops everyone with dead feet.
- **Wrong archetype for wrong persona:** The Nerve Sufferer does not respond to Archetype 6 (UGC text overlay). They respond to clinical authority (Archetypes 3, 7). Persona-archetype mismatch kills conversion.
- **Designed to look like an ad:** Archetype 6 must look native. The others must look premium/clinical. None of them should look like a Canva template with stock gradients.
- **No urgency mechanism:** Every static should have some form of urgency — even if it is just a timeline promise ("in 14 days") rather than a pricing deal. Without urgency, the reader saves it for later, which means never.
