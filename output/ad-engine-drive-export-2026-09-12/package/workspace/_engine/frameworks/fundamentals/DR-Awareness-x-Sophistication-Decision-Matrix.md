# Awareness × Sophistication Decision Matrix

**Purpose:** Turn the two primary DR dials (awareness level, sophistication stage) into a single decision table that dictates ad length, structure emphasis, and angle type before a single word is written.

**Source intelligence:** Brooks's Loom fundamentals + ad-structure breakdowns.

---

## Why This Matrix Exists

Every ad is governed by two dials that are usually treated separately:

- **Awareness** — how much the avatar already knows about active solutions
- **Sophistication** — how many competing claims the market has already heard

Treated separately, you get ads that are the right length but wrong differentiation, or the right claim but wrong pacing. Treated together, you get the correct structural emphasis on day one.

---

## The Matrix

| | **Stage 2 Market** (basic claims still work) | **Stage 3 Market** (new mechanism required) | **Stage 4 Market** (stacked mechanisms) | **Stage 5 Market** (identity sell) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Problem Aware** | Short-medium (60–90s). Heavy problem agitation. Simple claim. | **Long (3–5min).** Full 8 beats. Mechanism education is the ad. | **Long (3–5min).** Full 8 beats + stack justification. | Long (2–4min). Problem → identity bridge. |
| **Solution Aware** | Medium (45–75s). Skip problem agitation. | **Medium-long (90s–3min).** Heavy solution dismissal → UMP → UMS. | Medium-long (90s–3min). Dismissal + stacked mechanism. | Medium (60–90s). Dismissal + identity. |
| **Product Aware** | Short (30–60s). Direct claim. | Medium (60–90s). Why *our* mechanism is different. | Medium (60–90s). Why *our* stack is different. | Short-medium (45–75s). Identity + proof. |
| **Most Aware** | Short (15–30s). Offer/price. | Short (15–45s). Offer + mechanism reminder. | Short (15–45s). Offer + stack reminder. | Short (15–45s). Offer + identity reinforcement. |

---

## How to Read a Cell

Each cell dictates three things:

1. **Length range** — a floor, not a ceiling. Go shorter only if you can compress belief shifts.
2. **Structural emphasis** — which of the 8 beats do the heavy lifting.
3. **Claim ceiling** — the sophistication stage tells you what the market will still believe.

---

## Beat Emphasis by Sophistication Stage

**Stage 2** → Hook + Problem Agitation + Solution Introduction. UMP/UMS are light because a simple specific claim still works.

**Stage 3** → UMP and UMS become the spine of the ad. Solution Dismissal is non-negotiable — the viewer has tried things. This is where most winning DR ads live.

**Stage 4** → UMS gets expanded into multi-ingredient / multi-mechanism justification. Why the stack, why the ratios, why this combination.

**Stage 5** → Close carries the weight. Identity transformation, before/after, who you become. Mechanism becomes background, identification becomes foreground.

---

## Beat Emphasis by Awareness Level

**Problem Aware** → Problem Agitation is longest. Hook must *name the problem* in novel terms.

**Solution Aware** → Solution Dismissal is longest. Hook must call out a failed solution or dismiss a common belief.

**Product Aware** → UMS differentiation is longest. Hook must differentiate us from the category.

**Most Aware** → Close is longest. Hook can be an offer/price/urgency hit directly.

---

## Angle Selection by Awareness Level

| Awareness | Angle Families That Work |
|---|---|
| **Problem Aware** | Doctor-dismissal, social-shame, problem-education, hidden-cause |
| **Solution Aware** | Failed-solutions, "why nothing has worked," mechanism-reveal |
| **Product Aware** | Category-comparison, "ours vs. theirs," proof-density |
| **Most Aware** | Identity reinforcement, community, scarcity, loyalty |

---

## Length Discipline Rule

**Length is not a creative preference. It's a structural output.**

If you're writing a 4-minute ad for a solution-aware avatar in a Stage 2 market, you're over-educating and the ad will drag. If you're writing a 45-second ad for a problem-aware avatar in a Stage 3 market, you're under-educating and the ad will feel thin.

Match the length to the cell. Then write.

---

## Pre-Brief Checklist

Before a single line of copy gets written, answer:

1. **Cell:** which cell in the matrix are we in?
2. **Floor length:** what does the cell require as minimum?
3. **Beat emphasis:** which of the 8 beats carry the weight here?
4. **Angle family:** which angle family fits the awareness row?
5. **Claim ceiling:** what's the strongest claim this sophistication stage still believes?

If any of those five are unclear, stop and resolve them first. The matrix makes the brief.
