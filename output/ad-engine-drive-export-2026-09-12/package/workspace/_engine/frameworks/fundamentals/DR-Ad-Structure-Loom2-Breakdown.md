# Direct Response Ad Structure — Loom 2 Breakdown

**Source:** Loom recording — "Ad Structure & Application"
**Link:** https://www.loom.com/share/b81f55d3a5e34ac39acb3eb0e59b32d7
**Purpose:** Documents the 8-beat structure every direct response ad should follow, as walked through on a real hair-loss ad.

---

## The Cooking Metaphor

Brooks frames advertising like cooking — many ingredients, in a specific order, or the dish doesn't work. The 8 beats below are the ingredients. The order is the recipe. Every winning ad follows it.

---

## The 8-Beat Structure

```
1. Hook
2. Problem Agitation
3. Solution Dismissal
4. UMP (Unique Mechanism of the Problem)
5. UMS (Unique Mechanism of the Solution)
6. Solution Introduction
7. Objection Handling  (+ Future-Basing)
8. Close
```

Every ad follows this exact structure. Skipping a beat breaks the belief-shift chain.

---

## Beat 1 — Hook

- **Job:** pique curiosity and open a curiosity loop.
- **Length:** first 3–4 seconds.
- **Rule:** a great hook is *specific* and *introduces new information*.
- **Example (hair loss ad):** *"When I showed my dermatologist the research on silicone hair loss, she actually rolled her eyes."*
  - Calls out the problem.
  - Introduces novel information (silica and hair loss).
  - Opens a loop: what research? why did the dermatologist dismiss it?

A hook that is vague or reuses stale framing won't stop the scroll. Specificity + novelty is the entire mechanic.

---

## Beat 2 — Problem Agitation

- **Job:** anchor the viewer in the nightmare state. Make them feel the problem.
- **Content:** the person explains past failed experiences, how long they've suffered, what their life looks like with the problem.
- **Craft note from the Loom:** the ad starts in the *after-state* ("you should have seen her face at my follow-up appointment"), then says *"let me back up"* to juxtapose against the *before-state*. That juxtaposition is what makes the curiosity loop bite.

This is where the viewer says "that's me."

---

## Beat 3 — Solution Dismissal

- **Job:** dismiss every other solution the avatar has already tried and explain *why* each one failed.
- **Why this matters:** if you skip this, the solution-aware viewer mentally objects ("I already tried that, it didn't work") and the ad dies.
- **Format:** line by line — "I tried X, it didn't work because ___. I tried Y, it didn't work because ___."
- **Example:** *"Biotin, 1,000mg a day for eight months — nothing."*

This beat directly maps to **sophistication stage**. Stage 3+ markets require this. You cannot just skip to the mechanism.

---

## Beat 4 — UMP (Unique Mechanism of the Problem)

- **Job:** introduce the **real root cause** of the problem — the reason the problem is happening in the first place.
- **Content:** new information that reframes the problem for the viewer.
- **Example:** *"Ladies, we're focused on the wrong deficiency. It's not iron. It's not thyroid. It's silica."*
- **Why it works:** it dismisses the old frame ("it's stress / iron / thyroid") and installs a new frame ("it's silica deficiency") that only our product can address.

This is the most important belief shift in the ad. If they believe the UMP, they will accept the UMS.

---

## Beat 5 — UMS (Unique Mechanism of the Solution)

- **Job:** introduce the ingredient/mechanism that addresses the UMP.
- **Rule:** UMS is NOT the product. It's the *active ingredient or process* inside the product that solves the UMP.
- **Example:** *"Bioavailable silica from horsetail extract."* (UMS) vs. the actual product name (Solution).
- **Relationship:** UMP ↔ UMS are a matched pair.
  - UMP: silica deficiency
  - UMS: silica (bioavailable, from horsetail)

**The viewer's internal monologue after this beat:** "I need silica."

---

## Beat 6 — Solution Introduction

- **Job:** introduce the actual branded product.
- **Rule:** do NOT go straight to the product. The viewer now *wants* the UMS, so the product becomes the obvious vehicle to get it.
- **Example:** *"That night I found Ore."*
- **Tone:** casual, almost incidental. People hate being sold to. By this point the ad has done the persuasion — the product name is just the delivery.

---

## Beat 7 — Objection Handling (+ Future-Basing)

- **Job (objections):** handle the last objections before the close — price, safety, "does it really work," shipping, etc.
- **Job (future-basing):** paint the viewer's life *with* the solution. Week 1, week 2, week 3. Show the transformation.
- **Example:** *"Week 2, I ran my fingers through my hair in the shower. I stood there and cried happy tears."*

Future-basing is what Brooks folds in here — it's not a separate beat in the script, but it lives in the objection-handling zone and pre-closes the sale emotionally.

---

## Beat 8 — Close

- **Job:** soft CTA + reinforce the angle one final time.
- **Rule:** do NOT sell hard. The ad earned the sale with the earlier beats. The close should just hand the viewer the next step.
- **Components:**
  - Risk reversal (e.g. "180 days to try it")
  - Emotional callback to the angle
  - Simple CTA ("click below")
- **Example:** *"I wish someone told me about this three years ago. I'm telling you now. Click below to learn more — because no one's going to advocate for your hair except you."*

The close ends on the **angle**, not on the product. That's how you keep the emotional payload intact through checkout.

---

## Structural Diagnostics

When an ad isn't working, check the beats in order:

| Symptom | Likely Broken Beat |
|---|---|
| Low 3-second video view rate | Hook (not specific, not novel) |
| High drops in first 10 seconds | Problem agitation (not relatable, too slow) |
| Viewers say "I already tried X" | Solution dismissal missing or weak |
| Low click-through, high watch time | UMP/UMS confusing or not differentiated |
| Clicks but no add-to-cart | Solution intro rushed or product feels like an ad |
| Add-to-cart but no checkout | Objection handling weak |
| Good funnel metrics, no repeat | Close sold product, not identity |

## The Full Belief-Shift Chain

The 8 beats exist because of the belief-shift job:

1. Hook — *"I should care about this"*
2. Problem Agitation — *"That's me"*
3. Solution Dismissal — *"Everything I've tried has a real reason it failed"*
4. UMP — *"Oh. The real cause is different than I thought"*
5. UMS — *"Then I need [mechanism]"*
6. Solution Introduction — *"This product has it"*
7. Objection Handling + Future-Basing — *"It's safe, it works, and here's my life with it"*
8. Close — *"Let me try it — for myself"*

Each beat installs one belief. Skip one and the next one doesn't land.
