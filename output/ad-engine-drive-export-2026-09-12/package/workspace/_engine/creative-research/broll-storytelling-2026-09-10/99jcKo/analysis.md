# 99jcKo — B-roll selection and story function audit

Source: https://app.trendtrack.io/share/ads/resilia-99jcKo

148.048 seconds of video, 720 × 1280, 29.97 fps, 4,437 video frames. Original provider media is preserved in `source.mp4`; original public page in `share.html`; retrieval URL and metadata in `provenance.json` and `metadata.json`. This is reference analysis, not a proposed production storyboard.

## Actual review scope

All 4,437 frames were decoded. I personally inspected 296 chronological images on a nominal half-second grid across seven contact sheets and four consecutive source frames around each of 70 scene-score candidate detections across 12 boundary sheets. Three adjacent duplicate detections were rejected. The final map has 68 editorial units, including an offer graphic/composition change, and 67 incoming boundaries. The first change is a short horizontal blur/whip: blur begins at frame 136 (4.538s), while the scene-score peak was frame 137. All remaining retained boundaries were checked on outgoing/incoming consecutive frames. I did **not** manually inspect all 4,437 individual frames. Short low-change transitions between half-second samples could still be missed. Contact-sheet labels are nominal grid times; boundary frame labels and shot-map frame indexes are the frame-accurate evidence.

Local Whisper small.en and MLX Whisper large-v3-turbo transcripts are saved. Their wording is machine transcription, not approved copy. MLX misspells thymoquinone and softgels in places; embedded source captions were consulted for visual/narrative mapping. Audio was transcribed but not directly listened to; music, SFX, precise vocal delivery and mix are unaudited. This review makes no claims about conversion results. Ad performance metrics were not provided. Source medical assertions, statistics, outcome timelines and laboratory imagery are analyzed as persuasion devices, not validated evidence.

## What this concept does

This is a mechanism-led, narrated visual story, without a persistent talking-head presenter. A recurring bob-haired woman in teal clothes carries the human sections. The human scenes have coordinated lighting, identity, wardrobe and several visible transformations; they should be categorized as **photoreal reenactment/synthetic-looking human scenes, production origin unverified**, not authenticated TikTok or iPhone footage. It would be a mistake to infer the capture device or actual testimonial status from their appearance.

The central construction is **person → hidden system → visible symptoms → failed efforts → hidden cause → ingredient action → use → human payoff → comparison → purchase**. The repeated woman gives the otherwise fragmented scenes a protagonist. The interiors make the narrator's proposed cause appear visible. The human scenes make it personally recognizable. These are editorial-function inferences from the observed sequence, not causal conversion findings.

## Transferable B-roll rules grounded in this source

1. **Decide by sentence function before visual topic.** A sentence naming a symptom needs a recognizable experience; a sentence explaining transport needs moving material in a channel; a sentence naming a failed attempt needs the attempt itself. “Health” is too broad a retrieval category.
2. **Problem agitation should be embodied.** The strongest recognition inserts show a person checking her jaw, touching her belly beside a salad, or pulling on a stocking. The concrete gesture and surrounding contradiction matter more than a generic sad face. The office forehead-touch shot at 48.749s is a useful weak-match example: it does not specifically demonstrate the named edema.
3. **Cut on a new referent or change in the argument.** At 9.309s the image becomes face/neck; 10.777s becomes torso/legs. At 71.071s the massage switches inward as narration introduces what it cannot reach. These are semantic cues, not arbitrary two-second resets.
4. **Use animation when the important action is invisible.** “Flush,” “pool,” “clog,” “crowd out” and “clear” are depicted with changes of state. A static diagram or the same torso repeatedly would fail to show those separate operations.
5. **Vary scientific geometry, not just color.** This ad uses whole-body overlays, branching networks, longitudinal channels, tunnel POV, intestinal-wall close-ups and circular top-down microbial views. The vocabulary remains coherent while the ideas get different images. Scientific accuracy must be separately validated; dramatic images do not validate the source claims.
6. **Hold until the meaningful action resolves.** Rapid inventory inserts run roughly 0.7–1.4 seconds, while several mechanism transformations hold 3–5 seconds. The product use shot holds 4.538 seconds to show palm → mouth → water. The overall median editorial unit is 1.935 seconds; this is descriptive, not a mandatory pacing rule.
7. **Keep deliberate protagonist continuity while changing the scene.** The same woman can recognize different symptoms and try different things without every insert becoming a new unrelated person. Distinct location, framing, body detail and action make variety. Crops on the same mirror scene at 110.711/113.046s and retail scene at 130.564/132.232/134.067s are editorial emphasis, not evidence of fully new B-roll scenes.
8. **Mirror the problem in the payoff.** The outcome passage returns to face/jaw, legs and abdomen in the same order established earlier. This creates visible closure. In new work, depict truthful experience/outcome evidence; do not import this ad's unsupported transformation or time-to-result claims.
9. **Move to product when the explanation has earned a concrete next object.** Here the first clear pouch reveal is 104.071s, about 70% through the video. That placement is specific to a long mechanism-led concept, not a universal requirement to delay products.
10. **Show comparison as a task.** Phone browsing, reading bottles and comparing labels are more specific than a floating product montage. The lab switches to a precision/authority code, but its presence alone establishes no factual manufacturing provenance.

## Transitions, captions, camera and variety

Almost all retained changes are hard cuts. The initial person-to-microscopic-network change has a short horizontal blur/whip; several within-shot transformations morph anatomy, swelling, age or clearance. Those are visible transformations, not separately verified physical events. Camera treatments include gradual pushes, microscopic fly-throughs/pulls, body-region crops and mostly steady human compositions. No evidence supports calling the human footage handheld iPhone capture.

Bold white uppercase short phrase captions with dark edging sit around the middle/lower-middle of the vertical frame. They update faster than many visual scenes, so **a caption change is not itself a shot boundary**. Caption nouns often coincide with a new object or region. Near the end, a green shop button appears; at 141.475s the layout adds large green discount text and smaller guarantee copy above the woman. Final packshot holds roughly 1.935 seconds.

The ad repeatedly returns to a pink-channel/yellow-obstruction motif and the same protagonist. It also uses close crops from similar mirror/retail coverage. These recurrences should be classified as motif continuity or coverage variation, not automatically fresh scenes. The 4.5–7.6s and 123.3–126.8s branching-channel views are particularly compositionally similar; exact source reuse is not certified without frame matching. The intended new skill should preserve explanatory callbacks while flagging excessive similar composition or reused source clips. A different filename is not evidence of variety.
