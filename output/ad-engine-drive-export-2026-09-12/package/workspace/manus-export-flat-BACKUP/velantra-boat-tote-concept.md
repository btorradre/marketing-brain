# Velantra Boat Tote — Video Ad Concept & Brief

This document describes how to develop a video ad concept and creative brief for the Velantra Boat Tote (the "Boatkin"), a structured Birkin-silhouette canvas tote with contrast leather trim, a gold turn-lock clasp, and gold feet. It combines the product's specific facts (references, colorways, avatar, market sophistication, proven UGC patterns) with a general video-ad-concepting process, and produces a production-ready brief. Use it when asked to write a video ad concept for the Boat Tote / "Boatkin," or when concepting/briefing a video ad for this product generally. For actually generating imagery of the product itself, see the separate product-truth reference for this bag (its silhouette, colorway list, and construction details).

Before drafting any hook, angle, or concept, apply the golden nugget doctrine: identify the single most emotionally loaded deep frame in the research — the real motive that makes the buyer act, not the surface topic. State it in one explicit sentence before drafting.

## Product & audience context

**Product:** the Boat Tote — a structured, Birkin-silhouette tote with a natural cream canvas body and contrast leather trim, a gold turn-lock clasp, and gold feet.

**Price positioning:** roughly $50-150 (an "accessible luxury" positioning), set against the ~$1,200-1,600 range of the luxury Birkin-style bag it visually echoes.

**Colorways:** Two-tone (cream canvas + colored leather trim) in navy, red, pink, dark green, olive green, orange, sunny yellow, yellow, emerald, lady pink, light grey, and lineman. Solid (fully colored body) in green, navy, olive, orange, pink, red, and yellow.

**Useful source material before writing anything new**: mine any existing organic/UGC footage of this specific product for hook lines, beat structure, and pacing that has already proven to work, rather than inventing a concept from nothing. Also review any existing static ad creative for proven visual angles, and any prior reference-ad breakdowns done for this product.

### Avatar — "the Velantra Woman"

A coastal-affluent professional in her 40s, household income $150K+, with a Newport Beach / Hamptons / Nantucket sensibility. She's drawn to the *silhouette* of a famous luxury bag but actively rejects the logo, the price tag, and what she perceives as manufactured scarcity marketing around it. She buys quiet luxury on an "if you know, you know" principle — she wants recognition from her peers, not from strangers. She discovers new brands through peer word-of-mouth and trusted micro-creators, and sharing a find functions as an identity act for her ("look what I found" really means "look how good my taste is").

### Market sophistication & awareness defaults for this product

- **Sophistication level: 4-5 out of 5** — the "luxury-alternative bag" claim is a crowded space. Don't lead with a blunt "luxury for less" pitch; lead with a mechanism (how it's actually made) or a new-identity angle (a discovery story, a craftsmanship proof point, a specific-place anchor) instead.
- **Awareness stage**: problem-aware moving toward solution-aware for cold audiences; product-aware for retargeting audiences.
- **The core angle/wound**: "I want the recognizable shape without being the woman who paid a fortune for a logo" — a mix of taste-anxiety and a fear of looking like new money trying too hard. The vindication being sold is: the bag that earns a genuine "where did you get that?" without the markup behind it.

## How to use this

1. **Gather product context** — don't re-ask for anything already established above (colorways, avatar, price band); only ask the user for anything genuinely still open, like a specific proven-UGC clip to reference or a specific seasonal hook.
2. **Confirm the target format.** Default to a short-form UGC-style video (this product's proven material skews this way). Other options: a longer VSL-style landing-page video, a static-to-animated ad, or a talking-head format. Confirm with the user in one question rather than assuming.
3. **Mine any existing winning UGC or ad material for this product** before inventing new hooks — pull actual proven hook lines and beat structures from what's already worked, rather than starting from a blank page.
4. **Build the concept using this product's tuned defaults** (see the slot table below) as a starting point, adjusting based on what the user confirms or overrides.
5. **Write the finished concept as a brief** (see the template below).
6. **Hand off for production** once the brief is approved — this typically means generating the actual video/image assets using the product's verified reference photos, per whatever generation pipeline is in use.

### Product-tuned concept defaults for the Boat Tote

| Slot | Default | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Concept/vehicle | "The discovery" — a creator finds the bag at a coastal pop-up or similar real-world moment | Matches this avatar's place-anchored, word-of-mouth discovery behavior |
| Lead/hook | Curiosity or authority-subversion (e.g. "everyone on the ferry has this bag, and it's not the luxury one") | At sophistication level 4-5, a plain price-comparison claim is stale — a fresh frame is needed |
| Mechanism intro | A craftsmanship reveal — the solid brass turn-lock, reinforced canvas, a real warranty | Earns the "quality you can actually feel" belief that justifies the price |
| Solution intro | A discovery-style introduction — the product enters as something she stumbled onto, not something being hard-sold | Avoids triggering her suspicion of being marketed to by "just another small brand" |
| Proof | Peer/UGC social proof, or a "where did you get that?" testimonial beat | Peer recommendation is her single strongest trust trigger |
| CTA | Soft, light scarcity (e.g. "colors sell out each season") pointing to the product page | Hard, aggressive scarcity reads as exactly the manufactured urgency she rejects |
| Length | Roughly 20-40 seconds for UGC-style, or 60-90 seconds for a VSL-style format | Matched to the channel |

## Rules & standards — briefs handed to an outside editor

Any brief that will be handed to someone outside the internal team (an outside editor, a freelance video editor) must be fully self-contained — assume they have zero access to any internal files, folders, or systems. Never reference a local file path, an internal drive location, or an internal filename in an editor-facing brief.

**Keep it short.** A brief for an outside editor should be brief — a handful of pages at most. It should carry only: the product-truth block, the script, the timeline, the generation prompts, and any brief-specific guardrails/QA notes. Any general "how we generate assets" process documentation belongs in one shared internal process document, referenced once — never re-explained inline inside every individual brief. Cut strategic reasoning (why this angle works, why this format was chosen) down to one or two lines — an editor executes the brief, they don't need the full strategic case.

Concretely:
1. **Product references → live product page links, not internal file paths.** Point the editor at the actual live product page, deep-linked to the exact colorway variant if the storefront platform supports it, and name the specific gallery photo to reference or save.
2. **Creator references → a sourcing method the editor can actually follow**, e.g. a specific reference-image search strategy plus an identity description per creator — not internal generation-pipeline instructions. If avatars are AI-generated from reference photos, never clone a real, identifiable person's actual face without consent — generate a distinct, non-identical likeness inspired by a reference, not a duplicate of a real individual.
3. **Reference ads → public links only** (an ad library URL, a public share link, or a platform URL) — never a downloaded local video file path. If an internal reference clip has no public URL, describe it inline beat-by-beat instead of pointing to a file.
4. **No companion files** — everything the editor needs (segment prompts, scripts, tables) should live directly inline in the brief document itself, not scattered across separate attachments.
5. **Generated b-roll must be photoreal, not CGI-looking.** If a generated frame reads as a 3D-rendered model rather than a photograph, it must be regenerated — no exceptions. Never ship the first generation attempt as final: generate multiple variants of every scene and select the one free of visible artifacts before it goes in the brief.

Internal file paths and internal reference material remain completely valid for actual internal generation work (image-to-image references, internal replication pipelines) — this rule only governs what ends up inside a brief that's handed to an outside collaborator.
