## Editing language

**Shot structure.** Most changes follow a speaker, listener reaction, object being discussed, or a story chapter. The children’s exchanges commonly occupy roughly one to two seconds per coverage shot, but this is not a fixed-rate template. The reveal of the second exhibit gets a longer moving hold; the dedicated product explanation holds for just over six seconds. A cut count alone obscures those different jobs.

**Transitions.** Direct cuts dominate the conversation. At about 29.9–30.6s the exhibits visibly overlap in a cross-dissolve. At 59.7s the film cuts to black and the next scene fades in around 59.93–60.27s. These are different devices. The former connects two historical comparisons; the latter separates aspiration from discovery. Their exact editing presets and easing cannot be recovered from flattened media.

**Camera and blocking.** Alternating close-ups isolate attitudes. Shared frames establish relationships. Walking keeps the class moving through a spatially coherent museum; the teacher physically leads the story onward. The second exhibit is revealed with an upward/widening view, and the final dialogue uses a lateral move between children. Repeated lateral oscillations and abrupt in-shot crop changes are visible in places. They may be stylistic motion or generation/render artifacts; they should not be interpreted as intentional hard cuts or copied automatically. No reliable evidence establishes a speed ramp or original source speed.

**Visual continuity.** Cool blue architecture, warm amber exhibit lighting, glass reflections, repeating uniforms, eye lines and familiar faces maintain the world. Product lighting is brighter and more concentrated. The rocky base concretizes the formula’s earth-source claim. Cinematic finish can lend perceived credibility, but production quality does not verify historical or health claims.

**Captions and overlays.** Small white sans-serif phrase captions mostly sit around the lower middle of the vertical frame, separate from faces. They change independently of cuts. Some outgoing words persist onto the next character or onto black. No sustained karaoke coloring, large headline banners, explanatory anatomy overlays, price, offer, or explicit shop instruction was observed. The bottle label is part of the scene. This compact caption size should not be copied blindly for Motilli’s older audience; test larger readable type at phone size.

**Scene selection.** This is a single animated story world. The man, drink, second exhibit, forearm, pedestal and bottle are story objects, not unrelated stock B-roll. Repeated teacher coverage serves dialogue continuity; repeated random inserts would not serve the same purpose. The product mechanism is asserted in speech, not demonstrated with a scientific animation.
