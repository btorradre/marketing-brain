# 15 original Motilli animated mini-movie concepts

September 11, 2026 · Selection phase · Original fictional pitches, not customer testimonials, approved scripts or storyboards.

**Creative direction:** expressive, cinematic 3D animation with the warmth and character acting of a Pixar-style short; adult protagonists and adult emotional stakes. Target context is Motilli’s predominantly female, 50–70 GLP-1 audience. The desired outcome is participation in life: dinner, intimacy, travel, spontaneity and dignity.

Each pitch has a distinct story device, immediate action, an information gap, a revealing turn, and a callback. Opening dialogue below is original exploratory copy. Motilli enters after the character understands the problem and considers a routine. Specific physiology, efficacy, serving size and outcome timing are not yet scripted. The current formula/evidence must determine those passages; a fictional doctor does not establish them. Any benefit payoff must fit supportable product claims and honest passage of time. ER care remains clinical care.

## 1. The Anniversary He Thought She Forgot — first recommendation

**First frame / hook:** A dressed-up husband catches two champagne glasses as his wife abruptly pushes away from their candlelit anniversary table. He calls after her: “Was it something I said?”

**Story:** He rehearsed a loving speech. Her untouched meal and repeated disappearances look like rejection. He follows the sound of a small sob and finds her sitting behind the bathroom door, still wearing the earrings he gave her years ago. She did remember. She has been desperate to make it through this evening without explaining what has been happening to her digestion.

**Loop and turn:** Why is she avoiding him? The reveal changes absence from indifference into hidden effort. His hurt becomes understanding; her secrecy becomes a conversation about finding support.

**Product bridge / payoff:** Motilli is introduced later during her considered routine choice, after an unbranded explanation. Return to a later date night: she reaches for his hand before he can rehearse another speech.

**Belief shift / visual rationale:** Disrupted digestion can be taking intimacy and presence, not just bathroom time. The empty chair, closed door, earrings and joined hands make that cost visible without a symptom lecture.

## 2. The Bathroom Hostage Negotiation — strongest comedy

**First frame / hook:** Her husband drops to the floor and slides a handwritten negotiation offer beneath the bathroom door: “We can get you anything. Except another bathroom.”

**Story:** He treats his wife’s long disappearance like a hostage crisis. Their dog brings a slipper as ransom. She answers with dry humor until he asks what the captor wants. Her face drops: she genuinely does not know what else to try.

**Loop and turn:** What is happening behind the door? The joke reveals a real feeling of helplessness. He stops playing rescuer and starts listening.

**Product bridge / payoff:** A later, informed routine choice introduces Motilli. On a later evening she slips a note under his study door: he has been gaming too long and dinner is waiting.

**Belief shift / visual rationale:** A private problem can be talked about without humiliation. Floor-level framing, notes and the dog provide cinematic comedy; the held close-up creates the emotional turn.

## 3. The ER Calendar — strongest dramatic hook

**First frame / hook:** A worried woman on an ER bed is asked when she last had a bowel movement. She opens her phone calendar—and keeps scrolling backward.

**Story:** Her husband initially jokes to help her relax, then realizes how long she has hidden the difficulty. The staff attend to her; the story’s central admission is that she kept explaining away symptoms because she did not want to become a burden or lose her progress.

**Loop and turn:** How did she get to this moment without anyone knowing? The reveal is the sequence of private rationalizations, not a sensational diagnosis.

**Product bridge / payoff:** After appropriate care and a clear later-time transition, she considers everyday digestive support. Motilli is never the emergency intervention. A later calendar entry is an activity she looks forward to.

**Belief shift / visual rationale:** Quiet endurance is not the same as having a workable plan. The backward-scrolling calendar communicates time and secrecy more powerfully than a medical monologue. Highest need for careful causal/claim scripting.

## 4. The Woman Mopping the Ceiling Leak — clearest metaphor

**First frame / hook:** A wife in a dinner dress frantically mops a flooded kitchen. Her husband silently puts on swimming goggles.

**Story:** She has tried more towels, a bigger bucket and a second mop. Their bickering becomes absurd until he finally looks up: the water keeps arriving from the floor above. She pauses, recognizing her own habit of repeating a response without asking what is sustaining the problem.

**Loop and turn:** Why do all the fixes keep failing? The camera’s first upward reveal reorganizes the scene in one image.

**Product bridge / payoff:** That realization motivates her to learn about her digestive symptoms and evaluate support, with Motilli appearing only after the explanation. Their eventual dinner features one tiny bucket collecting a harmless drip from melting ice.

**Belief shift / visual rationale:** More of the same response may not answer the right question. This echoes the brand’s current spatial metaphor, but plumbing is an illustration, not proof of gastrointestinal causation.

## 5. The Suitcase That Wouldn’t Close

**First frame / hook:** A woman sits on her bulging suitcase while her husband pulls the zipper. It bursts open, scattering private bathroom supplies across the bed.

**Story:** He thought she had packed too many outfits. She thought she could hide the preparations for a simple weekend away. One small overnight trip has become a logistics operation organized around bathrooms.

**Loop and turn:** What could she possibly need for two nights? The reveal turns a familiar overpacking joke into recognition of the planning burden.

**Product bridge / payoff:** She chooses to investigate a more manageable routine; Motilli earns a place after that decision. On a later trip the bag shuts easily—until her husband tries to add three pairs of shoes.

**Belief shift / visual rationale:** The goal is freedom to go places, not merely ownership of another product. The suitcase physically contains the invisible burden and gives the ending a clean callback.

## 6. The Chair With Her Name on It

**First frame / hook:** A little girl hauls a red chair through a crowded school auditorium, fiercely protecting it from adults who try to sit down.

**Story:** We wonder who deserves this special seat. Cut to her grandmother at home, dressed and holding the invitation, debating whether she can safely leave. The child is not angry; she simply expects her favorite person to be there.

**Loop and turn:** Who is the chair for, and will she arrive? The grandmother’s desire is clear before her digestive worry is revealed.

**Product bridge / payoff:** Following a later support-routine sequence, return to another school event. Grandma arrives early and guards a chair for the child’s grandfather.

**Belief shift / visual rationale:** Her hesitation does not mean she has lost interest in family life. The bright red chair gives a recurring visual target. Keep the child loving; do not use accusation to guilt the grandmother.

## 7. The Husband Who Thought His Cooking Was Terrible

**First frame / hook:** A husband tips his entire carefully plated dinner into the trash after his wife pushes it away for the third evening.

**Story:** He secretly follows cooking tutorials, changes recipes and measures seasoning with absurd precision. She watches his effort with growing guilt. When he admits he thinks she hates his cooking, she finally explains that discomfort and unpredictable digestion have made meals fraught.

**Loop and turn:** What ingredient is he getting wrong? The missing information concerns her experience, not his recipe.

**Product bridge / payoff:** They learn and plan together; Motilli appears during her routine decision. Return later to him serving toast with the ceremony of a Michelin chef, and her affectionate eye roll.

**Belief shift / visual rationale:** A symptom can be misread as a relationship judgment. Plates, discarded recipes and small reactions externalize that misunderstanding. Stronger everyday comedy than concept1’s anniversary drama.

## 8. The House That Kept Getting Smaller

**First frame / hook:** A woman steps toward her front door and the entire entryway folds inward like a pop-up book.

**Story:** Each canceled plan removes another room from her animated world: the dance studio, the café, the airport lounge. Soon her bright, expansive life fits into a tiny space beside the bathroom. Nothing physically shrank; we have entered the way her choices feel.

**Loop and turn:** Who is shrinking her world? The reveal is the cumulative cost of organizing everything around digestive uncertainty.

**Product bridge / payoff:** Her decision to seek understanding opens the walls; a later routine introduces Motilli. The final pop-up is one modest, meaningful outing, not an implausible instant transformation.

**Belief shift / visual rationale:** Small adaptations can accumulate into a large loss of freedom. Paper architecture makes the emotional scale visible while clearly remaining fantasy. Best option for a visually ambitious short.

## 9. Mission Control: Upstairs Bathroom

**First frame / hook:** A wife in a bathroom headset declares, “I was supposed to be downstairs twenty minutes ago.” Her husband answers from a homemade mission-control desk.

**Story:** Preparing to leave for a dinner party becomes an elaborate launch sequence. He gives irrelevant updates; the dog hits the abort button. She finally removes the headset and admits that every outing has begun to feel this difficult.

**Loop and turn:** Why does an ordinary evening require a space mission? The fantasy collapses into a quiet, recognizable domestic moment.

**Product bridge / payoff:** Later support education and a considered Motilli introduction. End on a subsequent outing where he begins an elaborate countdown and she has already walked out the door.

**Belief shift / visual rationale:** Constant preparation is a burden worth addressing. Mission props magnify anticipatory anxiety; removing the headset marks the exact tonal turn. No internal conveyor-belt mechanism.

## 10. The Purse That Told the Truth

**First frame / hook:** At book club, a woman opens her purse for reading glasses and an avalanche of bathroom-related supplies tumbles across the table.

**Story:** Her carefully maintained composure collapses. She reaches to hide everything. Before she can invent an excuse, another woman quietly opens her own overstuffed bag. The awkward silence becomes candid, funny conversation.

**Loop and turn:** How will the group react? Expected judgment turns into recognition. The friends have different experiences and do not magically share one diagnosis.

**Product bridge / payoff:** One friend describes her own decision process; the protagonist independently evaluates Motilli after unbranded information. At the next meeting, she finds her glasses immediately but still has not finished the book.

**Belief shift / visual rationale:** Secrecy can exaggerate isolation. The spill reveals the practical burden in a single readable action. Fictional peers offer companionship, not fabricated verified testimonials.

## 11. The Postcard From the Trip She Never Took

**First frame / hook:** A woman opens a postcard showing herself on a sunlit Italian balcony. She has never been there.

**Story:** The card seems to come from a future version of her. She follows its small clues through a house full of postponed plans. We eventually discover that she wrote it to herself years ago as a promise, before digestive worries made every trip feel complicated.

**Loop and turn:** Who sent the impossible postcard? The answer is her own earlier hope, bringing back a desire she had quietly shelved.

**Product bridge / payoff:** She takes a practical first step toward understanding her symptoms and choosing support, including a later Motilli decision. The ending can be a local overnight trip and a new postcard, keeping progress human in scale.

**Belief shift / visual rationale:** Postponement does not mean the desire disappeared. The object connects past and present without copying the museum setting or inventing future medical certainty.

## 12. The Uninvited Dinner Guest

**First frame / hook:** A huge, impeccably dressed character named Maybe squeezes into the couple’s candlelit booth and orders for the wife.

**Story:** Maybe interrupts every decision: perhaps skip dinner, perhaps leave early, perhaps do not book the trip. Her husband cannot see this character. When she explains what has been happening, we understand Maybe as the personification of digestive uncertainty.

**Loop and turn:** Who is this rude third guest, and why does she obey him? The reveal turns an absurd dinner into an internal conflict made visible.

**Product bridge / payoff:** She seeks information and makes her own considered routine choice. Motilli enters there. At a later dinner, Maybe approaches; she puts her handbag on the spare chair and continues her conversation.

**Belief shift / visual rationale:** Uncertainty has started making choices on her behalf. The third chair externalizes that loss of agency. The character’s shrinking influence is metaphorical, not a promise that symptoms vanish.

## 13. The Bathroom Architect

**First frame / hook:** A retired architect unfolds a restaurant floor plan so enormous it knocks over her husband’s bread basket.

**Story:** He thinks she is admiring the building. She has actually marked every bathroom, exit and shortest route. Her professional skill has quietly become a tool for managing worry. When he asks where she wants to sit, she realizes she has not considered anything except proximity.

**Loop and turn:** Why did she bring blueprints to dinner? The answer reveals how thoroughly the problem has colonized her attention.

**Product bridge / payoff:** She decides to learn more and evaluate support, including Motilli later. At a subsequent restaurant, she chooses a seat for the view and critiques the architect’s ugly ceiling instead.

**Belief shift / visual rationale:** Being good at coping does not make coping effortless. Overhead plans, tabletop chaos and a final scenic window give this story a distinctive visual language.

## 14. The Dog Who Stole Her Secret

**First frame / hook:** The family dog bolts into the living room dragging a private bathroom-supply bag while guests are arriving.

**Story:** She chases him through a slapstick obstacle course. Her husband assumes it is a wrapped present. The chase ends in the laundry room, where her distress makes it clear the joke has touched something real. He closes the door to give her privacy and asks how he can help.

**Loop and turn:** What is in the bag that matters so much? The reveal explains the chase and changes the husband from accidental obstacle to ally.

**Product bridge / payoff:** Later, a supportive conversation and her informed routine choice introduce Motilli. End with the dog stealing an ordinary slipper while she lets her husband do the chasing.

**Belief shift / visual rationale:** The burden includes constant concealment. Dog action provides a strong first-frame hook; the closed laundry-room door protects her dignity at the reveal.

## 15. The Wrong Winner

**First frame / hook:** A bathroom scale launches confetti and announces a celebration while the woman beside it is dressed for a party she is about to cancel.

**Story:** Her progress gets an elaborate award ceremony in her imagination. But each trophy is interrupted by an empty dinner chair, a packed-away dress or an unanswered invitation. She finally stops the ceremony: she wants the health journey to include how her days feel, too.

**Loop and turn:** Why does she look unhappy at her own celebration? The reveal is that one meaningful kind of progress has not answered every need.

**Product bridge / payoff:** She seeks appropriate support without an implied change to prescribed medication. Motilli appears as a considered addition only within supportable claims. A later celebration is a small dance in the kitchen; the scale tries to join in.

**Belief shift / visual rationale:** A number is not the whole lived outcome. Confetti against visible disappointment creates a readable opening contradiction. Keep the protagonist’s existing progress valuable rather than portraying her treatment as a mistake.

## Selection guidance

| Priority | Concept | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Best first film | 1 — Anniversary | Clear misreading, adult emotional stakes, warm reversal, strong callback, manageable locations. |
| Best comic film | 2 — Hostage negotiation | Immediate situation, character banter and an emotional turn that can fit a short runtime. |
| Most dramatic | 3 — ER calendar | Extremely legible stakes and secrecy reveal; requires the most care in separating medical treatment from product role. |
| Most visually distinctive | 8 — Shrinking house | Animation has a necessary storytelling function, not just an attractive surface. |
| Closest to current brand metaphor | 4 — Ceiling leak | Clear spatial reversal; the metaphor must not stand in for substantiated physiology. |
| Best aspirational identity conflict | 15 — Wrong winner | Expresses the desire to keep meaningful progress while also participating in life. |

These are editorial judgments, not predicted performance scores. After selection, write the complete original dialogue and a concrete scene-by-scene editing plan before generating assets.
