Why sublingual instead of a capsule?

Have Questions? We Have Answers.
I've had numbness for years. Can this actually still help me, or is it too late?
Nerve damage that's gone on for a long time is harder to reverse than newer damage, but many people still see B12 status affecting their symptoms even after years of neuropathy. There's no way to promise a specific outcome for your situation, but restoring depleted B12 gives your nerves what they need to repair, whatever stage you're starting from.
Will this replace my gabapentin or Lyrica?
No, and you shouldn't stop any prescribed medication without talking to your doctor. Vitarosa isn't a substitute for medications your doctor has prescribed. It addresses a different piece of the puzzle, restoring B12 status, which may work alongside what you're already doing.
I already take a multivitamin. Isn't that enough?
Most multivitamins include a small amount of B12 in a form (cyanocobalamin) your body has to convert before use, and often include B6, which we deliberately leave out. If you're on metformin, a general multivitamin usually isn't giving your nerves what they specifically need.
Is this just another supplement that's not going to do anything?
That skepticism is fair, a lot of supplements overpromise. Vitarosa isn't built around a vague "vitamins are good for you" claim. It's built around a specific, documented mechanism: metformin depletes B12, B12 is required for nerve repair, and this delivers it in a form and method your body can actually use.
Can I take this if I'm not diabetic?
Yes. B12 depletion and its effect on nerve health isn't exclusive to diabetics or metformin users. Anyone dealing with nerve discomfort related to low B12, B1, or B9 status can benefit from this formula.
What's the correct dosage and how should I take it?
