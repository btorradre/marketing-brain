The Backdoor That Was Hiding The Whole Time

While I was deep in the Tanaka papers from Japan, I came across something I'd never been taught at medical school.

And the conjunctiva — the soft pink tissue sitting on the inside of your eyelid — is up to 30 times more permeable to active compounds than the cornea itself (Davies, 2000; Tanaka et al., 2018).

❌ In plain English: nature built a soft, thin, absorbent door directly to the back of your eye. And nobody in mainstream eye care has ever told you it exists.

Two thin tissues. Both designed by three million years of evolution to absorb. Both pressed up against the ocular environment of your retina.

It is the shortest distance between the outside world and your macula that does not involve a needle.

A nicotine patch uses the same principle to deliver nicotine into your bloodstream through the skin on your arm. An HRT patch does the same with oestrogen.

A scopolamine patch behind your ear has been used to prevent seasickness in the Royal Navy since 1979.

The transdermal route is not new. It is not fringe. It is one of the most established delivery systems in modern medicine.

It has simply never been applied properly to the macula until now.
