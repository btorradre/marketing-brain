# Motilli Product Development Brief: Custom Wild Celery Formulation
**Date:** 2026-08-11
**Budget:** $20,000 initial
**Mandate:** New, higher-quality, US-manufactured, custom-formulated product anchored on wild celery juice powder, for the women 45-70 occasional-constipation avatar. No stimulant laxatives. Real, verified formula and real CoAs from day one.

---

## 0. Non-negotiables before anything else

1. **The formula on the label must be the formula in the tub.** The previous Motilli supplement-facts panel was fabricated and every mg claim had to be stripped from the site. That can never happen again. This product launches with a master manufacturing record, batch records, and a batch-specific Certificate of Analysis from an ISO 17025 accredited third-party lab before the first unit ships. Every mg on the panel traces to a raw-material CoA plus finished-product potency testing.
2. **No stimulant laxatives.** No senna, no cascara sagrada, no bisacodyl, no aloe latex. This is both the safety position and the entire marketing position (non-habit-forming, works with the body).
3. **cGMP, FDA-registered US facility only.** 21 CFR Part 111 compliance is the floor, not a feature.

## 1. Core formulation for the constipation avatar

### 1a. The anchor: wild celery juice powder

**What it is.** Celery (Apium graveolens) juice, dried to powder. "Wild celery" typically refers to Apium graveolens in its uncultivated form or to celery seed material. Juice powder retains the minerals (potassium, sodium, natural nitrates), flavonoids (apigenin, luteolin), and phthalides (3-n-butylphthalide) but very little of the fiber, because juicing removes it.

**Honest evidence read.** There are no randomized controlled trials of celery juice or celery juice powder for constipation or bowel regularity in humans. The evidence base is preclinical (animal and in vitro work on phthalides, apigenin, and smooth-muscle and anti-inflammatory effects), traditional use, and a consumer phenomenon (the Medical Medium celery juice trend). Celery seed extract has small human trials for blood pressure and uric acid, not for motility. Plainly: celery is the brand anchor and the story carrier, not the clinical workhorse. The formula must be built so it would work even if celery contributed nothing beyond minerals and hydration ritual. It fits THE SIGNAL world well (mineral salts, plant nerve chemistry, morning hydration ritual), so it earns its place as identity plus supporting player.

**Dose.** 1,500-2,000 mg juice powder per serving. This is a meaningful, honest inclusion (roughly equivalent to a few ounces of fresh juice depending on concentration ratio, verify the supplier's ratio, typically 5:1 to 20:1) without pretending it is the active engine. Bulk organic celery juice powder is readily available from US wholesale suppliers (Medikonda Nutrients, NutriCargo, PFVN and others) with organic certification and per-lot CoAs.

**Format fit.** Fits easily in a powder. Would consume an entire gummy or capsule serving on its own at this dose.

### 1b. The evidence-ranked stack

Ranked by strength of human evidence for occasional constipation and regularity.

**Psyllium husk. Evidence: strong.**
- Mechanism: gel-forming soluble fiber, increases stool water and bulk, normalizes form in both directions.
- Evidence: multiple RCTs and meta-analyses; the American College of Gastroenterology gives soluble fiber (psyllium specifically) its strongest fiber recommendation for chronic idiopathic constipation.
- Effective dose: 5-10 g/day.
- Format fit: powder only. 5 g does not fit in gummies (a typical gummy carries ~0.5-1 g of everything) or capsules (would need 8-12 caps). Downside: gritty, thickens fast in water, "Metamucil for old people" texture perception. This matters for a premium daily-ritual product.

**Partially hydrolyzed guar gum, PHGG (Sunfiber). Evidence: moderate, and the best texture in class.**
- Mechanism: low-viscosity fermentable soluble fiber; normalizes stool form, feeds butyrate producers, improves regularity without the gel-brick texture.
- Evidence: multiple small-to-mid RCTs in constipated adults, elderly, and children showing improved stool frequency and form; also studied in IBS-C. Not as deep as psyllium's evidence base, but real controlled human data.
- Effective dose: 5-6 g/day.
- Format fit: powder only, and it is the reason the powder can taste like a beverage instead of wallpaper paste. Dissolves clear, no grit, no thickening. Sunfiber is a branded, clinically referenced ingredient with supplier CoAs. **This is the recommended fiber base.**

**Acacia fiber. Evidence: weak for constipation.** Well tolerated, prebiotic, but human trials are mostly tolerability and microbiome endpoints, not regularity outcomes. Use only as a texture/cost blend partner, not as a claim carrier.

**Magnesium. Evidence: strong.**
- Mechanism: poorly absorbed magnesium salts act osmotically, drawing water into the colon and softening stool; magnesium is also a cofactor in neuromuscular signaling, which fits THE SIGNAL story honestly.
- Evidence: randomized placebo-controlled trials of magnesium oxide in chronic constipation (notably Japanese RCTs, including a 2021 placebo-controlled trial where MgO ~1.5 g/day outperformed placebo on spontaneous bowel movements). Magnesium citrate is standard practice medicine at higher doses.
- Trade-offs: oxide is ~60% elemental, cheap, clinically validated, but poorly absorbed (which is partly why it works) and chalky. Citrate is ~16% elemental, bulkier per mg of magnesium, but tart (helps flavor), better tolerated at moderate doses, and reads premium.
- Effective dose: 200-350 mg elemental per serving for a daily gentle-osmotic effect (staying under the 350 mg/day supplemental UL for a no-questions daily product). As citrate that is roughly 1.3-2.2 g of material.
- Format fit: 300 mg elemental as citrate fits a powder trivially. It does NOT fit gummies: 2-4 gummies max out around 50-100 mg elemental and blow the sugar budget doing it.

**Green kiwifruit powder (Actazin). Evidence: good, and it is the differentiating hero.**
- Mechanism: actinidin (protein-digesting enzyme), kiwi fiber's high water-holding capacity, and prebiotic polyphenols; whole green kiwifruit (2/day) beat prunes and psyllium on comfort in a 2021 randomized comparative trial in constipated adults.
- Evidence for the branded powder: Actazin at 2,400 mg/day improved laxation (more than one additional weekly bowel movement) in controlled work, and a 2024 randomized double-blind placebo-controlled trial (85 participants, 28 days) showed 600 mg/day improved Bristol stool form and relieved occasional constipation. Livaux (gold kiwifruit) at 600 mg increased Faecalibacterium prausnitzii in functionally constipated adults in an RCT.
- Effective dose: 600 mg/day is the studied maintenance/regularity dose; 2,400 mg/day is the studied constipation-relief dose. Recommend **2,400 mg** in v1 so the product carries the full studied dose and the marketing can lean on the ingredient's clinical file without dose-washing. Distributed in the US via AIDP.
- Format fit: fits powder easily; 2,400 mg alone would be 4-5 capsules, impossible in gummies.

**Prunes / sorbitol. Evidence: strong for whole prunes, weak at powder-friendly doses.** The classic RCT (prunes vs psyllium) used ~50 g prunes/day, delivering ~6 g sorbitol plus fiber. A sprinkle of prune powder at 500 mg is label decoration, not efficacy. Either include ~5 g prune juice powder (eats the flavor system and the scoop) or leave it out. Recommend: leave out of v1; kiwifruit occupies the fruit-mechanism slot with better dose economics.

**Ginger. Evidence: weak for constipation, real for upper-GI motility.** Human data supports accelerated gastric emptying and nausea relief at 1-1.5 g; there are no good constipation RCTs. Include a modest 500 mg root extract as the "gets things moving" prokinetic note in the mechanism story, claimed only for digestive comfort. Be honest internally: this is a story-and-comfort ingredient.

**Artichoke leaf extract. Evidence: weak for constipation, moderate for bloat/dyspepsia.** RCTs exist for functional dyspepsia and IBS symptom scores at 320-640 mg. Better saved for the menopause/bloat line extension than the core constipation SKU.

**Probiotics with constipation RCTs. Evidence: mixed, be careful.**
- B. lactis HN019: a dose-response trial showed shortened whole-gut transit time at 10^9-10^10 CFU, but a later larger RCT missed its primary transit endpoint. Evidence is genuinely mixed.
- B. lactis BB-12: a large RCT (n>1,200) showed a modest increase in defecation frequency.
- Verdict: defensible at 5-10 billion CFU but adds cost, overage complexity (must overage CFU to guarantee label claim through shelf life), and stability risk in a hygroscopic powder. Recommend: **hold for v2** or a capsule companion SKU. Do not let a probiotic delay v1.

**Electrolyte/hydration support. Evidence: supportive, not primary.** Fiber and osmotic magnesium both need water. A small sodium + potassium inclusion (sea salt ~100-150 mg sodium, potassium ~200 mg from citrate plus what celery contributes naturally) turns the product into a morning hydration ritual and is honest physiology. Celery juice powder is itself a natural potassium/sodium source, which ties the anchor ingredient to a real function.

## 2. Delivery mechanism recommendation

**Recommendation: flavored powder, 30-serving tub with scoop for v1; stick packs as a v2 format once volume justifies the MOQ.**

| Format | Dose headroom | Fit for this formula | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gummies | ~2-4 g total per serving, most of it sugar and pectin | **Fails.** 5 g fiber + 2 g Mg citrate + 2.4 g Actazin + 2 g celery cannot physically fit. Meaningful fiber or magnesium doses do not exist in gummy format anywhere in the market; gummy fiber products are dose theater. | This is exactly the current Motilli product's ceiling. |
| Capsules | ~0.75-1 g per capsule | Marginal. The formula would need 12-15 capsules/day. Kiwifruit + probiotic alone could work as a 3-4 cap product, but the fiber and magnesium engine cannot ride along. | Good for a future companion SKU. |
| Powder scoop/tub | 8-15 g per serving, effectively unlimited | **Wins.** Full clinical doses of everything fit in one ~11 g scoop. | AG1/Bloom-style daily ritual, premium pricing normal ($40-70/30 servings), highest perceived value, best subscription behavior. |
| Powder stick packs | Same headroom as tub | Wins on UX, loses on MOQ (typical stick-pack minimums ~3,000+ units and higher tooling/changeover cost). | Do as v2. |
| Liquid shots | 30-60 ml | Fiber doses turn viscous and gritty in liquid; preservation and stability cost more; MOQs and per-unit costs are worse at low volume. | Not for this formula. |

**Why powder also wins strategically:** Motilli's existing product is a gummy. A powder is a second price point and a second ritual, it differentiates instead of cannibalizing, and it lets the ads tell the truth: "the doses that actually move the needle do not fit in a gummy, so we built the full-dose version." That is a natural upgrade path from the existing gummy customer base, and it puts Motilli in the Bloom/AG1 daily-scoop category where the 45-70 female avatar already lives (Bloom's own buyer skews mirror the Orelli research: women 45-54 heavy).

**Ritual:** one scoop in 12-16 oz of cold water, first thing in the morning, before coffee. The water is not incidental; it is part of the mechanism (fiber and osmotic magnesium both require it), which makes the ritual itself an honest claim.

## 3. Positioning honesty (what we can and cannot say)

**The frame Brooks wants:** "factually superior to laxatives." The compliant version of that frame:

**CAN claim (structure/function, truthful with this formula at these doses):**
- "Relieves occasional constipation" and "supports regularity." FDA's structure/function rule explicitly treats occasional constipation as acceptable S/F territory; chronic constipation is disease territory.
- "No stimulant laxatives. No senna, no cascara, no bisacodyl."
- "Non-habit-forming" (true: nothing in the formula has a dependency profile).
- "Works with your body's own rhythm" / "supports the gut's natural motility signal" (S/F language that fits THE SIGNAL world).
- "Clinically studied ingredients at clinically studied doses" (true only because we are using Actazin at 2,400 mg, PHGG at 5 g+, and magnesium at an evidence-based osmotic dose; this claim is exactly why we do not dose-wash).
- "Gentle" / "comfortable" comparative texture claims vs. the category experience.

**CANNOT claim:**
- Treat, cure, or prevent constipation as a condition; anything about "chronic constipation," IBS, gastroparesis, or any disease state.
- "Better/safer than laxatives" as a comparative drug claim. We can say what we do NOT contain and describe our mechanism; we cannot make head-to-head superiority or safety claims against OTC drugs.
- Anything about GLP-1 drugs by name as a treatment interaction claim (see section 4a for the compliant angle).
- Any mg or CFU number that a batch CoA cannot back. This is the fabricated-panel lesson written into policy.

**Required mechanics under DSHEA:**
- Every S/F claim carries the disclaimer: "This statement has not been evaluated by the Food and Drug Administration. This product is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease."
- File the 30-day structure/function claim notification with FDA after first marketing.
- Label review by a regulatory consultant before print (a few hundred dollars, budgeted below). Claims substantiation file: keep the Actazin RCTs, PHGG trials, and magnesium RCTs on file mapped to each claim.

## 4. Multi-avatar expansion paths

The base formula is deliberately a platform: fiber engine + osmotic mineral + clinically dosed fruit enzyme + celery identity. Each avatar is a variant or a repositioned front-end, not a new R&D cycle.

### 4a. GLP-1 users (existing Motilli GLP-1 angle)
- **Formula changes:** keep magnesium and Actazin at full dose; consider trimming PHGG to ~3-4 g because GLP-1 users have slowed gastric emptying and early satiety, and a large fiber bolus can worsen fullness and nausea. Add electrolytes more prominently (GLP-1 users chronically under-drink and under-eat). Optional variant: add a modest protein or amino inclusion later, but that is a different product; do not Frankenstein it into this one.
- **Positioning changes:** "Your appetite slowed down. So did everything else." Speak to the constipation-on-GLP-1 experience (it is one of the most reported side effects) using S/F language about occasional constipation and hydration, never naming the drugs in a treatment-interaction claim. This slots directly into the existing Bloom-GLP-1-stomach ad world Motilli is already running.

### 4b. Menopausal women (bloat, hormonal transit changes, bone synergy)
- **Formula changes:** same base; raise the case for magnesium (bone support S/F claim is legitimate and heavily shopped by this avatar) and add artichoke leaf extract 320-640 mg for the bloat/digestive-comfort layer. This is where artichoke earns its slot.
- **Positioning changes:** transit slows across the menopause transition and bloat becomes the felt symptom. Lead with bloat and "your gut changed when your hormones did," with regularity as the payoff and magnesium's bone claim as the trust-builder. The core 45-70 avatar already overlaps this; it may end up being the same SKU with a different funnel front-end rather than a different tub.

### 4c. Skincare / beauty-from-within (gut-skin axis)
- **Formula changes:** variant SKU adding hydrolyzed collagen peptides (2.5-10 g, real RCT support for skin elasticity/hydration at 2.5-5 g) and optionally a ceramide ingredient (e.g. wheat-derived ceramides ~350 mg, small RCTs on skin hydration). Powder format absorbs the collagen dose effortlessly, which is exactly why the format choice matters.
- **Positioning changes:** "clear gut, clear skin" gut-skin-axis story. Be honest internally: gut-skin-axis causal evidence is early; the collagen claims carry the efficacy load while regularity is the differentiator no other beauty powder leads with. This is a later launch; it needs its own flavor system and brand surface.

## 5. US manufacturing within $20,000

### Realistic path

$20k does not buy a 10,000-unit run with custom flavor development at a tier-one co-man. It does buy a 1,000-1,500 unit custom powder run at a low-MOQ manufacturer with real testing and professional packaging, which is exactly the right size for a validation launch against the existing Motilli traffic engine.

### Candidate contract manufacturers (verified August 2026)

1. **SMP Nutra** (Edgewood, NY). 155,000 sq ft cGMP, FDA-registered facility; runs identity, potency, micro, and heavy-metals on finished powder. Custom flavored powder MOQ ~2,500 units (some formulas 1,500); stock formulas from 1,000. Known for turnkey speed and broad format range. The 2,500 custom MOQ is the stretch point; negotiate or use them at the top of budget.
2. **Makers Nutrition** (Hauppauge, NY). Full-service co-man with MOQs advertised as low as ~500 units on some products, powder blends and sachet conversion, strong on turnkey packaging and ecommerce-ready presentation. Best fit if the low MOQ holds for a custom flavored powder; get it in writing.
3. **NutraScience Labs** (Farmingdale, NY). Custom formulation and powder blending specialist since 2015 with in-house flavorists, scalable runs, and fulfillment. Known for hand-holding first-time custom formulas. MOQs typically 1,500-2,500 for powders; quote directly.
4. **Nutricraft Labs**. Runs an explicit 1,000-unit MOQ program across formats, positioned specifically for founders validating a formula before scaling. Matches this project's budget shape exactly; diligence their cGMP registration and testing regime as part of the quote.
5. **Atrium Scientific** (California). FDA-registered, cGMP stick-pack specialist, stick-pack MOQ ~3,000 units. Not the v1 partner, but the named v2 partner for the stick-pack format.
6. **Superior Supplement Manufacturing** (San Diego, CA). Low-MOQ custom manufacturing across capsules and powders, commonly cited around 1,000-unit minimums, known for working with small DTC brands. Good second-coast option for quote leverage.

Get three quotes minimum (suggest SMP, Makers, Nutricraft) with identical spec sheets so pricing is comparable. The spec sheet is the formula in section 6 plus flavor target, tub size, and testing requirements.

At 1,000 units, $49.99 retail, and even a 50% sell-through in 90 days, the run pays back ~$25k gross, enough to fund run two at 2,500 units where per-unit cost drops meaningfully.

### Lead times

- Quotes and formulation: 2-4 weeks
- Flavor sampling and approval: 3-5 weeks (run in parallel with label design)
- Raw material procurement: 4-8 weeks (Actazin as an imported branded ingredient is the long pole; order early)
- Manufacturing + testing + release: 4-6 weeks
- **Realistic total: 14-20 weeks from signed quote to sellable inventory.** Plan for a Q4 2026 / early Q1 2027 launch.

### Corners NOT to cut

- Third-party finished-product testing on every batch. Ever. This is the fabricated-panel lesson.
- cGMP/FDA registration verification of the co-man (ask for their FDA registration number and most recent audit certificate; NSF or UL registration is a plus).
- Identity testing on the celery juice powder specifically (juice powders are a known adulteration/dilution category; specify the juice-to-powder ratio in the spec and test against it).
- Probiotic CFU overages and stability data if/when the probiotic version ships.
- Label regulatory review before printing. A reprint on 1,000 tubs is cheap; an FDA warning letter or a Meta account flag for disease claims is not.

## 6. Recommended starting SKU

**Working name:** Motilli Daily Signal (powder)
**Format:** 30-serving tub, ~11 g scoop, flavored (recommend a bright citrus-celery "morning tonic" profile: lemon + faint green freshness; do not fight the celery note, own it), mixed in 12-16 oz cold water, every morning before coffee.

**Formula per serving (~11 g):**

| Ingredient | Dose | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Sunfiber partially hydrolyzed guar gum | 5,000 mg | Gentle soluble fiber engine, stool normalization, invisible texture |
| Actazin green kiwifruit powder | 2,400 mg | The studied constipation-relief dose; enzyme + fruit fiber hero |
| Wild celery juice powder (organic) | 2,000 mg | Brand anchor, mineral/nitrate/flavonoid story, natural sodium-potassium source |

**Mechanism story (one line, THE SIGNAL world):**
"Constipation is not a fiber shortage, it is a weak signal. One scoop rebuilds the morning signal: celery's mineral salts carry the nerve impulse, magnesium pulls water down to meet it, and kiwi enzymes plus gentle fiber give the muscle something to move."

**Ad-facing truth check on that story:** magnesium's neuromuscular role, celery's electrolyte content, actinidin's enzymatic activity, and fiber's mechanics are all real; the causal "rebuilds the signal" framing is S/F storytelling, not a clinical claim, and every named ingredient is present at an honest dose. That is the difference between this label and the last one.
