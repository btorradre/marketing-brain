# Motilli — Demographic Research: GLP-1 vs IBS-C vs Chronic Constipation TAM
*Compiled 2026-05-11 from KFF, CDC NCHS, RAND, Statista, AJMC, NCBI, Hootsuite, plus US Census frame*

---

## The two hypotheses to test

**Your hypothesis 1:** "The age demographic I'm trying to target is 65+."
**Your hypothesis 2:** "IBS-C will have a wider total addressable market."

**Both hypotheses are partly wrong, but for instructive reasons.** Below is what the data actually says, and the third option neither of us discussed that is plausibly the largest unlock.

---

## Part 1: GLP-1 User Demographics (the actual numbers)

### Headline usage
- **~12% of US adults currently use a GLP-1** (~30M people). Up from 5.8% in Feb 2024 → 12.4% by late 2025. The wave is still cresting.
- **Women:** 15% currently using. **Men:** 9%. Strongly female-skewed.
- 587% rise in obesity/weight-loss prescriptions since 2019. 1,961% rise in non-diabetic prescriptions in same window.

### Age distribution of current GLP-1 users (KFF Nov 2025)
| Age | % currently using GLP-1 |
|---|---|
| 18-29 | not separately reported, low |
| 30-49 | meaningful (women 2x more likely than men) |
| **50-64** | **22% (PEAK)** |
| 65+ | **9%** (drops sharply — Medicare doesn't cover for weight loss) |

### Diabetic GLP-1 use (CDC NCHS, 2024 NHIS)
| Age | % of diabetics using GLP-1 |
|---|---|
| 18-34 | 25.3% |
| 35-49 | 29.7% |
| **50-64** | **33.3% (PEAK)** |
| 65+ | 20.8% (drops) |

### What this means for your "I'm targeting 65+" assumption

**You're not actually targeting 65+. You're targeting 50-64.** The data shows:
- 65+ is **only 9% of all GLP-1 users** (and only 1% of weight-loss-specific users).
- 65+ doesn't take GLP-1 in volume because **Medicare doesn't cover GLP-1 for weight loss.** This is a structural limit, not a marketing problem.
- Your existing ads ("women 50-70") and your existing product context ("Age 50-70, peak 45-55") correctly target where the volume actually is. If your live campaigns are returning a 65+ skew in your reports, that's a Meta delivery artifact, not your strategy.

**Your real audience: women age 45-64 (peak 50-64) on a GLP-1.** That's about 8-10M people based on cross-multiplying KFF rates by US Census. Not small. But finite — and saturable inside Meta's actually-targetable interest cohorts. Which matches exactly what your 90-day decay curve shows.

## Part 2: Facebook Demographics — Is Your Audience There?

### US Facebook users by age (Statista, December 2025)
| Age | % of US FB users | Approx US count |
|---|---|---|
| 18-24 | 18.6% | ~47M |
| 25-34 | 24.2% (largest) | ~61M |
| 35-44 | 19.0% | ~48M |
| 45-54 | 14.2% | ~36M |
| **55-64** | **11.7% (smallest)** | **~30M** |
| 65+ | 12.3% | ~31M |

### Engagement by age — the counterintuitive part

This is where the data flips most people's instinct:

- **54% of US adults 50-64 visit Facebook DAILY** (Pew). Higher than the 30-49 cohort (49% daily).
- **65+ are the most likely to use Facebook RELATIVE to their overall internet use** — they're not online for many things, but the things they DO online happen on Facebook.
- **50% of Boomers say Facebook is where they engage with influencers most** (vs Instagram/TikTok).
- **Seniors spend close to 50% of all US purchasing dollars** and are the **best mail-order / direct-response buyers by frequency, multi-purchase rate, and avg dollar amount** (CDMG long-running marketing-to-seniors data).
- **Older buyers read long copy.** This is why your long-form ads work in the first place — younger demos scroll past them.

### What this means for your audience math

Your "50-64 woman on a GLP-1" target sits in:
- An age cohort that's only ~12% of Facebook users (lower visibility)
- ...but the most engaged + highest-purchasing cohort on the platform
- ...and the most-receptive cohort to long-form direct-response copy

**You're not targeting wrong. You're targeting a smaller-but-denser corner of Facebook.** The lower volume is real. The higher dollar-per-impression is also real. This is a tradeoff, not an error.

**Where you'd be wrong:** if you were actually targeting 65+ as your primary, you'd be on top of a cohort that is (a) only 9% of GLP-1 users, (b) heavily Medicare-disqualified, and (c) less price-flexible. The 50-64 sweet spot you actually have is the right one.

## Part 3: IBS-C Demographics — The Hypothesis That Doesn't Hold

### Total prevalence
- **IBS overall:** 6.1% of US adults (Rome IV criteria) = ~15M people
- **IBS-C specifically:** 33.6% of IBS cases = **~5M US adults**
- **Female-skewed:** 2:1 women:men in Western countries; women specifically over-represented in the IBS-C subtype

### Age distribution — this is the big one
- **IBS peaks at ages 20-30.** Most patients develop first symptoms before 40.
- **25% LESS IBS** diagnosed in adults over 50.
- IBS-C is "predominantly seen in ages under 50."

### Reality check on the TAM hypothesis

| Market | US population | Age skew | Female skew |
|---|---|---|---|
| GLP-1 users | ~30M | 50-64 peak | Female-heavy (15% W vs 9% M) |
| **IBS-C** | **~5M** | **Under 50 peak (20-30 modal)** | **2:1 female** |

**IBS-C is 6x SMALLER than GLP-1, not larger.** And it skews **dramatically younger** than your existing product, copy, testimonials, and audience.

The TAM hypothesis doesn't hold. What might be confusing the picture: IBS-C is *actively scaling* for brands like Atrantil and Bimuno right now, and that's real. But it's not because IBS-C is a bigger market than GLP-1 — it's because (a) it's an established market with proven supplement demand and prescription competition (Linzess, Trulance, Amitiza) you can wedge against, (b) it's a Level 4 sophistication market where a fresh mechanism story breaks through, and (c) the audience is YOUNGER and therefore on different platforms (TikTok, Instagram), which the brands you've seen "printing" may be exploiting. Pure population math doesn't favor it.

**Specifically for Motilli, IBS-C would be a worse first pivot because:**
- Your testimonials are all 50-70-year-olds. They won't resonate with a 30-year-old IBS-C sufferer.
- Your "doctor characters" are positioned as motility specialists for GLP-1 — they'd have to be rebuilt for IBS-C.
- Your PDP, advertorials, and listicle pages are all GLP-1-led — they'd need full rewrites.
- IBS-C sufferers have already tried 10+ supplements. Your "celery prokinetic" wedge competes against established players with deep clinical-trial data, not against generic Miralax.

## Part 4: The Option Neither of Us Was Looking At

### Chronic Idiopathic Constipation (CIC) / Chronic Constipation in older women

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| **US prevalence** | **9-20% of adults** = **~35M people** |
| Women predominate | **4.4x more likely** than men |
| Age skew | **Increases with age**; **23.3% of women 60+** report it |
| Big jump after age 70 | Yes |
| 84% of CIC patients | Age 35+ |

**This is the demographic match you didn't ask about and that the data screams about:**

- **35M Americans** (larger than the entire GLP-1 user base of 30M)
- **Female-dominant** (4.4x ratio) — matches your existing customer base
- **Older-skewed** — matches your existing copy, testimonials, doctor characters, age 50-70 voice
- **23.3% prevalence among women 60+** — your "65+ audience on Facebook" hypothesis is actually scalable HERE, not in GLP-1
- Crosses over with GLP-1 (many GLP-1 women have age-related slowing too)
- Same mechanism story applies (apigenin = prokinetic = "your stomach has slowed down")
- Lower sophistication than IBS-C (women experiencing this for the first time post-50, not 20-year veterans)
- Less prescription competition (Linzess is for IBS-C; CIC has fewer Rx options people are loyal to)

### The mechanism overlap

GLP-1s slow gastric motility. **Age also slows gastric motility** (well-documented). The exact same upstream/downstream / wrong-organ wedge works for "your gut has gradually slowed down with age" as it does for "your GLP-1 slowed down your gut." Same product, same mechanism, same testimonials, broader doorway.

This is the angle test I'd actually want to run. Not GLP-1 vs IBS-C. **GLP-1 vs general "post-50 motility decline."**

## Part 6: What This Means For Your Next Move

### Re: the 65+ hypothesis

**You're not actually skewed 65+.** Your real-world target is women 50-64, which is the right cohort for your product, your copy, and the actual GLP-1 user base. If reports inside Meta show 65+ delivery skew, that's the algorithm finding cheap impressions among the most-engaged-but-Medicare-disqualified cohort. The fix isn't "target younger" — it's tighter campaign-level age caps (45-64) in your ad sets, which probably costs you 5-10% CPM but improves conversion mix.

### Re: IBS-C as the wider TAM

**It's not wider — it's about 1/6 the size and demographically inverted from your existing assets.** What you're seeing in brands "printing" on IBS-C right now is fresh mechanism wedges in an established category, on platforms (TikTok, IG) where their younger avatar lives. Replicating that for Motilli would mean rebuilding the entire creative and brand presentation from scratch. Possible — but not where the data points first.

### Re: the recommended actual test

**Run the broadening test on "post-50 motility decline / chronic constipation in older women," not IBS-C.** Specifically:

1. **Audience:** keep your existing Meta targeting (women 45-64, weight-loss interests, etc.)
2. **Copy rewrite:** swap the first 200 words of your top-performing long-form ad. Replace "If you're on Ozempic, Wegovy, or Mounjaro..." with "If you're a woman over 50 whose gut has slowly stopped working the way it used to — and laxatives, fiber, and probiotics aren't fixing it..."
3. **Keep everything else:** mechanism education, doctor character, testimonials, product story, CTAs — all the same. The mechanism is the same. The product is the same. The pain experience is the same.
4. **Test budget:** $400-500/day on 0756, 5-7 days, parallel to your current GLP-1-led ads.
5. **What you're measuring:** does the broader-hook ad sustain or improve LPV→ATC vs the GLP-1-led ad? Or does it kill the conversion rate by losing message-product specificity?

If broader wins → you've unlocked a 20M-person TAM expansion using same product, same mechanism, same testimonials. If it loses → you've confirmed GLP-1 is the moat and you stop second-guessing it.

Either outcome resolves the strategy question in a week.

### Re: the bigger structural question

Your real bottleneck isn't TAM. You're still earning 5% CTR, $1.10 CPC, 35% ATC-to-purchase. **You haven't been spending enough to saturate even the GLP-1 market at full conversion.** The CRO audit found the page-level conversion killers (sticky CTA off, wrong-mechanism bug on a key bridge, no review widget, page weight crisis). Fix those FIRST. Then run the TAM-broadening test on a less-broken funnel.

If the bridge-rebuild + sticky-CTA + review-widget fixes move your PDP from 2.9% to 4.5% LPV→Pur (very achievable), your GLP-1 audience economics work even at current scale. Then TAM expansion is upside, not survival.
