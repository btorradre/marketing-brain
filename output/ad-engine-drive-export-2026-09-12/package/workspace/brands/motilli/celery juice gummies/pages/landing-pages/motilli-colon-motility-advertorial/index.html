Why MiraLAX And Fiber Keep Failing You

For decades, the standard teaching has been that constipation is a water-and-fiber problem. So we hand out MiraLAX, recommend more fiber, and tell people to drink more water.

But here's the part I had to learn the hard way: for someone already drinking 80 to 100 ounces a day and eating plenty of fiber, "more water and more fiber" was never the missing piece. They've done that. It didn't work.

It's an easy thing to prescribe. It just doesn't fix why the colon stopped moving in the first place.

The real problem isn't hydration or bulk. Your gut has lost the bacterial signal it needs to move waste on its own.

If your car runs out of gas, you don't need a louder engine. You need fuel.
If the bacteria that signal your colon have thinned out, you don't need more water or fiber. You need to bring those bacteria back.

This is why so many people stay stuck with:

  • The MiraLAX ceiling — it works, then it works a little less, so the dose goes up. It's getting the stool out. It was never built to fix why the colon stopped moving on its own.
  • The fiber backfire — you add the gritty, bran-type bulk fiber and bloat up worse. With too few bacteria left to break it down, it just sits and ferments into gas. There's a reason for that, and it isn't your fault.
  • The probiotic letdown — "50 billion CFUs." Nothing changes. Most of it never reaches where it's needed, and even what survives usually isn't the kind that does this job.
  • The dose that keeps climbing — after months or years, the same amount does less, so the number creeps up. Nothing you've taken brings the bacteria back, so the cause keeps slipping.
  • The dismissal — third visit, fourth visit. Colonoscopy comes back clean. "It's chronic — you'll just have to manage it." But if everything's normal, why can't you go on your own?

How Your Gut Bacteria Thin Out — And The Constipation Begins

Here's what most doctors never learned: chronic constipation usually isn't about water, fiber, or "just getting older."

The real problem is simpler. And the fix is gentler.

Your colon doesn't move waste on its own. It's not automatic. It's not like your heart beating.

It runs on a signal called butyrate — made by specific beneficial bacteria in your gut — that triggers peristalsis, the wave-like muscle contractions that push waste through your system.

Without that signal, your gut muscles don't get the message to contract.

Think of it like this: your gut muscles are workers, ready and able. Butyrate is the foreman who tells them when to start. No foreman shows up, and the workers just stand there, doing nothing.

The bacteria that make butyrate are that foreman.

And over the years, they thin out.

Here's why:

  • Modern food starves them. Processed, pasteurized, low-fiber food gives these bacteria almost nothing to live on. They fade.
  • Round after round of antibiotics. Every course — for a sinus infection, dental work, a UTI — kills off the good bacteria along with the bad. They don't always come back.
  • Years of low-grade inflammation. Stress, processed food, and irritation in the gut tip the balance against the good bacteria and keep it tipped.
  • The slowdown itself. Once things slow, waste sits longer, ferments, and inflames the gut — which kills off even more of the bacteria you need.

This creates a cycle that feeds itself:

Bacteria thin out → less butyrate → gut muscles don't get the signal → waste sits for days → it ferments and inflames the gut → the inflammation kills off more bacteria → an even weaker signal.

Your gut isn't broken. It's been starved of the bacteria that run it.
