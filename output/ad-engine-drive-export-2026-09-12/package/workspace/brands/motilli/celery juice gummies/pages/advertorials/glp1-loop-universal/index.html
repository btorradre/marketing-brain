Why GLP-1 Constipation Gets Worse Every Week — and Why Nothing in Your Cabinet Can Break the Loop

If you are on a GLP-1 medication, there is a pattern you may already know by heart.

The days between bathroom trips stretch out. Three, then five, then more — maybe you've started counting, on a calendar, in an app, or just in your head. The bloating builds through the afternoon until you look and feel six months pregnant by dinner. Some nights you skip dinner entirely, because eating makes it worse.

And you have the drawer. Miralax. Fiber gummies. Magnesium. Stool softeners. Prune juice. Each one works once, or not at all — and the fiber somehow made things worse. Your doctor says it's a known side effect. Add fiber. Drink water. Give it time to settle.

It doesn't settle. And somewhere in the back of your mind, a question has started forming that you may not have said out loud yet: do I have to quit the medication to get my body back?

Before you answer that question, you deserve to see what is actually happening — because almost nobody explains it, and once you see it, everything else makes sense. Why the remedies fail. Why it worsens on a schedule. And why quitting was never the real choice.

The system nobody thinks about until it stops

Your colon is a muscle. To move waste, it has to contract — squeeze and push, over and over. And like any muscle, it doesn't act on its own. It waits for a signal.

That signal comes from a family of molecules called short-chain fatty acids — the most important one is butyrate. You do not make butyrate yourself. The bacteria living in your colon make it for you, by fermenting the fiber that passes through.
