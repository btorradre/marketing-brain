## 2. Brand Positioning

### Core Positioning Statement
> **"GLP-1 constipation isn't normal constipation. It needs a different protocol."**

### The Mechanism Stack (read this before writing any copy)

Motilli's mechanism is now told in **two stacked layers**, in this order. The top layer is the frame that stops the scroll and disarms a skeptical, opinionated audience. The bottom layer is the load-bearing biology that proves the frame is true AND closes the single most-asked objection. Never deliver the biology without the protocol frame on top of it, and never deliver the frame without at least one layer of biology underneath — the frame alone invites the weight-loss objection it can't answer.

**Layer 1 — Protocol UMP (the frame / the hook):**
GLP-1 constipation is not regular constipation. The standard tools — Miralax, magnesium, fiber powders, probiotics — were built for a normal gut that slowed down on its own. A GLP-1 gut runs on different rules and needs a different protocol. The reader isn't failing at constipation; they're running the standard-gut playbook on a body that no longer responds to it. This reframe lets a confident person be wrong without being stupid — it vindicates instead of corrects, and it makes the entire category of normal-gut tools the *wrong tool* rather than a cheaper competitor.

**Layer 2 — Biological UMP (the proof + the objection kill):**
The protocols differ for a concrete reason: the slowdown is in the **stomach**, not the colon — a different organ. Every standard tool acts on the colon, so it never reaches where the problem started.

The stomach does two separate jobs. One controls how fast food leaves — the part the medication slows down **on purpose**. That slowdown is the fullness. That's the weight loss. The user wants to keep it. The other job is the natural muscle movement that should still be carrying food through; on a GLP-1 that movement stalls too, so food sits and the days between bathroom trips stretch out. That is the backup.

The protocol restores the muscle movement **without touching** the part that controls fullness. That is the whole mechanism, and it is also the answer to the objection below.

### The #1 Objection — Close It Every Time (NON-NEGOTIABLE)
The loudest unprompted question this audience asks: *"If the shot works by slowing my stomach, and this gets it moving again, won't it cancel my weight loss?"*

Every mechanism-led asset MUST answer this early and explicitly, in the reader's own words. The answer lives in Layer 2: the medication slows the part that controls how fast food leaves (fullness — kept); the protocol restores the muscle movement that carries food through (backup — cleared). Two different jobs. You keep the results, you clear the backup, you don't choose. A mechanism-literate reader who hits "restores motility" without this resolution reads it as "fights the drug" and bounces — so the objection kill is a required beat, not an optional one.

### The 4 Pillars of the Brand Story
1. **"Different Protocol"** — GLP-1 constipation isn't normal constipation. Normal-gut tools were built for a body the reader no longer has.
2. **"Wrong Organ"** — Those tools all work on the colon. On a GLP-1 the slowdown is one organ earlier, in the stomach. They never reach it.
3. **"Works WITH Your Shot"** — Restores the stomach movement that carries food through, without overriding the appetite control the medication gives you. Keep the results, clear the backup.
4. **"Gentle, Not Violent"** — Supports the stomach's own motion rather than forcing the colon. No cramping, no urgency, no emergency bathroom trips. Predictable mornings.

### Mechanism Language Rules (apply everywhere)
- **Literal organ language only.** Stomach and colon are two different organs; the slowdown is in the stomach. The highway / on-ramp / off-ramp metaphor is **retired**.
- **No spatial framing.** Do not use "upstream/downstream," "six feet past," or similar. Say "the stomach" and "the colon" by name.
- **No anthropomorphic mechanism verbs.** Do not write "wake up the stomach" or "wakes up your stomach." Use "restores the stomach's natural muscle movement / motility" or "supports the stomach's own motion."
- **Two-zone stomach is the canonical anatomy.** The part that controls how fast food leaves (slowed by the drug = fullness, kept) vs. the muscle movement that carries food through (stalls = backup, restored). Keep these two jobs distinct in every mechanism explanation — it's the only logic that closes the weight-loss objection.

## 7. Voice & Tone

### 7.1 Voice Attributes
1. **Mechanism-first, not flowery.** Every claim ties to a named compound (apigenin, inulin) or an anatomy-specific promise (stomach, not colon).
2. **Direct, plainspoken, zero fluff.** No "wellness speak" ("journey", "balance", "holistic"). Instead: "cement stomach", "backed up for days", "emergency bathroom trips."
3. **Empathetic but unapologetic.** The brand validates suffering ("those brutal GI side effects") then immediately redirects to mechanism ("it's a stomach problem, not a colon one").
4. **Comparative.** The brand is always defining itself against laxatives, Miralax, fiber powders, probiotics — never in isolation.
5. **Outcome-specific, timeline-bound.** "Within 1–5 days…", "by week 4…", "90 days to feel the difference."

### 7.2 Tone Dials

| Dial | Motilli Setting |
|---|---|
| Clinical ←→ Casual | **60% clinical / 40% casual** — mechanism-heavy but written plain |
| Emotional ←→ Rational | **55% rational / 45% emotional** — facts first, feel second |
| Serious ←→ Playful | **80% serious** — this is a dignity problem, not a joke |
| Formal ←→ Familiar | **75% familiar** — "you", direct address, user POV |

### 7.3 Do / Don't Language

**DO use:**
- "GLP-1 constipation isn't normal constipation"
