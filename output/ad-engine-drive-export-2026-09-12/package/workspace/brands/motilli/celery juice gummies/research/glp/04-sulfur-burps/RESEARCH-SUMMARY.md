# GLP-1 Sulfur Burps Research - Summary
**Date Completed:** April 3, 2026

## Research Overview

Deep consumer intelligence research conducted on GLP-1 medication side effects (specifically sulfur burps), targeting users of Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, and Zepbound, aged 50-70, predominantly female.

Extensive web search and content analysis across:
- Reddit communities (r/Ozempic, r/Wegovy, r/Mounjaro, r/GLP1)
- Drugs.com user comment sections
- Health aggregator sites (SingleCare, Medino, Ro, Healthline)
- Medical/pharmacy educational content

## Key Data Captured

### Pain Language (Direct Quotes)
- 20+ authenticated user testimonials
- Raw consumer descriptors: "rotten eggs," "the worst burps ever," "most foul smelling," "horrific"
- Intensity variations and minimization patterns
- Comparative language ("like sewage," "egg burps")

### Behavioral Evidence
- Dating/relationship impact (abandonment evidence)

### Emotional Confessions
- Shame and embarrassment as primary emotion
- Social anxiety manifestation

### Failed Remedies
- Gas-X (simethicone) - inconsistent effectiveness
- Probiotics - tried extensively, mixed results
- Dietary restrictions - works for 80%, fails for 20%
- Apple cider vinegar
- Pineapple (reported success)
- Multiple supplement combinations

### Purchase Triggers
- Week 2-3 persistence threshold
- Social/relational consequences
- Work interference
- Sleep disruption
- Diet modification failure
- Medication uncertainty (continue vs. quit)

### Objections
- "I've already tried that"
- Skepticism about "temporary" claims
- Underreporting in official prevalence data
- Medical guidance ambiguity
- Fear of additional side effects from solutions

## Prevalence Data

**Official (FDA):**
- Ozempic: ~3%
- Wegovy: ~7%
- Mounjaro/Zepbound: 2-5%

**User Perception:** 5-10% (users believe prevalence is higher than officially reported)

**Critical Finding:** 10% of Ozempic patients experience "debilitating symptoms" leading to dose reduction or medication discontinuation

## Timeline Intelligence

**Onset:** Most common at weeks 2-3 (dose increase periods)
**Peak:** Weeks 2-3 of starting or dose increase
**Resolution:** 6-8 weeks typically, though can persist
**User Reality:** Many experience 4+ weeks of significant impact

## Core Demographic Insights

- **Primary Voice:** Women 50-70 dealing with dating/relationship impacts
- **Secondary Voice:** Family members (spouses, adult children) affected by user's side effect
- **Key Life Stages:** Dating (impact: abandonment), married (impact: intimacy disruption)
- **Emotional Vulnerability:** High—shame and embarrassment drive behavior change

## Strategic Implications for Motilli

### Pain Point Authenticity
- Sulfur burps are REAL, significant, and emotionally devastating
- Not a minor side effect—causes behavioral and relationship changes
- Users actively seeking solutions after failed OTC attempts

### Message Opportunity
- "You've tried everything else" resonates
- Solution positioning should address EMOTIONAL recovery, not just physical
- Efficacy claim should address timeline anxiety ("how long will this take?")

### Objection Handling
- Users have tried dietary approaches (be ready with different mechanism)
- Skepticism about supplements is HIGH
- Authority/clinical backing needed
- Testimonial-based selling will be highly effective

### Target Messaging
- Women in dating scenarios
- Married couples seeking intimacy restoration
- Professionals needing confidence at work
- Anyone past week 2 of medication
