# THE BRIEF

## OVERVIEW

This is a reply to the single most common comment we get: some version of "does it actually taste like celery?" The woman watching already believes the product might work and is stuck on the one thing nobody has answered for her. You are going to answer it flat in the first two seconds, give her the one physical reason the gummy format is the right one, and then eat one on camera so she can watch your face.

She is 50 to 65, she is on a GLP-1, and she is skeptical in a very specific way: she has been burned by things that were pleasant and did nothing. Play tired-of-being-sold, not excited.

## THE HOOK (non-negotiable, word for word)

> **"It does not taste like celery."**

Delivered flat. Almost bored. Like you are settling an argument you have had four times this week.

**First two seconds, visually:**
- You are already holding the jar at chest height, label facing the lens, before the first word.

## THE DEMO (this is the ad, do not skip it)

Line 7 is the only claim in the script that cannot be spoken. It has to be watched.

**Choreography, all inside the same unbroken take:**

1. On "I bought one jar just to prove I was right," you unscrew the white cap one-handed and set it on the counter. Do not put the jar down. Ever.
2. On "hand under my chin, ready to spit it out," you cup your free hand under your chin. Real re-enactment.
3. On "Then I ate a second one," you take a real gummy out and eat it. On camera. In frame.
4. Chew. Let the beat sit for about a second. Whatever your face does, we keep.
5. Then deliver line 8.

**Do not fake it.** No cut to a pre-chewed reaction, no cutaway, no "mmm." If the honest reaction is a small surprised nod, that is worth more than anything you could perform.

## THE COMMENT STICKER: sourcing rule

The reply sticker is the hook's other half and **it has to be a real comment from a real account.** Pull it, screenshot it, do not write it.

Where to pull from: the comment sections on our own posted videos and on the running paid creatives.

Shapes that qualify:

- "i could never / celery makes me gag"
- "ok but how does it actually taste"
- "im scared its gonna taste like grass"

If nothing usable exists yet in the comments, tell Brooks before shoot day rather than authoring one. We do not invent customer quotes, and a fabricated reply sticker is the kind of thing that gets an account reported.

## PRODUCTION NOTES

**Setting.** A real bathroom, door and shower curtain visible behind you. Not a kitchen, not a ring-lit desk. This is where the jar actually lives and where she takes hers at night, and the reference works partly because it looks like nobody produced it.

**Camera.** Phone front camera, handheld at arm's length, mirror image on. Chest-up framing, you centered. One static position, one continuous take, no cuts, no zooms, no push-ins.

**Light.** Overhead bathroom light with the door open behind you. Bright, flat, even. No colored light, no ring light halo in the eyes.

**Wardrobe.** A plain solid top in a mid or deep color, the thing you would actually have on at nine at night. No logos, no busy graphics, no white (it blows out under the overhead).

**Product handling.** The real jar, real label, in frame from the first frame to the last. Label toward the lens, fingers off the wordmark. Cap comes off during the demo and stays off. Dark green heart-shaped gummies, clear jar, green wrap-around label, white cap.

**Energy arc.** Flat and settling-it → slightly self-deprecating in the middle → genuinely leaning in on the mechanism line → the honest chew → flat again on the close. It ends where it started on purpose.

**Casting.** One woman, 48 to 65, on a GLP-1, no professional presenting polish. Same person the whole ad, obviously, since there is only one shot.

**Music.** Upbeat, generic, low under the voice. It should sound like a bed, not a choice.

## WHAT TO AVOID

- Never say "our," "we," or "us" about Motilli. You are a customer talking about a brand you bought. "They," "their," "this brand."
- No other product on camera and no other brand named. Nothing in frame on the counter.
- No numbers you cannot see on the jar. No milligrams, no percentages, no review counts, no star rating, no "clinically," no doctor.
- Do not say the word vitamins.
- The guarantee is ninety days from delivery and an opened jar still counts. That exact frame. Nothing softer, nothing bigger.
- No claims that it cures, treats, fixes, or reverses anything, and nothing about what it does to the medication.
- Do not put the jar down and do not cut away from yourself. If you fumble, restart the take.
- No em dashes in any card, no "it's not X, it's Y" phrasing anywhere.
