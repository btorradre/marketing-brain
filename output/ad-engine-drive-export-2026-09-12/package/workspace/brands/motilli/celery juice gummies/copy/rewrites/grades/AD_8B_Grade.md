## TOP 3 STRENGTHS

1. **The forum-reading crisis scene is structurally brilliant.** It does four things simultaneously: creates the emotional crisis, provides negative social proof, establishes that the fear is rational, and pre-loads urgency that makes the doctor's preparation protocol feel like a lifeline. The sensory detail — "my hands flat on the kitchen counter to stop them from shaking" — grounds it in the body. The husband entering and asking what's wrong, and her saying "nothing," is the kind of detail that makes a reader stop scrolling because they recognize the moment of being unable to explain a fear that lives in your body, not your words.

2. **The dual-function bathroom line is the best product proof in the piece.** "I went to the bathroom that morning the way I go to the bathroom every morning." This is the single most important efficacy claim in 3,200 words, and it's delivered as the most unremarkable sentence in the copy. It carries zero promotional energy, zero celebration, zero editorial emphasis — and that restraint IS the credibility. A SIBO patient reading this would understand the weight immediately. The anti-climax IS the proof.

3. **The guide character (GI doctor) is deployed with surgical precision.** She enters with credibility (three relapsed patients), delivers one reframe ("We support your gut first. Not after."), teaches the mechanism in plain language, and recommends the product — then exits. She doesn't become a spokesperson. She doesn't reappear in the close. Her contribution is bounded and specific, which makes it believable. ## TOP 3 WEAKNESSES (mandatory)

1. **The product confirmation paragraph crosses the dual-track line.** "Pectin-based, which mattered to me because pectin is a prebiotic fiber that feeds beneficial bacteria without feeding the SIBO bacteria the way psyllium or inulin can." This sentence reads as ingredient education, not narrator confirmation. The narrator's established voice processes things through physical experience and emotional response, not biochemical categorization. A SIBO patient would think "pectin, not psyllium — good, that won't feed my bacteria." She would NOT internally narrate the prebiotic mechanism. **Fix:** Compress the confirmation beat to 3-4 sentences maximum. Replace biochemical explanation with experiential verification: "Pectin-based. Not psyllium. That mattered — psyllium had set me off before." Let the narrator confirm through her body's history, not through science.

2. **The close over-explains after the restaurant image.** The restaurant scene — "I just ate dinner. Like a person whose body isn't at war with itself" — is the perfect ending. The subsequent paragraph ("That's what four months of keeping both — the medication and the remission — feels like. And I wish every woman on those forums could feel it too.") restates what the image already communicated. The reader already felt the coexistence. The reader already thought about the forum women. Telling them to feel what they already felt slightly deflates the accumulated emotional weight. **Fix:** End on "Like a person whose body isn't at war with itself." Delete or radically compress the final paragraph. If the forum callback is essential, reduce it to a single trailing line: "I think about the women who never got to prepare." Then stop.

3. **The forum-reading section sustains a single emotional register for too long without oscillation.** Five to six consecutive sentences of unbroken dread ("Woman after woman after woman...motility stopped...bacterial overgrowth roared back...") without a single flicker of self-doubt, distraction, or emotional counter-beat. Real fear oscillates — a person reading terrifying forum posts would also think "maybe these are worst-case scenarios" or "maybe my case is different" before the next wave of dread pulls them back. The sustained dread reads as composed rather than experienced. **Fix:** Insert one sentence of attempted self-reassurance that fails: after "Another said the sulfur burps were so bad she couldn't go to work," add something like "I told myself these were outliers. Then I kept scrolling." The failed reassurance makes the subsequent dread more real because it shows the narrator TRIED to escape the fear and couldn't.
