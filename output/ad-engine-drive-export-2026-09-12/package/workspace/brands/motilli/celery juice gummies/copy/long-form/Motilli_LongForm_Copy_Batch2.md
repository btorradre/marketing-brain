## AD 5A: "Why Fiber Made It Worse" — Fiber Gummies / Skeptic Convert

**Headline:** My doctor said fiber would help my Wegovy constipation and she was wrong

My doctor told me to take fiber gummies for constipation on Wegovy. So I did. Metamucil gummies. Every single day.

I got more bloated. More uncomfortable. More backed up than before I started.

Turns out fiber was the worst thing I could have taken.

And I'm not saying that to be dramatic. I'm saying it because I spent six hours on a Tuesday night reading Reddit threads and PubMed abstracts and GLP-1 forums at two in the morning trying to figure out why I felt like I had a bowling ball cemented inside my abdomen. Six hours. On a work night. Because I was that desperate and that confused and that angry that the one thing my doctor told me to do was making everything worse.

Let me back up.

I started Wegovy in September. The weight started coming off almost immediately. Twelve pounds the first month. I cried in the fitting room at TJ Maxx because I could button a pair of jeans I hadn't worn in three years. I felt like I was getting my life back. I felt like me again.

Then around week six the constipation started.

Not regular constipation. Not the kind where you drink some water and eat a salad and things work themselves out. This was different. This was five days with nothing. Then six days. Then I stopped counting because counting made me want to scream.

I told my doctor. She said it's common. She said to add fiber. She specifically said Metamucil gummies because they're easy to take and they taste fine and that's what she recommends to all her GLP-1 patients. I trusted her. I bought a bottle that afternoon.

The first three days I felt okay. Maybe a little more bloated but I figured that was the fiber doing its thing, you know, bulking up, getting things moving. I kept taking them. Day five I looked four months pregnant. I'm 57 years old and I haven't been pregnant in decades but there I was in my bathroom mirror looking at a belly that was rock hard and distended and painful to the touch.

I called the nurse line. She said give it time. I gave it two more weeks. Two more weeks of looking pregnant. Two more weeks of my husband asking if I was okay because I kept making faces at dinner from the pressure. Two more weeks of wearing my stretchy pants to work because nothing else fit around my midsection even though the scale said I was still losing weight.

Then one night I couldn't sleep from the bloating and I did what every woman my age does when medicine fails her. I got on my phone and started searching.

That's when I found the Reddit thread that changed everything I thought I knew about what was happening inside my body.

A woman on the GLP-1 subreddit, a nurse actually, explained it in a way my doctor never did. She said the medication slows your stomach. That's the whole mechanism. That's how it works. It slows gastric emptying so food sits in your stomach longer and you feel full. That's the part that kills the appetite. That's the part that makes the shot work so well for weight loss.

But here's what nobody tells you. When your stomach slows down that much, the muscles that push food through start to go quiet. Like a conveyor belt that's been turned to the lowest setting for so long that it starts to seize. Food sits there. It ferments. Gas builds. And everything downstream backs up because nothing upstream is moving.

Now here's where the fiber part destroyed me.

Fiber adds bulk. That's literally what it does. That's the entire point. It makes stool bigger and heavier so it's easier to push through the colon. And for normal constipation that works fine. Your colon does the pushing. The bulk gives it something to grip.

But my constipation wasn't in my colon. My constipation started in my stomach. The engine at the top of the whole system was barely running. And I was adding freight to the train. More bulk. More weight. More material sitting in a system that couldn't move what was already there.

I was literally making the traffic jam worse.

When I read that I put my phone down and stared at the ceiling for five minutes. Because it was so obvious. It was so stupidly, painfully obvious. And my doctor didn't know. Or maybe she did know how GLP-1s work but she didn't connect it to why fiber would be the wrong call. Either way I felt betrayed. Not by her as a person but by the advice. By the protocol. By the automatic response of adding fiber to constipation without thinking about where the constipation actually was.

I stopped the Metamucil the next day. The bloating got maybe ten percent better within a week. But the constipation was still there because the underlying problem was still there. My stomach was still slow. The engine was still stalled.

So I kept reading. I went deep. I'm talking PubMed deep. I'm talking searching terms like prokinetic compounds and gastric motility and natural gastroparesis interventions. I am not a scientist. I'm an office manager. But desperation makes you a researcher real fast.

That's how I learned about prokinetics. A prokinetic is something that wakes up the muscles in your stomach. Not your colon. Your stomach. The upstream part. The engine room. There are prescription prokinetics but they have side effects and my doctor wasn't going to prescribe one for something she considered a minor complaint. So I started looking at natural ones.

Apigenin kept coming up. It's a compound found in celery. And there was actual research showing it promotes gastric motility. Meaning it nudges those stomach muscles to start contracting again. Not violently like a laxative. Gently. Like reminding the conveyor belt that it's supposed to be moving.

I also kept seeing chlorophyll mentioned for sulfur burps. I had those too. The rotten egg belches that come out of nowhere in the middle of a meeting and make you want to crawl under your desk. Chlorophyll apparently neutralizes the sulfur compounds. That's a separate mechanism from the motility piece but it was hitting another problem I had.

Now here's where I have to be honest about my own skepticism because I think that's important. I do not buy things off the internet based on Reddit threads. I am the woman who reads every single review. I check Fakespot. I look up the company on the Better Business Bureau. I am not an impulse buyer and I am not easily convinced. My husband says I would investigate a glass of water before I drank it.

So when I came across this gummy that had celery juice extract with apigenin, chlorophyll, and a small amount of prebiotic fiber, I did not buy it immediately. I sat on it for four days. I read the ingredient panel. I checked for third party testing. I looked for the GMP certification. I read the one star reviews first because that's where you find the truth.

If it didn't work I'd stop taking it and be out nothing but time.

Week one I noticed the sulfur burps dropped by maybe half. I didn't connect it to the gummies at first because I'm suspicious of everything and also because sulfur burps are unpredictable so maybe it was coincidence. But they kept staying lower. By day ten they were almost gone.

Week two I had my first normal bathroom visit in three months. I'm not going to describe it in detail because you know what I mean. But it was the kind of normal that made me realize I had completely forgotten what normal felt like.

By week four I was going almost every day. Not forced. Not strained. Not after a laxative or a mag citrate or forty minutes of hoping. Just happening. The way it used to happen before the shot.

And here's the thing I didn't expect. The bloating went down. Significantly. Because food was actually moving through my stomach instead of sitting there fermenting and expanding and pressing on everything. The fiber I was taking before had been adding to the pile. These gummies were clearing the pile by getting the muscles to actually do their job.

I'm four months in now. I'm still on Wegovy. I'm still losing weight. I did not have to choose between the shot and being able to use my own bathroom like a normal person. That choice that I thought was inevitable, the shot or my digestion, turned out to be a false choice. I just needed something that worked on the right part of the system.

I tell this story now because I know there's a woman reading this who just bought Metamucil because her doctor told her to. And I need her to hear me. Your doctor is not wrong that you need help. Your doctor is wrong about what kind of help you need. Fiber is not your answer when the engine is stalled. You don't load more cargo onto a train that can't move. You fix the engine first.

They worked. And I haven't bought Metamucil since.

## AD 6A: "The Hostage" — Trade-Off / Avatar (Bathroom Floor)

**Headline:** I almost cancelled my Wegovy prescription from my bathroom floor at midnight

Last Thursday I sat on my bathroom floor for 35 minutes at 11 PM, in enough pain that I opened the app to cancel my next Wegovy prescription.

I stared at the cancel button for two minutes. Then I closed the app.

Because the hunger was worse.

I need to say that again so it lands. I was sitting on cold tile, tears running down my face, my stomach so distended I could feel my heartbeat in my abdomen, and I chose to stay on the medication. Not because the pain wasn't real. Not because I'm tough. Because I remember what it was like before the shot. I remember standing in front of the refrigerator at 9 PM unable to think about anything except what I was going to eat next. I remember the food noise so loud it drowned out everything else in my life. I remember planning my whole day around meals and snacks and then hating myself after every single one.

The shot took all of that away. And I would do almost anything to not go back.

So I sat there on that floor and I accepted it. This is the deal. This is the trade. You get to be free from the food but your body is going to punish you for it. Seven days without a bowel movement. Sulfur burps so bad my daughter asked me from the other room if something was rotting. Nausea that hits at random, sometimes in the morning, sometimes right after I eat, sometimes at 3 AM for no reason at all. Bloating that makes me look like I'm carrying a pregnancy I'm two decades too old for.

I accepted all of that as the price.

And I think that's the part nobody talks about. Not the side effects. Everyone talks about those. Every forum, every Facebook group, every TikTok comment section is full of women listing their symptoms like war wounds. What nobody talks about is the acceptance. The quiet deal you make with yourself at 11 PM on your bathroom floor that this is just your life now. That you've traded one prison for another and this one is slightly more bearable because at least you're losing weight.

I made that deal in October.

By November I had a system. Miralax every night. Mag07 before bed. Dulcolax when things got really bad. Ginger chews for nausea. Gas-X for bloating. I had a little basket on my bathroom counter with everything in it. My husband called it the pharmacy. I didn't laugh.

None of it worked the way I needed it to. The Miralax would eventually produce results but the cramping was awful. Mag07 was unpredictable. Some nights nothing. Some nights explosive. Dulcolax felt like my intestines were wringing themselves out. And the bloating never went away because none of these things were addressing why the bloating was happening in the first place.

Here's what I didn't understand until December. I didn't understand it because my doctor never explained it and because everything I'd ever learned about constipation was wrong in this specific context.

Normal constipation happens in the colon. Your colon gets sluggish or dry or whatever and stool sits there too long and gets hard and difficult to pass. That's what Miralax is for. That's what fiber is for. That's what all of those things in my little bathroom basket were designed to fix. Colon problems. Downstream problems.

My problem wasn't downstream.

GLP-1 medications slow your stomach. That's how they work. They slow gastric emptying. Food sits in your stomach two, three, four times longer than it normally would. That's what kills your appetite. That's the whole mechanism behind the weight loss.

But when your stomach slows down that drastically, the muscular contractions that push food forward start to weaken. Food sits there and ferments. That's the bloating. Sulfur-producing bacteria go to work on that stagnant food. That's the sulfur burps. Your small intestine gets backed up because nothing is coming through from above at a normal rate. And eventually your colon backs up too because the whole system is a chain and when the top link stops moving everything below it stops too.

I was treating the last link in the chain. Every single product in my bathroom basket was designed for the last link. And the first link was the one that was broken.

When I finally understood this I felt two things at the same time. Relief that it made sense. And fury that I had spent three months and probably two hundred dollars on products that were never going to work because they were aimed at the wrong part of my body.

I learned this from a woman in a private Facebook group for GLP-1 patients over 50. She's a retired pharmacist. She posted a long explanation about gastric motility and prokinetic compounds and why everything we were all taking was downstream when the problem was upstream. She used an analogy I haven't been able to forget. She said imagine a highway where a bridge collapsed at mile marker one. Every mile after that is gridlocked. And everyone is down at mile marker thirty trying to clear traffic. It doesn't matter how many tow trucks you send to mile marker thirty. Until you fix the bridge at mile marker one nothing moves.

That's what GLP-1 constipation is. A mile marker one problem.

She mentioned that certain natural compounds act as prokinetics, meaning they stimulate the stomach muscles to contract. She specifically talked about apigenin, which is found in celery, and how it promotes gastric motility. Not colonic motility. Gastric. The upstream part. The bridge.

She also talked about chlorophyll for the sulfur burps. Different mechanism. Chlorophyll binds to sulfur compounds and neutralizes them. Simple. Direct. Not masking the smell. Eliminating the chemistry that creates it.

And then she mentioned a small amount of prebiotic fiber. Not a big fiber dose like Metamucil that would just add bulk to an already stalled system. A micro-dose. Just enough to feed the good bacteria without making the backup worse.

She said she'd been taking a gummy that had all three of these things and that it was the first thing in five months that had actually made a difference.

I did not believe her. I want to be clear about that. I was not a hopeful woman at this point. I was an angry woman who had been burned by promises before. I had Miralax promising gentle relief. Mag07 promising overnight cleansing. Dulcolax promising predictable results. None of them delivered on those promises because none of them were built for what was actually wrong with me.

But I looked up the gummy because I'm stubborn and I was still desperate and the pharmacist's explanation made too much sense to ignore. The upstream versus downstream thing. That was the part that got me. Because it explained every failure. Every product that should have worked and didn't. Every morning sitting on the toilet willing something to happen. It all made sense once you understood the problem was at the top not the bottom.

The company was called Motilli. The product was a celery juice fiber gummy. It had apigenin from celery juice extract, chlorophyll, prebiotic fiber in a small dose, and some vitamins. Clean label certified. Third party tested. Made in the US.

I ordered it because the pharmacist said to give it at least six weeks before judging.

Week one. Sulfur burps cut in half. I noticed this before I noticed anything else. The belches that had been making me avoid conversations in close quarters started spacing out. Then reducing in intensity. By day nine I went an entire day without one.

Week two. Something shifted in my abdomen. I don't know how to describe it except to say it felt like movement. Like something was happening in there that hadn't been happening. And then I went to the bathroom and it was the most uneventful, normal, unremarkable experience I'd had in months. I almost cried because of how normal it was.

Week three through six was a slow build. Not dramatic. Not overnight transformation. Gradual. Like a system coming back online one circuit at a time. More frequent bowel movements. Less bloating. Less nausea. The rock hard distended feeling that I'd been carrying around since October started to soften.

I'm writing this in March. I started the gummies in late December. The bathroom basket is still on my counter but it's mostly for show at this point. I took Miralax once in January and zero times in February. The Dulcolax is expired. I should throw it away.

I'm still on Wegovy. I'm still losing weight. I did not have to quit.

That's the part I need the woman on the bathroom floor to hear. You don't have to quit. You don't have to make that trade. The shot is not holding you hostage if you can fix the part of the system it's disrupting. And the part it's disrupting is upstream from everything you've been trying to fix.

The deal I made on my bathroom floor in October, the one where I accepted digestive misery as the price of freedom from food noise, I don't have that deal anymore. I cancelled it. Not by cancelling my shot. By fixing the bridge at mile marker one.

I sat on that bathroom floor five months ago choosing between my body and my sanity. I thought those were the only two options.

They weren't. And I don't sit on the floor anymore.

## AD 7B: "Your Gut Is Talking to Your Brain" — Post-Ozempic Anxiety / Functional Medicine Practitioner

**Headline:** If anxiety appeared out of nowhere after stopping your shot read this now

If you stopped your GLP-1 and suddenly have anxiety you've never had in your life, crippling, death-level, waking-up-in-terror anxiety, your doctor will tell you it's stress.

It's not stress. It's your gut.

The medication changed your microbiome for months. When you stopped, your gut lost its ability to signal your brain that you're safe.

I need to explain what I'm seeing in my practice because I believe we're in the early stages of a problem that nobody in mainstream medicine is talking about yet. And by the time they start talking about it, millions of women will have suffered through something terrifying and been told it was in their heads.

I'm a functional medicine practitioner in Scottsdale. I've been in practice for eleven years. For the last eighteen months, roughly seventy percent of my new patients have been women who were on GLP-1 medications and either stopped or are trying to stop. And they are presenting with a pattern so consistent it cannot be coincidence.

The pattern is this. Woman takes Ozempic or Wegovy or Mounjaro for six to eighteen months. She loses weight. She feels great. Then she stops. Maybe her insurance stopped covering it. Maybe she hit her goal weight. Maybe the side effects became too much. Whatever the reason, she discontinues the medication.

Within two to six weeks she develops anxiety unlike anything she's experienced in her life. Not regular worry. Not nervousness. Full body dread. A feeling that something terrible is about to happen that doesn't go away. Waking up at 3 AM with her heart pounding and a sense of impending doom so intense she's convinced she's dying. Intrusive thoughts. Inability to relax. A constant buzzing in her nervous system that makes her feel like she's plugged into an electrical outlet.

She goes to her doctor. Her doctor says it's probably stress. Maybe related to the weight regain that often follows stopping the medication. Maybe hormonal. Maybe she should try an SSRI.

She's not stressed. She's not hormonal. And an SSRI might take the edge off but it's not going to fix the actual problem because the actual problem isn't in her brain.

It's in her gut.

Let me explain this as carefully as I can because the mechanism matters. Your gut and your brain talk to each other constantly through a nerve called the vagus nerve. This is the longest cranial nerve in your body. It runs from your brainstem all the way down to your abdomen and it carries signals in both directions. Brain to gut. Gut to brain.

Roughly eighty percent of those signals travel upward. From gut to brain. Your gut is talking to your brain far more than your brain is talking to your gut.

What is it saying? It's providing status reports. It's reporting on the state of your internal environment. Is there enough good bacteria? Is the motility normal? Is the lining intact? Are the right neurotransmitters being produced? Because your gut produces about ninety percent of your body's serotonin. Your gut produces about fifty percent of your dopamine. Your gut is not just digesting food. It is manufacturing the chemicals that regulate your mood, your sleep, your sense of safety, your ability to feel calm.

When everything in the gut is functioning properly, the signals traveling up the vagus nerve essentially tell your brain that you're safe. All systems normal. Environment stable. You can relax.

Now here is what GLP-1 medications do to this system.

They slow gastric motility dramatically. Food sits in the stomach and upper GI tract far longer than normal. This changes the environment. Bacteria that thrive in a slow moving system begin to proliferate. Bacteria that require normal motility to survive begin to die off. The microbiome shifts. Not subtly. Significantly.

Studies are beginning to show that GLP-1 medications alter the composition of the gut microbiome within weeks. Certain beneficial strains decrease. Certain opportunistic strains increase. The overall diversity, which is the hallmark of a healthy microbiome, drops.

While you're on the medication, you might not notice this shift because the medication itself is managing many of the downstream effects. The appetite suppression, the blood sugar regulation, the weight loss, all of these create a context where the microbiome changes are masked by the benefits.

Then you stop the medication.

The appetite regulation stops. The blood sugar management reverts. The gastric motility starts to increase again but it's coming back in a gut environment that's been fundamentally altered. The microbiome that's now in charge is not the microbiome you started with. It's a microbiome shaped by months of slowed motility, altered pH, changed bacterial ratios, and reduced diversity.

And this altered microbiome starts sending signals up the vagus nerve. But they're not the signals your brain is used to receiving. They're not the all clear signals. They're distress signals. The gut is essentially telling the brain that something is wrong. The environment is unstable. The chemistry is off. Danger.

Your brain receives those signals and does exactly what it's designed to do with danger signals. It activates your fight or flight response. It floods your system with cortisol and adrenaline. It puts you on high alert. It tells you something terrible is about to happen because from the brain's perspective, your body just reported a threat.

That's the anxiety. That's the 3 AM panic. That's the sense of doom. It's not psychological. It's physiological. Your gut is sending alarm signals and your brain is responding appropriately to those signals. The problem isn't in the brain's response. The problem is in the signal itself. The signal is coming from a disrupted gut.

This is what I try to explain to every woman who sits across from me with dark circles under her eyes telling me she thinks she's losing her mind. You are not losing your mind. Your vagus nerve is carrying accurate distress reports from a gut environment that is genuinely in distress. Your brain is doing exactly what it should do with that information. The solution is not to override the brain's response with an SSRI. The solution is to fix the gut so it stops sending distress signals.

How do you fix it?

You have to address the motility. The motility disruption is what created the bacterial shift in the first place. Even after stopping the medication, the gut muscles don't just snap back to normal function. They've been running at minimal capacity for months. They need to be reactivated. Gently. Not with harsh laxatives that create cramping and urgency. With prokinetic compounds that stimulate the natural muscular contractions of the stomach and upper GI.

You have to address the bacterial environment. The sulfur-producing bacteria that proliferated during the slow motility period are still there. They're producing hydrogen sulfide, which is inflammatory, which further irritates the gut lining, which generates more distress signals to the brain. You need something that neutralizes the sulfur chemistry while the motility recovers.

And you have to support the beneficial bacteria. A small amount of prebiotic fiber, not a massive dose that overwhelms a recovering system, but a micro-dose that feeds the strains you want to come back without creating the bulk and backup that made things worse on the medication.

I started recommending this three-part approach to my patients about eight months ago. The prokinetic piece, the sulfur neutralization piece, and the prebiotic micro-dose piece.

The first patient I put on this protocol was a 54-year-old woman who had been off Mounjaro for two months and was having panic attacks so severe she'd been to the emergency room twice. Her ER visits showed nothing cardiac. Her blood work was normal. She'd been prescribed Xanax and was taking it daily and hating the way it made her feel.

I explained the gut-brain axis to her. I explained what the medication had done to her microbiome. I explained the vagal signaling mechanism. She looked at me like I was speaking a foreign language but she was desperate enough to try anything.

I had her start a celery juice gummy that contains apigenin, which is a natural prokinetic compound derived from celery. The same gummy contains chlorophyll for the sulfur chemistry and a measured amount of prebiotic fiber. The product is called Motilli and I had encountered it through a colleague who was seeing similar patterns in her post-GLP-1 patients.

I told her to give it four weeks minimum. The gut doesn't rebuild in a day.

At her two-week follow up she reported the panic attacks had decreased from daily to every few days. She was sleeping through the night about half the time, which was an improvement from zero nights a week. The sulfur burps she'd been having since the medication had nearly stopped.

At her six-week follow up she cried in my office. The anxiety was still present but it had downgraded from a nine to a three. She was sleeping most nights. She had stopped taking the Xanax. She said she felt like her nervous system was coming back online. That's her phrase. Coming back online. I thought it was the most accurate description I'd ever heard.

I have now put over forty patients on this protocol. Not all of them respond at the same rate. Some feel changes in the first week. Some take a full month before anything shifts. The gut microbiome rebuilds on its own timeline and that timeline varies. But the pattern of improvement is consistent enough that I'm now recommending it as a first-line intervention for every post-GLP-1 patient I see with unexplained anxiety and sleep disruption.

I want to be clear about what this is and what this isn't. This is not a cure for anxiety disorders. This is not a replacement for appropriate psychiatric care. If you have a pre-existing anxiety condition that predates your GLP-1 use, that's a different conversation.

But if you were a person who never had anxiety before your shot. If you stopped the medication and within weeks felt like your brain was being hijacked by fear. If you're lying awake at night with a racing heart and a sense of dread you cannot explain and your doctor is telling you it's stress or hormones or in your head. It might be your gut.

I don't typically talk about this publicly. But I'm watching too many women suffer through something that has a physiological explanation. And I'm tired of watching them get handed SSRIs for a problem that starts below the diaphragm, not above it.

Your gut is talking to your brain. Right now, after the medication changed everything, it's saying the wrong things.

You're not losing your mind. Your gut just forgot how to tell your brain that you're safe.
