## AD COPY

**Headline:** She added fiber on her doctor's orders and spent six hours learning why it backfired

My doctor said fiber would help my Wegovy constipation and she was wrong

I want to be careful about how I say this because I respect my doctor. She's a good doctor. She's been my doctor for nine years and she's the one who got me on Wegovy in the first place and the Wegovy has changed my life.

But when she told me to take Metamucil for the constipation, she was wrong. Not wrong in a small way. Wrong in a way that made everything significantly worse and cost me two months of misery I didn't need to have.

I bought the Metamucil that afternoon. The big bottle. The gummies, because she said gummies are easier to take and that's what she recommends to all her GLP-1 patients. I stood in the supplement aisle at CVS and felt good about it. My doctor told me what to do and I was doing it. That's how it's supposed to work.

My name is Karen. I'm fifty-seven. I've been an office manager for twenty-two years, which means I spend my life organizing other people's chaos, and I'm good at it. I started Wegovy in September and lost fourteen pounds the first month. I could zip a skirt I hadn't worn since my daughter's college graduation. I remember standing in my closet holding the zipper pull and just staring at it because it went all the way up without me lying on the bed or sucking in or doing any of the negotiations you do with a zipper when you know it's not going to cooperate.

Then the constipation started around week six.

Not regular constipation. I want to be specific about that because I think women hear constipation and they think oh, drink some water, eat a salad. This was not that. This was five days with nothing. Then six days. I stopped counting after the first month because counting was making me anxious and the anxiety was making the bloating worse and I was already miserable enough.

I told my doctor. She said it's common on GLP-1s. She said take Metamucil.

Three days on the Metamucil and I felt okay. A little extra pressure but I told myself that was the fiber working, doing its thing, bulking up, getting things moving. That's what fiber does. That's what everyone says fiber does.

Day five I stood in my bathroom and looked at my stomach in the mirror and my first thought was I look pregnant. I'm fifty-seven years old. I haven't been pregnant in twenty-six years. But my belly was distended and rock hard and when I pressed on it with my fingertips I actually flinched because the pressure inside was that intense.

I called the nurse line. She said give it time. Keep taking the fiber. Your body is adjusting.

So I kept taking it. Two more weeks. Two more weeks of my pants not fitting around my midsection even though the scale said I was losing weight. Two more weeks of wearing the black stretchy pants to work because nothing else would button. My coworker Janet asked if I was okay on a Tuesday because I kept shifting in my chair. I said I was fine. I was not fine. I was sitting at my desk calculating whether I could make it to the bathroom and back without anyone noticing how long I'd been gone, because when it finally did come it came all at once and I could be in there for twenty minutes and there is no way to explain a twenty-minute bathroom break to your coworker without her knowing exactly what's happening.

I tried Gas-X on top of the Metamucil because the bloating was also producing these sulfur burps that tasted like rotten eggs and came up without warning in the middle of conversations. I would be on the phone with a vendor and feel it building and have to mute myself and let it out and then unmute and hope they couldn't hear the pause. The Gas-X didn't help. Maybe it helped with regular gas. It did not touch the sulfur.

I tried Miralax on a Saturday because I figured if the fiber was adding bulk then maybe I needed something to pull water into the colon and get things moving from below. The Miralax gave me cramps. Bad ones. Like someone was wringing out my intestines. And still nothing moved.

I ordered a probiotic from Amazon. Forty billion CFU. Someone in a Wegovy Facebook group said it fixed everything in two weeks. I gave it three weeks. It did not fix everything. It gave me more gas.

The whole time I'm still taking the Metamucil because my doctor told me to and I trust my doctor and I keep thinking maybe I just haven't given it enough time. Maybe next week it'll kick in. Maybe my body needs to adjust. This is what you tell yourself when the thing your doctor recommended is making you worse. You blame your body, not the recommendation.

Then on a Tuesday night in November I couldn't sleep. The bloating was so bad I couldn't lie flat. I was propped up on three pillows with my hand on my stomach and I could feel it. Hard. Full. Like something had been poured into me and set like concrete. My husband was asleep next to me and I was crying. Not the big kind. The exhausted kind. The kind where you're angry and confused and you can't believe that something your doctor specifically told you to take is doing this to you and nobody warned you and nobody is helping and it's two in the morning and you have to be at work in five hours.

I picked up my phone. I was not looking for another supplement. I was done with supplements. I was looking for an explanation. I typed into Reddit: "why does fiber make Wegovy constipation worse."

That search returned a thread that I read for the next four hours.

A woman in the thread, a nurse who was also on a GLP-1, explained something that made me put my phone down and stare at the ceiling.

The medication slows the stomach. That's how it works. That's the actual mechanism of the drug. It slows down the muscles in the stomach wall so food sits there longer. That's why you feel full. That's why you eat less. That's why the weight comes off. The stomach muscles slow down and food just sits.

But when those muscles slow down, food sits too long. It starts to ferment. Bacteria break it down and produce sulfur gas. That gas comes up as burps. It pushes outward as bloating. And because the muscles that move food through the system are barely working, nothing moves downstream. That's the constipation. Not a colon problem. A stomach problem. The engine at the top of the whole system goes quiet and everything below it backs up.

Now here's the part that made me want to throw my phone across the room.

Fiber adds bulk. That is literally the entire job of fiber. It makes stool bigger and heavier so the colon has something to grip and push through. And for normal constipation, where the colon is the issue, that works. The colon does the pushing. The fiber gives it something to work with.

But my constipation was not in my colon. My constipation started in my stomach. The muscles at the top of the system were barely running. And every morning for two months I had been shoveling more bulk into a system that couldn't move what was already there.

I was loading freight onto a train with a dead engine.

More bulk. More weight. More material sitting in a stomach that couldn't push it through. The fiber wasn't helping the traffic jam. It was making the traffic jam worse. Significantly worse. Measurably worse. Every single day.

Miralax pulls water into the colon. Downstream. Doesn't touch the stomach.

Gas-X breaks up gas bubbles in the intestine. Downstream. Doesn't touch the sulfur that's forming upstream because food is fermenting in a slow stomach.

The probiotic adds bacteria to the gut. But the problem isn't bacteria. The problem is the muscles that move food through aren't contracting because the medication is specifically designed to slow them down.

Every single thing I tried works on the wrong end of the system. Every one. Including the fiber my doctor told me to take.

I stopped the Metamucil the next morning. Within a week the bloating improved maybe ten percent. Not enough. The underlying problem was still there. The stomach was still slow. The engine was still stalled.

But now I knew what to look for. And I am the kind of person who looks.

My husband says I would investigate a glass of water before I drank it. He's not wrong. I read ingredient panels. I read one-star reviews before I read five-star reviews because the one-star reviews are where people tell the truth. I run supplements through Fakespot. I check the Better Business Bureau. I do not buy things off the internet because someone in a Facebook group said it changed their life.

But I do buy things off the internet when I've spent three separate nights reading studies and I understand the mechanism and I've built a list of what the solution actually needs to contain and I can't find a single thing in a store that has all of it.

The first night I found apigenin. A compound concentrated in celery. Study after study showing it promotes gastric motility, which in plain language means it nudges the stomach muscles to start contracting again. Not violently like a laxative. Gently. Like tapping the engine and reminding it that it's supposed to be running. I bookmarked seven studies. But I wasn't going to start juicing celery every morning and hope I was getting a therapeutic dose of one compound. That's not how I operate.

Two nights later, completely different search. I was reading about the sulfur burps specifically because I'd had one in a meeting that afternoon that made me cup my hand over my mouth and pretend I was coughing. Someone in a thread mentioned chlorophyll. Not for the motility problem. For the sulfur specifically. Chlorophyll binds to sulfur compounds and neutralizes them. Different problem. Different mechanism. I didn't connect it to the apigenin research at first. It was a separate piece solving a separate symptom.

Then about four days after that I came across a post from a woman who said she'd tried high-dose fiber, tried apigenin on its own, tried chlorophyll on its own. Each one helped its specific piece. None of them solved the full picture. What finally helped her was something that combined a prokinetic compound with chlorophyll and a micro-dose of prebiotic fiber. Not a therapeutic Metamucil dose that would add freight to a stalled system. A tiny amount. Just enough to support beneficial bacteria without creating more backup.

That was the third piece. The fiber wasn't the enemy. The dose was the enemy. A micro-dose of the right kind of prebiotic fiber could support the gut without adding bulk. But a full Metamucil dose in a slow stomach was a disaster. It was the difference between a drop of oil in an engine and flooding the engine.

Three separate nights. Three separate discoveries. Apigenin for the stalled muscles. Chlorophyll for the sulfur. Micro-dose prebiotic fiber for gentle support. I had my list.

I spent the next two days searching for something that had all three. Most things had one. A celery supplement with apigenin but no chlorophyll. A chlorophyll capsule with no prokinetic. A fiber gummy with a full dose that would put me right back where I started. Nothing passed the checklist.

Then I was scrolling through the results on one of those searches, I think I was looking for celery juice extract at a specific concentration, and a small company's product page loaded between two other listings I'd already dismissed. Celery juice fiber gummies. I almost scrolled past it. The name on the bottle was Motilli. I'd never heard of it.

I did not buy it. I sat on it for four days.

I read the ingredient panel. Celery juice extract with apigenin. Chlorophyll. A small amount of prebiotic fiber. Vitamins A, C, K, B6, folate, which I later learned are the ones GLP-1 patients tend to be depleted in because when your stomach isn't processing food properly you're not absorbing nutrients properly either. Pectin-based, not gelatin, which matters because gelatin can trigger nausea in a stomach that's already compromised from the medication. The celery juice was an extract at a concentrated dose, not the generic celery powder I'd seen in other products where the apigenin content was negligible.

I checked Fakespot. I looked up the company. I read every one-star review they had and there weren't many but I read them all. I checked for third-party testing and GMP certification. I found both.

Then I ordered one bottle. One. If it didn't work I'd stop taking it and I'd be out twenty-four dollars and nothing else.

The first three days I felt nothing. I texted my sister, who also has Wegovy constipation and had been following my research saga, and I said "another dud." She said give it a week.

Day four I was at my desk and I realized I hadn't had a sulfur burp since lunch the day before. I didn't connect it immediately. I'm suspicious of coincidences. But I noted it.

By day ten the sulfur burps were mostly gone. Not masked. Not covered up the way Gas-X was supposed to cover them up. Just gone. The chlorophyll was doing exactly what the research said it would do.

Week two I had a bowel movement on a Tuesday morning that was so normal I almost didn't register it. Then I registered it. I sat there and thought about the fact that this was the first time in almost four months that my body did what it was supposed to do without a laxative or a supplement that made it worse or forty minutes of waiting and hoping. Just normal. I had forgotten what normal felt like.

Then week three hit a wall. Wednesday was terrible. Bloating came back. The sulfur burps spiked. I sat in my car in the parking lot at work during lunch and pressed my hand against my stomach and thought of course. Of course this was too good to be true. I almost stopped taking them that night.

Thursday was better. Friday was better than Thursday. By the following Monday the burps had settled and the bloating had eased back to where it had been at the end of week two. My body was adjusting. It wasn't a straight line. It was a bad day inside a good week inside a good trend, and the good trend held.

By the end of week four I was going almost every day. Not strained. Not forced. Not after Miralax cramps or a prayer. Just happening. The way it happened before the shot.

And the bloating went down. Not because something was pushing the bloating away. Because food was actually moving through my stomach instead of sitting there fermenting and expanding and pressing outward against everything. The fiber had been adding to the pile. These gummies were getting the muscles to move the pile.

Then on a Wednesday morning in week five I stood in my kitchen making coffee and I realized something was missing. I wasn't holding my stomach. I wasn't pressing on my abdomen to test how distended it was. I wasn't standing in front of the mirror calculating which pants would hide the swelling. I was just standing there. Making coffee. Like a person whose body isn't a project to manage every single morning.

I stood there for an extra minute because I didn't trust it.

It's been four months. I'm still on Wegovy. I'm still losing weight. I didn't have to choose. That's the thing I want to be clear about. I thought I was going to have to choose between the shot and being able to use my own bathroom like a normal person. That turned out to be a false choice. I just needed something that worked on the right part of the system.

My sister started three weeks after I told her what I'd found. She had the same results. Not identical. Her timeline was slower. The sulfur burps took longer to clear. But by week four she called me and said she'd worn regular jeans to church for the first time since starting Mounjaro. She said she cried in the bathroom afterward. Not from pain. From relief.

I'm telling this because I know there's a woman reading this who just bought a bottle of Metamucil because her doctor told her to. And I need her to hear me. Your doctor is not wrong that you need help. Your doctor is wrong about what kind of help you need. You don't load more freight onto a train with a dead engine. You fix the engine.

The Metamucil went in the trash two months ago. And that Wednesday morning in my kitchen, standing there with my coffee and nothing to manage and nothing to calculate and nothing to hide, that's my morning now. Every single morning.

That's what fixing the right part of the system gave me back.
