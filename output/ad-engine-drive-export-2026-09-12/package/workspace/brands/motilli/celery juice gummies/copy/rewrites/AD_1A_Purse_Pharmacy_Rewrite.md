## AD COPY

**Headline:** She carried six remedies in her purse for eleven months and not one of them was working

My purse became a pharmacy and none of it was working.

Gas-X. Tums. Charcoal capsules. Ginger root chews. Peppermint oil capsules. Three brands of mints I rotate depending on the day.

My purse weighs four pounds more than it should. Every pocket has something in it for the sulfur burps. And not one of those things has ever actually stopped a burp from happening. Not once in eleven months.

I know this because I keep a mental scorecard. Not on purpose. You just start tracking when you've been managing the same thing every single day for almost a year. You time it. You chew two Gas-X at 11:45 because lunch is at noon and maybe this time you'll get ahead of it.

It's never different.

I'm Debra. Fifty-eight. I've been on Wegovy since April of last year and I need to say this before anything else — I love the medication. Forty-one pounds gone. A1C down. My knees don't scream when I get out of bed. My doctor is genuinely proud of me for the first time in fifteen years and that feeling alone is worth the copay.

But the sulfur burps came. The bloating that makes me look five months pregnant by dinner. The constipation that turned into a cycle — nothing for four days, then a full morning I won't describe. You already know.

So I built the purse pharmacy.

It started with Tums. Then my friend Linda said charcoal capsules were the answer. My daughter sent me a link for peppermint oil capsules on Amazon. I read a thread in a Wegovy support group where someone swore by ginger root and someone else said simethicone and someone else said fennel tea and someone else said a probiotic with forty billion CFU cleared everything up in a week.

I tried that too.

The thing about adding something new every two weeks is you never know what's working. You just keep layering. You tell yourself the combination is helping while you're still burping sulfur at your grandson's baseball game and praying the wind doesn't shift.

I kept the Pepto tablets in a contact lens case so nobody would see the pink.

That detail alone should have told me something.

The night it all came apart was October. My niece's engagement dinner at my sister-in-law's house. Fifteen people. Beautiful table. Her future in-laws meeting our family for the first time. I was feeling fine, which is always my first warning because I always feel fine right before I don't.

I reached for the peppermint capsules and knocked my whole bag off the chair.

It hit the tile and exploded open. Everything came rolling out. The Gas-X tin. The charcoal bottle. The ginger chews. The mints. A gallon-size Ziploc with five different supplements rattling around inside it. The contact lens case with the Pepto.

Fifteen people at a silent table watching six remedies roll across the floor.

My sister-in-law's mother picked up the charcoal capsules. Read the label. She didn't say anything. Just looked at me the way people look at you when they're deciding between concerned and amused and they land on concerned.

My niece said, "Aunt Deb, are you okay?"

I laughed. Said something about a sensitive stomach. Shoved it all back in my purse and smiled and ate three bites of chicken piccata and spent the rest of the evening trying not to move because movement makes it worse.

In the car on the way home I cried. Not the dramatic kind. The quiet kind where you leak a little because you're tired and embarrassed and you can feel your stomach sitting there like a brick and you already know what tomorrow morning is going to be.

My husband didn't ask what was wrong. He already knows. He handed me a napkin from the glove box.

That's the thing nobody sees. By six o'clock every night I'm in elastic waistband pants because nothing else fits around my midsection even though I've lost forty-one pounds. I plan my mornings around the bathroom. I plan my dinners around what might trigger the sulfur. I plan my purse like a field medic packing a kit, and none of the supplies in the kit do what they're supposed to do.

I was spending more time managing what the medication does to my stomach than enjoying what the medication does for my body. And I couldn't tell anyone that because the medication is working. The weight is coming off. Everyone says I look amazing. And I'm standing there smiling with a purse full of remedies and a stomach full of gas and a bathroom routine I wouldn't wish on anyone.

That night after the dinner I couldn't sleep. I did what I always do — opened my phone and went back to the support groups. But this time I didn't type what I usually type. I didn't search for another remedy. I didn't ask what helps.

I typed something different.

"Why don't gas remedies work on GLP-1 medications."

I found an article by a gastroenterologist and the explanation was so simple I couldn't believe nobody had told me.

GLP-1 medications slow the stomach. That isn't a side effect. That is the mechanism. That is how the drug kills appetite — it tells the stomach muscles to stop contracting as often so food sits longer so you feel full. That's the whole design.

But when food sits too long in a slow stomach, bacteria break it down. That breakdown produces sulfur gas. The gas rises as burps. It also pushes downward and creates bloating. And the muscular slowdown itself is why nothing moves through — that's the constipation.

One cause. Three symptoms. All starting in the same place. The stomach.

Every single thing I owned — the Gas-X, the charcoal, the peppermint, the ginger, every one of them — was designed to work in the colon. Gas-X breaks up gas bubbles in the intestine. The gas had already formed and escaped by then. Charcoal absorbs general gas downstream. Peppermint relaxes smooth muscle in the lower tract — but my problem was that the upper muscles weren't contracting enough. Ginger is a mild digestive aid that does nothing for sulfur and nothing for motility. Probiotics colonize the colon. My colon wasn't the problem.

I was standing at the wrong end of a clogged pipe trying to fix it from the wrong direction. For eleven months.

I said "you've got to be kidding me" out loud in my kitchen at two in the morning.

The article mentioned compounds called prokinetics. Things that encourage the stomach muscles to contract again. Not laxatives, which push from below. Prokinetics wake up the muscles that the medication is putting to sleep. One compound mentioned specifically was apigenin, concentrated in celery, which has natural prokinetic activity.

That was the first piece. Something to get the stomach moving again.

But sulfur was its own problem. The fermentation was producing hydrogen sulfide — and absorbing general gas downstream, which is what charcoal does, doesn't touch the sulfur specifically. What neutralizes sulfur compounds is chlorophyll. Binds to them. Changes the chemistry. Different problem than motility, different fix.

That was the second piece.

And then the third piece fell into place on its own. GLP-1 patients aren't absorbing nutrients properly because food isn't processing the way it should. Vitamins A, C, K, the B vitamins — they drop when the stomach can't do its job. A micro-dose of prebiotic fiber feeds the good bacteria without adding bulk to a system that can't move what's already there. Not a Metamucil dose. A small amount. Enough to support recovery without creating more backup.

Three separate things. Prokinetic activity for the motility. Chlorophyll for the sulfur. Micro-dose fiber and key vitamins for the nutrient gap. Each one made sense alone. But I needed all three working together.

I went looking for anything that combined them. Most supplements had one piece. A celery capsule here. A chlorophyll pill there. Nothing put all three in one place.

I was still scrolling through results for celery-derived apigenin when a company's page loaded between two forum threads. Small brand. Not one I'd seen in any of the groups. Celery juice fiber gummies. I almost scrolled past it.

The company was called Motilli. I read the ingredient panel. Celery juice extract with the apigenin. Chlorophyll. A small amount of prebiotic fiber. Vitamins A, C, K, B6, folate. Pectin-based, which matters because pectin is gentle on a stomach that's already struggling — no gelatin, no capsule to dissolve through a system that can't dissolve what's already in it.

I checked for third-party testing. GMP certified. I read the one-star reviews first because that's where you find the truth.

I ordered it that night. I did not expect it to work.

The first three days I felt nothing. I thought here we go again. Another thing for the purse.

Day four I realized I hadn't reached for the Gas-X tin at lunch. Not because I decided not to. Because I forgot. That had never happened in eleven months.

By the end of the first week the sulfur burps were still there but softer. Less frequent. I went from six or seven a day to maybe two. And the two that came weren't the rotten-egg kind that make you cup your hand over your mouth. They were regular burps.

Week two was bad. Tuesday the sulfur came roaring back. Bloating so bad I changed pants at lunchtime. I sat in my car in the parking lot and thought of course. Of course this was too good. I almost stopped.

But Wednesday was better. Thursday was better than Wednesday. By Friday the burps had settled back down to where they'd been at the end of week one. It wasn't a straight line. It was a bad day inside a good trend. The good trend held.

Week three the evening bloating broke. Not got a little better. Broke. The six o'clock elastic-waistband ritual just stopped. I ate dinner at my table in my normal clothes and cleaned up the kitchen without unbuttoning anything.

The constipation took the longest. Five weeks before I had what I'd call a normal routine. But it came. And it stayed.

It's been four months. I cleaned out my purse last week. I kept one roll of regular mints because I like mints. Everything else went in the trash. The charcoal. The ginger chews. The Gas-X tin I'd been refilling since January. The contact lens case with the Pepto.

My purse weighs what a purse should weigh.

My husband picked it up off the counter to move it and stopped. Looked at the purse. Looked at me. He said, "It's light." I said, "I cleaned it out." And he got a look on his face I can only call relief. Like he'd been carrying the weight of that purse too, in his own way, for eleven months.

When my niece gets married in June I'm going to sit at that table and eat the dinner. Not three bites. The dinner. My husband is going to be next to me. And I'm not going to have a single thing in my purse except my phone, my lipstick, and my keys. He's going to reach for my hand under the table and I'm not going to angle away because I'm worried about what my body might do. I'm going to hold his hand and watch my niece dance with her husband and be there. Fully there. In my body. At the table. With nothing to manage and nothing to hide.

If your purse looks like mine did — if you're carrying six things that don't work because nobody told you they were designed for the wrong organ — the problem isn't that you haven't found the right remedy.

You've been looking in the wrong part of your body.

Motilli gives you 90 days. If your stomach doesn't change, full refund. No company guessing about its product makes that offer.
