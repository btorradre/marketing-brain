# The Struggle

I have a cabinet over my bathroom sink with eleven bottles in it. Miralax, magnesium, three kinds of fiber, a probiotic that cost sixty dollars, the Mounjaro pen in the door shelf. Last month I threw out ten of those bottles and kept the pen. I go to the bathroom like a normal person now.

I want to tell you about the eleven bottles first, because I still can't believe I let it get that far.

I didn't buy them all at once. Nobody decides to own eleven constipation products. You buy one. It doesn't work, so a couple weeks later you buy the next one, and you don't throw the first one out because what if it kicks in eventually. You do that for a year, and one day you go looking for a Q-tip and the shelf won't shut all the way.

I'm 59. Every bottle on that shelf was for the same thing. The same thing I'd dealt with every single day since the month I started the shot.

The shot did what nothing else in my life had done. Forty-some pounds gone in under a year, the food noise finally quiet. I am not going off it. I need you to understand that before I tell you the rest.

But somewhere in the first month, I stopped going to the bathroom.

Not the regular kind of stopped. Four days. Then five. By day six my stomach felt like it had a brick in it, sitting up high behind my ribs, and no amount of waiting moved it. I'd lost weight everywhere and still couldn't button my pants by dinner because I was so backed up I looked swollen.

So I started buying things. Miralax first, because that's what everyone says. Every morning, and then twice a day when every morning stopped doing anything. Then magnesium. Fiber gummies, fiber powder, a third fiber in a different flavor because maybe the flavor was the problem. A probiotic the woman at the counter swore by. Prune juice. Smooth Move tea. Senna for the nights I'd try anything.

Some of it did nothing. The fiber actually made the bloating worse, which I couldn't make sense of at the time.

I went back to my doctor. He gave me about nine minutes. Drink more water, add more fiber, give the Miralax time. I told him I'd been on the Miralax daily for three months. He said we could think about lowering my dose.

That was the moment it hit me. The only fix anyone offered was less of the one thing that was working.

Because that was the choice they kept laying in front of me like it was reasonable. Keep the shot and keep living six days backed up with a powder in my coffee every morning. Or give up the shot and watch all of it come back. I didn't want either one, and I was tired of pretending those were the only two.

I started reading at night. There's a Facebook group for women on Mounjaro, a few thousand of us, and I'd scroll the threads after my husband went to bed. Most of the answers were the same products already in my cabinet.

Then one woman wrote something that stopped me. She said every single thing in my cabinet works at the exit, and the shot doesn't work at the exit. It works by turning down the signal between your brain and your gut, and that same signal is what tells the whole thing to move. She said go look up what the medication actually turns down before I bought one more bottle.

So I looked it up. And it's almost stupid how simple it turned out to be.

There's a signal that runs between your brain and your gut. It sets the pace for all of it. Your stomach empties on it. Your intestines push on it. It fires on its own, every single day, and you've never once thought about it.

These medications turn it down. That's the whole point. Hunger goes quiet, you eat less, the weight comes off. That's where my forty pounds came from.

But that same signal runs everything past your appetite, so the entire line slows at once. Food sits up top for hours and ferments, which is where the rotten-egg burps come from. A trip that used to take a day starts taking three or four. And your colon's job the whole way down is pulling water back out of stool, so give it triple the time and it pulls out far too much.

That's the brick behind my ribs and the cement lower down. Not a plug at the end. A line that had gone quiet, from the top.

Which explained the water. I drank it by the gallon for a year and it never got where it needed to go. My own colon took it back out on the way down.

And that one fact explained every bottle I owned.

Miralax pulls water into the colon and forces the thing out. It doesn't restore anything, which is why the next backup starts the same day, and why a full year of taking it every morning left me exactly where I started. I wasn't fixing anything. I was forcing it, over and over, and calling it maintenance.

I looked up the daily part after that, because by then I wanted to know what I'd actually signed up for. There was a 2023 study in the journal Neurology, half a million people, and regular laxative users had roughly a fifty percent higher risk of developing dementia. The strongest link was for osmotics, which is the category Miralax is in. It's an association and not proof of cause, and I'm not going to pretend otherwise. But I'd been taking it every day for a year with no end in sight and nothing to show for it, and that was enough on its own.

Magnesium worked the same end. The probiotic fed bacteria way down the line.

The fiber was the one that had made me worse, and here's why. I'd been eating a hundred grams of protein a day since month two, because that's what you're supposed to do on this drug to keep your muscle, and there's essentially no fiber in eggs and chicken and a shake. So everyone's answer was fiber, three different kinds of it in my case.

Bulk fiber works by swelling into a mass that stretches the gut wall, and the stretch is what sets off the push. On a gut whose signal has been turned down, nothing answers the stretch. So it sat there and added to the pile. And it needs water to swell, the same water my stool was already losing on the slow way down. Bigger and drier at the same time.

Not one of the eleven did a thing about the gas.

It wasn't that I'd picked the wrong bottles. The shot had turned down the signal that runs my gut, and I'd spent a year forcing the far end of it. I wasn't failing at this. Every product on that shelf was built to force, and forcing was never going to fix anything.

Once I understood that, the question stopped being which bottle to buy next. It turned into a list. A signal that had gone quiet, gas from food sitting too long, and everything downstream dried out behind it. Whatever I needed had to do three separate things. Not one. Three.

It's a compound concentrated in celery, and once I pulled the actual research I found a study where they'd slowed an animal's digestion down on purpose and apigenin got the muscle in the gut wall contracting again. It supports the gut-brain signaling and the natural wave rhythm your gut is supposed to run on by itself. A nudge at the pace instead of a shove at the exit.

The drag on your gut is a separate thing, local, right down at the gut. Apigenin works on that end. That was one piece. It moved the pace. It didn't touch the burps.

What kept coming up was chlorophyll, the copper-bound form, chlorophyllin, used since the 1950s for body and breath odor for exactly this reason. It binds the hydrogen sulfide before it comes back up.

Two pieces now. And I still wasn't all the way there, because a year of slow trips had left everything downstream wrung dry.

It dissolves completely, so there's no bulk to pile up on a gut that isn't moving, and it forms a soft gel that holds the water inside the stool so a slow colon can't strip it out.
