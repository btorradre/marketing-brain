- Car cry: "The quiet kind where you leak a little because you're tired and embarrassed"
- Husband's napkin from the glove box (wordless understanding)

**Assessment:** Seven-plus mirror moments, each surgically specific to the GLP-1 user's unspoken daily experience. The contact lens case detail, the 11:45 timing ritual, the elastic waistband by 6pm, the managing-vs-enjoying paradox — these are the lines a reader screenshots and sends to someone saying "this is literally me." The suffering is shown through the erosion of daily life across multiple rooms and relationships, not stated through labels. Full marks earned.

### Belief Shift 2 — "My approach is wrong about something": 4/5

**Evidence:**
- No doctor scene. The trust vacuum is created through the failed-remedy cascade itself: "The thing about adding something new every two weeks is you never know what's working. You just keep layering."
- The Wegovy support group thread serves as the failed-advice ecosystem: "someone swore by ginger root and someone else said simethicone and someone else said fennel tea and someone else said a probiotic with forty billion CFU cleared everything up in a week."
- The narrator's own realization: "That detail alone should have told me something" (re: hiding Pepto in a contact lens case).

**Assessment:** There is no doctor scene. This is a deliberate design choice given the scope declaration — Debra's "doctor" equivalent is the remedies themselves and the support-group advice ecosystem. The copy SHOWS the approach failing through eleven months of layering, which is effective. The reader arrives at "my approach is wrong" through witnessing Debra's purse grow heavier. However, the lack of a specific authority figure being shown as limited means the trust vacuum is somewhat diffuse — it's a vacuum against peer advice and OTC remedies rather than against a specific trusted source. This works for the solution-aware audience (they've already moved past the doctor), but it's slightly less potent than a scene where a specific authority figure is shown to be wrong. Loses one point for the diffuse target of the trust vacuum.

### Belief Shift 3 — "The problem is in my stomach, not my colon": 5/5

**Evidence:**
- Metaphor installation: "I was standing at the wrong end of a clogged pipe trying to fix it from the wrong direction. For eleven months." (One metaphor, one sentence to install.)
- Cause-and-effect chain delivered through Debra's late-night research: "GLP-1 medications slow the stomach. That isn't a side effect. That is the mechanism."
- Villain teardowns — each 1-2 sentences, applied after the metaphor:
  - "Gas-X breaks up gas bubbles in the intestine. The gas had already formed and escaped by then."
  - "Charcoal absorbs general gas downstream."
  - "Peppermint relaxes smooth muscle in the lower tract — but my problem was that the upper muscles weren't contracting enough."
  - "Ginger is a mild digestive aid that does nothing for sulfur and nothing for motility."
  - "Probiotics colonize the colon. My colon wasn't the problem."

**Assessment:** One metaphor (wrong end of a clogged pipe), installed in one sentence, applied consistently to every villain. Each teardown is 1-2 sentences. No competing metaphors. No re-teaching. The mechanism arrives through Debra's kitchen-table research at 2 AM, emotion-driven but not emotion-carrying. The contrast between the visceral dinner scene and the calm explanation IS the credibility. A reader could retell the stomach-vs-colon distinction to a friend after one read. Full marks.

**Assessment:** Each component is discovered individually with narrative momentum between them ("That was the first piece... That was the second piece... And then the third piece fell into place on its own"). Existing products explicitly fail the checklist. Product enters sideways during a scroll. One point deducted because the existing-products-fail step is compressed to a single sentence ("Most supplements had one piece. A celery capsule here. A chlorophyll pill there.") — this moment could afford one more beat of specific frustration to make the reader feel the gap more viscerally before the product fills it.
