## Reference autopsy

**Format grammar (this is what we replicate 1:1):**
- Creator is a **keyed cutout**, chest-up, parked **lower-center** of frame, occupying roughly the bottom 40%. Never full-frame, never a cut. He is on screen the entire 34 seconds.
- Background is full-bleed and **changes every 1 to 2 seconds**. Two background families, alternating:
  1. **Site scroll** — the brand's own PDP in a mobile browser (title, price, star count, gallery). Used for brand/spec beats. Roughly half the runtime.
  2. **B-roll** — literal illustration of whatever he just said. Folding clothes on a bed, wrinkled linen macro, a suit on a rack, studio product stills.
- **Title card** burned over the top third for the first ~3s only: serif, black on a cream card, rounded corners, three lines.
- **Auto-captions** burned in, small, centered, sitting at the creator's chest line. White, slight shadow, ~3 to 5 words per card.
- One continuous take, one continuous voiceover. No cuts in the creator layer.

**Script grammar (this is how you speak to this avatar):**

| Beat | Reference line | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | "This is the one travel bag that every guy needs this summer." | Declarative. No question, no "POV", no hype word. |
| Brand | "It's from a brand called Halfday, and it's basically solved every problem I've ever had packing." | Names the brand plainly and in the **third person**. He is a guy reporting on a brand, never the brand. |
| Problem | "Usually for a trip, you fold your clothes, you shove them in your suitcase, you get where you're going, and they're wrinkled and you're praying the hotel has an iron." | Second person "you". A run-on chain of small indignities, not a pain point. |
| Dry beat | "Spoiler, it usually doesn't." | One short deadpan aside. This is the only joke in the ad and it is what makes it not read as an ad. |
| Mechanism | "But the Halfday bag has a built-in garment compartment, so you hang your clothes rather than folding them." | ONE feature, explained mechanically, with the "so" that turns it into a benefit. |
| Payoff | "Everything looks exactly how you packed it. And as a bonus, it's still small enough to carry on." | The result, then a throwaway second benefit framed as a bonus. |
| Close | "Stop traveling like it's 2015. Shop the Halfday Premium Garment Duffel at halfdaytravel.com." | Dismissive one-liner, then product name and URL, flat. No "click the link now." |

**Register:** flat, fast (~240 wpm), zero exclamation energy, no adjectives doing sales work. Says the brand name three times. Talks in specifics and numbers, never in feelings.

## The redirect

We keep the skeleton exactly. We do **not** keep the mechanism, because we do not have a garment compartment. Per the sell-our-product law, every proof point below is a real Eleanor Weekender feature.

**Our mechanism = material honesty at a price the audience is told does not exist.**

That is the headline finding of our own men's VOC (`brands/velantra/research/voc/2026-08-02-mens-travel-bag-voc.md`): the entire men's weekender conversation runs $400 to $3,000, the single most-liked comment in a 192-comment dataset is **"Vegan leather, no thanks"** (54 likes), the top reply is **"Stop saying vegan leather please. Call it plastic,"** and men asking for something under $100 get told to buy used. We name our materials literally and our price plainly, and the gap argues itself. No competitor is named.

**VOC rules honoured:**
1. Materials named literally: leather, canvas, brass. Never "premium materials."
2. Interior beat present, on camera and in copy. Nobody in the category shows the inside.
3. "No logos anywhere on it" said out loud. This is a stated purchase requirement for men, not a detail.
4. The bag is never gendered. No "man bag," no "masculine," no Birkin reference.
5. Longevity framing, not season. The close is about not replacing it.
6. Product and brand named early and plainly, unambiguous URL close.
7. Hand or forearm carry only in every frame. Never a shoulder carry.

### v2 revision (Brooks notes on the v1 cut)

> "the creator is blocking most of the broll and the screenshot of the website is not centered" · "the voice sounds a bit robotic" · "you used avatar iv, regen with avatar v"

1. **Creator was far too big.** v1 ran him at 780px wide (72% of frame), bottom-anchored and always centred, so he covered the product in every beat. Measured off the reference reel with a 10% grid: **their creator is only ~28% of frame width and MOVES AROUND THE FRAME between beats**, tucked into whatever corner the background is empty in. v2 drops him to **480px (44%)** and **alternates him left/right per beat** via a single `overlay` x-expression built from the beat table, so he never sits over the bag. Captions moved from y=1255 to **y=1500 (78% down)**, which is where the reference puts them.
2. **PDP screenshot was clipping, not off-centre.** The 390px viewport capture cut the theme's text off on the right ("The Eleanor Weekend…", "Fits the overhea…") because the layout needs more width than 390 CSS px. A mobile user-agent did **not** fix it. Binary-searched the widths: 430 still clips, **500 is the first width where the layout completes**. Re-captured at `--window-size=500,3200 --force-device-scale-factor=3` -> 1500px wide -> scaled to 1080. Also fixed the closing pan, which had overshot past ADD TO CART; it now settles on the full buy box (title, price, swatches, ATC, spec icons).
3. **The robotic voice was the tempo hack, not the model.** v1 was already on `eleven_v3` stability 0.0 (the Creative preset), but it carried `atempo=1.16` to force the read down to the reference's 34s, and ffmpeg's WSOLA stretch is what added the artefact. v2 **trims the script instead and applies no tempo change at all** — natural read, 41.7s. The ad runs longer than the 34s reference; that is the deliberate trade for a clean voice.
4. **Avatar IV -> Avatar V. You upload an image, create an avatar from it, then select Avatar V as the engine.** Three steps, all API:
   1. `POST /v3/assets` (multipart) with the green-screen keyframe -> `asset_id`.
   2. `POST /v3/avatars` `{type:"photo", name, file:{type:"asset_id", asset_id}}` -> creates the avatar. It trains in seconds and comes back with `supported_api_engines: ["avatar_v","avatar_iv","avatar_iii"]`.
   3. `POST /v3/videos` `{type:"avatar", avatar_id:<look_id>, audio_asset_id, engine:{type:"avatar_v"}, resolution:"1080p", aspect_ratio:"auto"}`.

   The only constraint is ordering: the engine is selected against a **created avatar**, so you cannot inline the raw image in the video call the way Avatar IV allows (`type:"image"` + `image:{type:"asset_id"}`). Create it first, then pick the preset. Avatar V preserved the green backdrop the same way and gave noticeably more natural hand motion. Look id `52a007d11911438b87381d3c2ee07544`, group `8d7b4b28aa9d410b95fbbe076acef5f8` — **reuse it for the whole men's set instead of retraining.** New green samples `0x47C341`, keyed at `0.10:0.03`.

### Hard-won specifics (do not relearn these)

- ⚠️ **chromakey similarity 0.15 made the creator semi-transparent** — it bled alpha into his charcoal tee and the b-roll showed through his torso. **0.08 / blend 0.03 is correct**, matching the Dark Chocolate finding. Test the key over a flat red card before compositing; over green-ish b-roll the failure is easy to miss.
- ⚠️ **Caption cards must be clamped to the next card's start.** Overlapping `enable` ranges draw two cards on top of each other and render as garble ("bato:actually use:ored"). Clamp: `end = min(end + 0.12, next_start - 0.02)`.
- ⚠️ **ElevenLabs says "Volantra" from the plain spelling**, same hard prior Seedance has. Six spellings probed: `Vel-AN-tra`, `Vellantra`, `Veh lantra`, `VELL-an-tra`, `Veh-LAHN-truh`, `Vehlantra`, `Veh-lan-tra` all failed. **`Vell-Ahn-Trah` and `Vel Lantra` both land correctly**; `Vay-lahn-truh` works too. Use `Vell-Ahn-Trah` in the TTS input, keep the plain spelling in the human-readable script.
- The a/e in STT read-back flips between "Velantra" and "Velentra" across tempo changes of the same audio. That is STT jitter on an ambiguous vowel — **only the "Vel" vs "Vol" onset is a real signal.**
- **B11-interior-second was cut from the edit**: all three variants rendered a cream/canvas interior, which is a hard FAIL against the real bag's caramel leather. B10 and B13 both rendered the caramel correctly and covered the beat.

### 🚩 Two claim conflicts found against the live PDP (flagged to Brooks, not fixed here)

1. **Hardware is "Gold-tone", not brass.** The men's VOC doc and the product skill both say "brass". The live PDP Care block says *"Hardware: Gold-tone turn-lock, clasp plates, and buckles."* The script originally said "leather, canvas and brass" and was **rewritten to "full grain leather over woven canvas"** — the exact live claim, and a stronger flex for this audience anyway.
2. **The PDP and the physical bag disagree about the interior.** Live PDP: *"Interior: Natural cotton-canvas lining with a leather slip pocket."* Brooks's own 2026-08-08 product footage (and the corrected skill) say the interior is **smooth caramel leather** with a wide caramel slip pocket. The script was routed around it to the one thing both sources agree on — **"there's a leather slip pocket inside"** — so the ad is true either way. **The PDP still needs correcting.**
