## Why de-season it

The fall read works because the colorways happen to land in fall tones, not because the product is seasonal. The Eleanor is a travel bag, and travel does not have an off season. Pegging it to a window caps it to that window and forces a rewrite every quarter. These three run year round.

The replacement peg in each script is a **trip**, not a month. Same shape as the fall read, same proof stack, same close.

## V3: "The Spec Check" (men's cut)

Built from the men's VOC scrape: `brands/velantra/research/voc/2026-08-02-mens-travel-bag-voc.md` (192 Instagram comments across three men's bag reels).

Four VOC findings drive every line:

1. The most-liked comment in the entire dataset is a material rejection ("Vegan leather, no thanks," 54 likes). Materials get named literally or the bag is assumed to be plastic.
2. Interior lining is an open complaint that nobody in the category answers ("lining on a lot of bags is kind of disappointing tbh"). We have a real answer.
3. "No logos" is a stated purchase requirement for men, not a neutral detail.
4. The femininity fight in these comment sections punishes creators who over-defend. The script never genders the bag, never says "men's bag," and **drops "Birkin inspired"** because that phrase hands the objection a weapon in this audience.

### Register (this is the part that was wrong in draft 1)

Draft 1 carried the VOC facts but was written in our register, not theirs. It opened "check three things," which is a marketer's checklist. Nobody in 192 comments talks like that. Three corrections, taken straight off the scrape:

1. **Lead with the objection, not the feature.** Their top comment is a refusal, not a question ("Vegan leather, no thanks," 54 likes). So the script answers the refusal in the first line and moves on.
2. **Proof is measured in time and numbers, not adjectives.** "I've had it for 5 years now. Still looks almost new." "over 4 decades." "$895." "48cm." "12/10." Never "premium," never "crafted."
3. **Short flat sentences.** Almost nobody in the set wrote a subordinate clause. Fragments are correct here: "Reinforced handles. Contrast stitching."

Their approval vocabulary, for reference: clean, solid, holds up, well made, no branding, top shelf, heat, W.

### PRIMARY: "Is It Real"

> "First thing you want to know is whether the leather is real. It is. Leather flap, canvas body, brass hardware, and no branding on it anywhere. This is the Weekender from Velantra. Three days of clothes fits in it, and it still goes in the overhead bin. Full or half empty, it holds its shape. Reinforced handles, contrast stitching, and the inside is lined with a leather pocket on the back wall. Army green or brown. A hundred and fifty nine right now. I left the link below."

~95 words, roughly 30 seconds.

### ALT 1: "Five Years"

Their proof unit is time. Every durability comment in the set is measured in years, never in how it looks today.

> "The only thing that actually matters with a bag like this is what it looks like in five years. Leather flap, canvas body, brass hardware. Reinforced handles, contrast stitching, no branding anywhere on it. This is the Weekender from Velantra. Three days of clothes fits in it and it still goes in the overhead bin, and it holds its shape whether it's packed full or nearly empty. Lined inside, with a leather pocket on the back wall. Army green or brown. A hundred and fifty nine right now. I left the link below."

### ALT 2: "The Inside"

Takes the open complaint nobody in the category answers and makes it the hook instead of a bullet.

> "Let me show you the inside, because that's the part that tells you what a bag is. Lined in canvas, leather pocket on the back wall, one open space, so a full weekend actually fits. This is the Weekender from Velantra. Leather flap, canvas body, brass hardware, no branding on it anywhere. Three days of clothes, and it still goes in the overhead bin. Full or empty, it keeps its shape. Reinforced handles, contrast stitching. Army green or brown. A hundred and fifty nine right now. I left the link below."

**Visual beats:** open on the leather in macro, close enough to read grain, because the first line is a material claim and it needs to be shown while it is said. Then brass turn lock and clasp plate, **interior shot** (the differentiating beat, do not cut it), three days of clothes going in, handles under load, hand carry walking out, army green cut.

**Wording note:** say "brown," not "light chocolate," on camera. Men in the scrape name colors flat: brown, olive, black, tan, midnight green, toffee. "Light chocolate" is our SKU name and it reads as women's merchandising language to this audience. Same for "army green," which is already native and maps onto the olive and midnight green they ask for by name.

**Casting note:** male VO over product b-roll, or a male creator, hand carry only. Army green fronts this cut. Army green maps to olive and midnight green, the single most-requested color in the scrape.

**Do not:** stage a shoulder carry, say "man bag" or "masculine," compare to any other bag or price, mention where it is made, or use any seasonal language.
