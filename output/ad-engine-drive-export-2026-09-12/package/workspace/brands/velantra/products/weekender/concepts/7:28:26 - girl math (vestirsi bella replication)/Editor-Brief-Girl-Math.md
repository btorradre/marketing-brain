## 1. Why the swipe works

A 34.8s first-person travel VO with no face to camera and no product-page moment. The entire ad is the bag, carried, in real places.

| Beat | Job |
|---|---|
| **"Girl math is investing in a bag that…"** | A permission structure, not a claim. It reframes the spend as arithmetic before a price is ever mentioned, so the viewer stops defending her wallet and starts doing the sum. |
| **The range promise as two named endpoints** ("from the airport to a Michelin star restaurant") | Sells versatility without the word versatile. Both endpoints are aspiration, neither is a feature. |
| **Mechanism withheld until 0:05** | The promise lands first. The product name and the mechanism arrive together, after she already wants the outcome. |
| **The split-screen wear-mode demo (0:09–0:11)** | The highest-information 2 seconds in the ad. Three modes, zero copy. A lazy replication drops this and the ad loses its proof. |
| **"I used to pack three bags. Now I pack one."** | The girl math, closed. Past-tense testimony doing the work a claim can't. |
| **Capacity proven by enumeration** ("camera, travel documents, daily essentials") ending on an objection-kill ("never felt bulky") | Lists beat adjectives. The objection is answered inside the same breath as the benefit. |
| **Range restated experientially** ("long walking days… but it still worked for dinners out") | The same claim as the hook, now backed by lived experience instead of aspiration. |
| **Longevity close** | Moves the purchase from a trip expense to a wardrobe line item. |
| **No face for the first 5s, faces only ever in third person** | Nothing to reject, nobody to fail to identify with. She projects herself in. Captions carry everything for sound-off. |

**The mechanic we are replicating:** permission-structure hook → named-endpoint range promise → mechanism reveal → arithmetic testimony → enumerated capacity → material proof → longevity close.

### What we deliberately do NOT copy

| Swipe element | Why it's out | Replacement |
|---|---|---|
| **3-in-1 convertible (crossbody / tote / backpack)** | The Eleanor has two rolled top handles and does not convert. This is the swipe's entire spine, and copying it would be a straight lie. | **The arithmetic moves from *how you wear it* to *what goes in it*.** Our true math is on the live PDP: it holds three days and slides into the overhead bin, so the second bag and the checked bag both go away. Same "one replaces three" payoff, honest mechanism. |
| `Designed in Australia and handmade in Italy` | Velantra is made in China. Origin claims are a hard standing ban. | The material-and-structure RTB, both live PDP language: full-grain leather over tightly woven canvas, and a frame that keeps its shape packed full or barely at all. It does the same quality job and it is observable on camera. |
| `Michelin star restaurant` | Fine on its own, but it is the origin beat's twin — imported prestige. We have our own day-arc. | Endpoints pulled from the PDP's own voice: the Friday morning flight, Saturday night dinner. |
| `This doesn't feel like a bag for one trip, it feels like a bag I'll wear for years` | The "not X, it's Y" contrastive reveal is a banned house pattern. | `I bought it for one weekend. It hasn't stayed home since.` Same longevity reframe, spoken like a person. |
| The split-screen wear-mode grid | No modes to show. | Slot 8: the packed interior. It is our highest-information frame — three days of clothes visible at once. |

## 5. B-roll prompts

Per SOP §2: GPT Image 2 keyframe (attach the **Light Chocolate closed hero** — the front-on product photo, cream canvas and cognac leather, gold hardware) → QA against Section 2 → Google Omni motion, 10s, 9:16, cut ~3s from the early-to-mid stretch. Omni's native audio is muted in the edit.

**Generate 3 variants of every scene and pick the one that is not artifacted** (SOP §3b). Judge photoreal first, then product truth, then composition. A frame that nails the bag but reads as CGI is a reject. Keep the rejects beside the pick.

Every scene prompt below is assembled as **`[PREAMBLE]` + the scene text + `[PHOTOREAL]`**. Both blocks are mandatory and verbatim — they are what stops the frames coming back as product renders.

### `[PREAMBLE]` — opens every i2i prompt

> Use the attached photo ONLY as the reference for the bag's shape, proportions, materials, colours, stitching and hardware. Do NOT copy its lighting, its white background, its clean edges or its polished studio product-photo look. That attachment is a catalogue image and the picture you produce must not resemble one. Create …

### `[PHOTOREAL]` — closes every prompt

> CRITICAL RENDERING INSTRUCTION. This is a real photograph casually taken on an iPhone 15 Pro by an ordinary person, handheld, in one second, with no lighting equipment, no tripod and no styling. It is NOT a 3D render, NOT CGI, NOT a product visualisation, NOT Blender or Octane or Unreal or Keyshot, NOT ray traced, NOT a commercial or catalogue product photograph, NOT an advertisement, NOT retouched, NOT airbrushed, NOT studio lit. If it looks polished or computer generated it is wrong. Photographic evidence that must be present: visible digital sensor noise and grain through the shadows and midtones, highlights slightly blown out where the light source hits, mild chromatic aberration on high contrast edges, faint JPEG compression artefacts, focus that is slightly imperfect so nothing is tack sharp, a trace of handheld motion blur, and framing that is a little crooked and off centre the way a real snapshot is. Real light only: one dominant available light source, mixed colour temperature across the frame, uneven exposure, and real shadows falling off naturally with visible ambient bounce. Real surfaces: the leather is creased, faintly scuffed, unevenly grained and dulled where it has been handled, never a uniform polished finish; the canvas shows individual woven fibres, slubs and small wrinkles; ordinary dust, lint and fingerprints are present. The setting is a real lived-in place with ordinary clutter, not a set. No on-screen text, lettering, signage or graphics anywhere. Vertical 9:16.

**Reject on sight:** uniform polished leather with no creases or scuffs · clean even lighting with no blown highlights and no grain · perfectly centred framing · tack-sharp everywhere · spotless surfaces · a background that reads as a set rather than a real room.

## 6. Voice, music, captions

**Voice (ElevenLabs):** the cloned **"Woman Over 40"** voice on **eleven_v3**, **Creative** stability preset (`stability: 0.0`), similarity 0.80, speaker boost on. Calm and even, a friend telling you what she actually does rather than selling. Zero hype, no upward inflection at line ends. **One seamless full-length track**, never per-line splices. VO ≈ −14 LUFS. Generate the three hook variants as three full takes from the same voice so the cuts are interchangeable.

> **v3 has no `speed` or `style` parameter**, and the Creative preset varies pace a lot run to run (2.5–3.4 w/s observed across takes). Roll **3 takes per version and pick on pace** — target ~3.3–3.4 w/s. All nine takes are kept in `_production/vo/` as `vo-<ver>-t<n>.mp3`; the picks are recorded in `vo-picks.json`. The earlier multilingual-v2 read was rejected as robotic.

**Music:** soft, low, no lyrics, no drop, sitting well under the VO (≈ −24 LUFS). All Omni-generated ambient audio muted.

> **Outstanding:** the delivered cuts are VO-only. There is no licensed Velantra music in the vault, so the bed still needs to be dropped in before this runs.

**Captions:** match the swipe's restraint rather than the house bold-outline style — the Eleanor's register is quieter and the reference proves this reads at feed size. Small white sans-serif, regular weight, sentence case, centered, sitting low at roughly 72% frame height. Max 6 words per card, max 2 lines. Transcribes the VO word for word. Product is always **The Eleanor Weekender** or **the Eleanor**.

> **As built:** 50px at 1080 wide, with a soft blurred dark halo behind the glyphs. Bare white (as specced) was illegible on the brighter slots — marble, cream canvas, white bedding — which the swipe never had to deal with. The halo is a shadow, not an outline, and it disappears on the darker frames.

## 9. Guardrails + QA

1. Script verbatim. No country-of-origin claims anywhere, in copy or on screen.
2. No other brand or designer bag named, shown, or implied. The three bags in Scene C are plain and unbranded.
3. Never claim or imply the bag converts, adjusts, or is worn any way other than by its two top handles.
4. The bag is closed in every generated shot. The only open-bag frame in the ad is the pre-approved library clip. Nothing is ever opened, fastened or adjusted on camera.
5. **Frame QA before animating.** Every keyframe gets checked against Section 2 side by side with the reference photo. Never animate an unaudited frame. On the four moving-bag scenes (B, E1, E2, F) pull frames from the start, middle and end of the finished clip and recheck — engines break the construction mid-motion even from a clean first frame.
6. Light Chocolate only. Army Green never appears.
7. Raw iPhone lighting on every generation including the macro. Anything that reads studio-lit or commercial gets regenerated rawer.
8. No native generated text anywhere. All copy is overlaid in post.
9. If a scene fails three times, substitute a library clip or a gallery photo with a slow push-in. Never stretch a neighbouring clip to fill the gap.
