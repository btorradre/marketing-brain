### Why it works

* **Feature avalanche as ASMR.** Roughly 24 cuts in 33 seconds, ~1.4s average. Every cut is one capability, demonstrated rather than claimed. Nothing is argued, so nothing can be disagreed with.
* **Double encoding.** The overlay text is the voiceover, word for word, accumulating on screen as it is spoken. The viewer reads and hears the same claim at the same moment. It also makes the ad work with sound off.
* **Cumulative text, sentence-scoped.** Words stack up the left third, then the stack clears at the sentence boundary. The stacking itself creates forward pull because the sentence is visibly unfinished.
* **One bag, one colorway, one continuous space.** Every cut is legible as the same object in the same room. That congruence is what makes a feature list feel like a single demonstration instead of a slideshow.
* **Hands, not a face.** No talking head, no eye contact, no persuasion posture. The viewer is over the shoulder of a competent person, which converts the ad from a pitch into a demonstration.
* **Emotional engine:** competence and control first (everything has a place), then status (premium leather), then a payoff surprise (it rolls). Satisfaction, then pride, then delight.
* **Golden nugget:** packing competence as masculine identity. The ownership fantasy is being the man who walks out in one clean motion while everyone else wrestles a roller and three totes.
* **Awareness:** solution aware in, product aware out, by pure demonstration.

## The redirect

**Four of the reference's eight beats are mechanisms we do not have.** The Weekender has no garment section, no five pockets, no shoe or laundry compartment, no electronics panel, no wheels and no telescoping handle. A literal trace would require fabricating features, so the skeleton ports and the surface is re-derived from what the bag actually does.

| Reference beat | Our beat | Why |
|---|---|---|
| doubles as a garment bag | fold-back flap opens to a caramel leather interior | our actual opening mechanism |
| plenty of room for a week | three days of clothes, real stacks going in | live PDP claim, honestly sized |
| five pockets | the one wide caramel slip pocket, used | we have one, so we show one |
| carry-on size | slides into the overhead bin | live PDP claim |
| laundry and shoe pocket | cut, nothing to substitute | no fabrication |
| electronics compartment | cut, nothing to substitute | no fabrication |
| premium leather | cognac leather grain, contrast stitching, handle bases, key bell | direct port, this one is genuinely ours |
| **it rolls like a suitcase** | **it keeps its shape when you press down on it** | our structural magic trick, demonstrable on camera |
| walk out rolling it | walk out carrying it in one hand | carry truth: hand or forearm only |

**Engine kept, payoff swapped.** The reference's delight spike is a gadget surprise. Ours is quiet structural confidence, which sits better on Velantra's timelessness spine than a mechanism reveal would. The price lands immediately after as the second beat of a one-two.

**Congruence rule (Brooks, this build):** one colorway, one room, one light source, one bag. Every scene is the same object photographed from a different angle in the same space. Light Chocolate throughout.

**Contrast note:** Light Chocolate is cream canvas, so it disappears against white bedding. The staging answer is a charcoal grey wool throw across the foot of the bed and a dark walnut bench, both written into the locked setting block. The bag sits on charcoal or walnut in every beat where it is the subject.

## Production

1. **Stills** — GPT Image 2 i2i on kie (`gpt-image-2-image-to-image`, 9:16, 2K, 10cr each). Three variants per scene, 39 total, sequential with a retry loop. Every scene wires at least one real-product still from `real-product-2026-08-08/` as a reference.
2. **QA** — frame-QA subagent pass on all 39 against the real-product stills and the open/closed calibrations. Pick one variant per scene on photoreal first, then product truth, then composition. Rejects stay on disk beside the pick.
3. **Animate** — Google Omni (`gemini-omni-flash-preview`, Interactions API) i2v from each picked still. **The three macros (S09, S10, S11) get exactly one Omni roll each**; on any hardware drift they fall back to a Ken Burns push-in from the approved still, per the standing macro law (macros are 0/5 on re-roll and Omni engraves pseudo-lettering onto metal plates).
4. **Assemble** — ChatCut: trim each clip to its target, hard cuts, cumulative burned captions, ElevenLabs VO laid across the whole assembly as one continuous track.

### What the QA loop caught (full detail in `qa/QA-LOG.md`)

Four adversarial still passes plus two drift audits over the animated clips. Five of thirteen
scenes were regenerated. **Three of the defect classes traced to errors in our own prompt, not
to model randomness:**

1. *"Belt straps hang loose down the sides"* sent the straps down the cream canvas front. The
   real straps hang near the SIDE edges; the fastened state lies horizontal on the band. Both
   states are real, which one auditor got wrong in the opposite direction.
2. The closure block was gated on `hardware=True`, so S03 (the open-bag hero) received no strap
   guidance at all and failed worst of any scene.
3. *"The small gold oval turn lock is mounted on the leather band"* bred a DUPLICATE oval on
   every open-bag still. The band carries the knurled post; the oval belongs to the flap tab.

Also fixed: side gussets growing oval plates, leather rendering as suede/nubuck/patent, canvas
losing its weave, handles drooping into slack loops, and the turn lock rendering as three
different objects across S07/S08/S09.

**The structural reason most drift was survivable:** every cut uses only the first 1-3.7s of a
10s clip, so mid-clip and end-clip drift never reaches the timeline. Only frame-zero defects
forced a regeneration.
