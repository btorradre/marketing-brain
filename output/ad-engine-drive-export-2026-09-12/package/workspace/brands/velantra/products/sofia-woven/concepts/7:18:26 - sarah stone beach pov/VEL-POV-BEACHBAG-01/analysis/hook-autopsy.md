# Hook autopsy — "POV: you found your new favorite beach bag"

## Exact hook
"POV: you found your new favorite beach bag" — classic second-person **"POV: you…"** family, discovery subformat ("you found…").

## Golden nugget it rides on
She doesn't want a bag that carries her beach stuff — she wants to BE the put-together woman on the sand whose beach day looks effortlessly curated, and the bag is the visible proof.

## Psychological driver
Identification + wish-fulfillment. "Found" is the load-bearing word: it frames the purchase as serendipity, not shopping — zero sales pressure, pure "this happened to me and it could happen to you." The scroller is cast INSIDE the fantasy ("POV: you") at the exact moment of payoff: standing on the sand with the bag already hers. It also carries a quiet status wink — "favorite" implies a bag wardrobe, a woman with taste ranking her collection.

## Text–motion congruence
The hook claims "new favorite beach bag" and the motion spends 8 seconds PROVING it without a word: she opens it, pulls out a camera (the curated-summer prop — she documents her life), smiles, and starts shooting. The bag is never pitched; it performs. Static filmed-by-a-friend framing makes the whole thing read as a candid moment the text happens to narrate — congruence between amateur camera and "this is just my life" claim.

## Bridge to our product
The Velantra Straw Tote in Caramel is visually SUPERIOR to the reference bag for this exact frame — structured Birkin-style silhouette in woven straw reads instantly as "the elevated beach bag." Awareness calibration: the 2 AM scroller isn't searching "straw tote," she's ambiently wishing her summer looked more together — so the hook must sell the identity moment, not the product specs. Proven Velantra angles this format carries: coastal put-together identity, sand-to-lunch versatility, compliment magnet. Constraints honored: no competitor mentions, no origin claims, founder is "Jessica" if ever named.
