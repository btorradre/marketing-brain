|---|---|---|

Note the whiplash for the record: **VEL-SOFIA-EDIT-01 shipped yesterday (7/24–25)** and its entire §2 was devoted to *removing* competitor comparison from a ranking reel, moving the field of candidates inside the Sofia's own colorways. This concept does the opposite on purpose. Both can coexist as separate tests — but they should not run in the same ad set, because they teach the audience contradictory things about whether Velantra names other brands at all. Note the 7/26 tone law (§4) narrows the reversal considerably: this concept *names* competitors but never attacks them, so what is actually being overridden is the no-naming rule, not the no-wound rule. ## 3. Risk register (Brooks's call, stated once, mitigations built in)

Naming brands while stating what their bags do and do not give you carries three live exposures. None is a reason not to ship; each has a mitigation baked into the script. Note the 7/26 tone pass materially lowers exposure on all three: the script now praises each bag before stating the gap, so nothing in it reads as disparagement.

| Risk | Mitigation in this concept |
|---|---|
| **Lanham Act §43(a) false advertising** — a false statement of fact about a competitor's product is actionable; opinion and substantiated fact are not | Every quality claim is either (a) attributed to published reviewers, or (b) a physical description of the product that is self-evident on screen. Nothing is invented. §7 tracks the sourcing per claim. |
| **Meta ad rejection** — comparative/competitor-attack creative draws "Unacceptable Business Practices" and IP flags at elevated rates | Have a fallback cut ready: `-ALT` version replaces brand names with the archetype ("the two thousand dollar crochet tote") and blurs logos. Same edit, two audio tracks. Build both in one pass. |
| **Brand takedown on the b-roll** — competitor footage is their copyrighted campaign material | Use short clips, keep them commentary-adjacent, and prefer neutral PDP stills over campaign film. Have Velantra-shot substitutes ready for any clip pulled. |

## 4. The lineup — ranked #5 → #1 by descending price

The ranking runs **most expensive to cheapest**, ending on the Sofia. This is the reference's own price-descent structure — it opens "if money isn't an object" and works downward — used unchanged.

The strategic payload is the **descent itself**: each step down the price ladder costs the viewer less and gets her more, so by the time #1 lands at $119.99 the conclusion is already built. The ad argues about the *price/quality gap* rather than about any one company, which is both the stronger argument and the safer one.

**The Sofia holds the #1 slot.** The hook promises the best summer bags at every price point and the countdown delivers exactly that — #1 is the answer, not a bait-and-switch. Four competitors, ranked by descending price, each one acknowledged as genuinely desirable before the value gap is stated.

### 🔒 Tone law — the whole concept turns on this (Brooks, 2026-07-26)

**We are not claiming the Sofia is the best of a bad field. We are claiming it is the best all-around option out of a field of genuinely great bags.** The other four are not filler and not targets — they are, sincerely, some of the best summer bags on the internet. The hook is true.

What separates the Sofia is **the discrepancy between price and quality**, which is Velantra's core thesis. Every competitor beat is therefore built as *strength first, trade-off second* — and the trade-off is a **trade-off, never a defect**:

| Bag | Its genuine strength | Its trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Saint Laurent | Stunning, hand woven raffia, beautifully made | $2,700 is a lot for woven raffia |
| Prada | Gorgeous crochet, instantly recognizable | It is crocheted yarn, at $2,000 |
| Loewe | The shape is perfect | The palm is delicate, so you look after it |
| Cult Gaia | Genuinely gorgeous, best price so far | Statement piece: holds little, hand-held only |
| **Sofia** | **Does all of it — woven, leather, structured, carries everything** | **None. $119.99.** |

The thesis line is *"the only question is how much of what you are paying for is actually the bag."* That is the price/quality gap stated without naming a villain.

**Three things this buys us.** It makes the hook honest, so the ad never feels like bait. It keeps the smart-money avatar intact — she is not being told her taste was wrong, she is being handed a better-value version of exactly what she already wants. And it collapses the §3 legal exposure to near zero: praise plus an accurate trade-off is not disparagement.

**What this bans:** "the whole bag," "that is all it is," "not really about the bag," "you have to be careful with it," anything that reads as a sneer, and any framing where a competitor's bag is the problem. If a line would make a Saint Laurent owner feel stupid, it is the wrong line.

**⚠️ Prices verified against each brand's own site; three moved.** Prada is **$2,050**, not the $3,050 a resale listing claimed. Loewe resolved to exactly **$690**. Saint Laurent went through two revisions: a retailer listed the Panier *Small* at $2,650, but the PDP Brooks supplied 2026-07-26 is the **Panier Medium at $2,700** — that is the bag we now hold real imagery for, so it is the bag we quote. **Material correction from the same source: the Panier is raffia, not palm.** Palm is Loewe's material. The script said "woven palm" for Saint Laurent and was wrong; fixed.

## 6. Format spec — how the reference is actually built

Watched frame-by-frame. This is not a talking-head ad and not a montage; it is a specific hybrid, and copying it exactly is most of the win:

- **Full-bleed b-roll** fills the entire 9:16 frame — brand campaign footage, model shots, product-on-white, hands holding. Each bag gets 2–6 seconds.
- **A small rectangular cut-out of the creator** floats on top, roughly 30–40% of frame width, hard-edged rectangle with visible crop borders (deliberately unpolished — it reads as a screen-recorded reaction, not a designed lower-third).
- **The PIP moves every cut** — top-center, bottom-left, mid-left, mid-center. It is repositioned each time so it never covers the bag. This constant repositioning is a real retention device; do not park it in one corner.
- **The talking-head is one continuous take.** Same grey ribbed knit, same handheld mic, same room, start to finish. Only the crop and position change. This is why the VO must be **one seamless full-length track** (`feedback_vo_single_seamless_cut`).
- **On-screen text: hook only.** Two blocks — "The Best Summer Bags On The Internet" upper-third, "At Every Price Point" lower-third. Bold white, heavy black stroke, tight leading. No price cards, no brand lower-thirds, no captions anywhere else in the reference.
- **Audio: voice only.** No music bed. Room tone carries the cuts.
- **Opening frame is a 4-up grid collage** of four different bags with the hook text over it.

### The one place we add to the reference

Brooks asked for the bags to be **numbered**, and a price-comparison ad cannot run its argument through VO alone — the numbers have to be legible and screenshot-able. So we add a **number + price card** per bag that the reference does not have.

Spec it in the reference's own type so it feels native rather than bolted on:

```
        #5
   PRADA RAFFIA TOTE
       $3,050
```
Bold white, heavy black stroke, centered, upper-third, punching in on the cut and holding for the full beat. Same typeface as the hook cards. Nothing else changes — no captions, no music, no transitions.

Four bags instead of five. ### 🔒 Hook-grid congruence law (Brooks, 2026-07-26)

**The hook grid must show exactly as many bags as the VO claims to rank.** The script says *"I ranked all five,"* so the grid holds **five** — the four named competitors plus the Sofia. It shipped as a four-cell grid at first and Brooks caught it: a viewer counts the tiles in the first two seconds, and four tiles under "all five" is a mismatch they feel even if they never name it. If the lineup ever changes, `_production/grids.py` changes with it. That file is the single source of truth for the grid and carries this law in its docstring.

**Layout: 2 + 2 + 1, every cell an identical 540×640 portrait, the fifth centred on the bottom row.** Not a full-width fifth cell — these are all tall product shots on white and a 1080×640 letterbox crop decapitates them (tried it; Saint Laurent lost both the strap tops and the base). Identical cells also make the five read as one set rather than four-plus-a-banner. Hook type is anchored at 0.335 / 0.665 so it lands in the **row gutters** (rows break at y=640 and y=1280) instead of across a bag.

**The Sofia sits in an ordinary cell, unlabelled and un-emphasised.** It is on screen from second zero and is not named as #1 until ~61s — the answer hiding in plain sight, which is a far better reveal than introducing it cold. **Do not move it to the centred fifth slot**; the odd position carries emphasis and would telegraph the ending. Saint Laurent takes that slot: it is the first bag the VO names and the strongest silhouette on white.

**PIP discipline:** never let it sit in the same position two beats running, and never let it cover the bag or the number card. Held still only on the Turn — the stillness is what marks that beat as the argument.
