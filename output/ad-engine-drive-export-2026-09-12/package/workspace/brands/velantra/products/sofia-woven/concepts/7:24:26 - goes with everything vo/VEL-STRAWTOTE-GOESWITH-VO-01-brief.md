## Why the reference works

The reference never *argues* that the bag is versatile. It **runs the proof as an edit**: seven different outfits, seven different locations, seven different times of day — cut at roughly one per second — with the *same* bag in every frame. By the time the VO says "it goes with everything," the viewer has already watched it happen seven times. The line lands as confirmation, not as a claim.

Three-act spine:

| Act | Time | What it does | Model? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A — Occasion montage** | 0:00–0:07 | 7 shots, ~1s each. Versatility demonstrated, never stated. Nothing said about the bag yet. | Yes, but face rarely visible |
| **B — Product studio** | 0:07–0:12 | Cut to clean cream backdrop, no model. Slow, tactile, product named + material proof. Pace drops hard — this contrast is the whole reveal. | Hand only |
| **C/D — Back to life** | 0:12–0:19 | Walking, motion, ownership language, longevity close. | Yes |

Craft notes: hard midday sun with crisp shadows, handheld, no music-forward mix, thin white centered subtitles in the upper third mirroring the VO word-for-word. Wardrobe is disciplined to black + one denim/white variation, so the tan bag is the only color that moves through the edit.

**Structural swap for us:** the reference's Act-B material beat is provenance ("full-grain LWG certified leather → looks better with wear"). We cannot use provenance (origin claims banned). Our equivalent proof is **structure**: hand woven, structured body, holds its shape, holds a whole day. Same slot, same function, legal claim.

### Model-discontinuity handling

We cannot hold one identity across 16 independently generated keyframes. Rather than fight it, the shot list is **designed so identity never has to hold**: no shot presents a clear, front-on, unobstructed face. Every human beat is cropped, back-turned, high-angle, in profile, or behind sunglasses. The reference already does this in several of its own shots — leaning into it fully reads as an intentional editorial montage rather than a continuity error.

The two things that *do* stay constant across every frame — and are the only things that need to — are **the caramel Straw Tote** and **the black/white/denim wardrobe discipline**.

### Product-truth locks applied

- **Flap:** ONE seamless sheet folded all the way forward, lying flat. Verbatim closed-flap pin on 15 shots; the open-mouth pin (contents enter *behind* the flap, per the opened reference image) on S01 only.
- **Closure-interaction law:** no shot animates hands working the closure. S06/S07 touch the *handles* only. S01's hand never touches the flap.
- **No shoulder strap:** the tote has only its two rolled top handles — every carry is by hand or hooked over the forearm. The prompts explicitly forbid an invented crossbody strap (the reference model wears hers crossbody; that shot language could not be copied literally).
- Every keyframe goes through the mandatory fresh-context frame-QA subagent pass before animation.

## Production log

**Cut points are derived from Whisper word timings** on the chosen VO take (`vo/eden-clone/words.json`), so every shot change lands on the intended word rather than an estimate. Each 6s Kling clip is trimmed from its **head** — i2v drift grows late, so the head is the safe region.

### QA rounds

**Keyframes, round 1** — 15/16 generated; S03 was refused by the content filter for its POV phrasing ("looking down her own body") and was rewritten as a plain high-angle description. Fresh-context audit failed 5: S01 (sunglasses ghosting *through* the flap), S05 (flap rendered as three separate panels with gaps to the top edge), S13 (corner sparkle watermark + ghost overlays), S02 (smudge on the leather), S14 (white spike off the flap edge).

**Keyframes, round 2** — all 5 re-rolled after hardening three things in the manifest: the closed-flap pin now states the leather runs as *one uninterrupted top band* and that notches start low and never reach the top edge; the open-flap pin now requires the contents to sit entirely behind the leather in Z-depth with the rear woven mouth visible; the style line now bans watermarks, glyphs, smudges and stray edge highlights. S05 was also re-blocked — the bag moved off her dark clothing onto the sunlit bench so its top edge silhouettes against a light background.

A second audit pass then flagged S02/S03/S05/S13 as "three separate panels." **Adjudicated as false fails**: cropping the flap region of each frame and the canonical `caramel 1.png` to matched scale shows the reference *itself* carries stitching tracing each cut shape's perimeter, which was the auditor's stated discriminator. The top band is continuous in all four. Recorded here because it will come up again — the discriminator that actually works is *"does a vertical gap reach the top edge?"*, not *"is each shape outlined?"*.

### Clip mutation (new failure signature)

All 16 clips were audited at their **used window only** (end-of-window frame per shot, since only the head of each 6s clip ships). 14 held. **S12 and S16 mutated mid-motion in a way not previously recorded**: the two crossed belt straps merged into a single horizontal strap and a **gold turn-lock / buckle grew at its centre** — invented hardware on a bag that has none. Both were within the shipping window, so both were re-rolled with:

- an anti-mutation clause added to the Kling pin (straps stay crossed in an X, never merge; no buckle, turn lock, clasp, stud, ring or metal fitting ever appears), and
- **reduced bag motion** in the motion prompt — the mutation correlated with the bag swinging freely, so both shots now carry it held still and level.

Both re-rolls hold the X and stay hardware-free across start, middle and end of the used window.

### S01 opening mechanism — round 4, and the actual doctrine (Brooks catch)

Round 3 was still wrong. Brooks: *"The flap should fold over the entire bag. When you open the bag, you fold the flap over."*

**The correct reading, now recorded in memory:** the flap folds over the WHOLE bag — from the top rear edge, forward across the top, down the front. So the fold *is* the top of the bag, and the mouth underneath is **covered by the leather**. Nothing stands above the flap: no straw wall, no side walls rising past it, no visible interior.

Which means a packing shot **looks closed**. The bag matches the closed hero exactly; the hand just slips the sunglasses in behind the flap's top edge and they disappear behind the leather. Every earlier attempt failed because I kept trying to *show* an opening — rounds 1-3 were all variations on rendering an open bag, which is the one thing this mechanism never does.

S01 now wires the **closed hero** (`caramel 1.png`) only. `straw birkin opened.png` was dropped entirely — it shows the flap flipped back and was the thing dragging every attempt toward the wrong geometry.

### S01 handles — round 3

The first fix to the open-flap pin **over-corrected and broke the mechanism**. Telling the generator the open mouth must be "clearly visible **rising above and behind** the flap" made it render a tall upright box of straw with a short leather panel applied partway down the front face — so the flap was no longer the top of the bag — and the two rolled handles collapsed forward into a bunched leather mass over the center panel. Both the keyframe and therefore the clip were wrong. It shipped in V1 because the end-of-window audit checked the flap band and the X belts and did not check the handles.

Three corrections, now in both pins:

1. **Flap position:** "the flap's folded top edge IS the top front edge of the tote — the leather starts at the very top of the bag, not partway down it, and there is never a wall or band of straw standing above the flap."
2. **Opening depth:** the mouth behind the flap is "a shallow opening seen from above in strong foreshortening," never a tall upright box.
3. **New HANDLES clause (added to the closed pin too, since it is a general risk):** the 2 rolled handles rise as two separate clean loops, well apart, each through its own slot — they never collapse flat onto the flap, never bunch into a mass, never form a blob over the center panel, and are never wrapped or threaded around anything.

S01 also now wires `straw birkin opened.png` **first** in `input_urls` so the open state is the identity anchor rather than the closed hero.

**Worth a decision from Brooks:** `straw birkin opened.png` shows the flap **flipped back** off the mouth, which contradicts the locked rule that the flap never lifts and is always folded all the way over the front. The law was followed (the shipped S01 has the flap folded forward and flat with the mouth open behind it), but that reference image will keep pulling generations toward the flipped-back geometry every time an open-bag shot is attempted. Either the image should be retired as an open-bag reference, or the rule needs an explicit note about what that PDP shot is.
