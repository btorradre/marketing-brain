## 1. Strategic frame

**Jessica is not the designer who came down from the industry. She is the customer who got fed up.**

- Vestirsi anchors its founder story in *origin* (honeymoon in Italy, grandparents, artisans). Velantra can never make origin claims → Jessica's story anchors in *identity*: she IS the Velantra Woman. Her story is the avatar's story told back to her.
- DR mechanics baked in: **authority subversion** (the industry says luxury = price = logo; Jessica says luxury = how it holds up on a Tuesday) + **vindication** (the customer was right to refuse the game) — per the hook-philosophy laws.
- The story exists to make the naming system inevitable: "one woman at a time" is not a gimmick bolted on; it's the conclusion of her arc.
- Every brand pillar becomes HER belief: durability ("decades, not seasons"), quiet luxury (no logos), beauty+function refusal-to-choose, the warranty ("we'll make it right" = her personal promise).

## 2. Story Laws (what Jessica NEVER says)

1. **No origin claims** — no Italy, no Europe, no "atelier" language additions, no artisan-geography. "People who build things the old way" is the ceiling.
2. **No named competitors** — "the designer names" / "the beautiful ones" only, never a brand.
3. **No verifiable biography** — no city, no employer, no school, no husband's honeymoon. Her bio is emotional, not factual: years of commutes, carry-ons, and bags that betrayed her. Unfalsifiable and mirror-perfect for the avatar.
4. **Never Brooks** — Jessica is the only public founder.
5. **The women are her design philosophy, not real people** — she "knows their names" as a way of designing; never "Sofia is my sister/customer."

## 3. THE STORY — About page (full version, ~250 words)

> ## I carried the wrong bags for twenty years.
>
> I'm not a designer by training. I'm a woman who needed a bag that could keep up — and couldn't find one.
>
> The beautiful ones cost a month's rent and demanded to be treated like art. The practical ones fell apart by spring. And somewhere along the way, I accepted what every woman I know has accepted: that you choose. Beautiful or useful. Precious or durable. A bag you love, or a bag you can actually live in.
>
> Then one morning I looked down in a meeting and saw the bag I'd saved months for — shape collapsing, strap fraying at the seam — and I finally understood. That bag was never designed for my life. It was designed for a shelf.
>
> The industry has it backwards. They design objects, then ask women to build a life around them. Nobody was designing for the life itself — the 8 a.m. meeting. The farmers market. The Friday flight. The harbor in July.
>
> So I stopped waiting. I found people who build things the old way — reinforced stitching, solid brass, materials that earn character instead of losing it — and I started designing. Not a collection. One bag, for one woman, with somewhere to be. By the time each bag was finished, I knew her name.
>
> Margot. Camille. Eleanor. Sofia.
>
> You'll recognize them. You might be one of them.
>
> You're probably all four.
>
> — Jessica
