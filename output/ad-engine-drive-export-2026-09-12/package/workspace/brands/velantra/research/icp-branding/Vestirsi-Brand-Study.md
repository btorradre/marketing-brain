# Vestirsi Brand Study — How They Tell a Story Through Products

**Date:** 2026-07-22 · **Studied for:** Velantra naming/identity overhaul
**Site:** us.shopvestirsi.com · **Founder:** Monica Upton (Australian, Italian heritage, ex-luxury retail)
**Price range:** $249–$639 · **Companion doc:** `Velantra-Naming-System-The-Women.md`

---

## 1. The Story Spine (three stacked layers)

**Layer 1 — Origin myth (the anchor):** Monica couldn't find "a beautifully made leather bag that felt elevated, timeless, and considered without a four-figure price tag." On her honeymoon in her grandparents' native Italy she met artisans and built the supply chain herself. Every product inherits this: handmade in Italy, LWG-certified tanneries, artisans "working with leather for generations."

**Layer 2 — Fusion identity:** "Italian Leather Craftsmanship Meets Australian Laidback Luxury." Two words that shouldn't go together (heritage craft + laidback ease) = the brand's entire personality. Every product is that sentence in object form.

**Layer 3 — Values loop:** 10% of net profit on designated bags → women's mental health charities. By a woman, for women, supporting women. Buying a bag becomes an act of the persona's values, not just her wardrobe.

## 2. The Naming System

Every silhouette is a woman's name: **Bella, Ashley, Rita, Vera, Celeste, Eden, Sloane, Scarlett, Serena, Savannah, Jess, Clara.** Grammar: `[Name] [Color] [Silhouette]` → "Eden Dark Chocolate Suede Shoulder Bag."

- Name = character/emotion; descriptor = function/SEO. Both always present.
- **Name families:** winners become franchises — Bella Mini / Classic / XL = "The Bella Family." The name scales across sizes and materials (Mini Woven Bella) without losing equity.
- **Crucially: they never explain the names.** No "who is Bella" story anywhere. The names are texture, not narrative — an opportunity they leave on the table.

## 3. Collections as Chapters of Her Life

- **Vita Vissuta** ("life lived") — everyday essentials, "bags made for real life, shaped by the women who live it," pieces "you reach for without thinking"
- **The Resort Edit / Resort 26'** — her vacation self (Jess pouch, woven pieces)
- **The Structured Edit** — her work polish
- **The Woven Collection** — seasonal craft story
- Material-based browsing (suede, pebbled, raffia) as a secondary axis

Italian collection names deepen the origin story even where product names don't.

## 4. Core Persona: ONE woman, many modes

"The VESTIRSI woman": ~28–45, professional, post-logo, values "quality, confidence and considered style," wants elevation without effort or four figures. The catalog is **not many personas — it's one woman's week:**

| Product | The mode of her life it serves |
| :--- | :--- |
| Bella 3-in-1 (flagship, $459) | Workweek/commute — laptop, "work-to-weekend," the capable professional |
| Vera Bowler / Baguette | Office polish |
| Rita / Vera Camera / Clara (Vita Vissuta) | Off-duty errands — hands-free, unthinking reach |
| Eden / Scarlett / Serena / Savannah (suede hobos) | Soft social weekends — texture, slouch |
| Sloane / Celeste clutch-crossbodies | Dinner, evenings, events |
| Jess pouch + Resort Edit | Vacation self |
| Mini / Classic / XL sizes | Duty-load within the same character |

**The flagship embodies the thesis:** Bella 3-in-1 ("One Bag, Three Ways To Wear") IS the multi-faceted-woman story in hardware. Hero product = brand argument.

**Commerce effect:** because each bag owns one *moment* (only Bella claims versatility — the gateway), owning 3–4 Vestirsi bags is the natural end-state. Price ladder $249 → $299 → $399 → $459 → $639 maps entry → collection. The story architecture is also the AOV/LTV architecture.

## 5. Lessons for Velantra

1. **Names validated** — full women's-name system at a $249–639 price point, editorially endorsed (Vogue, Marie Claire, David Jones). Our "The Sofia" instinct is the proven pattern.
2. **We must invert the anchor.** Vestirsi's spine is origin (Italy). Velantra can never make origin claims → our spine must be *persona* ("designed for one woman at a time"). Where they anchor on WHERE it's made, we anchor on WHO it's for.
3. **Tell the naming story they don't.** Vestirsi never says who Bella is. Velantra's vignette system (Sofia's harbor walks, Eleanor's carry-on) turns names from texture into narrative — our differentiator.
4. **Synthesis on persona architecture:** Vestirsi = one woman, many moments; our draft = many muses. Merge them: the muses are facets of ONE Velantra Woman. She IS Sofia in July, Margot on Tuesday, Eleanor on Friday. Every PDP vignette is the customer recognizing a version of herself → owning multiple bags = collecting her own facets.
5. **Name families for winners:** when the Straw Tote line extends, it's "The Sofia Mini," not a new name. Franchise the name.
6. **Collections as life-moments:** adopt the chapter structure (work / everyday / summer / evening edits), each woman "living" in her chapter.
7. **Title grammar confirmed:** `[Name] — [Descriptor]` keeps emotion + SEO, matches our planned "The Sofia — Woven Straw Tote."
8. **Optional layer 3 later:** a values loop (Vestirsi: women's mental health) completes "by women, for women" — consider once renames land.
