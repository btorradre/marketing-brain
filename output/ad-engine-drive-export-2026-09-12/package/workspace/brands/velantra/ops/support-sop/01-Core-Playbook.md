# Velantra Customer Support: Core Playbook
**Version 1.1 · 2026-08-13 · Supersedes all prior verbal instruction**
*v1.1 change: backorder branch added to 6.1. Sold-out orders are disclosed openly, never described as "processing."*
**Channels in scope:** customerservice@velantrafashion.com · Facebook and Instagram comments · Facebook and Instagram DMs · live chat
**Escalation contact:** Brooks Orradre

> This document was written after an audit of the previous 90 days in which 46% of customers who wrote in never received a reply. Every rule below exists because something specific went wrong. Read section 1 twice.

---

## 1. The six laws

These are not guidelines. Breaking any one of them is a performance issue on the first occurrence.

### Law 1: Zero Unanswered
**Every customer thread receives a human first response within 24 hours. Every one. No exceptions.**

Not "most." Not "the important ones." If a thread is 24 hours old with no reply from us, that is a failure that gets reported, whether or not you have an answer yet. If you do not have the answer, you send a holding reply (template R-00) inside the window and then get the answer. Silence is never an option.

Our own public policy page promises a response within one business day. Every hour past that is a written promise we are breaking.

### Law 2: We Always Have The Last Word
A thread is not finished when you reply. It is finished when the customer's problem is resolved and **the last message in the thread is ours**.

If the customer replied after your last message, the thread is open and it is on your desk. 19% of last quarter's threads were answered once and then dropped with the customer still waiting. Those customers filed disputes.

### Law 3: A Refund Always Beats A Chargeback
A $150 refund costs us $150. A $150 chargeback costs us $150, plus a ~$15 non-refundable fee, plus damage to the dispute ratio that can get the merchant account frozen or terminated. We win only about 24% of disputes once filed.

**You are pre-authorized to refund in full, on the spot, up to $250 per order, without asking anyone.** Do not debate a customer to save a sale. Refund and move on. Above $250 or 3+ orders at once, still resolve same-day and copy Brooks.

### Law 4: The Second Ask Is An Emergency
If a customer has written to us more than once about the same issue, that thread is automatically P1 regardless of topic. Open it with an apology for the delay, resolve it in that same reply, and do not ask them to repeat information they already gave us.

If they have written three or more times: refund in full immediately, let them keep the item, and apologize. Do not investigate first. The cost of the item is smaller than the cost of what they are about to do.

Last quarter, 119 customers had two or more unanswered threads. One wrote "for the 8th (??) time." Those are chargebacks and BBB complaints in the making.

### Law 5: Never Invent
You may state only what you can see in Shopify, in the live policy pages, or in the carrier tracking. If you do not know, say "let me confirm that and come right back to you," then confirm it.

Never invent or guess: delivery dates, product materials, dimensions, interior colors, stock timing, refund timing, or warranty coverage. Never quote a product spec from memory. Check the live product page.

A wrong fact given confidently creates a returned order and a furious customer. If a customer quotes a product detail back to you that you cannot verify, escalate to Brooks rather than confirming or denying it.

### Law 6: Delete The Comment, Never The Customer
Negative comments come off the ad. Comment moderation is brand hygiene and it protects the creative, the CPM, and every cold prospect who lands on the post.

But removing a comment does not remove the customer. She is still angry, she still has an order, and she still has a bank. **Deletion is never a standalone action.** It is always half of a pair:

> **Delete the comment → DM the customer within 1 hour → resolve same day.**

A deletion with no DM behind it is a breach of this SOP and gets reported by name in the daily close-out. That single gap is what produced the current pile-on: comments were removed and the people behind them were never contacted, so they came back angrier and started telling each other it was a scam.

Capture the commenter's name, profile link, and comment text **before** removing anything, because once it is gone you cannot find them. Log every removal in the deletion register.

Two exceptions that stay up and get answered publicly: **buying questions from prospects** (that is revenue) and **positive comments**. Full procedure in `06-Social-Comment-Moderation.md`.

## 2. Who you are talking to

Velantra sells structured leather and canvas handbags and totes at roughly $130 to $250. The customer is predominantly a woman aged 35 to 65 buying a considered, gift-adjacent item for herself. She is not buying a commodity. She waited for it, she pictured it, and she told someone about it.

That shapes everything:

- **A delay is emotional, not logistical.** She may have bought it for a specific trip, event, or weekend. Ask what the deadline is, because it changes what "make it right" means.
- **She is comparing us to brands with excellent service.** Slow, stiff, or evasive replies confirm a fear she already has about buying from a smaller brand online.
- **She is very likely to escalate to a third party.** This audience goes to their bank, the BBB, and the state Attorney General faster than a younger buyer. Treat every unhappy customer as one reply away from doing that.

**Tone:** warm, direct, human, specific. Write like a person at a small company who has the authority to fix it, because you are. Short paragraphs. No corporate hedging. No "we apologize for any inconvenience this may have caused."

**Never:**
- Argue, or explain why the policy is reasonable
- Blame the carrier, the warehouse, or the customer
- Use "unfortunately" as the first word of a reply
- Ask a customer to repeat information already in the thread
- Send a reply that does not contain either a resolution or a specific next step with a date

## 4. Priority and SLA

**Coverage behind this SLA:** the mailbox is worked a minimum of 5 hours a day, Monday through Friday, plus at least one sweep on each weekend day. Every response time below assumes that shift is actually happening. Full rule in section 2 of `03-Daily-Operating-Rhythm.md`.

The clock starts when the customer's message arrives, not when you open it.

| Priority | Ticket type | First response | Resolution |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P0** | Chargeback threatened, "calling my bank," "dispute," BBB, Attorney General, attorney, any legal or regulator language | **1 hour** | Same day, with a refund already issued |
| **P0** | Reply to any chargeback or dispute outreach **we** sent | **1 hour** | Same day |
| **P1** | Cancel request (the window is 1 hour, so speed is the whole game) | **1 hour** | Immediately |
| **P1** | "I don't recognize this charge," duplicate charge, fraud or scam accusation | **2 hours** | Same day |
| **P1** | Any customer contacting us a 2nd+ time on the same issue | **2 hours** | Same day |
| **P2** | Damaged, defective, wrong item, warranty claim | **4 business hours** | 2 business days |
| **P2** | Return or refund request | **4 business hours** | Same day decision |
| **P3** | WISMO, tracking, delivery question, address change | **4 business hours** | Same day |
| **P4** | Product question, sizing, colorway, general | **Same business day** | Same business day |
| **C0** | Public comment saying scam, fraud, report them, or naming BBB / AG | **1 hour** | Public reply with a completed action, then DM |
| **C1** | Public comment with a named order problem | **2 hours** | Public reply, then DM |
| **C3** | Public buying question from a prospect | **2 hours** | Answer it. This is revenue in the queue. |

Comments and DMs are tickets. They count in the daily close-out and they carry the same refund authority. Full procedure in `06-Social-Comment-Moderation.md`.

**The hard floor above all of this: nothing goes 24 hours without a first response, ever, regardless of priority.**

Coverage window: 7 days a week. Weekend coverage may be a single sweep, but the sweep must happen, and P0 and P1 items must still be answered inside their SLA on weekends. Chargebacks do not observe weekends.

## 5. Triage: what to do first every time you open the inbox

Work this order. Do not work the inbox top to bottom by date, that is how the last person drowned.

1. **P0 sweep.** Search the unanswered set for: `chargeback OR dispute OR "my bank" OR "attorney general" OR BBB OR attorney OR fraud OR scam`. Handle every hit before anything else.
2. **Repeat-contact sweep.** Any sender with more than one open thread. Consolidate into one reply, resolve, apologize for the delay.
3. **Aging sweep.** Anything approaching 24 hours with no first response. Holding reply at minimum.
4. **Cancel requests.** Time-critical, fulfillment may still be interceptable.
5. **Everything else**, oldest first.
6. **Fraud alerts.** Work the Disputifier "New Fraud Order Detected: Refund Recommended" queue. These are pre-shipment cancellations that prevent a dispute entirely.

## 6. Decision trees

### 6.1 Where is my order (WISMO): 16% of volume

```
Pull the order in Shopify. Look at real tracking, not the estimate.
   │
   ├─ Unfulfilled, no tracking, the item is out of stock (BACKORDERED)
   │     → Tell her straight, in the first reply: we sold out faster than we
   │       expected, her order is on backorder, and here is when it ships.
   │       Four things in that reply, all of them:
   │         1. The apology, for the delay and for her having to ask.
   │         2. The reassurance: her order is confirmed and held, nothing was
   │            cancelled, and it ships as soon as inventory lands.
   │         3. The most accurate ETA we actually have, from the current PO and
   │            restock schedule. If it is an estimate, say the word "estimate."
   │            Never invent a date to end the conversation (Law 5). If we do not
   │            have a date, say we do not have one yet and give her the date by
   │            which we will.
   │         4. The exit: "If you'd rather not wait, tell me and I'll cancel and
   │            refund you in full today."
   │       Do not describe it as "processing," "in the queue," or "with our
   │       warehouse." She has a charge and no bag, and a vague answer to that is
   │       the exact shape of a chargeback. Template R-23.
   │
   ├─ Tracking moving, inside the stated window
   │     → Send live tracking link + the specific expected date. Done.
   │
   ├─ Tracking moving, past the stated window
   │     → Apologize, give the new date, and OFFER the refund proactively:
   │       "If you'd rather not wait, say the word and I'll refund you in full today."
   │       (This is explicit policy. Offering it before they ask kills the dispute.)
   │
   ├─ No movement 7+ days, or stuck at "label created"
   │     → Treat as lost. Customer's choice, replacement or full refund. Their call, today.
   │
   ├─ Shows delivered, customer says not received
   │     → Ask them to check with household and neighbors and the mail carrier, 24h.
   │       If still missing: this is technically non-returnable per policy, but
   │       REFUND OR RESHIP ANYWAY on a first occurrence. Log it.
   │       Fighting this one costs more than the bag. Escalate only if the same
   │       customer has claimed non-delivery before.
   │
   └─ Customer named a deadline (trip, event, gift)
         → If we cannot hit it, say so immediately and offer the refund now.
           Never let them find out on the deadline.
```

### 6.2 Return or refund request: 27% of volume combined

```
   ├─ Within 30 days, unused, tags on
   │     → Approve immediately. Send the return authorization and instructions
   │       in the SAME reply. Never make them ask twice.
   │
   ├─ Within 30 days, order value under $150, customer is angry or delayed
   │     → Refund now, let them keep it. Cheaper and it ends the risk.
   │
   ├─ Outside 30 days
   │     → Check the delivery date, not the order date. If they contacted us
   │       INSIDE the window and we failed to reply in time, the window is
   │       still open. Our delay never costs the customer their rights.
   │       That situation applies to a large share of the current backlog.
   │       Otherwise: offer store credit or a warranty review if there is a defect.
   │
   ├─ Wants an exchange
   │     → Return plus a fresh order. Send the return authorization and offer
   │       to place the replacement order the moment the return ships,
   │       so she is not waiting twice.
   │
   └─ Damaged / defective / wrong item
         → Photos of item, packaging, and label. Then her choice:
           free replacement or full refund. No return shipping cost to her.
           Do not ask her to ship a damaged bag back at her own expense.
```

### 6.3 Cancel request

```
   ├─ Under 1 hour since order
   │     → Cancel in Shopify and refund in full. Confirm in writing immediately.
   │
   ├─ Over 1 hour, not yet fulfilled
   │     → TRY to intercept anyway. If it works, cancel and refund.
   │       Policy is the floor, not the ceiling.
   │
   └─ Already shipped
         → Explain plainly, apologize, and set it up so it costs her nothing:
           "It's already on its way, so I can't pull it back. Here's what I'll do:
            refuse it at the door or email me when it lands and I'll send you a
            prepaid return label and refund you in full."
           Prepaid label here even though policy says customer-paid. A cancel
           request that turns into a fight becomes a dispute.
```

### 6.4 Chargeback threatened: P0

```
STOP everything else.
   │
   ├─ Refund in full IMMEDIATELY, before you reply. Then tell them it's done.
   │
   ├─ Do not debate, do not explain, do not ask them to reconsider,
   │   do not ask them to return the item first.
   │
   ├─ Reply within the hour with the refund confirmation and the amount.
   │
   └─ Log it: customer, order, amount, what caused it.
       Report every one in the daily close-out so upstream causes get fixed.
```

If a dispute is **already filed**, do not also refund. Refunding a filed dispute means paying twice. Hand it to Brooks and follow the portfolio Anti-Chargeback SOP (`_engine/sops/Anti-Chargeback-SOP.md`) for representment evidence.

### 6.5 "I don't recognize this charge" / fraud or scam accusation

Usually the statement descriptor, not actual fraud. Reply fast with proof:

Order number, date, items, amount, shipping address, tracking link, and the descriptor that appears on their statement. Close with an unconditional refund offer. This single fast reply prevents a "fraudulent transaction" dispute, which is the hardest category to win.

If they claim the brand is a scam: do not argue the point, do not defend the company. Refund, apologize, and give them the physical address and a real contact. Defensive replies to a scam accusation get screenshotted.

### 6.6 Regulator or legal contact (BBB, Attorney General, attorney)

P0. Never reply substantively on your own. Acknowledge inside the hour with a neutral holding reply (R-09), refund the customer in full same day, and escalate to Brooks immediately with the full thread. Brooks handles the formal response.

## 7. Escalate to Brooks (same day, do not sit on it)

- Anything over $250, or 3+ orders from one customer at once
- Any BBB, Attorney General, attorney, press, or legal contact
- Any dispute already filed with the bank
- Any customer claiming injury, safety issue, or product defect that could hurt someone
- Any product-fact question you cannot verify from the live site
- Any influencer, creator, wholesale, or partnership inquiry (route it out, it is not a support ticket)
- Any day the unanswered count rises instead of falling
- Any pattern: 3+ customers reporting the same defect, wrong item, or shipping failure in a week

Escalating is never penalized. Sitting on something is.

## 8. What is out of scope for this inbox

The support mailbox has been absorbing work that is not support. Route these out on sight, with a one-line handoff:

| Type | Route to |
|---|---|
| Influencer / creator partnership negotiation | Partnerships, not support. Forward and drop. |
| Wholesale or stockist inquiries | Brooks |
| Press | Brooks |
| Job applications | Brooks |
| Supplier and 3PL correspondence | Brooks / ops |

Do not negotiate a partnership deal from the support queue. Last quarter, some of the longest threads in the mailbox were 15 to 24 message creator negotiations running on the same clock as refund requests.

## 9. Definition of done

A thread may be marked resolved only when **all** of the following are true:

1. The customer's actual question was answered, not deflected.
2. Any action we promised has been **done**, not scheduled. Refund issued, label sent, replacement ordered.
3. The last message in the thread is ours.
4. The thread is labeled with its outcome (see `03-Daily-Operating-Rhythm.md`).

If you promised a follow-up (a restock date, a warranty review, a return arriving), the thread is **not** resolved. It goes in the Waiting queue with a due date, and you own chasing it. The customer never chases us.

## 10. The one-line version

**Answer everyone inside 24 hours, refund freely, never let a customer ask twice, and always have the last word.**
