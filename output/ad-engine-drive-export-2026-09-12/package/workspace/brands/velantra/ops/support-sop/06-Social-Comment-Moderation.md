# Velantra Support: Social Comments and DMs
**Version 2.0 · 2026-08-10 · Applies to Facebook and Instagram ad comments, organic comments, and DMs**
**Tool:** Brandwise (app.brandwise.ai)

> Written during a live pile-on: 606 comments in queue, 57 flagged Needs Reply, 55 DMs.

---

## 1. Law 6: Delete the comment, never the customer

**Comment moderation is brand hygiene. It is not a resolution.**

Negative comments come off the ad. That is the policy and it protects the creative, the CPM, and every cold prospect who lands on the post. But a deleted comment does not delete the customer. She is still angry, she still has an order, and she still has a bank.

So deletion is never a standalone action. It is always half of a pair:

```
DELETE the comment  →  DM the customer within 1 hour  →  RESOLVE same day
```

**A deletion with no DM behind it is a violation of this SOP.** That is what produced the current thread. Comments were removed and the people behind them were never contacted, so they came back and posted five more times, angrier, and started telling each other it was a scam. The deletion was not the problem. The silence after it was.

### The rule in one line

> **Take the comment down, then take care of the person. Every time, no exceptions, within the hour.**

### What gets deleted with no follow-up needed

- Bot and spam comments, crypto and dropship link spam
- Slurs, harassment, sexual content
- Competitor promotion
- Doxxing, anyone posting a third party's personal information

### What gets deleted **and always triggers a DM**

- "My order hasn't arrived"
- "No one answers customer service"
- "This is a scam" / "do not buy" / "I've been taken"
- "Smaller than advertised" / quality complaints
- Anything from a person with a real order

Every one of these is a customer with money at stake and a bank they can call. Removing the comment buys us quiet on the ad. The DM is what actually prevents the chargeback.

### What never gets deleted

- **Buying questions from prospects.** "Are they for women or men too?" is revenue. Answer it publicly, leave it up. A visible answered question sells the next reader.
- **Positive comments and tags.** Reply and react. These are what a cold visitor should find.

## 2. Protecting the customer's data

Deleting is done through Brandwise **after** the customer's identity is captured, never before.

Before you remove anything, record: name, profile link, the full comment text, timestamp, and the ad or post it was on. Once it is deleted you cannot recover it, and if you delete first you have lost the only way to find that person.

This is what the deletion register (section 6) is for.

---

## 3. The DM: this is where the experience is won

The DM is the whole point of the policy. It should be better than what the customer would have gotten in public, not worse.

**Send within 1 hour of deleting the comment. No exceptions.**

### What a good DM contains

1. Their name, and a specific acknowledgment of what they actually said
2. A concession with no defensiveness and no policy language
3. A **completed action**, not an offer to look into it
4. The truth about what went wrong, in one sentence
5. A real way to reach a human

### Worked example, replying to the actual comment in the thread

> Hi Bonne, I saw your comment and I want to sort this properly rather than trade messages in public.
>
> You ordered on July 24 and the tracking has been stuck at "info received" ever since, which means the carrier never scanned it. Nobody got back to you when you emailed either. Both of those are on us, not you.
>
> I've refunded your order in full today, $[amount], back to your original card. It'll show up in 5 to 10 business days and there's nothing to send back. If the parcel ever does turn up, please keep it.
>
> You were also right about the phone number on our site, it was a placeholder that was never updated and it's being fixed. I'm at customerservice@velantrafashion.com and I'll answer you personally from here.
>
> Sorry it took this long.

That message does the job the deletion cannot. It refunds her before she calls her bank, it concedes the two things she was factually right about, and it gives her a human. She has no reason left to file a dispute or write a review.

### Never send in a DM

| Never | Why |
|---|---|
| "We're sorry you feel that way" | Reads as contempt |
| "Per our policy..." | She is not on our side about policy |
| "We are a legitimate business" | Amplifies the accusation. Prove it with a refund instead. |
| "Check your spam folder" | Blames the customer |
| "Please allow 5 to 7 business days for a response" | She already waited. Answer now. |
| An identical copy-paste sent to eight people | They talk to each other in these threads |
| Any denial of the word "scam" | Never repeat the accusation back. Refund and move on. |

## 4. Triage order

| Tier | What it is | Response | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **C0** | "Scam," "fraud," "report them," "calling my bank," BBB, Attorney General, attorney | **1 hour** | Capture, delete, DM, refund same day. Escalate legal or regulator mentions to Brooks. |
| **C1** | Named order problem: not received, not shipped, wrong item, smaller than advertised | **2 hours** | Capture, delete, DM, resolve same day |
| **C2** | Piling on with no order behind it | **4 hours** | Capture and delete. DM if they have any order history. |
| **C3** | **Buying question from a prospect** | **2 hours** | **Leave it up.** Answer publicly. This is revenue. |
| **C4** | Positive comment or tag | Same day | Leave up, reply, react |

**C3 is not the lowest priority.** At the time of writing there were genuine pre-purchase questions sitting unanswered in a queue full of complaints. Those are the people still willing to buy.

## 5. Repeat commenters

One person posting the same complaint eight times is one unresolved problem, not eight comments to remove.

1. Search their name across every post and ad before doing anything.
2. Capture and remove all of them in one pass.
3. Send **one** DM that resolves it completely, with the refund already processed.
4. If they post again after that, remove and reply once in the DM thread. Do not start a new conversation each time.

If someone keeps posting after a full refund and a genuine apology, escalate to Brooks. At that point it is a reputation matter, not a support ticket.

## 6. The deletion register (mandatory)

Every removed comment gets logged the same day. This is the control that makes the policy safe: it makes it impossible for a customer to be silenced and forgotten, which is the exact failure that created this situation.

| Date | Name | Profile link | Comment text | Ad / post | DM sent | Resolution | Refunded |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Rules:
- **A row with a blank "DM sent" at end of shift is a breach.** It gets reported in the close-out by name.
- Spam and bot deletions are logged as one aggregate count, not row by row.
- The register is reviewed weekly. Any pattern in it (same complaint, same product, same shipping lane) goes to Brooks.

## 7. Prevention: making the comments stop being written

Removing comments manages the symptom. These are the things that stop them existing, and they are what "a positive experience" actually requires.

### Proactive contact, before she has to ask

- **Delay email at day 7.** If an order has not had a carrier scan within 7 days, we email her before she notices. Template R-21 in the Response Library. Say the new date, and offer the full refund unprompted.
- **Stall alert at day 10.** No scan by day 10 means the parcel is treated as lost. She gets a choice of reship or refund without asking for it.
- **Delivery confirmation.** When tracking shows delivered, she gets a short "it should be with you" note. Cheap, and it kills a whole category of WISMO.

Every one of the comments in the current thread describes a customer who waited in silence. Not one of them describes a customer who was told what was happening.

### Transparency at the point of sale

- **Honest ship windows on the PDP and at checkout.** If fulfillment currently runs 14 to 21 days, say 14 to 21 days. Every day between the promise and the reality converts directly into a comment.
- **A working phone number.** See section 9.
- **Creative that matches the product.** Several independent commenters say the bag is smaller than the ads suggest. If that is true, no support script fixes it and it produces these comments permanently.

### Fast, real answers

- Everything in `01-Core-Playbook.md`. A one-hour reply to an unhappy customer means she never writes the comment in the first place.
- 46% of emails went unanswered last quarter. That number, not the comment section, is the root cause.

## 8. Paid spend tripwires

Every one of these comments is attached to a **running ad**, so cold traffic is being bought and delivered into it.

Tell Brooks the same hour if any of these fire:

| Tripwire | Action |
|---|---|
| 10+ negative comments on one active creative in a day | Recommend pausing that creative |
| 3+ people using "scam," "fraud," or "report" on one ad in a day | Pause and escalate immediately |
| Comment volume on one post exceeds 100 in a day | Staffing alert |
| Any comment naming BBB, an Attorney General, an attorney, or press | Escalate immediately |
| Deletion register shows 20+ removals in a day | The ad is manufacturing complaints faster than we can resolve them. Escalate. |

Worth knowing when reading ad metrics: **Meta counts negative feedback whether or not the comment is later removed.** Hiding and deleting clean up what a prospect sees, which is why we do it, but they do not repair delivery, CPM, or account quality. Only fewer angry customers do that. This account was already banned once on July 27, so the account-health angle is real.

## 9. Two claims in the comments that are factually correct

Both are quoted repeatedly as scam evidence, and both are fixes rather than scripts.

**The phone number is fake.** The contact page lists `+1 (555) 012-3478`. That is a placeholder from the reserved 555-01XX fictional range and it has never rung. Customers are literally right, and it is the strongest single piece of evidence in the thread. Replace it with a number that works, or remove the field and lead with email.

**The site contradicts itself on returns.** The banner promises "30-day postage paid returns" while the refund policy says return shipping is the customer's cost. Until that is resolved, support honors the banner and sends a prepaid label. Never argue a contradiction that is live on our own site.

## 10. Daily rhythm for social

Add to the daily close-out in `03-Daily-Operating-Rhythm.md`:

```
SOCIAL: [date]
  Comments in                    [n]
  Buying questions answered      [n]
  Positive comments engaged      [n]
  Comments removed (customer)    [n]
  Comments removed (spam/bots)   [n]
  DMs sent to removed commenters [n]   ← must equal customer removals
  Removals with NO DM yet        [n]   ← must be 0, name them
  DMs in / answered              [n] / [n]
  Refunds from social            [n] / $[total]
  Creative flagged for pause     [name or none]
```

The line that matters is **removals with no DM**. If that is ever above zero, the policy has degraded back into the thing that caused this.

## 12. DM templates

>
> You're right to be frustrated. [One sentence of what actually went wrong.] I've refunded order #[number] in full today, $[amount], back to your original card. It'll take 5 to 10 business days and there's nothing to send back.

> I'm at customerservice@velantrafashion.com and I'll answer you personally. Sorry it took this long.

>

> Hi [Name], you emailed us and nobody got back to you. That's on us and I'm sorry it took a comment to get a reply.
>

> I'm passing your feedback on the photos to the team as well, because if the images are overselling the size that's ours to fix.

> If you have an order number or the email you checked out with, send it over and I'll sort it today. We're a real shop at 259 Spring Street, Mooresville NC, and if something went wrong I'll fix it.
