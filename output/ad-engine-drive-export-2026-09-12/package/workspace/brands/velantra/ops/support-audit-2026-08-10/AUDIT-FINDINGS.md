# Velantra Customer Support Audit
**Mailbox:** customerservice@velantrafashion.com
**Window analyzed:** 2026-05-12 to 2026-08-10 (90 days)
**Sample:** 3,152 threads / 7,196 messages pulled live from Gmail, plus the 580-thread open backlog triage
**Verdict:** Systemic coverage failure. Roughly half of all customers who wrote in never received a reply.

## 2. The failure was coverage, not writing

This matters for hiring the replacement, so it should be said plainly: the replies she did send were good.

- 1,728 distinct reply openings across 2,045 replies. Near-zero blind copy-paste.
- Templates were personalized by name and situation.
- Tone was warm, apologetic, on-brand.

The job failed on volume, speed, and follow-through. She could write one good email. She could not run a queue. Those are different skills, and the hiring bar for the replacement has to test the second one.

Read that top to bottom and the whole story is there.

**Three consecutive weeks (May 18 to Jun 7) with a 0% answer rate.** 143 customers wrote in and got literally one reply between them. Nobody upstream noticed for three weeks. That is not only her failure. There was no tripwire, no daily number, no report. The business had no way to know the support function had stopped.

**Then the ramp broke her.** Volume went from 31 threads/week in early May to 433 in early August, a 14x increase. Output peaked at 459 replies in the week of July 20 and then fell off. From July 20 onward the answer rate goes 81% → 58% → 21% → 1% while volume keeps climbing. She was underwater from roughly July 27 and never surfaced.

The capacity ceiling was about 450 replies/week, call it 90/day. Current demand is 433 new threads/week plus follow-ups, which is roughly 4,160 inbound messages per 90 days, about 46/day of raw inbound and considerably more once multi-turn threads are counted. One person at that ceiling cannot cover current volume with any margin.

## 6. Mid-thread abandonment

431 threads (18.9%) got a reply and were then dropped with the customer waiting. These are worse than the never-answered ones, because the customer has proof we are alive and choosing not to answer.

Examples from the abandoned pile:

- 16 messages: *"That is not an option. Your phone number is not a real number. This is really frustrating. Can you please file..."*
- 15 messages: *"I have still not been refunded for my order that was never sent/received. And I see that you are back at advertising..."*
- 16 messages: *"okay thank you because I really need these for the first weekend in august"* (a promise made, then silence)

No thread was ever formally closed. There was no definition of "done," so nothing was ever done.

Disputes ran 1 to 6 per week through May and June. From July 20 they run 14, 14, 20. That is the exact window in which the answer rate fell from 81% to 21% and the backlog aged past 30 days. Per the portfolio Anti-Chargeback SOP, every dispute costs the order value plus a roughly $15 non-refundable fee plus dispute-ratio damage, and our historical representment win rate is about 24%. Nearly all of these were preventable with a same-day reply and a refund.

## 8. Structural problems that were not her fault

Worth separating, because the replacement will hit the same walls if these are not fixed.

1. **No visibility.** Three weeks at 0% passed unnoticed. No daily number, no dashboard, no alert.
2. **Single point of failure.** One person, no backup, no declared absence protocol. When she stopped, support stopped.
3. **The inbox does double duty.** Influencer and partnership negotiation threads (the @moments.by.nastia, @olivia.demb, @mycozyhouse_ Boat Tote partnership threads, some 24 messages long) run through the same mailbox as refund requests. Two different jobs on two different clocks competing for the same attention.
4. **Volume grew 14x with no staffing change.** Demand went from 31 to 433 threads/week between May and August. Headcount stayed at one.
5. **No definition of done.** Nothing distinguished a resolved thread from an abandoned one, so 431 threads sat in a state nobody could see.
6. **Upstream defects fed the queue.** 16% WISMO and 16% returns is a fulfillment and expectation-setting signal, not purely a support signal. Support was absorbing the symptom.

## 9. What the new SOP had to fix

Each rule in the new playbook traces to a finding above.

| Finding | Rule it produced |
|---|---|
| 46.4% never answered | Zero-Unanswered Law: every thread gets a first reply inside 24h, no exceptions, counted daily |
| 3-week blackout unnoticed | Mandatory daily close-out report to Brooks, plus automatic tripwires |
| Single point of failure | Absence protocol, named backup, 48h notice |
| Median 39.7h vs published 1 business day | Tiered SLA with a 24h hard floor that beats the published promise |
| 431 abandoned mid-thread | Definition of Done and open-loop labels, we always have the last word |
| 119 customers asking 2+ times | Repeat-contact auto-escalation |
| 25 aged chargeback threats | P0 same-hour, refund-first, no debate |
| Our own chargeback outreach ignored | Outbound-initiated threads carry the same SLA as inbound |
| Partnership mail mixed with support | Inbox split and routing rules |
| 433 threads/wk vs 450/wk ceiling | Documented capacity math and a staffing tripwire |
| Fabrication risk on product specs | Never-Invent Law, cite live policy and Shopify only |
