## What the reference is

A woman with a phone walks a lawn car show and asks well-dressed women "What are you wearing today?" Each one runs her outfit head to toe by brand. The camera tilts down to whatever she names: belt, shoes, bag, rings, watch, necklace. The interviewer says "Stunning" and "You look phenomenal" and moves on. Six women in under two minutes. Overcast light, vintage cars behind them, hats everywhere, no music, no mic in shot. The bag gets its own front-on close-up every single time.

Why it works: the rundown is a flex the viewer is allowed to enjoy. Nobody is selling. The bag lands as one item in a life that is clearly already well dressed.

## What ours is

Same lawn, same question, same tilt-down grammar, same close-ups. Three women, one Velantra bag each. Every other item she names is clothing, shoes or jewelry from a house that does not make handbags. The only bag brand ever spoken is Velantra.

**The one structural change, and it is law-forced:** she names the bag by 0:06, not last. House script formula (Brooks, 8/21) puts "This is the [Name] from Velantra" inside the first six seconds. The reference's bag close-up sits mid-segment anyway, so the shot order barely moves.

**The interviewer never speaks.** The question and the follow-ups are burned title cards, same ruling as the August street series. The reference's voice-over-the-phone interviewer would be a second voice in a single-pass generation and breaks the one-speaker lock. Cards: "What are you wearing today?" · "And the rest?" · "Show us the rings" / "And the watch?" / "Show us the arm".
