05B — The Beachhead, Segmented

Four doors into the 48M. One proposition.

An angle targets a shared situation and emotion, not a demographic. The segments overlap — fear and shame co-occur constantly — so they are doors, not partitions. Every door resolves to the same line: “It’s just Tylenol. In a gummy.”

Angle 1 · Convenience
48M
the full beachhead
Ease → relief

Headache hits, no water — desk, car, flight, gym. The universal, low-threat door: weekly dosing defines the beachhead, and no-water moments come with it. Nothing to confess.

The pill sticks at the back of her throat; her body fights it. 57% of strugglers have actually choked on medication. The loudest lane — ~70% of classifiable VoC voice.

Angle 3 · Adult Shame
~17M
the workaround-runner (8–25M)
Shame → vindication

Kids’ grape medicine, crushed pills, a hidden system. 53–59% modify their meds; fewer than 1 in 4 have ever told a health professional. The deepest lane — she DMs, she doesn’t comment.

Role · Conversion depth
Angle 4 · Untreated Pain
~10M
the avoider (range 5–12M)
Defeat → resolution

Skips the dose and suffers through: 14% delay, 8% skip, 4% quit their meds entirely over swallowing. Nearly silent online — and already paying the cost of no solution.

Role · Highest intent
The Proprietary Overlay — VoC, n=4,095

Across 4,095 scraped consumer comments, fear outruns shame 2.6 : 1 by voice volume. Launch weighting: Fear > Shame > Untreated Pain for hook volume; Convenience for reach. Registers stay pure — a shame hook never blends into a convenience vignette.

The Emotional Spine — Every Ad, Every Door

Recognition (“that’s me”) → Relief (“I’m not the only one”) → Revelation (“wait, this exists?”) → Liberation. The hook’s only job is Recognition in three seconds.
