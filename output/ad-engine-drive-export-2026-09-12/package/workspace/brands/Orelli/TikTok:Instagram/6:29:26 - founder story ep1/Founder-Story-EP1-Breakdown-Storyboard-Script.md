### The structural laws this reel runs on

1. **Hook = sacrifice + absurdly specific product + open loop**, all inside 7 seconds. "Ditched our internships" (stakes), "Cheetos out of grass-fed beef" (specificity), "here's how it's going" (serialized loop).
2. **Every VO claim gets receipt footage.** "Fell asleep at the gate" cuts to literal footage of him asleep at the gate. The proof IS the entertainment.
3. **When no footage exists, cut to a meme or a single-word black card.** Never generic stock B-roll. The money beat becomes a Breaking Bad meme. Abstract words become black cards.
4. **The product appears about 6 times and is never pitched.** No benefits, no offer, no link. It rides the dash, gets fanned at the camera, gets shared with friends. A prop inside the friendship.
5. **Fails outnumber wins 2 to 1 before the payoff.** Missed flight, wrong deck, THEN the win. Vulnerability is the retention engine, and it earns the win.
6. **Reflection before CTA.** One honest line about how hard it is, then the follow CTA framed as joining a story already in motion: "follow us, two best friends who have no idea what they're doing."
7. **Cut cadence 2 to 4 seconds.** One line = one visual idea. Emotional beats (memes, text cards) reset attention every few seconds.

## 2. What we take from the other two references

**Eskiin ad (Wes + Maddie, filtered showerhead). Take the first 10 seconds only.**
- Hook shape: "Watch this, if you don't know what chlorine and water parasites are doing to your hair and skin." An imperative plus a personal stake for the viewer.
- Then instant humanization: "I'm Wes. This is my sister Maddie. Never set out to build a beauty brand. Just trying to fix our own problem."
- We borrow the intro CADENCE (name, name, one-line origin) but reverse their origin trope. Eskiin plays the accidental founder. Ours is the opposite by Brooks's call (7/29): we always dreamed of building this, since we were kids. Never write the "never planned to start a company" line for Orelli.
- We stop borrowing there. From 0:35 on it's a hard product sell (15-stage filtration, triple discount, 45% off). They can sell because the product exists. We can't and shouldn't. Ours stays a story.

**TikTok @ellieamyyx, "trying to swallow tablets core." Take the hook visual.**
- Dim warm bedroom, harsh phone light, front camera propped up, water bottle in hand, pill visibly in mouth, head thrown all the way back, gag, hands over the face. 18 seconds, no dialogue, just caption and audio.
- Why it works: pure recognition content. The comment section is our avatar tagging herself. This exact visual is our episode 1 cold open.

## 3. Story positioning ideas

**Idea 1 (recommended): "The 100 million secret."**
Hook on the struggle visual. Brooks reveals the number nobody talks about: 4 in 10 adults struggle to swallow pills, over 100 million people. The pain relief aisle has known for 40 years and its best answer was a smoother coating on the same hard pill. So he's building the fix and documenting the whole thing. Compliance-clean (no invented personal struggle, no product claims). Infinitely serializable: every development milestone becomes an episode (flavor lab, first sample, MOQ sticker shock, preorder day).

**Idea 2: "We're building it for our sisters." (CONFIRMED 7/29, now in the script.)**
The discovery moment is real: both founders' sisters struggle to swallow pills. This is the relatability engine, the same mechanism that makes BUFFS work. BUFFS's ICP is college kids watching two college kids build a company, so the audience latches onto the belief "that could be us." Our ICP is Maya, the adult who gags on pills, and she latches onto the sisters: "that's me." The founders are the builders, the sisters are the audience's mirror. It also mirrors Eskiin's emotional core ("This is my sister, Maddie... losing her hair fast") without their accidental-founder trope.

**Idea 3: "Brooks and Bennett vs the medicine aisle."**
The duo dynamic, the BUFFS spine. Two outsiders with zero pharma background building an actual OTC drug: the FDA-registered manufacturing calls, the paperwork, the flavor tests, the rejections. Both founders on camera from the first frame. The buddy dynamic is the follow-button hook, exactly like BUFFS's two-best-friends premise.

The script below fuses all three: the sisters give it heart (Idea 2), the number gives it scale (Idea 1), and the duo gives it a series premise (Idea 3). **The series is always Brooks AND Bennett, never a solo-founder story.**

## 5. Manufacturing story (INTEGRATED 7/29, built on the white-label truth)

**The truth (Brooks, 7/29):** we do not own the formula. We're effectively white-labeling from a manufacturer. Many manufacturers said no during the search; we found one that was already in the process of developing this. Brooks approved "a little exaggeration," which lives in the DRAMA (the phone montage, the "No. No. Not possible." rhythm), never in the FACTS.

**The story frame that makes the truth an asset: the partnership was the plan, not the fallback.** (Reframed 7/29 after Brooks's authority note.) Medicine demands authority, so the beat can never sound like "we couldn't do it ourselves so we found people who could." The authoritative version of the same truth: a real medicine HAS to be made by a real drug manufacturer, so the founders' job was always to find the right one and partner with them. The search (manufacturer after manufacturer saying no) stays, because the no's discredit the problem's difficulty, not the founders. The resolution is a deliberate selection, followed by a clean division of labor that keeps the founders in command: their scientists lead the chemistry, we drive the standard, the brand, and distribution. The earlier self-deprecating "we're 20 years old / we weren't about to invent a medicine ourselves" beat was CUT for discrediting the founders; humility lives in captions and lighter episodes, never in the credibility beat of a medicine ad.

**Hard truth boundaries for this block (and every future episode):**
1. Never claim we invented, formulated, or own the formula. No "we built it from scratch," no "our formula," no lab-coat cosplay. "Partner" is the load-bearing honest word, framed as the deliberate plan.
2. The rejection count stays vague ("manufacturer after manufacturer") unless Brooks wants to state a real number.
3. Never "FDA approved." "FDA-registered drug manufacturer" is the allowed phrasing (registration is a fact), pending legal confirmation that the partner's facility is actually FDA-registered.
4. **Naming the manufacturer on camera needs two confirmations from Brooks:** the agreement allows it publicly, and the exact name to use. Until then, the descriptor "an FDA-registered drug manufacturer" carries the beat on its own.

**Where it sits in the script:** between "So we went all in" and the product reveal, in Eskiin's cadence (obstacle → rejections → the yes → what we bring). See the V4 VO in section 4.

**Future beat this sets up:** the sister test stays in the bank for EP2+ (first real samples, judged by the sisters on camera). It was cut from EP1 since samples don't exist in hand yet; EP1's manufacturing story ends on the partnership and the mission instead.

## 6. Production board: hybrid talking-head + B-roll (shot by shot)

**Format:** the spine is the two of you talking to camera at one home location (kitchen table or couch, phone on a cheap tripod, natural light). B-roll overlays roughly 70% of the runtime; the edit returns to your faces at every emotional pivot: the bridge, the intro names, "that was it," "that was always the plan," and the close. VO stays one continuous voice-memo take by Brooks; shoot the talking-head lines spoken for real so the editor can switch between sync sound and VO freely.

**Caption system (stolen from both references):**
- Eskiin chips: 1-4 word caption chips cutting every 1-2 seconds. WHITE chip = default narration. RED chip = villain words ("bitter," "chalky," "Not possible."). YELLOW chip = payoff words ("You just chew it.", "later this year").
- BUFFS black cards: full-frame black, one white word, for the hardest punches ("GAG." "PANIC." "ALL IN").
- BUFFS meme rule: when a moment has no footage, a meme or text card beats generic stock. Use sparingly here since medicine needs authority: max 1-2 meme inserts, only in the search/rejection stretch, never near the product.

## 7. Compliance guardrails (read before shooting)

1. **Announcement framing only.** No "I tried it," no taste claims, no "it works," no before/after. The taste gate has not passed and there is no finished product in hand. Same 16 CFR §255.1 standard we hold creators to.
2. **The sisters' story must stay true and stay origin-story.** Brooks confirmed the sisters' struggle (7/29), so it's in. Keep every detail real (swap my suggested specifics for what they actually did), get both sisters' consent before their faces or photos appear, and do not put product opinions in their mouths ("she loves them," "they work for her") until they've genuinely tried the finished product. The founders' own arc stays as-is: dream first, no invented personal pill struggle for Brooks or Bennett (standing decision 7/25).
3. **OTC claims cap:** relieves pain, reduces fever. Nothing about superiority, speed, or safety versus pills.
4. **Don't name Tylenol on camera** in episode 1. "The pill in your cabinet" carries the meaning. Legal check pending on the comparison line.
7. **Never claim we invented, formulated, or own the formula.** We white-label from a manufacturer that was already developing this. "Team up with the people who actually could" is the honest load-bearing phrase; the drama lives in the search, never in fake ownership. Full boundaries in section 5.
5. **Recreate the hook clip.** Never repost the original creator's footage without a license.
6. **Numbers to use:** "4 in 10 adults" and "over 100 million people" (our 104M figure), "40 years" for the category-stagnation beat. All verified in the strategy docs.
