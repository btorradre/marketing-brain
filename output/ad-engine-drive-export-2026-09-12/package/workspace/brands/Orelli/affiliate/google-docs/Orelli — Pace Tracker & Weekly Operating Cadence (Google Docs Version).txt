ORELLI — PACE TRACKER & WEEKLY OPERATING CADENCE

The instrument that tells you every Friday whether the program is on pace, and exactly what to do if it isn't. Ten weeks, four gates, one 30-minute weekly meeting.

Program clock: Week 0 is the week of July 28, 2026. The preorder opens in Week 4, on August 24. The window closes in Week 9, on October 4.

Week 0 — July 28 to August 2. Phase: Foundation. The one thing that must happen: preorder page built, the payment-processor conversation held, legal engaged, Kalodata seat opened.

Week 1 — August 3 to August 9. Phase: Foundation. Asset Pack shot and assembled, affiliate app live with codes, all five documents finalized. GATE A.

Week 2 — August 10 to August 16. Phase: Source. Sourcing engine running, Lane A's 30 offers out by hand, first 150 DMs.

Week 3 — August 17 to August 23. Phase: Source. 200 more DMs, onboard signers same-day. GATE B: 25 signed.

Week 5 — August 31 to September 6. Phase: Open. Read the first real conversion rate, fix the page if needed. GATE C: conversion at 1% or better.

Week 6 — September 7 to September 13. Phase: Scale. Push repeat-posting hard, kill dead angles, milestone bonuses start firing.

Week 7 — September 14 to September 20. Phase: Scale. Taper new outreach, shift to activating signed-but-silent creators.

Week 8 — September 21 to September 27. Phase: Scale. Last activation push, assemble the raise data pack.

Week 9 — September 28 to October 4. Phase: Close. Scarcity close ("founding price ends Friday"). GATE D: extend or close.

3. THE FRIDAY MEETING — 30 MINUTES, SAME FIVE QUESTIONS

Every Friday, 30 minutes, Brooks plus whoever runs sourcing. Do not let it become a status update.

1. Are we on pace? Fill in the column. Circle every red cell.
2. What's the bottleneck this week? Exactly one. Run it through Section 4.
3. Which angle is winning? Rank the four by preorders per post. Reallocate next week's briefs toward the winner.
4. Who are our top 3 and bottom 10 creators? Send the top 3 a personal message from Brooks. Nudge or retire the bottom 10.
5. What decision is due? Check the gates in Section 5.

After the meeting, update the pace table, the tracker's status column, and the running raise data pack.


4. DIAGNOSTIC — IF X IS OFF PACE, DO Y

Work top-down. The first item that's red is your actual problem; the ones below it are usually symptoms.

Creators sourced is red. The real problem is probably that nobody is doing the work, or the filters are too tight. This week: loosen the GMV floor to $500, add Lane 4 categories. If it's a capacity problem, hire the VA today — this is not a founder task.

DM reply rate under 15%. The real problem is that the opener is about the brand rather than about her. This week: re-read the first line of 10 sent DMs. If any leads with "Orelli" instead of her own words, that's the bug. Quote her struggle evidence verbatim. Check you're not sending links in touch 1.

Reply-to-signed under 30%. The offer isn't clearing the four negatives. This week: lead with the two things creators actually value, which are 20%-locked-for-life and free product off the first run. Then push more volume into Lane A paid, at $150 flat — money resolves this objection instantly.

Signed-to-posted under 45%. This is onboarding logistics, not willingness, and it is almost always fixable. This week: are codes issued within the hour? Did she get her personal one-pager, or a folder? Offer to write her first three lines — this single intervention recovers a large share of non-posters. Tighten the bounty deadline to 7 days.

Posted-to-repeat under 40%. She doesn't know repeat posting is expected or paid. This week: message every single-post creator personally with her actual numbers — "your video did 14K, here's what number two usually does." Make the milestone ladder visible. Consider a 3-post bonus.

Click-through under 0.5%. The CTA is buried or absent. This week: is the link comment actually pinned? Is she saying "search Orelli" out loud? Audit 10 live posts — expect to find several with no pinned comment at all.

Conversion rate under 1.0%. The problem is the page, not the creators. This week: stop adding creators. Fix the page — is the 2-pack default-selected? Is the ship date reassuring rather than alarming? Is the refund promise above the fold? Add the willingness-to-pay survey's own respondents as social proof. Re-test for a week before scaling.

Bundle take under 40%. Page defaults and creator language. This week: default-select the 2-pack. Reframe as "one for home, one for your bag." Add the per-bottle price. Brief creators to say "I got two."

Preorders red but everything above is green. Traffic is fine; the offer isn't. This week: genuinely re-examine whether $30 four months early is the ask. Fallback: add a free waitlist path beside the paid preorder so the traffic isn't wasted.


5. THE FOUR GATES — EACH IS A REAL STOP

5.1 GATE A — end of Week 1, August 9: can we open at all?

Checklist: preorder page live, tested, with ship-date and refund language on it · legal has reviewed preorder terms and the competitor-comparison assets · the payment processor has been told about the preorder model and hasn't objected · affiliate app live, codes and links generating · Asset Pack complete and phone-tested · all five creator documents finalized · tracker built with the full schema.

If it fails: delay Phase 1 by a week. Do not start outreach without the pack. A creator who signs and gets nothing for four days is a creator who never posts.

5.2 GATE B — end of Week 3, August 23: 25 creators signed

Checklist: 25 signed agreements with compliance acknowledged · at least 8 from Lane A, the paid proven sellers · all 25 onboarded with code, link, one-pager, and assigned angle.

If it fails: push the preorder open date by one week. Opening to 8 creators wastes the launch moment and produces a conversion-rate read too noisy to act on.

5.3 GATE C — end of Week 5, September 6: conversion at 1% or better

The most important gate. By now there are roughly 6,000 clicks, which is enough to trust.

At 1.5% or better: green. Scale hard, spend the remaining outreach budget.
Between 1.0% and 1.5%: proceed, but fix the page in parallel. Every point of conversion is worth more than ten creators.
Under 1.0%: stop recruiting. Take one week of page work. Re-read at Week 6. Adding creators to a page that doesn't convert burns roster goodwill we need in December.

5.4 GATE D — Week 9, October 4: extend or close?

300 preorders or more: close on schedule, bank the scarcity moment, roll into launch prep.
Between 150 and 300: extend to November 1 with the same roster. Marginal cost is near zero and creators are already onboarded.
Under 150: close, and reframe the raise around conversion rate, CAC, and roster rather than volume (see the Proposal, Section 2.1). Do not extend a program that isn't working — you'll burn the roster before December, and that roster is worth more than the preorders.


6. THE PARALLEL GATE THAT ISN'T OURS, BUT BLOCKS EVERYTHING

The taste gate must pass by October 15. It is owned outside this program (master strategy Section 11), but this program is fully exposed to it.

Formulation round 1 acceptable, checked by Week 4. If it fails: run a second round, with no impact on the preorder yet.
Blind panel at 80% or better "would buy again," checked by Week 7. If it fails: warn creators the timeline may move.
Final go/no-go, October 15. If it fails: refund every preorder in full, with a personal note from Brooks, and keep the roster intact for a later launch.

Standing rule: preorder cash sits in a separate account and is not spent before October 15. Cap preorders at 2,000 units — the number we can refund without insolvency.

If the gate fails, the roster is still the asset. Handle the refund personally and well and most creators will stay; handle it badly and we lose the distribution asset that was the real point of the whole exercise.


7. THE RAISE DATA PACK — ASSEMBLE AS YOU GO, NOT AT THE END

Add to this weekly starting Week 4. By Week 9 it should be finished, not started.

Preorder conversion rate on cold creator traffic, from Shopify. Why it lands: people paid $30 for a product that doesn't exist, four months early, with no reviews. It is the strongest demand signal available pre-revenue.

Marginal CAC, measured — commission divided by customers. Why it lands: $7.50–8.20 against $15–25 for paid social. The thesis, proven rather than projected.

Creator roster, from the tracker. Why it lands: 55 creators with verified TikTok Shop GMV, signed, compliant, and ready for day one. A distribution asset with a dollar value.

Content library, from Drive. Why it lands: 100-plus rights-cleared assets and a hook bank ranked by real performance.

Angle performance ranking, from the tracker, measured as preorders per post by angle. Why it lands: it proves the VoC research produced predictive creative direction, not just a deck slide.

Total organic reach, the sum of views. Why it lands: 2.6M-plus impressions at zero media spend.

Cost to produce all of the above, from the ledger: roughly $26,500.

The headline: we spent $26.5K, acquired customers at $8 before the product existed, and built a 55-creator distribution asset. That is a better slide than any revenue number this window could realistically produce.

9. STANDING RULES — THE THINGS THAT QUIETLY DECIDE THIS

1. Reply to inbound creator DMs within four hours. Reply speed predicts close rate at this tier more than anything in the offer.
2. Code, link, and one-pager within one hour of signing. Never "I'll send it tomorrow."
3. Review every creator's first post before promoting it. Roughly 20 minutes each, and the cheapest insurance in the program.
4. Message the top 3 creators personally, every week. They will produce a disproportionate share of the revenue and they are the ones most needed in December.
5. One tracker, one owner. A creator DM'd by two lanes is a creator lost.
6. Never spend preorder cash before October 15.
7. Post count is the metric to maximize. This window is hit-driven — every additional post is another lottery ticket on a topic the algorithm already rewards.
