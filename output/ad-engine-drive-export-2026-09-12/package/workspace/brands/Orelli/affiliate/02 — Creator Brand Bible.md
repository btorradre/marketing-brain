## 2. Why it exists

There are around **104 million American adults who struggle to swallow pills.** Roughly 40% of the country. And for a huge slice of them it isn't a preference — their throat physically closes, they gag, sometimes they throw up.

So they do what you've probably done:
- Buy children's grape medicine and do the dose math on a box that says "for children only"
- Crush pills into applesauce, yogurt, peanut butter
- Cut them in half and still panic
- Or just… take the headache. Skip the dose. Suffer through it.

Meanwhile the pain-relief industry has spent forty years competing on **coatings and gel-caps** — tiny tweaks to make a pill fractionally easier to swallow — and not one of them ever asked the obvious question: *what if you just didn't have to swallow it?*

Gummy vitamins figured this out a decade ago. Adults are perfectly happy to chew. Pain relief never caught up.

**That's the whole gap. Orelli is the thing that should have existed already.**

## 3. Who you're actually talking to

Picture one person. Every video is to her.

**She's a woman between 25 and 38. She has never been able to swallow a pill.**

- She's struggled since she was a kid. She assumed she'd grow out of it. She didn't.
- Her body fights her — throat closes, heart pounds, she gags, sometimes it comes back up. *"My brain literally red-flags me as dying."*
- She has a private system nobody knows about. Children's grape Tylenol in the cart. Crushing pills into yogurt. Or nothing at all.
- She's been made fun of for it. By family, partners, coworkers. She's embarrassed to tell her doctor.
- **The belief underneath all of it: "I'm the only adult who still can't do this."**

**Here's the part that matters most for your content:**

She doesn't convert when she hears about the product. She converts the second she feels **seen.** The moment you say *"wait — you can't swallow pills either?"*, she feels recognized for possibly the first time in her adult life.

**That recognition is the sale. The product is just the relief that comes after.**

Which is why your story comes first and the product comes last. Always.

## 5. The four angles

You'll be assigned one. Stay in it — don't blend them. A shame video and a convenience video are two different emotional registers, and mixing them blunts both.

### Angle 1 — The Choking Fear *(the most viral lane)*
**The moment:** the pill is at the back of her throat and it will not go down. Throat closes. Heart races. Gagging.
**Feeling:** fear → release
**Her inner voice:** *"It's not that I won't. My body physically won't let me."*
**Tone:** intimate, tight, close to camera. Visible tension, then the exhale.

**Hooks:**
- "POV: the pill's at the back of your throat and it just… won't go down."
- "My biggest fear isn't heights. It's a 500mg tablet."
- "Your throat closing up isn't willpower. It's a reflex. Stop fighting it."
- "I've thrown up more pills than I've swallowed."

### Angle 2 — The Adult Shame *(the deepest lane)*
**The moment:** a grown woman putting children's grape medicine in her cart, hoping nobody she knows is in the aisle.
**Feeling:** shame → vindication
**Her inner voice:** *"I thought I was the only adult who still can't do this."*
**Tone:** confessional, lo-fi, whisper energy → head up at the end.

**Hooks:**
- "POV: you're a grown adult sneaking children's grape Tylenol into your cart."
- "I'm [age], I have a whole career, and I still can't swallow a pill."
- "You're not childish. You were just never given an adult version."
- "Things I've never told anyone: I take kids' medicine. I'm 31."

### Angle 3 — The Untreated Pain
**The moment:** she's in real pain and takes nothing, because the pill is scarier than the pain.
**Feeling:** defeat → resolution
**Her inner voice:** *"I've let a whole day get ruined because the pill scared me more than the headache."*
**Tone:** honest, a little heavier, slower reveal.

**Hooks:**
- "Be honest — how many times have you chosen the headache because the pill was worse?"
- "I let migraines ruin entire days rather than fight a pill down."
- "I've told myself 'I'm not that sick' just to avoid taking something."
- "Stop choosing the pain. The pill was the only problem."

### Angle 4 — Convenience *(broadest, lowest-pressure)*
**The moment:** headache hits, no water anywhere — desk, car, flight, gym.
**Feeling:** ease, relief from friction
**Tone:** bright, fast, upbeat. No confession required.

**Hooks:**
- "Pain relief you can take with zero water, anywhere."
- "POV: headache hits, no water in sight, and you're completely fine."
- "People who swallow pills dry genuinely scare me."

## 6. Three formats that are already proven

### Format A — "[Age] and this is how I swallow pills"
The highest-performing format in this entire niche. Copy it directly.

Text on screen: **"[your age] years old and this is how I swallow pills"**. Then just show the truth — the psych-up, the grimace, the gag, the failure. Jump-cut across different outfits and rooms so it reads as *every single time, forever.* No voiceover needed; music and text carry it.

Then, instead of ending in resignation like every other version of this video: cut to the Orelli reveal.

### Format B — The confessional
Face to camera, phone propped up, your actual house. Tell the real story. The time it happened in front of people. The thing your family says. The children's medicine in your cart.

Zero production. **Polish reads as "ad." Confession reads as "true."**

### Format C — Stitch the pill hack
Stitch one of those "here's how to swallow a pill" tutorial videos — the head-tilt trick, the water-bottle trick. Let it play just long enough to register. Then cut to you. Slow head shake. *"No. Stop."*

> "Cute hack. I've tried every single one. You know what's easier than tilting your head like a baby bird every time you get a headache? Not swallowing a pill at all."

**Tone rule: never mock the person who made the hack, and never mock a doctor.** Play it warm — *"great tip, also tried it a hundred times, here's what finally worked."* Respect → reframe → reveal. Punching down at the people trying to help kills the whole thing.

## 7. The one rule that changes how you write this

**You have not tried Orelli. Nobody has. It doesn't ship until December.**

So you cannot review it, and you cannot say you use it. That's not a brand preference — it's federal law, and it's covered fully in your Compliance Sheet.

**What you're making is an announcement, not a review.**

| ✅ Say this | ❌ Never say this |
|:--|:--|
| "I can't swallow pills." | "I tried it." |
| "Someone finally made adult Tylenol as a gummy." | "It tastes amazing." |
| "It's 500mg — same as Extra Strength." | "It works so well." |
| "I preordered it." *(only if you did)* | "This is my go-to now." |
| "It ships in December." | "Better than Tylenol." |

**Why this is genuinely good for your video:** it forces the whole runtime onto your story — which is the part that actually makes people stop scrolling. The product gets eight seconds at the end. That's the correct ratio anyway. Every ad that leads with product dies.

## 9. Format rules

- **Vertical. Shot on your phone. In your actual home.** No ring light, no B-roll, no captions app that looks expensive.
- **Hook text in frame one**, top-center, readable with the sound off.
- **15–30 seconds.** Under 15 is too short for the story beat. Over 35 loses the room.
- **Your face for the first 10 seconds.** The product does not appear until the last third.
- **Use the Asset Pack** for the product visual — a clean bottle render or a screen recording of the site. Edit it in over your footage. Don't try to fake having the bottle.
- **No music over the confession beat.** Room tone is more believable. Music is fine on the format-A jump-cuts.
