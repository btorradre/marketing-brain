What If Statins Only Do ONE Thing? And Familial Hypercholesterolemia Needs Three?

Here is what nobody explains when they hand you that prescription.

Statins block HMG-CoA reductase, the enzyme your liver uses to produce cholesterol.

For most people with high cholesterol, that is enough. Production is the problem. Block production, problem solved.

For people with familial hypercholesterolemia, production is only one third of the problem.

The FH problem is three-fold

Problem #1: Overproduction

Your liver makes far too much cholesterol.

✓ Statins address this
Problem #2: Poor Clearance

Your LDL receptors do not pull cholesterol back out of your bloodstream properly. In FH those receptors are broken or missing. Even if you slow production, the LDL already circulating just sits there.

✗ Statins do not address this
Problem #3: Excessive Oxidation

When LDL sits in your bloodstream too long it oxidizes. It rusts. Oxidized LDL is the sticky kind, the kind that attaches to artery walls and builds the plaque that kills you. Regular LDL is a problem. Oxidized LDL is the killer.

✗ Statins do not address this either
