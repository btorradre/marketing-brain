# Choosing the picture by the job of the line

These are creative starting points, not a mandatory script structure or exclusive footage assignments. Preserve the approved script. The quoted example lines below are illustrative prompts, not factual claims or reference quotations.

Apply the concept treatment before using this matrix. A person, object or lived activity can be shown through matching animation, real video or a real photograph depending on the concept. Fully animated concepts use animated people/scenes for nightmare and desired states; HeyGen-style VSLs may use real human photos/videos for those beats and scientific animation for mechanism education. Read [concept matching](concept-matching.md) for source/generation routing. A row describes the story job and content, not an automatic change of medium.

## One footage style, multiple story jobs

The user gave phone-style human footage during agitation as an example. Choose content, style and story function independently. These examples all fit casual phone footage, while performing different jobs:

| Story job | Possible phone-style scene | Why it belongs there |
|---|---|---|
| Hook / recognition | An immediately readable action related to the opening line | Establish relevance or curiosity |
| Agitation | A person interrupts dinner because of discomfort | Make the lived difficulty visible |
| Discovery | Someone compares information on a laptop and notes the relevant finding | Show the search and change in understanding |
| Explanation / demonstration | Hands operate the actual closure or show a simple relevant process | Make how it works understandable |
| Product introduction / routine | A person opens the correct package and performs the actual use step | Make the solution tangible and practical |
| Proof | Authentic footage demonstrates the specific supported feature/result | Let the viewer inspect relevant evidence; style alone proves nothing |
| Payoff / aspiration | The person enjoys dinner, a trip or a task relevant to the story | Show the valued activity, comfort or capability |
| CTA | Product in hand with the appropriate invitation or gesture | Connect the item to the next action |

Conversely, a single story job may be served by a human scene, object detail, process animation, source document or presenter moment. Choose the candidate that best communicates this line in this sequence; do not force a style change merely because the script enters another stage.

## Recognition, agitation and stakes

| Line's job | Select | Avoid | Timing and story reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| Identify an uncomfortable moment | A plausible person in the right setting doing the exact action: stop eating, shift in seat, leave bed, pause on stairs | Stock frown, generic belly-clutch for every symptom, unrelated anatomy | Enter when the situation is named; show its readable physical cue |
| Show recurrence | A different daily context: morning at bed edge, evening at table, clothing choice before leaving | Reusing the opening footage with a new crop | The new context adds persistence instead of repeating information |
| Show a contradiction | Put both sides in one intelligible shot or clear sequence: small plate + person unable to finish; roomy jeans + uncomfortable posture | Showing only one noun while losing the contradiction | Hold enough for the viewer to connect both sides |
| Show previous effort | Specific attempted routine: stir a powder, read shelf labels, prepare food, track habits | A pile of random products, pretending a competitor demonstration proves failure | Show the actual effort at its verb; switch to the supported gap when the narration explains it |
| Show emotional cost | Missed/interrupted activity relevant to this audience: dinner conversation, playing with family, dressing for an event | Dramatic suffering unrelated to copy, extreme acting, generic hospital scene | The scene answers why this problem matters beyond the symptom |
| Land an admission or reframe | Presenter reaction/eye contact; sometimes a quiet pause in the lived scene | An insert that covers the very expression carrying the meaning | Leave room for the thought to land |

Generate or source human footage to look like a plausible observed moment, not a model posing as a symptom. Match the audience and intended protagonist. Do not infer that a person has a condition from appearance or label an illustrative actor as a real patient.

## Discovery, explanation and mechanism

| Line's job | Select | What makes it useful |
|---|---|---|
| Introduce a discovery | Presenter; actual research behavior; relevant verified source excerpt when evidence is the point | The change in understanding is clear, with no fabricated citation |
| Establish where a process occurs | One orienting body/organ view or object overview | Viewers know where the next close-up belongs |
| Explain movement | Longitudinal pathway, contraction sequence, visible object/particle displacement | Direction, rate/state or interaction is readable without relying entirely on caption |
| Explain a barrier | Cross-section or close surface with correct sides and crossing behavior | Viewer can identify what passes, what stays and why |
| Explain contact/interaction | Appropriate micro view with visible partners and a single event | Scale serves the question; it is not decoration |
| Explain an everyday analogy | A real-world process that accurately maps onto the one causal relationship | Clearly an analogy; do not silently turn it into anatomy or multiply competing metaphors |
| Introduce ingredient | Recognizable raw material, preparation or ingredient photograph/inset | Identity cue; distinct from the action allegedly performed |
| Show solution action | Previously understood problem state changes through a legible action | The mechanism addresses the established gap; no magical glow standing in for an explanation |
| Distinguish several ingredients | Separate material identities and separate appropriate actions/views | The viewer can tell each role apart before the combined product is shown |
| Synthesize | Presenter or a simple integration view | Stop adding microscopic detail once the purchasing question is clear |

For any mechanism, write `location/scale → before-state → actor/action → after-state → relevance to spoken claim`. If the claim or mechanism is unsupported, do not invent scientific detail to make the animation look persuasive. Flag the dependency and plan only what is established; preserve approved narration separately rather than quietly rewriting it.

Observed source lesson: in YIXBhp, a nerve-level view moves to a whole-body route, then a signal-level image and food-seeking behavior. The image changes because the explanatory question changes. This is not a recipe to use that biology for another product.

## Proof, product and payoff

| Line's job | Select | Key distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Establish an actual result | Authentic relevant demonstration/document/testimonial with enough context to interpret it | Illustration is not proof; unrelated people's footage is not one before/after |
| Show a selection criterion | Readable actual label, relevant test/feature, side-by-side evidence or person comparing real options | A lab coat, factory or microscope image alone establishes no credential, origin or efficacy |
| Reveal brand | Accurate selected product in hand/on table/in its intended setting | Ingredient appearance is not necessarily brand reveal; preserve the concept's reveal order |
| Explain use | Hands complete the actual routine, with correct form/count/size and necessary objects | Complete the useful action; don't cut before opening, placement or interaction is understandable |
| Show relief | Specific regained behavior: finish the meal, return to the conversation, wake comfortably, carry the bag easily | Relief is more legible as capability than a beauty pose |
| Broaden emotional payoff | Return to the important activity introduced earlier | New footage can answer the old loss without becoming an unsupported documented result |
| Reduce purchase uncertainty | Relevant actual policy/page/size/compatibility evidence | Show what answers the objection, not a generic shopping montage |
| Ask for action | Item plus accurate destination/gesture, with an appropriate end hold | Stop expanding the story; make the action easy to identify |

Do not introduce a sales card or packaging in an article/listicle bridge whose approved direction excludes them. The CTA must fit the actual destination, not the reference's destination.

## Placement choices

- **Full-frame:** the action needs space, the person must be experienced as a real moment, the process must be followed, or a document must be read.
- **Inset:** the face carries the persuasion while a simple object, ingredient or diagram supplies one supporting referent. Keep clear of face, gesture and captions.
- **Split view:** comparison, paired inputs or simultaneous relationship is the point and both sides are legible. Do not use split view simply to fit more assets.
- **Presenter:** the expression, interpretation, qualification, chapter change or conversation is already doing the job. An intentional no-insert decision is valid.

## Practical examples for existing formats

**Customer discovery:** “I'd eat a few bites at dinner and feel like I'd swallowed a brick.” Choose a domestic table-level shot of the approved protagonist setting down a fork beside a scarcely touched plate. The action makes early fullness recognizable. Return to face for her interpretation. Do not insert literal bricks or internal-body imagery unless the approved direction calls for an analogy.

**Podcast explanation:** keep the host visible for the question; keep the guest for the opening answer. During a supported explanation of stomach movement, insert one clear contraction view; return to the guest for the implication and the host for the follow-up. A separate low-fiber-food point needs a plate/food view, not the same contraction clip.

**Non-health transfer:** a bag owner searching through a tote gets a candid overhead search action; a construction explanation gets a close detail of the relevant pocket/closure; capacity gets a truthful packing demonstration; payoff gets the owner quickly retrieving the needed object in a new scene. The decision logic transfers without importing any health-science imagery.
