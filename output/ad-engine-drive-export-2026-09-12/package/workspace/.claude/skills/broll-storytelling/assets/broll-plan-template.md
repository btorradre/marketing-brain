# [Concept] — B-roll / editing plan

Date/version: […]. Existing plan: […]. Status: analysis / proposed selection / production-ready / revised.

## Inputs and constraints

Brand/product and verified references; approved script/version and speaker; audience situation; format; objective/destination; target ratio/runtime; narration speed/alignment; selected assets; current user constraints. Recover an existing plan before making a second competing schedule.

## Reference and original direction

Source and source-media path; measured FPS/duration; shot/event-map and consecutive-frame evidence; actual inspection scope; observed visual structures, transitions, captions, movement and audio; interpretations and unresolved evidence. If no source: original direction, no invented reference findings.

## Editorial approach

Target concept and visual treatment: dominant medium; specific animation/photographic style; character/product identity; world/locations; palette, materials, lighting and camera/motion language; permitted medium changes and their story purpose. State what transfers from the reference and what the target intentionally changes. Fully animated concepts use matching animated B-roll throughout; HeyGen VSLs may intentionally mix scientific animation with real human photos/video.

Primary story structure and why; face/full-frame/inset choices; hook; approved narrative and product/offer ordering; emotional costs and payoff callbacks where relevant; varied explanatory views; deliberate presenter/character continuity.

The rationale is a primary deliverable, not an optional label. Make it specific to the proposed/selected scene; “matches the script” or “adds engagement” is insufficient. Explain intentional presenter holds too. Carry each rationale onto its corresponding Cut Room card when delivering a storyboard. Update the explanation when a replacement changes the scene's action or purpose.

## Voice, captions and audio

Preserved or selected voice/version, delivery direction, continuous speech plan, exact caption alignment, placement and visual competition; known or proposed music/SFX roles; mix priorities. Label unaudited reference audio rather than inventing it.

## Hook, product reveal and close

Intense opening action/emotion already readable in the first frame, calibrated to the approved reference and spoken hook; ingredient-versus-brand introduction; actual offer/destination; product identity; CTA gesture; end hold and duration rationale. Preserve approved reveal constraints. Hook-rate improvement remains an objective until campaign evidence establishes it.

## Sourcing and generation

Existing approved assets; found real photos/videos with source URLs/timecodes and usage status; concept-matched generated stills or animation; GPT Image 2 scene/character references and first images/overlays; Google Omni motion/duration; product and presenter references. Explain route per beat. One dominant action per generated insert. Save selected files and a beat-linked manifest with origin, style, action, duration, provenance and inspection status. Keep stills when appropriate; distinguish actual real footage from generated photoreal reenactments.

## Production and QA

Plan → source/generate → inspect selected assets → align final narration → update schedule → Cut Room whenever delivery includes a storyboard → DaVinci Resolve edit when authorized → inspect finished cut and export. Check literal fit, silent legibility, placement, action completion, uniqueness, captions, product/presenter identity, sequence and CTA. Verify actual Cut Room board/assets and live editor operations before reporting success.

## Revision record

Changed beats, preserved approvals, replaced asset IDs/card images, updated alignment dependencies, remaining gaps. Put old reference/rejected thumbnails in comparison evidence, not on proposed production cards.
