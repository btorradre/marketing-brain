# DR OS — the laws every skill in this system obeys

Read this before running any `dr-*` skill. These are not style preferences. Each one is here because breaking it has already cost this account money.

---

## LAW 1 — Brand context loads first, always

Before any research pass, any angle, any hook, any brief: read the brand folder.

1. `brands/<brand>/` for products, research, ops, and prior concepts.
2. `brands/<brand>/00-brief.md` if it exists (avatar, awareness, sophistication, offer). Several brands do not have one yet. When it is missing, reconstruct from `brands/<brand>/research/` and `brands/<brand>/products/` and say which files you used.
3. `brands/<brand>/ops/` for house laws. Velantra's live at `brands/velantra/ops/claude-project-instructions.md` and they override anything in this OS.

Never work a brand from general category knowledge when the answer is in the folder. Copy is written in the avatar's own recorded language, not in marketing language about the avatar.

## LAW 2 — Nothing enters the bank unsourced

Every angle, pain point, objection, and transformation carries a verbatim quote and a resolving path or URL. Format: `"exact quote" — (source: path#Lnn, date)`.

Never invent a study, a sample size, a percentage, a doctor, a press mention, a review count, or a customer quote. If authority is needed and the proof does not exist in the folder, state the thing plainly and unattributed, or say "I don't have that in the folder, confirm it and I'll write it in."

Interpretation is allowed but must be labelled `[INFERRED]` with the reasoning shown. An inference is never promoted to a quote.

## LAW 3 — Topic is not motive

The Golden Nugget Doctrine governs every output. Name the deep frame before drafting. A surface angle buys mild curiosity; a deep frame makes the reader feel caught. If the research has not surfaced one, mine further rather than defaulting to the topic.

## LAW 4 — Six-month shelf life, or it does not ship

No transition angles. No day-to-night, no seasonal switch, no "the bag you stop swapping at 5pm," no holiday beat as the idea itself. These carry a one-month shelf life and the whole library dies with the calendar.

A seasonal word survives only as a wrapper ("I think I found the perfect fall bag") and only when deleting the season leaves every reason to believe intact.

## LAW 5 — The swap test

Drop a competitor's product into the angle. If the ad still reads perfectly, the angle sells the category and not us. Rewrite before writing a line.

The fix is always the same: lead with a verdict, then stack our own physical facts. Those specifics double as the criteria a shopping buyer uses, which is how we become the best one without comparing to anything.

## LAW 6 — Competitor intelligence never becomes competitor copy

`dr-market-intel` exists to find the gap. Its output is a brief direction, never a line of ad copy.

For Velantra specifically: no "unlike other brands," no "cheap bags that fall apart," no generic competitor mentions, no price comparisons to unnamed luxury brands. The one carve-out is a format that IS a comparison (a ranked roundup, "I tested five bags"), and inside it Hermes and "Birkin" stay out entirely, competitors are treated as genuinely excellent and given a trade-off rather than a defect, and no competitor price is quoted unless it was verified on that brand's own site. If a line would make a Saint Laurent owner feel stupid it is the wrong line, because she is the avatar.

No villain and no manufactured problem. The avatar is not wounded, she is looking for a good product.

## LAW 7 — Every claim gets a demo beat

Demonstrate, never describe. If a script says it holds a laptop, the video shows the laptop going in. A claim with no on-screen demonstration does not survive the brief review in `dr-ugc-brief`.

## LAW 8 — The creator is never the brand

A UGC creator says "they're," "their," "this brand." Never "our," never "we." One creator per ad, locked as the same reference in every shot.

## LAW 9 — Voice tells that kill a draft

Applies to every line of customer-facing copy this OS produces (hooks, scripts, briefs quoting deliverable lines).

- **No em dashes.** Anywhere. Period, comma, or parentheses.
- **No "That's not X. It's Y."** and no variant. No punchy negation-then-affirmation beat closing a paragraph.
- **No parallel repetition stacks.** "More leads. More calls. More revenue." Each line must escalate, not echo.
- **No rhetorical section openers.** "But what does that actually mean?"
- **No "Imagine if" openers.**
- No over-polished rhythm, no tidy tricolons, no symmetrical parallelism. Short noun-phrase fragments are fine, that is the real voice.
- Never personify objects. A bag does not want, know, or remember.

## LAW 10 — One ad, one awareness level

An ad cannot be "top and bottom funnel." Assign one level, cite the hook that proves it. Frequency above 5 is not a bidding problem, it is an exhausted audience at that level, and the unlock is almost always higher up the funnel.

## LAW 11 — Self-audit before presenting

Run the draft against Laws 2, 4, 5, 6, 7, 9 and fix violations before the user sees it. Do not ship a first draft and wait to be corrected. Every deliverable ends with a two-line audit confirming the angle passes the six-month test and the swap test.

## LAW 12 — Flag a conflict once, then build

If a request breaks a law, say so in one or two sentences, then deliver the full thing under a stated assumption. Do not stall and do not silently work around the request by changing the concept.
