# Velantra Ad Script Formula

This is the house formula for every Velantra (a handbag brand) ad script: a short hook, then an immediate "This is the [Product] from Velantra" intro, an attribute stack, a lived proof beat, a capacity list, colorways, the live offer, and a link CTA. Use it for any Velantra ad script in any format (greenscreen voiceover, UGC, organic review replication, founder-to-camera, static caption, email) and for any Velantra bag product.

This formula was derived from the brand's single top-performing ad ("greenscreen v3," a Weekender ad) and confirmed against three other shipped, proven scripts. It is the default, not one option among several — deviate only for an explicit, deliberate reason.

Before writing, gather real product-truth facts about the specific bag being advertised (its actual physical construction, materials, hardware, mechanism, and current live pricing/colorway count). Every claim in the script must be checkable against that truth and against the live product page.

## The formula

| # | Beat | Job | Budget |
|---|------|-----|--------|
| 1 | HOOK | Self-ID or doubt, resolved into a verdict. Opens the loop. | 1-2 sentences, 12-20 words, 3-4s |
| 2 | PRODUCT INTRO | "This is the [Name] from Velantra." Say it early, say it plainly. | 5-8 words, 2s |
| 3 | ATTRIBUTE STACK | 3-5 specific physical facts of OUR bag, run together in one breath | 13-25 words, 5-7s |
| 4 | PROOF | The lived beat. First person, time-stamped, something she watched it survive. | 15-25 words, 5-7s |
| 5 | CAPACITY | What actually goes in it, as concrete nouns | 8-14 words, 4s |
| 6 | VARIANTS | How many colorways | 5-7 words, 1.5s |
| 7 | OFFER + SCARCITY | The sale that is live today, and that colors go | 10-14 words, 3s |
| 8 | CTA | "I left the link below." | 5 words, 1s |

**Total: 85-95 words, 28-30 seconds, ~185 wpm.** That pace is for a voiceover ad. A real-time demo where hands are doing work runs slower, ~160 wpm, so budget ~78 words for a 30-second demo instead.

Beats 5 and 6 are the first to cut when runtime is tight. Beats 1, 2, 3, and 8 never get cut.

## How to use this

1. Load the brand's voice/context and the specific product's verified truth (materials, construction, mechanism, live pricing and colorway count) before writing a single word.
2. Write the script beat by beat, in the exact order above, hitting each beat's word budget.
3. Run the beat rules (below) against each individual beat as you write it.
4. Run every hard law (below) against the finished script.
5. Run the self-audit checklist (below) before calling it done. Any failure is a rewrite, not a note to fix later.
6. Match the visual format to the ad type being produced (see Visual Format section).

## Beat rules

### 1. Hook

Four proven opening patterns — pick based on the viewer's awareness level:

- **Self-ID + verdict** (unaware / problem-aware): "If you're looking for the perfect summer travel bag, I have the one for you."
- **Observation + verdict** (solution-aware): "Fall is almost here and everyone I know swaps out their straw bags for suede and leather. I think I found the one that stays out all year."
- **Permission** (solution-aware, warm): "If you've been thinking about the [product], this is your sign."
- **Doubt + reversal** (product-aware): "I thought this woven tote was gonna fall apart. I was so wrong."

Rules:
- **The reversal or verdict lands inside the hook, never at the end of the ad.** Resolving it immediately is what opens the curiosity loop. Holding it for the ending turns the script into a narrative arc, and a narrative arc does not sell here.
- **Doubt is only ever directed at OUR bag.** "I thought THIS was gonna fall apart" is her own expectation of the thing she bought — that's allowed. "Another floppy [category] bag" is a claim about products the brand doesn't make, and that framing is banned.
- No villain, no problem agitation, no attacking a product category. The avatar is already searching for a solution, so the job is to close her open loop, not open a new wound.
- She is already mid-thought on the very first word. No throat-clearing, no warm-up line.

### 2. Product intro

**Mandatory, verbatim shape: "This is the [Name] from Velantra."**

- Always lands by the 0:06 mark. Never buried inside a run-on clause, never delayed to build a category case first. An affiliate marketer delays naming the brand because they're selling the whole category — a brand's own ad is different: the entire benefit stack needs to accrue to the actual brand, so name it immediately.
- Use the full product name ("The Sofia Woven Tote," not "this bag").
- Never use an epithet in place of the name — no "the summer one," "the everyday one," "the polished one." Name only.
- One product per ad.

### 3. Attribute stack

Three to five **specific physical facts about our bag**, run together in one breath with commas.

- Facts only, stated flat. Example set for one bag: "Birkin-inspired, structured, real brass hardware, full-grain leather over woven canvas." Example set for another: "hand woven, structured, one-piece leather flap folded over the top, crossed front straps, cream contrast stitching, no logo anywhere."
- **Apply the swap test to this beat.** Mentally drop a competitor's bag into the script in place of ours. If the sentence still reads true, the stack was generic — it was advertising the whole category, not this specific product. Rewrite until the facts are unique to this bag.
- Attach the consequence with "so": "it's fully structured, it holds its shape, and the flap folds all the way over the top, so nothing falls out of it."
- **Always include a no-logo fact if it's true of the product** — the absence of visible branding is a top purchase driver for this brand's avatar, and it's a hard, verifiable fact.
- These facts double as the criteria a comparison-shopping buyer is already using, which is how the ad wins "the best one" without ever comparing to anything by name.

### 4. Proof

One lived beat. First person, past or present-perfect tense, with a time stamp on it.

- "I've thrown it in the trunk every weekend this summer, and it still looks brand new."
- "I've been carrying mine all season and I'm still obsessed."
- In a demo-format ad, this beat becomes the demo itself — she performs the action on camera and says almost nothing over it. **Demonstrate, never describe.** Hold silence on the payoff frame; let the visual carry it.

### 5. Capacity

Concrete nouns, three or four, listed. Never "roomy," never "fits everything" left standing alone.

- "two outfits, shoes, your toiletries" / "your wallet, a water bottle, a sweater for when it gets cold"
- "It actually fits everything" is fine only as a lead-in phrase immediately followed by the concrete list.

### 6. Variants

"It comes in two different colors." / "the caramel, the one everyone asks me about."
The colorway count must be true on the day the ad runs — verify against the live product page before shipping, every time.

### 7. Offer + scarcity

"They're having a fall sale right now, and the colorways go fast."

- Must be visibly true on the live product page the day the ad ships. Never invent a countdown timer, a specific stock number, or a fake deadline.
- Scarcity should be soft and factual — "colors go fast," never a fabricated "only 3 left."
- Never mention buy-now-pay-later financing.

### 8. CTA

"I left the link below." Flat delivery, no performed urgency.

## Hard laws (check every one before delivering)

1. **No em dashes.** Anywhere. Use a period, comma, or parentheses instead.
2. **No "That's not X. It's Y." contrastive-reveal construction**, or any variant of it.
3. **No staccato fragment stacks in spoken lines.** Every voiceover line must be a complete sentence, and cadence should vary naturally. Never solve a tight timecode by chopping a sentence into fragments — let the line run slightly past the cut instead.
4. **Never personify the bag.** The product is always "it," never "she" or "her" — even when the bag itself carries a woman's name, referring to it as "she" reads as describing an actual person. This is absolute, on every surface, including internal working documents.
5. **No competitor comparisons.** No other brand named or shown on screen, no price comparisons, no "dupe," "replica," "alternative," or "knockoff" language.
6. **A named design inspiration (e.g. "Birkin-inspired") is legal in voiceover and captions only when it's the brand's own stated, truthful mechanism and already appears on the live product page** — never use it as a comparison, never pair it with a competing luxury brand's price, never rank the product against anything by name. Frame it as shape-and-scale description (a mechanism), not a name-drop for borrowed prestige. Note: this kind of reference term should never appear inside an image/video *generation* prompt even when it's fine in the spoken script — treat generation prompts and spoken copy as having different rules here.
7. **No origin claims.** Ever.
8. **No claim the brand cannot back up** — especially about products the brand doesn't make.
9. **No fabricated citations, numbers, studies, or reviews.**
10. **No "transition" angles** (day-to-night, a seasonal switch as the actual selling mechanism). A season word is fine as a light wrapper on the hook, but if swapping "fall" for "spring" would break the ad's logic, the positioning itself was the problem — fix the positioning, not the season word.
11. **The product must be on screen for essentially the entire ad.** No generic or random stock-product filler frames. Any dismissive comparison lives only in the voiceover, spoken over shots of OUR product, never illustrated with someone else's product on screen.
12. **Say the brand name correctly, every time** — some AI voice and video tools are known to mis-render brand names phonetically (e.g. "Velantra" as "Volantra"); proofread every generated read against the correct spelling/pronunciation.

## Visual format (greenscreen voiceover — the default ad format)

- Vertical 9:16, 1080×1920. Creator cut out on a green-screen background, positioned bottom-left, roughly a quarter of the frame's height. Full-bleed product b-roll fills the rest of the frame behind them.
- **A new visual every ~3.5 seconds.** Alternate between still images and motion clips — never run more than 2 stills or 3 b-roll clips back-to-back.
- Word-synced burned-in captions: white bold rounded sans-serif font, a thin dark outline, sentence case, no terminal punctuation, 1-2 lines, 4-8 words per card, centered just above the creator's cutout.
- The creator's cutout jumps from bottom-left to bottom-right exactly once, around the three-quarter mark of the ad.
- **The final beat is a screen recording of the live product page**, showing the real price, the real strike-through discount, the real colorway swatches, and the real Add to Cart button. Use a clean browser with no visible extensions. The cart/price shown must reflect a real, currently-live sale — never an invented graphic or fabricated discount.
- Use one continuous voiceover take rather than splicing together separately-recorded lines. Target roughly -14 LUFS loudness for the voiceover; mute any audio embedded in generated b-roll clips.

For organic review-style replications, the visual format inverts: one continuous handheld take, no music, no cuts, and captions appear only on the thesis/key beats rather than being fully word-synced. The underlying beat formula (hook → intro → attributes → proof → capacity → variants → offer → CTA) does not change.

## Self-audit before delivering

Run every item below. Treat any failure as a required rewrite, not a minor note:

- Does the product intro read exactly "This is the [Name] from Velantra" and land by the 0:06 mark?
- Does the hook resolve into a verdict or reversal within the hook itself, rather than at the end?
- Swap a competitor's bag into the script mentally — does it still read true? If yes, the attribute stack is too generic and needs rewriting.
- Will this angle still make sense in six months (i.e., is it tied to something durable, not a fleeting trend)?
- Is the no-logo fact included, if true of this product?
- Is every attribute a verified physical fact of our specific bag, checked against real product truth and the live product page?
- Is every price, colorway count, and stock claim actually true on the live page today?
- Zero em dashes. Zero "she/her" used for the bag. Zero fragment-stack sentences in spoken lines.
- Is the word count and runtime inside budget for the target words-per-minute of this format?
- Read it out loud — does it sound like a real person talking, or like written copy?
