# DR UGC Brief

A bad brief produces bad footage no matter how good the creator is. These briefs are not shot lists — they are creative strategies written in plain language, and every instruction in them exists to protect the angle from being diluted in production.

## Golden nugget doctrine

Before writing, name the golden nugget the angle is built on — the real emotional motive underneath the surface problem, not the topic itself. The brief's internal header states it explicitly, and the hook and body should be checked against it: an ad that stays at the topic level buys mild interest; one that reaches the motive makes the viewer feel caught.

## Operating rules

1. **The brief serves the angle, not the creator.** Every instruction exists because without it the angle degrades on set.
2. **Specificity prevents mistakes.** Write every instruction as if you cannot be reached to clarify it, because you cannot.
3. **The hook is non-negotiable.** The creator has freedom in the body. The opening three seconds are delivered as written. Say this explicitly in the brief.
4. **Tone comes from awareness level.** A solution-aware customer is skeptical. A problem-aware customer is frustrated. The creator's energy has to match — explain which one they're playing.
5. **Show, don't tell.** Instead of "seem relaxed," write "film in your kitchen like you're telling a friend something you just figured out."
6. **No file paths, no internal jargon.** No internal IDs, no internal tool or system names, no awareness-level vocabulary in the creator-facing document. Those live in an internal header only, never in the part sent to the creator.

## Step 1 — Load everything the brief depends on

Read the source angle and its chosen hook. If either doesn't exist yet, write the hook first rather than improvising it inside this brief.

**Product truth is mandatory.** Load whatever product-truth documentation exists for the exact product being briefed — locked specs, dimensions, materials, mechanisms — and carry it into the brief verbatim. If the product has a signature mechanism or construction detail that must appear correctly in every piece of content (a specific opening mechanism, a specific material behavior), that block is not optional here. If any dimension or spec is genuinely unknown for this product, no brief may state a size for it — say it's unknown rather than guessing.

Load the voice-of-customer research for the phrases that must appear verbatim in the creator's dialogue.

## Step 2 — Write the brief

**Internal header first (never sent to the creator):** the angle name, awareness level, target persona, the golden nugget, and where the hook came from.

Then write the creator-facing document:

### Overview (3 sentences maximum)
What this ad is trying to do and who it speaks to.

### The hook (non-negotiable)
- The exact opening line, word for word
- Visual direction for the first 2-3 seconds: setting, framing, body language
- What the creator must NOT do in the opening

On top of funnel, the opening never leads on the product. If the first frame is a product beauty shot, the brief is wrong.

### The body
- Key points in order, written as talking points rather than a full script
- The emotional journey: what the creator's energy is at each stage
- Which customer phrases must appear verbatim
- **Demo beats.** Every claim gets a paired on-screen demonstration, written as a two-column list so nothing ships as a bare assertion:

  | Claim she makes | What the camera shows while she says it |
  |---|---|
  | it holds a 16 inch laptop | the laptop sliding in, lid closing over it |
  | the material softens instead of scuffing | thumb pressed into the material, releasing |

- What objections to address, and how

### The close
- How the ad ends
- Specific CTA language

### Production notes
- Recommended setting, and why it serves this angle
- Wardrobe and styling direction
- What to avoid
- One creator throughout, same person in every shot. If this is an AI-generated production, lock the creator as the same reference image or identity in every generated shot.

### What success looks like
One paragraph describing the finished ad if the brief was executed correctly. This is what any reviewer grades the footage against.

## Step 3 — Run the gates

Fix every violation before the brief ships.

| Gate | Kill condition |
|---|---|
| **Demo beat** | Any claim in the body with no paired visual. |
| **Creator voice** | Any "our," "we," or "my brand." The creator says "they're," "their," "this brand." |
| **Product truth** | Any spec, dimension, material, colorway, or capability not actually true of the product. |
| **Voice tells** | Em dash, "not X it's Y," parallel repetition stacks, in any line the creator actually speaks. |
| **Swap test** | The script would read identically for a competitor's product. |
| **Six-month test** | The concept dies with the season or moment. |
| **Clean brief** | Any internal ID, internal tool/system name, or internal vocabulary in the creator-facing section. |

## Step 4 — Write and hand off

Save the finished brief as a standalone document. If the team tracks concepts before production starts, log this one — product, concept name, angle, one-sentence thesis, format, type — and note the returned tracking ID back on the angle record.

Close with a two-line self-audit confirming the six-month test and the swap test both pass, then name the intended production method: AI-generated UGC, a real creator shoot, a multi-shot production, or a reference-ad replication.

## House laws this brief enforces

These are the specific rules from the wider direct-response discipline that this brief exists to protect. Treat violations as blockers, not notes.

**Every claim gets a demo beat.** Demonstrate, never describe. If the script says it holds a laptop, the video shows the laptop going in. A claim with no on-screen demonstration does not survive the brief review — this is the single most common reason a brief gets sent back.

**The creator is never the brand.** A UGC creator says "they're," "their," "this brand." Never "our," never "we." One creator per ad, locked as the same reference in every shot.

**Nothing enters the brief unsourced.** Any spec, dimension, material, or capability quoted in the brief has to be actually true of the product, not assumed or inferred from a similar product.

**The swap test.** Drop a competitor's product into the script. If it still reads perfectly, the angle sells the category and not this brand — rewrite it around the brand's own physical facts before it ships.

**The six-month test.** No transition angles, no seasonal switch as the idea itself, no holiday beat as the concept. A seasonal word survives only as a light wrapper, and only when deleting the season leaves every reason to believe intact.

**Voice tells that kill a draft**, in any line the creator actually speaks: no em dashes; no "that's not X, it's Y" or any variant of that negation-then-affirmation beat; no parallel repetition stacks ("more leads, more calls, more revenue" — each line has to escalate, not echo); no rhetorical section openers like "but what does that actually mean?"; no "imagine if" openers; no over-polished symmetrical rhythm. Short, imperfect, natural phrasing is the real voice. Objects are never personified — a bag doesn't want, know, or remember.
