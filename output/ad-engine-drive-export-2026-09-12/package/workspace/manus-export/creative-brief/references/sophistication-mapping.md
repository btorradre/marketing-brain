# Hook sophistication mapping — adjusting a hook to the target audience's awareness level

When adapting a reference ad to a new brand or audience, **the hook block changes shape based on the target audience's awareness level.** Getting this wrong makes the ad either condescend (talking down to a sophisticated buyer who already knows the basics) or confuse (going over the head of someone who doesn't yet know they have the problem).

## The five awareness levels

1. **Unaware** — doesn't know she has a problem.
2. **Problem-aware** — knows she has the problem, doesn't know the cause.
3. **Solution-aware** — knows the cause and knows solutions exist, doesn't know this specific product.
4. **Product-aware** — knows this product exists, hasn't bought yet.
5. **Most-aware** — knows the product and wants it, just needs an offer trigger.

Most direct-response ads target levels 2, 3, or 4. Level 1 needs editorial/discovery-style content instead of a direct ad; level 5 needs a straightforward offer, not a persuasive narrative.

## Hook shape per level

**Problem-aware hook** (e.g. a hormonal-symptom-focused reference ad)
- Pattern: symptom callout → "if this is you" → curiosity gap → "I want to show you what I learned."
- Time budget: 30+ seconds on a symptom inventory before introducing the underlying cause.
- Visual: relatable body shots, mirror selfies, real bodies that look like the target viewer.
- Function: get her to see herself in the ad first, then earn the right to explain the cause.

**Solution-aware hook** (e.g. an ad targeting people already on a specific category of medication/solution)
- Pattern: name the existing medication/situation, name the symptom, open a curiosity gap about why nothing so far has worked.
- Time budget: roughly 15 seconds up front, then straight into the mechanism.
- Visual: a "graveyard" of previously-tried products, a montage of what she's already attempted.
- Function: skip the discovery phase entirely (she already knows she has the problem) and go straight to "you've tried X, Y, Z — here's why none of it worked."

**Product-aware hook**
- Pattern: name the product directly, plus a genuinely *new* angle she hasn't considered before (a new ingredient, a new study, a new mechanism).
- Time budget: roughly 5-8 seconds — go straight to what's different this time.
- Visual: the product on screen almost immediately.
- Function: re-engage someone who's heard the pitch before with a genuinely fresh angle.

## Mapping a reference ad's level to a new target level

| Reference | Target | Adjustment |
|---|---|---|
| Problem-aware | Problem-aware | Direct adapt; preserve the hook structure as-is. |
| Problem-aware | Solution-aware | **Compress the hook and "vindication" blocks by roughly 50%.** She already knows she has the problem — skip discovery and go straight to "here's why your existing efforts haven't worked." |
| Problem-aware | Product-aware | Compress both the hook and the vindication section. Lead with the new differentiator instead. |
| Solution-aware | Problem-aware | **Expand the hook block.** She doesn't know she has the problem yet, so a full symptom inventory and identification sequence is needed first. |
| Solution-aware | Solution-aware | Direct adapt; swap in the new product/condition specifics. |

## What stays the same regardless of the audience's sophistication level

- **The total number of belief-shifts.** If the reference ad makes 8 distinct belief-shifting moves, the adapted version should make 8 as well. Compress *within* each shift, never by cutting shifts out entirely.
- **Vindication-before-mechanism ordering.** This is what earns the right to teach the mechanism — required at every sophistication level.
- **The blame-transfer line** — something to the effect of "it's not your fault, the things you tried simply weren't going to work." Required at every level.
- **Mechanism-explanation length** — roughly 70-90 seconds regardless of sophistication level; this doesn't compress.
- **A "reader-surrogate" line** — something like "I'm reading this thinking…" Required.
- **A callback to the original hook** at the point the mechanism resolves. Required.
- **Alternating talking-head and graphic/diagram shots** during the mechanism-explanation section. Required.

## Brand- or avatar-specific overrides

Always check for and apply any brand-specific voice/objection rules before writing, since these override the general sophistication defaults above:
- Banned metaphors or mechanism-phrasing choices.
- A required mechanism-lead order (e.g. always naming a specific ingredient first).
- Banned urgency/fear levers.
- Which objections to proactively address vs. which to deliberately leave alone (a less-sophisticated audience may not yet have certain objections that a more sophisticated one does — don't introduce an objection the audience doesn't have yet).
- Confidence vs. evidence calibration — some audiences respond better to a confident one-line dismissal of an objection; others need deeper proof.
