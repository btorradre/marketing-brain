# DR Funnel Strategy

Most accounts fail on architecture, not on individual ads: too much budget at one awareness level, the same personas hit repeatedly, no system for bringing new people in at the top while converting warm ones at the bottom. This skill pulls together whatever angle research, awareness-level audits, market-gap analysis, and voice-of-customer work already exists into a persona architecture, a funnel map, a 90-day roadmap, a tracker naming convention, and three ready-to-execute brief directions. The goal is not to find one winning ad — it is to build creative infrastructure where every dollar spent generates signal that makes the next brief better.

## Golden Nugget Doctrine (applies to every step below)

Before any hook, angle, script, concept, or audit verdict is produced, name the **golden nugget**: the single most emotionally loaded deep frame in the research — the real motive that makes buyers act — never the surface theme.

- **Topic ≠ motive.** "Memory loss" is a topic (surface hook: "my memory feels as young as when I was 25"). The frame is: "I thought I was getting dementia just like my mum did, until I discovered this." Surface angles buy mild hope/curiosity; deep frames trigger identification so strong the reader feels caught.
- **The test.** For every candidate angle ask: *is this the topic, or is this the motive?* If it's the topic, dig one layer deeper.
- **Where it goes.** The golden nugget leads — very top of the piece. Never buried in the body.
- **Deliverable.** State the golden nugget in one explicit sentence before drafting. If the research hasn't surfaced one, keep mining until it does — never default to a surface angle.

## Operating rules

1. **Strategy before execution.** Funnel architecture first, individual briefs after.
2. **Every creative decision is justified.** No format or awareness level is recommended without the reason.
3. **The funnel is not three ads.** It is multiple angles, multiple formats, and multiple personas operating across awareness levels at once.
4. **Frequency is a health metric.** Healthy sits between 2 and 4. Above 5 means the top is starved, and that is diagnosed before any creative is prescribed.
5. **The system compounds.** Every recommendation names what feeds back into the durable angle/research record for next time.

## How to use this

1. **Synthesize — do not re-derive.** This is the one step that reads everything that already exists on the brand: whatever angle bank/research exists, any prior awareness audit, any market-gap analysis, any voice-of-customer index, and whatever performance tracking spreadsheet records what has actually shipped and what it did. If an input is missing, name the gap and go run that research first rather than filling it with assumption — a funnel strategy built on an imagined research base is a document nobody can execute.

2. **Section 1 — Account diagnosis.**
   - Current awareness-level distribution, with counts and denominators.
   - Identified gaps in the funnel.
   - Frequency assessment and what it signals.
   - The single biggest creative bottleneck right now, named in one sentence.
   - Distinguish a creative-supply problem from a delivery problem from an offer problem — prescribing creative for an offer problem burns a quarter.

3. **Section 2 — Persona architecture.** Write 3 to 5 distinct personas. For each: name and description (one specific person in a specific situation, never a demographic), where they sit on the awareness spectrum, the primary pain or desire driving them (quoted from the voice-of-customer research), and one hook direction written specifically for them. Where the brand runs two avatars on one core message, keep them as separate personas in the creative and let the creative self-select — don't split ad targeting on that basis.

4. **Section 3 — Full funnel map.**
   - **Top of funnel (unaware to problem aware):** goal, recommended formats, angle directions, example hook. No product-first opening.
   - **Middle of funnel (problem aware to solution aware):** goal, formats, angle directions, example hook. This is where "failed solution" angles from customer research do their best work.
   - **Bottom of funnel (product aware to most aware):** goal, formats, offer and CTA direction, example static concept. Reviews, proof, guarantee, and specifics.
   - Every angle direction should be traceable to a real research finding. A direction with no traceable source means the research base is thin there — which is itself the finding.

5. **Section 4 — 90-day creative roadmap.**
   - **Phase 1, weeks 1-4, foundation:** priority angles to test and why, minimum creative volume, what signal to look for.
   - **Phase 2, weeks 5-8, validation:** how to read phase 1 data, what to scale/kill/iterate, second-wave brief directions.
   - **Phase 3, weeks 9-12, compounding:** how to iterate winners into new formats and hooks, how to expand proven angles to new personas, what a healthy account looks like by then.
   - Iteration means one axis at a time: same angle new hook, same hook new format, same angle new awareness level. Changing two axes at once produces a result nobody can attribute.

6. **Section 5 — Creative tracker setup.** Recommend (or extend) a single performance-tracking spreadsheet with columns for concept family, parent winner, iteration axis, format, engine/production method, hook summary, and performance fields (spend, CPA, ROAS, hook rate, hold rate, CTR). Recommend a naming convention sortable by avatar, angle, hook, and format: `<AVATAR>_<ANGLE-ID>_<HOOK-N>_<FORMAT>`, e.g. `MENOSKIN_MOT-A-014_H2_UGC` — mirroring the account structure (avatar = campaign, angle = ad set, hook = ad variation). Give one worked example, and explain how to rank after 30 days at both levels: by angle (which ad sets keep getting funded) and by hook within a surviving angle (which variation to iterate). A convention that only supports one of these forces the analysis to be rebuilt every month.

7. **Section 6 — The first three briefs.** In priority order. For each: angle, target persona, awareness level, format, hook direction, and why this is the right brief to run first.

8. **Write and hand off.** Save the finished strategy as a standalone document.

9. **Self-audit before presenting.** Every recommendation traced to a real research finding, no invented frequency or spend figures, every angle direction carrying a source or an explicit note that research is thin there, and every example hook passing the six-month test, the swap test, and the voice-tell check below.

10. **Close with two lines**: brief number one, and the recommended next step. Usually this means writing hook variations for brief one (five hooks aimed at one specific person at one awareness level, each passing the voice-tell/swap/six-month/product-truth/brand-law gates), then a production brief for whichever format was chosen — or a full copywriting pass if brief one is long-form.

## What "the angle bank" means in Section 3 and 4

Wherever this document cites an angle direction, it should trace to a specific angle record — not a vague theme. An angle record is a specific problem in the customer's own words (never a claim), carrying: a short internal name, the problem itself, the golden nugget underneath it, one verbatim source quote, a one-person persona (never a demographic), an awareness level, and a status (fresh / active / fatigued / retired). If no such record exists for a direction cited here, say so plainly — that gap is itself a finding, and the next step is to go build that research rather than inventing the angle on the spot.

## House laws (govern every DR OS step)

**Law 1 — Brand context loads first, always.** Never work a brand from general category knowledge when the answer is in its own research.

**Law 2 — Nothing enters the output unsourced.** Every finding carries a verbatim quote and a traceable source. Format: `"exact quote" — (source, date)`. Never invent a study, sample size, percentage, doctor, press mention, review count, or customer quote. Label inferences `[INFERRED]`.

**Law 3 — Topic is not motive.** The Golden Nugget Doctrine governs every output.

**Law 4 — Six-month shelf life, or it doesn't ship.** No transition/seasonal-moment angles as the idea itself.

**Law 5 — The swap test.** If a competitor's product could drop into the angle and it still reads perfectly, rewrite onto the brand's own facts.

**Law 6 — Competitor intelligence never becomes competitor copy.** No generic competitor mentions, no unnamed price comparisons, no villain, no manufactured problem.

**Law 7 — Every claim gets a demo beat.** Demonstrate, never describe.

**Law 8 — The creator is never the brand.** "They're," "their," "this brand" — never "our," never "we."

**Law 9 — Voice tells that kill a draft.** No em dashes. No "That's not X. It's Y." No parallel repetition stacks. No rhetorical section openers. No "Imagine if" openers. No tidy tricolons. Never personify objects.

**Law 10 — One ad, one awareness level.** Frequency above 5 signals an exhausted audience, not a bidding problem.

**Law 11 — Self-audit before presenting.** Check every draft against Laws 2, 4, 5, 6, 7, 9.

**Law 12 — Flag a conflict once, then build.** State the conflict in one or two sentences, then deliver under a stated assumption.
