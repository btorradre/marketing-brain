# Velantra Inspired Birkin — Video Ad Concept & Brief

Use this when writing a video ad concept and creative brief for the Velantra Inspired Birkin — a structured leather Birkin-silhouette top-handle bag positioned as "the Birkin shape without the logo or the price."

## Golden Nugget Doctrine (apply to every piece of creative you write)

Before writing any hook, angle, script, concept, or audit verdict, name the **golden nugget**: the single most emotionally loaded deep frame in the research — the real motive that makes buyers act, never the surface theme.

- **Topic ≠ motive.** "Memory loss" is a topic (surface hook: "my memory feels as young as when I was 25"). The frame is: "I thought I was getting dementia just like my mum did, until I discovered this." Surface angles buy mild hope/curiosity; deep frames trigger identification so strong the reader feels caught.
- **The test:** for every candidate angle ask "is this the topic, or is this the motive?" If it's the topic, dig one layer deeper (memory loss → becoming my parent; GLP-1 bloat/constipation → exiled from my own dinner table; weight loss → the stolen victory).
- **Where it goes:** the golden nugget leads — right at the top, as the hook. Never buried in the body.
- **Deliverable:** state the golden nugget in one explicit sentence before drafting. When analyzing a reference ad/funnel instead of writing fresh copy, state the nugget it's built on and whether it leads with it. If the research hasn't surfaced one, keep digging through reviews/voice-of-customer/forums until it does — never default to a surface angle.

## Product context (use this, don't re-derive it)

- **Product:** Velantra Inspired Birkin — structured leather Birkin-silhouette top-handle bag.
- **Category:** the flagship Birkin *alternative* — captures the architecture without the brand tax.
- **Price band:** $50–$150 (Velantra) vs. the four-to-five-figure house bag it echoes.
- **Colorways (leather):** jeans-blue, midnight-blue, biscuit, ebene, white-rose, gris-etain, gris-meyer, green-gold (brown/silver hardware), white-gold-brown, white-taupe, white-blue, yellow, pink, plus assorted extra studio angle sets.
- **Reference material:** named leather colorway product shots and multi-angle studio positions, used as image-to-image seeds. There is no existing native UGC library for this product yet — either borrow a beat structure from a sibling product's ripped UGC (the Boat Tote or Straw Birkin lines) or invent one from the avatar and market sophistication below.

### Avatar — the "Velantra Woman" ("Caroline," 40–48)

Coastal-affluent professional, anti-logo, in-the-know. This product sits dead-center on her core tension: she covets the Birkin silhouette but rejects the price, the artificial scarcity, and its ubiquity among the ultra-wealthy. She reframes the alternative not as a "knockoff" but as a superior, more personal choice.

### Market sophistication & awareness

- **Sophistication: 5** — the most claim-saturated angle in the line. "Birkin dupe for less" is dead on arrival. You must lead with a fresh mechanism, a new identity, or a strong concept — never a price comparison.
- **Awareness:** solution-aware moving toward product-aware (she already knows Birkin alternatives exist; the question is why yours).
- **Angle / wound:** "I want the Birkin shape but I refuse to be the woman who paid $12k for a logo — and I don't want a cheap obvious fake either." Vindication: the bag that reads as taste, not as a dupe, and earns the "where did you get that?" from people who actually know bags.

## How to use this

1. Start from the product context above — never ask the user to re-supply product, colorways, avatar, or references.
2. Confirm the format with one question. Default: a TikTok-style UGC "the bag people think is a Birkin" reveal, or a longer-form video sales letter for the full reframe.
3. If no native UGC beat structure exists for this product, mine a sibling product's proven UGC (Boat Tote or Straw Birkin) for a beat skeleton to adapt.
4. Fill out the seven-slot concept flow below, framed for video. At sophistication level 5, push away from any price-led default — lead with identity or mechanism instead.
5. Produce the Video Ad Concept Brief using the template below.
6. Once the concept is approved, hand it to whatever video production pipeline is available (image-to-image product generation, video generation from keyframes, or a UGC-style production process) — this skill only owns the concept and brief.

## Rules & standards

### Editor-facing brief rules (hard rule — never include internal file paths)

Any brief that reaches an editor or gets published for production must be fully self-contained. The editor has no access to your internal files, so never put an internal file path, folder name, or local filename in an editor-facing brief.

**Brevity:** briefs should be short — roughly 5–6 pages max. Don't re-explain your internal production process inside every brief; document that once, separately, and link to it. A brief itself should only carry: the product-truth block, the script, the timeline, the generation prompts, and brief-specific guardrails/QA. Cut strategic exposition (why the angle works, why this format) down to one or two lines — the editor executes, they don't need the reasoning.

1. **Product references → live product page links.** Point the editor at the live product page for the exact colorway/variant, and name the specific gallery photo to reference.
2. **Creator references → a real reference photo plus a written identity block.** Source a candidate creator photo (e.g. via an image search for the right look), and write a verbatim description (age range, hair, build, wardrobe, energy) rather than cloning a real identifiable person's face.
3. **Reference ads → public links only** (an ad library URL or a public platform URL), never a downloaded local video file. If an internal clip has no public URL, describe it beat-by-beat instead of pointing to a path.
4. **No companion files** — everything the editor needs (segment prompts, scripts, tables) should live inline in the brief document itself.
5. **Generated B-roll:** use a still-image generation model for keyframes and a motion/video generation model to animate them. Whatever engine you use, the 3D-model/CGI look is banned — if a frame looks like a computer-rendered 3D model, it must be regenerated, no exceptions. Never ship the first attempt: generate three variants of every scene and pick the one that reads as real photography, not a render.

Internal file paths are fine to use for your own internal generation work — this rule only governs what you hand to an editor or external collaborator.
