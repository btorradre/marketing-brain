# Skeleton Ad B-roll

Explain **why each scene belongs at this line**, then source or generate assets that fulfill that purpose. Use the observed reference as evidence of a visual approach, not as a universal template or proof of conversion.

## Choose the skeleton’s dramatic role

This skill concerns animated skeleton/anatomy characters. A real tabletop skeleton used in a filmed demonstration belongs to the voiceover/demonstration workflow unless the target intentionally combines both.

Choose the actual variant from the reference, rather than turning every skeleton concept into the same clip:

| Variant | Visual organization | What the skeleton contributes |
|---|---|---|
| Parallel comparison | Stable left/right identities, labels and distinct states | The viewer follows two paths without repeatedly relearning who is who. |
| Laboratory character with guide | Large action stage plus small recurring narrator/guide | The actor performs the routine and reveals internal changes; the guide interprets. |
| Translucent lifestyle body | Recognizable domestic actions with visible anatomy, plus appropriate micro views | The same character connects a lived difficulty with an internal explanation and later activity. |

Lock skeletal proportions, skull/eye design, materials, transparency, organ placement, character identifiers and world lighting in reusable approved image references. For two characters, lock which side and color mean which path. Color must have a consistent semantic role; a red-to-gold recolor alone is not a new scene.

## Build a readable action chain

| Line function | Scene family | Why this view, here |
|---|---|---|
| Hook / comparison setup | An immediately readable character action, simultaneous contrast or internal/external contradiction | Establish the specific question before the body becomes an abstract diagram. |
| Daily difficulty or emotional cost | Skeleton doing the affected task in a distinct real-life setting | The skeleton is an actor: posture, task and environment reveal what the problem costs. |
| Consumption / routine | Hand, accurate product and visible ingestion or application action | Establish how the intervention enters the story before jumping inside the body. |
| Mechanism | Organ-location view → relevant tissue, vessel or cell-scale process | Each scale change should answer a new question. Explain the transition and keep orientation legible. |
| Claimed progress / outcome | New activity or changed ability connected to an earlier limitation | Stairs, work, play, clothing or a shared evening are more specific than a glowing body smiling. |
| Product / CTA | Accurate item held or used by the character; intentional real-product bridge if the concept calls for it | Resolve the illustrated story into a tangible object and next action. |

Use a distinct scientific view for each mechanistic idea. Do not repeatedly return to the same translucent torso or stomach cutaway. Avoid suggesting that skeleton visibility makes a claim scientifically established. Fantasy parasites, instant transformations and ingredient effects in source ads are unverified dramatizations; use supported target facts and label illustrative metaphors appropriately.

## Generate and inspect a coherent sequence

Create approved GPT Image 2 character/world and first-frame references, then Google Omni action clips. For each scene specify: character ID, world, framing, actual anatomy needed, starting pose/state, one readable action and ending state, narrative cue, product reference and permitted transition to the next scale. Keep complex lettering, counters and comparisons controllable in the edit.

Example original brief: the established skeleton stands halfway up a domestic stair flight, hand resting on the rail, looking at the remaining steps; preserve the same skull and body proportions; distinct composition from the earlier seated scene. Why: the spoken line describes a task interruption, so the environment explains the cost while keeping the animated world intact.

QA limb/joint continuity, organ location, hand/object contact, ingestion trajectory, consistent comparison sides, readable silhouettes and varied scientific views. In a guide layout protect the guide, main action and captions separately. If switching to native human/product footage, explain the bridge in the plan; a fully animated brief stays animated unless that hybrid is intended.

## Work from this concept, not its label alone

Read the shared [B-roll storytelling skill](../broll-storytelling/SKILL.md) for reference inspection, per-scene reasoning, asset provenance and production handoff. Read [ad editing style](../ad-editing-style/SKILL.md) when analyzing or specifying cuts, transitions, zooms, captions and sound. Use one shared editing plan rather than conflicting plans.

Inspect the actual reference media and narration. Record shot boundaries, visual actions and changes of medium; inspect consecutive source frames around mapped boundaries. A thumbnail, transcript or automated watch manifest cannot establish a frame audit. Separate observed picture, inferred intention and proposed target choice. State the reviewed spans and audio limitations honestly. Read [reference examples](references/reference-examples.md) for this skill’s empirical basis and limits.

For every beat and intentional hold, deliver: exact approved line; visible action; medium; full-frame/inset/split/hold choice; entry and exit cue; provisional duration; incoming/outgoing transition; source or generation brief; asset gap; **Why this scene, here**. Explain the line’s job, what the picture adds, why it suits this concept, and why it enters/exits there. “Adds engagement” is not a reason. A still can be the correct asset when its static detail is the information; use video when change over time matters.

## Production and selection contract

Save/update the concept’s `edit/editing-plan.md` before generating ad assets, voice or a timeline. Include the hook, mechanism/product order, captions/audio direction, CTA/end hold, asset gaps and delivery QA; timing remains provisional until final narration alignment. Planning adds no new approval gate. Research/skill creation alone does not authorize producing an unrelated ad.

When sourcing is requested, search for the specific subject + action + setting + framing, inspect the actual candidate media, select usable in/out points and save its URL, creator/license status and local asset ID. TikTok is a discovery source, not automatic permission for commercial reuse. If the right permitted footage cannot be found, generate an illustrative scene within the target’s authorization rather than silently accepting a weak match. Keep found, generated, illustrative and verified evidence assets distinguishable.

Use GPT Image 2 for first frames/stills and Google Omni for video in this workspace; check the available provider capabilities before promising duration or format. Use approved identity/product references and inspect selected outputs. DaVinci Resolve is the editor; verify the live connection and preserve existing work. HyperFrames and Remotion are banned. A storyboard delivery follows Cut Room’s skill and includes the actual selected first asset set and working board URL. Follow the shared skill and workspace rules for these operations.

Audit the whole sequence for visual variety: each separate B-roll beat needs a distinct composition, subject detail and action. A crop, mirror or recolor is not a new scene. Consistent presenter coverage and a deliberately held demonstration are not excuses to label repeated inserts as fresh B-roll. On visual-only revisions preserve approved narration speed, word alignment and captions.

Do not turn dramatized mechanisms, unrelated footage or generated people into purported scientific evidence, real customer outcomes or verified professional endorsements. Use supported target claims; the reference’s reach does not validate its claims or prove that its visual choices caused performance.
