# B-roll storytelling

Make the image advance the spoken story. First identify what the viewer needs to recognize, understand, believe or do; then select a visible subject and action that accomplishes it. A keyword match is insufficient: a stomach picture can be on-topic while failing to show slow movement, fullness or a recovered meal.

**The central output is an explanation of why each scene was chosen.** The user should be able to understand the editorial decision, not just see a shot list or prompt. Explain why this particular action, visual treatment and placement help tell this part of the story. Use that purpose to guide selection before sourcing/generation, then check that the actual selected asset fulfills it.

Work in this order: **inspect the reference → understand the target concept → establish its visual treatment → map the script → save the editing plan → source/generate matching assets → inspect their fit in sequence**. The concept governs how each story function is represented. An animated concept gets matching animated B-roll, including animated characters for human experiences; a HeyGen-style VSL can mix scientific animation for mechanism education with real photos/videos of people for nightmare-state and desired-outcome lines. Do not apply one reference's medium to every concept.

Apply this across the entire ad. The user's example of human/TikTok/iPhone-style footage during problem agitation illustrates one useful pairing; it does not restrict that footage to agitation or define the skill's scope. The same visual style can serve discovery, explanation, demonstration, proof, payoff or a closing invitation when its content fits. Learn the relationship between the line, image and surrounding shots rather than turning an example into a fixed assignment.

This skill was built from four Resilia references supplied September 10, 2026. Read [reference lessons](references/reference-lessons.md) for source-specific examples and the limits of the review. They demonstrate different visual structures, not one universal formula. The user describes them as high-converting; no performance data was supplied.

## Recover the actual assignment

Read the brief, approved narration, selected presenter/product references and existing editing plan. Preserve format, word order, delivery speed, brand-reveal timing and accepted assets unless the user requests changes. On a visual-only revision, update affected visuals and plan rows; keep approved narration and caption alignment.

Determine which job is authorized: reference analysis, B-roll selection, storyboard, asset production or editing. Analysis and skill creation do not themselves require new ad assets. Do not turn a research request into unsolicited video production.

When reference footage exists, inspect it using [reference audit protocol](references/reference-audit.md). Resolve inaccessible footage from existing downloaded sources/public media where possible. Never invent a shot breakdown from a title, thumbnail, transcript or automatic manifest.

## Establish the concept's visual treatment

For HeyGen-style VSLs, podcasts, skeleton ads, animated ads and AI-voiceover VSLs, select the relevant [concept-specific skill](references/concept-skills.md). These add format-specific decisions and new TrendTrack visual examples; this shared skill still governs scene reasoning and production handoff.

Read [concept matching and production](references/concept-matching.md) before choosing or producing assets. Infer the intended concept from the user's brief, actual reference footage, script, existing board and approved assets. The reference shows an approach; the user's target determines what transfers. When they differ, record the intended adaptation rather than copying the source literally.

Save a compact treatment in the editing plan: concept format; dominant medium; animation or photographic style; character/product identity; world/locations; palette, texture, lighting and camera/motion language; permitted medium changes and their purpose; source/generation approach. Use existing direction before asking for clarification. Ask only when unresolved alternatives would materially change the concept, and continue independent reference analysis while waiting.

| Target concept | Matching B-roll approach |
|---|---|
| Fully animated: 2D, 3D, illustrated, clay-like or another specified style | Generate characters, environments, objects and process scenes in that particular style. Match drawing/rendering, proportions, materials and movement across scenes; include human experiences through animated action. |
| HeyGen-style presenter VSL | Preserve the presenter as the anchor. Use scientific animations for relevant mechanism education; source real photos/videos of people for lived difficulty, nightmare state and desired outcome when that is the intended treatment. Use product footage, stills and diagrams where the line warrants them. |
| Candid UGC / phone footage | Source or generate plausible everyday scenes matching the creator's world; use explanatory animation only where the concept intentionally calls for it. |
| Podcast / interview | Preserve conversational coverage; choose inserts fitting the show's look and the answer's content, which may deliberately mix real footage, photos and scientific animation. |
| Photo-led, editorial, documentary or collage | Source relevant real photos/documents/footage or generate appropriate illustrative assets; preserve the reference's composition and juxtaposition logic. Do not unnecessarily animate every still. |
| Product/lifestyle or another concept | Derive its treatment from the brief/reference: product detail, demonstration, aspirational live action, stylized rendering or an intentional combination. The examples above are not a closed list. |

Keep **semantic variety within stylistic coherence**. Every B-roll scene should add a distinct action/composition, while still looking as though it belongs in this ad. A medium change should be intentional: education may switch to animation in a presenter VSL; an entirely animated story normally stays animated through problem, solution and payoff.


For extreme raw human action clips, TikTok B-roll footage or text-free emotional sourcing, use [TikTok B-roll Sourcing](../tiktok-broll-sourcing/SKILL.md). That specialization requires extreme intensity for every selected clip, verified no-text intervals and an exact visible-action match.

## Choose the structure before individual clips

| Structure | Main picture | B-roll's role | Protect these moments |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fully animated story | Coherent animated world/characters or visual explanation | Matching animated scenes advance every relevant story beat | Consistent style and identities; distinct actions; coherent transitions |
| HeyGen-style VSL | Consistent generated presenter | Deliberately mixed real footage/photos, scientific animation and product assets | Presenter continuity; purposeful medium switches; exact script |
| Personal UGC / discovery story | Consistent human storyteller | Full-frame lived moments, specific attempts, explanatory inserts | Confession, interpretation, reaction, reassurance, personal invitation |
| Direct-to-camera educator | Face/gestures as continuous anchor | Small semantic inserts; full-frame only when the action needs it | Credibility, chapter markers, subjective experience, synthesis |
| Podcast / interview | Speaker turns, listener reactions, consistent eyelines | Brief relevant inserts within the answer | Questions, response openings, reactions, conversational handoffs |
| Narrated visual story | Scene sequence and optionally recurring protagonist | Every image carries a step in the argument | Coherent protagonist, cause/consequence order, visible actions |
| Guided mechanism demonstration | Process imagery with intermittent guide | Human consequences punctuate the explanation; ingredients/product make it tangible | Orientation, pointing, scale changes, interpretation, final action |

Choose deliberately; do not convert every concept into a fully covered montage. A presenter remains human coverage. Strong facial delivery can carry agitation without a separate distressed actor. For each insert choose **full-frame**, **inset**, **split view**, or **stay on presenter** independently of its visual category.

## Select by story function

Use [beat-to-visual matrix](references/beat-to-visual-matrix.md) when mapping a script. Within the established concept treatment, choose **story function**, **subject/action**, **medium/style**, **placement/timing**, and **source/generation route**. The following are content candidates, not exclusive assignments or instructions to break the concept's style. For an animated concept, render the people, everyday settings, objects and actions below in the approved animation style.

| Beat | Useful visual candidates | What must visibly happen |
|---|---|---|
| Recognition / problem agitation | Person/character experiencing the problem in a relevant setting; real photos/video or animated action according to the treatment | The particular frustrating action, limitation or contradiction in the line |
| Failed effort | Person performing the actual attempted solution; specific object detail | Effort and unresolved situation become understandable |
| Emotional stakes | A meaningful activity interrupted or avoided | Show what the problem costs in daily life |
| Discovery / reframe | Presenter, research behavior, or verified relevant document | Introduce the new interpretation without preempting it |
| Problem mechanism | Appropriate diagram/process view, or real demonstration of a visible product/task problem | Before-state → causal action → consequence |
| Solution mechanism | Distinct process view or hands-on product demonstration matching the intervention | Show what changes and why it addresses the established problem |
| Ingredient identity | Recognizable material, preparation or clean ingredient image | Identify what it is; an ingredient still does not explain efficacy |
| Product / use | Accurate product in a real context, hands and actual routine | Show what the buyer gets and does |
| Proof / comparison | Actual relevant evidence, readable comparison or verified demonstration | Make the decision criterion inspectable |
| Payoff | A specific regained activity, ideally answering an earlier loss | Show changed behavior, not a generic smile |
| Offer / CTA | Actual item, destination/policy where relevant, clear gesture | Make the next action and object unmistakable |

**Choose footage style across the whole story.** Phone-style human footage can show discomfort, researching an answer, trying a routine, demonstrating a feature, a genuine customer experience, an enjoyable activity or a product-in-hand invitation. The action and spoken context determine its job. For agitation specifically, trying to finish dinner, pausing on the stairs or leaving bed may communicate more than a vague unhappy face. Use everyday objects alone when the action is the story, such as a fork set beside an unfinished plate. Do not replace lived experience with a glowing organ just because the product is health-related.

Evaluate alternatives for important beats rather than automatically assigning human footage to problems, animation to explanations and polished footage to solutions. A real-world demonstration can explain a mechanism; a casual phone clip can introduce the product or deliver the payoff. A mechanism hook may start inside the body; an educator may remain on camera. Let the approved concept and the line's job determine the choice.

Apply the workspace's September 10 hook instruction: choose an intense, immediately readable opening action or emotion, already underway in the first frame, matched to the spoken hook and the audience's problem/desire. Calibrate intensity against the approved reference and record the choice in the editing plan and selected Cut Room hook card when delivering a storyboard. Ordinary supporting scenes can remain restrained. Increased hook rate is the objective, not an established outcome without campaign data.

## Explain why this scene belongs here

Include a concise, user-facing **“Why this scene, here”** rationale for every proposed/selected scene and intentional presenter hold. Usually one to three sentences is enough; explain complex choices further only when useful. Connect:

- **The line's job:** the specific idea, tension, belief, question or emotion the script introduces at this point.
- **The visual's contribution:** what the actual subject/action makes recognizable or understandable, beyond repeating the narration as a caption.
- **The concept fit:** why this medium/style and full-frame/inset/presenter choice suit this concept and this beat.
- **The sequence and timing:** why it enters on this cue, what it connects to or sets up, and why the action needs this duration or ends here.

Weave these into a concrete explanation rather than repeating four labels on every card. Discuss a plausible alternative only when it clarifies a meaningful choice. “Matches the script,” “creates engagement,” “adds variety” or “shows the desired outcome” alone are insufficient: identify what in this scene does that work. If the same rationale could accompany several unrelated shots unchanged, make it more specific or reconsider the selection.

**Example — lived difficulty in a presenter VSL:** “The nearly untouched plate and the hand setting down the fork make the contradiction visible: she wants to eat but stops after very little. A real domestic clip makes this feel like a recognizable experience; enter on the meal detail, hold through the fork action, then return to her face for her interpretation.”

**Example — mechanism in a fully animated concept:** “This side-on view lets the viewer follow the movement from entry to exit, making the slowdown described in the line visible. Matching the established animation style preserves the world; hold through the change in movement before cutting to the character's resulting difficulty.”

**Example — desired outcome:** “Finishing dinner and staying in the conversation resolves the earlier scene where she had to leave the table. The new action gives relief a concrete social meaning; it belongs at the regained-activity line after the explanation, with enough time to see her remain engaged.”

These are illustrative editorial explanations, not new health claims. For a reference, distinguish observed facts from inferred purpose: explain how the image appears to serve the story, without claiming to know the original creator's intention or to have proved its conversion effect. For a revision, explain why the replacement better serves the same beat or changed concept. Keep the rationale in the editing plan and corresponding Cut Room card notes whenever delivering a storyboard, and revise it if the selected asset changes.

## Map the visual decision

For every meaningful narration beat, specify:

`exact line → story job → viewer takeaway → visible subject/action → concept-matched medium/style → placement → entry cue → action/comprehension duration → exit cue → next-shot relationship → why this scene, here → reuse/source/generate route + selected asset or gap`

Give every B-roll idea an observable verb. “Woman with bloating” is incomplete. “At the dinner table, she stops after two bites, sets down her fork and leans away from the plate” is executable. Choose one dominant action; do not force a miniature five-step story into a two-second insert.

For an internal mechanism, name the location and scale, starting state, moving element, direction/interaction and resulting state. Choose the view that makes that particular process clear. Use an organ view for location, a wall cross-section for a barrier, a channel for transport and a micro view for an interaction only when scientifically appropriate. Keep an analogy distinguishable from literal anatomy. No repeated generic torso standing in for different mechanisms.

For ambiguous lines, prefer the image that clarifies the proposition over the literal noun. “It wasn't my lack of effort” may belong on the speaker; “I stirred powder into water every morning” earns an action insert. If nothing useful is added by covering the presenter, leave the presenter visible.

## Cut when the idea or action changes

For a detailed breakdown or implementation of cuts, transitions, zooms, overlays, captions, sound and pacing, use the companion [ad editing style skill](../ad-editing-style/SKILL.md). Keep scene-selection reasons and editing reasons together in the same plan with shared beat IDs; the editing skill handles how the chosen scenes are assembled.

Place an insert when the relevant referent becomes understandable: the symptom, visible attempt, named object or causal action. Keep it while its action or evidence needs to be read. Return to the presenter when the line interprets, qualifies, reassures or asks the next question.

Use these relationships consciously:

- **Cause → consequence:** internal process → lived difficulty → presenter interpretation.
- **Attempt → gap:** attempted solution → supported explanation of the unresolved gap.
- **Ingredient → action → consequence:** identify the material, show the relevant process, connect it to the intended experience.
- **Cost → regained activity:** establish a specific limitation and later answer it with a distinct positive scene.
- **Separate jobs → combined product:** give inputs separate roles before showing the combined format.
- **Question → explanation → reaction:** preserve podcast turns and return to the listener when the thought is complete.

A new caption, noun or sentence does not automatically require a new shot. A rapid list may use brief recognition images; a demonstration needs time to show its change. Do not impose a universal two-second rule or copy a reference's timestamps, B-roll percentage or product-reveal percentage onto a different voice track.

Default to a direct cut when no special handoff is needed. Use transitions for a reason: a dissolve can connect related scales, a controlled chapter transition can mark explanation becoming payoff. Preserve observed reference treatment when authorized, but do not add a preset to every clip. Maintain continuous approved narration through visual changes. Do not assume music/SFX are absent because the transcript omits them.

## Source or generate the planned B-roll

Once the editing plan exists, carry out the authorized asset work using [concept matching and production](references/concept-matching.md). Choose existing approved assets, found real photos/videos, generated stills or generated video per beat. Source for observable action and visual fit; generate when the concept requires original animation, a specific scene or controlled identity. Do not stop at prompts or search phrases when the user authorized actual sourcing/generation.

For animation, use GPT Image 2 to establish and inspect matching scene images, then Google Omni for the planned animated action. For real human photos/video, inspect actual sources and record provenance. If a generated photoreal reenactment is appropriate, identify it as generated; do not silently substitute it when genuine real footage is required. Keep still photos as stills when that serves the concept. Discover available live sourcing/generation tools and use the relevant approved provider workflow; report an actual capability gap without replacing the requested medium or model silently.

Save the selected files and a beat-linked asset manifest with origin, medium/style, source URL/timecodes or generation provenance, intended action/duration, review status and unresolved gaps. Inspect every selected image and each video's motion, then inspect the sequence for stylistic fit, semantic fit and variety. A source URL or completed generation job alone is not an inspected selected asset.

## When the treatment calls for phone-style footage

Read [human footage and sourcing](references/human-footage.md) before sourcing or generating human scenes. Specify who plausibly held the phone, camera position, ordinary light, real surroundings, performance intensity appropriate to the beat and one readable action. “Shot on iPhone” alone is not a sufficient prompt. Preserve the intense opening-hook requirement; use quieter behavior where the supporting story calls for it.

Choose a plausible capture mode: handheld selfie, family-member observation, mirror phone, first-person object view or propped phone. A propped phone can be steady. Do not add random shaking, heavy grain, beauty lighting, cinematic focus pulls or a slow-motion commercial look to manufacture authenticity.

Separate **appearance** from **origin**. Tag an inspected scene as phone-style if warranted; call it TikTok-sourced, real customer footage, measured before/after or iPhone-captured only with source evidence. Generated reenactments are not genuine customer testimony, clinical footage or verified product results.

## Audit variety at the whole-ad level

Compare every proposed insert by source identity, subject/body detail, setting, framing, action and explanatory job. Use actual thumbnails and motion when available. Two filenames, crops, mirrors, recolors or digital zooms do not establish two unique scenes.

Vary composition and visible action while preserving the deliberate presenter/protagonist and coherent scientific explanation. A recurring person is not repeated B-roll if the scene and action genuinely change. Conversely, changing the person while repeating the same belly-clutch crop is weak variety.

For scientific scenes, change the explanatory view and action, not just the glow color. For payoff, use a new scene that answers the earlier problem; do not replay or recolor the agitation footage. Record intentional repetition in a reference as observed evidence, but follow the user's stricter variety requirement in new work.

On revisions, inspect and select the replacement itself. Replace the visible proposed card image; put old thumbnails in a separate comparison artifact.
