# BTO DTC Marketing Operating System

## Overview

This is the governing marketing doctrine for BTO EC Ventures. Use it to decide what the customer needs to understand, which message and format can do that job, how each funnel stage advances the same conversation, and what the performance data says to change next.

The operating chain is:

`product truth → customer evidence → strategy → creative → funnel continuity → measurement → learning`

Marketing is not the act of forcing beliefs. Start inside the customer's existing understanding. Confirm what is already true, clarify what is uncertain, extend an accepted premise when the evidence supports it, and omit beliefs that do not need work. The best copy lets the customer reach the conclusion herself.

## When to Use

Use this skill for:

- avatar, voice-of-customer, market, competitor, product-market-fit, or positioning research;
- desire, angle, concept, hook, mechanism, offer, or copy strategy;
- written ads, video scripts, UGC, VSLs, advertorials, listicles, PDPs, landing pages, or search ads;
- funnel selection, congruence analysis, CRO, creative assessment, or performance diagnosis;
- creative testing roadmaps, winner analysis, iteration, or scaling decisions.

Do not use it as a substitute for the current brand master brief, product truth file, legal evidence, or format-specific production SOP. Those sources provide the facts and execution constraints; this skill governs the reasoning that connects them.

## Authority Order

When sources conflict, use this order:

1. Product truth, scientific evidence, law, platform policy, and consent
2. Current brand master brief and current offer terms
3. Latest format-specific or production SOP
4. This operating system and the current shared copy strategy
5. Classic direct-response principles
6. Swipes, competitor examples, historical winners, and legacy skills

Recency alone does not override product truth. A winning swipe proves only that a structure may have earned attention or conversion; it does not prove its claims, characters, statistics, urgency, or compliance.

## The Strategy Object

Before drafting, establish the smallest useful strategy object:

- **Sub-avatar:** one person in a recognizable situation, not a demographic cloud.
- **Awareness:** what she already knows and how she feels about it.
- **Sophistication:** how exhausted the market is with claims and mechanisms.
- **Desire:** the category of suffering or progress already present in the market.
- **Angle:** the specific emotional or psychological doorway into that desire.
- **Concept:** the narrator-reader relationship and narrative or visual vehicle.
- **Mechanism:** the plain cause-and-effect explanation connecting problem to solution criteria.
- **Belief job:** which ideas are already held, which need evidence, which need a careful bridge, and which can be omitted.
- **Funnel job:** the exact entry state and exit state of this asset.
- **Offer and proof:** what is actually true, available, and verified.

If any missing field would materially change the work, research it before writing. Do not fill a product-truth gap with a plausible invention.

## Core Decision Laws

### 1. Customer language before marketing language

Collect first and synthesize second. Use verbatim reviews, support tickets, surveys, forums, comments, search queries, and sales conversations. Preserve the source. Separate customer language from competitor language. A pain point is evidence; an angle is a strategic choice built from that evidence.

### 2. Awareness sizes the education

- Unaware and problem-aware audiences need recognition, validation, and emotional clearing.
- Solution-aware audiences need explanation, criteria, and differentiation.
- Product-aware audiences need proof, objection handling, offer clarity, and comparison against the alternatives already in mind.
- Most-aware audiences usually need the offer, the reason to act, and reassurance—not a new lecture.

Awareness includes emotional history, not just information.

### 3. Sophistication sizes the mechanism and objection burden

- Early markets can accept a direct, specific promise.
- Claim-tired markets need a believable reason why.
- Mechanism-tired markets need a genuinely different pathway, stronger proof, or a sharper failure explanation—not a more elaborate name.
- Exhausted markets may respond better to identity, trust, belonging, or brand meaning.

Do not mistake a new brand for a new market.

### 4. Belief work is diagnostic, not compulsory

For each potentially relevant belief, choose one action:

- **Confirm fast:** the customer already believes it.
- **Clarify:** the customer is uncertain and needs a simple explanation or proof.
- **Bridge:** the customer holds an incomplete model; begin with an accepted premise and extend it gradually.
- **Do not fight:** the proposed reframe attacks a foundational belief or lacks enough evidence.
- **Omit:** the belief is irrelevant to this asset's funnel job.

Never rebuild all five classic beliefs merely because a template lists them. Never assault the customer's worldview. The copy earns a conclusion by making the causal chain easier to see.

### 5. Simple mechanism language

Express the mechanism as a short causal chain:

`familiar condition or action → understandable process → consequence → solution criterion`

Use words the customer uses and physical verbs she can picture. Keep one structural metaphor at most. Translate or cut terms that require a search. A proprietary name is useful only when it makes a real mechanism easier to remember and retell; it must not replace the explanation.

The logic supports the proof. It is not proof by itself. Verify every biological, technical, quantitative, and comparative claim.

### 6. Criteria before product; timing remains flexible

Establish what a credible solution must do before asking the customer to judge the product. Once the criteria are understood, the concept and awareness level determine whether the product appears early, late, softly, or directly.

“Product must always be discovered late” is a legacy tactic, not a universal law.

### 7. Congruence means psychological continuity

The exit state of stage N must be the entry state of stage N+1. Preserve and advance:

- the same specific angle or wound;
- the same mechanism language;
- compatible emotional temperature;
- an equal or stronger credibility source;
- the promise made by the click.

The next stage should confirm briefly and add what the previous stage could not. It must not restart, swap wounds, or deliver a different mechanism.

### 8. Length follows belief distance and format

There is no universal long-copy percentage or word count. Use the shortest complete path that can move this audience from the asset's entry state to its exit state. Compression means removing repetition, not removing necessary proof or objections. For video production, obey the latest word-count and asset-math SOP.

### 9. Diagnose before rewriting

Find the first measurable bottleneck. Separate findings from hypotheses. No metric means no metric diagnosis; no upstream and downstream creative means no congruence diagnosis. Quote the actual creative when diagnosing it. Fix the first broken transition before rewriting the whole funnel.

### 10. Learn patterns, not templates

Reverse-engineer the psychological and causal role of a winner. Preserve the transferable principle, then vary narrator, format, setting, emotional engine, hook, discovery vehicle, and close. Never copy characters, scenes, claims, or phrasing as if they were part of the framework.

## Legacy Conflict Rules

These current laws override older Hermes skills and historical documents:

- Confirm held beliefs quickly; do not force every asset through a fixed belief cascade.
- Awareness determines story depth; there is no fixed 55–65% story requirement.
- Criteria must precede product evaluation; reluctant late discovery is optional.
- Product share and reveal timing are format- and awareness-dependent.
- A hook usually opens a loop, but a mechanism-reframe hook may explain the essential new fact.
- Advertorials confirm and escalate; they do not re-teach the ad from zero.
- Listicles are usually product-aware proof and objection bridges, but funnel choice must be based on education burden, trust deficit, mechanism complexity, and actual tests—not “always advertorial.”
- Native editorial readability never authorizes a fake publication, fake byline, fake expert, or hidden sponsorship.
- “No hedging” never authorizes overclaiming. Narrow the claim to what the evidence supports.

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Writing from a framework before reading the customer.** Frameworks organize evidence; they do not manufacture it.
2. **Calling a benefit an angle.** The angle is the doorway or reframe; the benefit is what the customer gets.
3. **Making the mechanism sound intelligent instead of understandable.** Translate until the customer can explain it to a friend.
4. **Using story as camouflage for unsupported claims.** A first-person voice does not convert a claim into evidence.
5. **Treating engagement as conversion proof.** Evaluate the metric for the job of that asset and the downstream result.
6. **Changing multiple variables at once.** Preserve the winner and change one primary axis unless the concept itself is being replaced.

## Verification Checklist

- [ ] Current product truth, brand brief, offer, and claims were read.
- [ ] Customer language is sourced and separated from competitor language.
- [ ] Awareness and sophistication are stated independently.
- [ ] Desire, angle, concept, mechanism, belief job, and funnel job are not conflated.
- [ ] Every important mechanism sentence is simple, causal, and evidence-compatible.
- [ ] Existing beliefs are confirmed or bridged rather than forcibly contradicted.
- [ ] Solution criteria appear before the customer is asked to judge the product.
- [ ] The asset exits at the state the next funnel stage expects.
- [ ] Proof, people, reviews, studies, numbers, urgency, and scarcity are real and traceable.
- [ ] Length is justified by the belief job and the current production SOP.
- [ ] The recommendation identifies the first bottleneck or the exact hypothesis being tested.
