# Psychological appeals and buying decisions

These are interpretations of the sampled advertising, not controlled findings about audience psychology. A trigger earns its place by resolving a specific hesitation. It is not a magic phrase to insert. Choose a primary appeal, then add supporting material only where it advances the decision.

## Choose the appeal from the unresolved hesitation

| Audience hesitation | Appeal and writing decision | Source cases | What the new product needs |
|---|---|---|---|
| “Nobody understands the exact part that bothers me.” | Recognition: name a specific unresolved sensation or routine, then account for it. | L2, E1 | Actual customer language or a clearly hypothetical use situation. |
| “Maybe I am doing everything wrong.” | Validation: recognize effort and explain a supported mismatch, without blanket blame or reassurance. | N1, N3, G1 | Evidence for the proposed explanation; the ad is not a diagnostic source. |
| “I have already wasted money on this.” | Reason to reconsider: turn prior attempts into a shared missing requirement. | G1, A1, NH1 | Accurate comparison and a product that actually addresses that gap. |
| “Solving this means losing something I value.” | Preserve a desired gain: define both sides of the trade-off and show how the product serves the combination. | G2, Z2, F1 | Support for the combined promise. Do not infer medical compatibility. |
| “This sounds like another interchangeable product.” | Selection agency: teach a useful label, construction, or delivery check. | N5, L1, SA1, C1 | A verified attribute with a meaningful role; no unsupported exclusivity. |
| “I do not understand why that feature matters.” | Comprehension: connect feature → action → relevant consequence. | N1, Z1, C1, U1 | A supported literal relationship. An analogy clarifies; it does not prove. |
| “Why should I keep listening?” | Curiosity: open a concrete question with a relevant payoff. | R2, L3, B1 | An honest answer and a clear destination if the answer belongs on an article. |
| “Does this fit someone like me?” | Identification: show the situation and practical constraint rather than demographic shorthand. | C2, G3, AR1 | Relevant use evidence. Do not invent customer results or personal history. |
| “Can I trust this explanation?” | Earned authority: accurate expertise, transparent commercial role, and useful reasoning. | N1, R1, P1, U3 | Actual qualifications and evidence. Costumes and study-style visuals do not suffice. |
| “What would improvement actually mean?” | Concrete future use: return to a valued daily activity. | L2, Z2, C2, B2, F6 | Supported benefits or accurately attributed experience; no invented timelines. |
| “Will it be inconvenient or create another downside?” | Friction reduction: answer routine, suitability, or use questions with the actual facts. | G1, S1, U3 | Approved directions and substantiated downside claims. Never default to “no side effects.” |
| “Is the quality worth the price?” | Participatory proof and value: let the viewer inspect relevant evidence and connect it to use. | F4, F2 | Genuine comparison assets, materials, and prices. |
| “Why buy from this seller?” | Founder motivation and product decisions: explain the real unmet requirement behind the design. | A2 | Verified history and transparent commercial motivation. |
| “Why act now?” | A real offer or availability event: explain terms and next action clearly. | U2, F5 | Current offer, stock, or deadline evidence. Historical swipe terms expire. |

## Marketing devices belong at different points

**Opening:** recognition, a concrete question, a relevant contradiction, or a visible comparison. The hook should identify the promised payoff. A striking line that attracts people interested in a different subject weakens the pitch even if it gets attention.

**Explanation:** why a specific attempt left a gap, what action is needed, and why a product feature performs that action. This is where “new mechanism” rhetoric does its work. Use ordinary function when that is sufficient; do not invent proprietary names or clinical causes.

**Product selection:** checklist match, convenient combination, discovery, or early reveal. Read `structures-and-bridges.md` for the modes. Distinguish an ingredient's possible role from proof for the commercial formulation. The same category can carry contradictory ad narratives: Nivara sells a swallowed formulation while CAVAÉ sells a different delivery route. Their coherence does not establish which scientific claims are true.

**Proof:** match the evidence to the uncertainty. Visible packing can support a capacity demonstration for the actual bag; a customer quote can establish what that person reported; a finished-product study may support only its studied outcome and population. These forms of proof are not interchangeable. Competitor metrics and testimonials never migrate with the script structure.

**Close:** restore the opening activity, explain the actual routine and action, and state real terms where relevant. Risk reversal requires an actual guarantee. Scarcity requires actual limited availability. Social proof requires real evidence. Do not add these devices automatically.

## Hook-to-body congruence

For each proposed hook, record privately:

`Who recognizes it / question it opens / answer the body supplies / feature that makes the answer relevant / next action.`

If the body answers another question, repair the connecting passage or change the hook. Example: a label-inspection opening requires an actual inspection criterion, while an expense-ledger opening requires an explanation of prior failure. The same generic ingredient paragraph will not necessarily satisfy both.

In hook-only tests, keep speaker, supported mechanism, product, proof and offer stable where practical. Change the entry point and necessary transition. If an opening changes the audience, core problem, or promise, label it an angle test and adapt the body accordingly. Similar competitor ads show related executions; they do not reveal the competitors' experimental controls.

## What to avoid transferring from the swipes

- **Fear ladders (S2):** escalating disease claims can create suspense, but the underlying medical story is unverified. Do not imply inevitable progression or substitute a supplement for care. Transfer only the idea of an ordered explanation when its steps are accurate and relevant.
- **Borrowed authority (P2):** retelling a partner's explanation changes narrator position, but does not authorize an invented practitioner or endorsement.
- **Shock (R3):** the unusual incident earns relevance only when the story moves into the viewer's actual concern. Profanity and embarrassment are not compulsory authenticity markers.
- **Social status (F7):** named celebrities, awards, popularity, and sellouts remain historical advertising assertions unless independently supported for the new brand.
- **Timelines (R4, Z2):** progressive story beats can aid comprehension; exact results by day or week require evidence. Do not manufacture a slow first week to make an invented testimonial sound credible.

## Adaptation examples: original exercises

These are fictional product exercises, not source quotations or actual customer experiences. Assumed product facts: an organizer has separate cable channels, retaining straps, and a rigid base. No speed measurement or protection claim is supplied.

**Recognition opening:** “Ever pull out your charger and bring the headphones with it?” The body should explain how separate channels and straps keep coiled cables separated. It should not jump into an unrelated premium-material pitch.

**Inspection opening:** “Before choosing a cable organizer, check what keeps each cable in its own space.” Demonstrate the channels and straps; do not claim it is the only organizer that has them.

**Preserve-the-gain opening:** “Want to switch bags without repacking every cable?” Explain that the organizer moves as a unit and the rigid base keeps it from folding. Do not invent a measured time saving.

**Product-first opening:** “This is LoopDock. Each cable gets its own strapped channel.” Explain the corresponding function immediately. A hidden-cause lesson and late reveal would add unnecessary work for this product-aware demonstration.

These four openings can share verified product facts. They still need different transition sentences because they open different questions.

## Evaluate the requested copy

Check the opening's payoff, unsupported premises, narrator truthfulness, explanation depth, feature relevance, proof fit, emotional closure, destination, and runtime estimate. Fix actual failures rather than chasing a numerical score.

For a requested testing plan, distinguish attention and retention from qualified clicks, destination behavior, and purchase economics. A curiosity click is not a purchase. State the primary variable and decision metric for the actual funnel. Neither historical reuse nor this editorial review proves conversion lift.
