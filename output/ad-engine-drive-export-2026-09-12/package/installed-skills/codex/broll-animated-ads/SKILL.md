# Animated Ad B-roll

Explain **why each scene belongs at this line**, then source or generate assets that fulfill that purpose. Use the observed reference as evidence of a visual approach, not as a universal template or proof of conversion.

## Establish the visual world before choosing scenes

Identify the exact animation treatment: line work or rendering, proportions, surfaces, lighting, palette, character acting and camera behavior. “Animated” is not a sufficient generation prompt. A flat 2D emotional story and a glossy 3D internal-body adventure need different assets even when their scripts share a problem/solution structure. Read the skeleton skill as well only if skeleton/anatomy characters are central.

Choose the dominant storytelling approach, allowing hybrids:

- **Character emotion:** the protagonist’s attempts, reactions and relationships carry the meaning. Mechanism scenes may be brief or absent.
- **Visible mechanism:** the protagonist introduces an experience, then internal locations or personified elements make otherwise invisible actions intelligible.
- **Object/process explanation:** materials, machines or objects perform the action; anthropomorphic characters are optional, not required by this skill.

## Translate each story function into this world

| Story need | Animated scene choice | Why / continuity |
|---|---|---|
| Recognition and hook | Specific action already underway, with a strong readable silhouette or expression | The first frame should make the situation intelligible without relying only on captions. |
| Emotional stakes | A relationship, desired activity or familiar task disrupted | Show what matters to this character; a generic sad face is often insufficient. |
| Failed effort | The character performs the actual attempted solution, sees a limited result, then encounters recurrence | Cause and consequence create the need for a new explanation. |
| Mechanism | A deliberately designed process with starting state, change and consequence | Keep scale and visual vocabulary understandable; a decorative glow does not explain a causal step. |
| Ingredient list | Distinct identity, location and action for each relevant element | Reusing one particle burst for every ingredient hides their differences. Personification is an option only when compatible with the chosen tone. |
| Solution / routine | Accurate product and a simple character action | Make the transition into a new attempt concrete. |
| Payoff | Regained activity or relationship that answers an earlier loss | Return to the character’s world; preserve identity so the change has meaning. |
| Close | Recognizable item and action cue in the approved style; deliberate graphic card if needed | Maintain the concept through the purchase decision. |

Animate human experiences as matching characters in a fully animated concept. Do not drop unrelated photorealistic stock into a 2D story merely because the line mentions a person. A product graphic, real evidence excerpt or review card is a purposeful exception only when the treatment explicitly permits it; source authenticity still matters.

## Production briefs and acceptance

Create reusable character, environment, prop and palette references with GPT Image 2 before generating scene first frames and Google Omni clips. Keep approved faces, silhouettes and product details stable; variation belongs in actions, viewpoints and settings. Supply each clip’s start state, action trajectory, end state and incoming/outgoing relation. Retain a reference frame for identity, not as an excuse to repeat its composition.

Example original scene rationale: the dog backs away while its owner moves to the far side of the sofa; the line describes lost closeness, so two characters’ changing distance expresses the consequence more clearly than another ear close-up. A later distinct cuddle scene can resolve that cost. For a mechanism scene, name the relevant structure and supported action rather than asking for “scientific magic.”

Inspect first frames and motion for character drift, unwanted style switches, broken contacts, floating props, changing anatomy, unclear action and product deformation. Review emotional progression across the ad, not only image beauty. Reject a payoff that does not answer a previously established desire. Frame-to-frame continuity and varied compositions must coexist.

## Work from this concept, not its label alone

Read the shared [B-roll storytelling skill](../broll-storytelling/SKILL.md) for reference inspection, per-scene reasoning, asset provenance and production handoff. Read [ad editing style](../ad-editing-style/SKILL.md) when analyzing or specifying cuts, transitions, zooms, captions and sound. Use one shared editing plan rather than conflicting plans.

Inspect the actual reference media and narration. Record shot boundaries, visual actions and changes of medium; inspect consecutive source frames around mapped boundaries. A thumbnail, transcript or automated watch manifest cannot establish a frame audit. Separate observed picture, inferred intention and proposed target choice. State the reviewed spans and audio limitations honestly. Read [reference examples](references/reference-examples.md) for this skill’s empirical basis and limits.

For every beat and intentional hold, deliver: exact approved line; visible action; medium; full-frame/inset/split/hold choice; entry and exit cue; provisional duration; incoming/outgoing transition; source or generation brief; asset gap; **Why this scene, here**. Explain the line’s job, what the picture adds, why it suits this concept, and why it enters/exits there. “Adds engagement” is not a reason. A still can be the correct asset when its static detail is the information; use video when change over time matters.

## Production and selection contract

Save/update the concept’s `edit/editing-plan.md` before generating ad assets, voice or a timeline. Include the hook, mechanism/product order, captions/audio direction, CTA/end hold, asset gaps and delivery QA; timing remains provisional until final narration alignment. Planning adds no new approval gate. Research/skill creation alone does not authorize producing an unrelated ad.

When sourcing is requested, search for the specific subject + action + setting + framing, inspect the actual candidate media, select usable in/out points and save its URL, creator/license status and local asset ID. TikTok is a discovery source, not automatic permission for commercial reuse. If the right permitted footage cannot be found, generate an illustrative scene within the target’s authorization rather than silently accepting a weak match. Keep found, generated, illustrative and verified evidence assets distinguishable.

Use GPT Image 2 for first frames/stills and Google Omni for video in this workspace; check the available provider capabilities before promising duration or format. Use approved identity/product references and inspect selected outputs. DaVinci Resolve is the editor; verify the live connection and preserve existing work. HyperFrames and Remotion are banned. A storyboard delivery follows Cut Room’s skill and includes the actual selected first asset set and working board URL. Follow the shared skill and workspace rules for these operations.

Audit the whole sequence for visual variety: each separate B-roll beat needs a distinct composition, subject detail and action. A crop, mirror or recolor is not a new scene. Consistent presenter coverage and a deliberately held demonstration are not excuses to label repeated inserts as fresh B-roll. On visual-only revisions preserve approved narration speed, word alignment and captions.

Do not turn dramatized mechanisms, unrelated footage or generated people into purported scientific evidence, real customer outcomes or verified professional endorsements. Use supported target claims; the reference’s reach does not validate its claims or prove that its visual choices caused performance.
