# AI UGC VSL Scripting

Build a spoken argument the listener can follow once. Make each product feature relevant by explaining its job. Preserve the requested product, audience, voice, duration, awareness level, and destination.

This skill derives from the September 2026 TrendTrack study: 29 tracked entries, 233 distinct retrieved transcripts, 49 selected cases, and sampled frames from 17 videos. Reuse and longevity informed pattern selection; they do not prove profitable scale. The sampled advertising's health claims, testimonials, credentials, and AI origin were not independently verified. Use the structural lessons, not competitors' claims as product evidence.

## Start with the brief

Resolve from supplied material or existing product references:

- Product and relevant verified features; supported mechanism and evidence limitations.
- One primary audience situation, its current explanation, and the desired change.
- Speaker: actual customer, founder, qualified expert, demonstrator, or fictional narrator.
- Audience awareness and action: purchase, product-page visit, article click, or another specified destination.
- Requested format, duration or word limit, actual offer and guarantee, and any existing script to preserve.

Use available context before asking. Choose a reasonable voice, structure, or planning pace when those are optional. If the product or evidence needed for the central promise is missing, ask one targeted question while completing any independent outline work. Never silently invent a clinical mechanism, first-person experience, study, credential, result, discount, stock shortage, or guarantee. An illustrative fictional story must remain identifiable as fiction rather than a fabricated customer account.

For a narrow edit, fix the requested passage and its immediate transitions. Do not rebuild the entire funnel or require a full intake when the available script answers the relevant questions.

## Extract and budget before drafting a VSL

For VSL analysis, a new full VSL, or a substantial structural rewrite, read [VSL extraction and timing](references/vsl-extraction-and-timing.md). Use supplied full references first; when category discovery is requested, use the available ad-research connector. Extraction works without a specific vendor or a five-ad minimum. Report actual coverage rather than inventing a cohort.

Build an ordered beat map and allocate seconds and spoken words before drafting. Separate **observed source timing**, **text-derived estimates**, and **proposed target timing**. Track UMP and UMS separately, recurring curiosity loops and proof, and solution introduction versus product introduction versus spoken brand. The approved reference's wording is a voice benchmark; its length is not automatically the new runtime target.

For a first-person, problem-aware customer-discovery VSL, read [customer-story structure and voice](references/customer-story-structure-and-voice.md). Its default progression is hook → agitation → personal consequence → renewed curiosity → discovery → UMP → prior-solution interpretation → solution question/criteria → UMS → relevant selection distinction → product → use/experience → emotional payoff → offer/action. Preserve user-approved ordering. Compress optional journeys and duplicate scenes before deleting the UMP-to-UMS bridge. Other narrator/awareness modes still use the structures below.

## Choose the argument before polishing the voice

For a fresh script or structural rewrite, consult [structures-and-bridges.md](references/structures-and-bridges.md) and use the mode matching the brief. The common argument is:

**Lived frustration → explanation of previous failure → understandable cause → solution requirements → product fit → relevant proof → restored activity → next action.**

This is a dependency map, not a compulsory beat list. Product-aware ads may start with the product. A simple demonstrable product may need very little mechanism explanation. An article bridge may leave the product argument to its destination. Expert, founder, and customer narrators need different reasons for knowing what they say.

Privately identify the few beliefs that actually need to change. For each, record:

`current belief → required belief → supported reason → practical consequence → next question`

A belief shift is not a slogan denying the old belief. It needs a reason the listener can understand and a consequence that matters to the purchase. Do not attack all alternatives; discuss only the relevant, supportable limitations.

Distinguish:

- **Problem mechanism:** the supported account of why the relevant problem occurs or persists.
- **Solution mechanism:** the action that addresses that problem.
- **Product:** the concrete way to perform that action, with evidence for its relevant features.

Do not manufacture a hidden cause just because a competitor used one. Ordinary function, delivery, construction, compatibility, or convenience can supply the product rationale.

## Control explanation depth

Before writing a mechanism passage, identify what the audience already knows and the one question the passage must answer. Explain only the missing links that change understanding or choice.

A sufficiently explained passage lets the listener answer, where relevant:

1. What is happening, in ordinary language?
2. How does that produce the specific problem just described?
3. Why does the previous approach leave this particular gap?
4. What must a solution do, and how does the relevant product feature do it?

These answers may span several sentences. Do not force all four into one sentence or every paragraph. Omit a question already answered or irrelevant to this audience. If evidence does not establish an answer, qualify or narrow the argument rather than inventing a causal link.

**Under-explained:** a label substitutes for an explanation; a technical noun has no ordinary meaning; the script jumps from symptom to ingredient; an analogy lacks a literal connection; the feature is named without explaining its job. Repair the missing link before adding emotion or certainty.

**Over-explained:** multiple sentences restate the same proposition; extra biology never changes the decision; the same cause gets several metaphors; every alternative receives a separate lecture; the script keeps teaching after the product criteria are clear. Remove the repeated or irrelevant material while retaining the causal bridge.

**Stopping rule:** move forward once the listener has enough supported information to understand the next decision. Another sentence should add a necessary cause, distinguish a relevant option, answer an unresolved objection, or make the consequence concrete. Otherwise cut or combine it.

Before a first draft or substantial prose rewrite, read the relevant examples in [sentence-workshop.md](references/sentence-workshop.md). Use the examples to calibrate depth, not as reusable copy.

## Sentence structure and spoken continuity

These rules are central to the skill, including during short edits:

- **One main proposition per sentence.** A cause and its immediate consequence can stay together. Split when a sentence introduces multiple independent claims, unrelated conditions, or unfamiliar concepts that cannot be followed in one hearing.
- **Complete thoughts before emphasis.** Use a short sentence or fragment to land a point already established. Avoid chains of isolated adjectives, noun fragments, or tiny sentences that sever cause from consequence.
- **Give technical terms a job.** Introduce a necessary term with its plain-language meaning, then connect it to the familiar symptom or task. Omit terminology that contributes no useful distinction.
- **Keep subject and referent clear.** Replace “it,” “this,” “they,” or “that” with the actual noun when two possible referents compete. Make the actor and action understandable before adding qualifications.
- **Use connectors for real relationships.** “Because” supplies a cause, “so” a consequence, “but” a genuine turn, and “and” a related addition. Use them where needed; do not require a percentage of sentences to begin with them. A connective cannot repair a missing premise.
- **Vary sentence length by function.** Let an explanatory sentence carry a relationship, a shorter sentence state its consequence, and a concrete detail make it recognizable. Do not impose a universal word count, breath count, or long-short rhythm.
- **One stable analogy at a time.** Map it to the literal process and stop once it helps. Do not let a metaphor imply efficacy or precision absent from the evidence. Do not switch from doors to batteries to drains for the same idea.
- **Link sentences through thought, not just vocabulary.** The next sentence should answer the current question, supply needed evidence, resolve an objection, or introduce a relevant new consequence. Avoid abrupt jumps from education to an unrelated sale.
- **Speak from the narrator's actual position.** A customer may explain what they learned in ordinary language; do not make them recite unsupported expert conclusions. An expert needs actual qualifications, not a costume or invented title. A founder must not sound like a commercially disinterested reviewer.
- **Use concrete language without fabricated specificity.** An everyday activity can clarify a supported use case. Exact lab numbers, timestamps, quotations, outcomes, and personal scenes require a basis if presented as real.

Treat repeated “here's the thing,” “let me explain,” and “that's when everything changed” as candidates for removal. Keep an invitation to listen only when it earns attention or clarifies a genuine transition. Do not add verbal stumbles, slang, profanity, or dialect merely to signal authenticity.

For every paragraph, test: **What new thing does the listener understand, and what question are they ready to ask next?** If neither changes, remove the paragraph or replace it with the missing explanation. Emotional recognition is a legitimate contribution; it does not need a technical fact to justify every sentence.

## Make the product introduction follow

Choose the bridge from [structures-and-bridges.md](references/structures-and-bridges.md): checklist match, complete option, discovery, early reveal, or article bridge.

For a mechanism-led script, establish a feature's relevance before asking the listener to value it. The product introduction should answer the purchasing question raised by the previous passage. Avoid a feature list that appears without a reason or “only formula” claims without substantiation.

Track three distinct moments when useful: **solution category introduced, product visible, brand spoken.** Do not treat transcript word position as video time. Do not require the brand to appear in the last quarter: the source sample includes both early and late reveals.

Close with the same activity or constraint that made the opening matter. Match proof to the claim it supports. Ingredient presence does not prove clinical outcomes; a credential does not prove effectiveness; a testimonial does not establish typical results. State the actual next action and offer terms. Do not create scarcity to compensate for a weak argument.

## Draft, then revise for the listener

1. Draft the causal and emotional progression in plain spoken language. Keep the internal belief map out of the script unless the user requests strategy notes.
2. Read once for **missing links**. Fill only the explanation needed to reach the next belief.
3. Read once for **excess**. Remove duplicate reassurance, repeated science, extra metaphors, and transitions that announce rather than advance.
4. Read once for **speech**. Fix clause overload, fragments, unclear pronouns, tongue-twisting phrases, and inappropriate narrator knowledge. Simulate a spoken read if audio is unavailable; do not claim a recorded read was tested.
5. Check requested length. Prefer measured speaker pace when available. Otherwise state a planning assumption and estimate duration from spoken words plus pauses. A word-count estimate is not rendered runtime. Shorten examples, secondary objections, and repeated setup before deleting the causal links that make the product understandable. For a very short ad, choose a narrower promise.
   For a full VSL, reconcile the draft against the preallocated beat budget. Count one selected hook per runtime; report alternate hooks separately. Confirm that explanations pay off their curiosity loops, each UMS job answers the established problem or requirement, and the product meets the criteria already taught. Audit adjacent beats for question → answer → reaction → next question continuity.
6. Apply [review-rubric.md](references/review-rubric.md). Repair actual failures. Do not pad the deliverable with a checklist or iterate endlessly toward a numerical score.

## Deliver the requested artifact

**Storyboard delivery:** Whenever this workflow produces a storyboard for Brooks, create or update it in **Cut Room**, under the correct brand project, and deliver the verified board URL in the same turn. Include the first generated asset set with every storyboard: consistent presenter, every distinct covering scene/B-roll insert and overlay, and a first image for every planned video scene. Generate with the workspace-approved image model, inspect actual outputs and attach them to their Cut Room cards. Text-only boards are an interim state unless explicitly requested as the final scope. Read the workspace’s Cut Room skill/documentation and use its board builder; standalone HTML or Markdown does not complete storyboard delivery. Preserve approved narration and put competitor reference frames in a separate reference lane. Keep DaVinci Resolve as the production editor when specified by the workspace.

When handing an approved VSL to storyboarding, preserve its exact narration and hook variants as the source of truth. Map performance, reactions, visuals and cut cues to those words; do not silently rewrite to fit an older runtime or make the narrator sound more like an ad. Use the workspace's current editor and editing-plan instructions. Keep script-derived timing provisional until voice alignment, and distinguish the observed reference edit from proposed staging when the source video is unavailable. An AI UGC “yapper” is a continuous conversational performance, not a requirement to add a new B-roll shot to every sentence. Match the actual reference's coverage and cut rhythm when inspected.

Default: a ready-to-speak script in connected paragraphs, with speaker labels only for multiple voices. Add visual notes only when requested or necessary to understand a visual claim; separate them from spoken copy. For an article bridge, make the promised information and destination explicit.

For a revision, provide the clean revision and briefly explain material changes to logic or meaning. If requested, include alternatives that test one identified variable while keeping the mechanism and other material facts consistent. Do not silently expand one requested script into a full campaign.

Add a short word-count/runtime estimate when duration matters and brief notes for any unresolved fact that affects use. Do not hide unsupported placeholders inside a supposedly finished script. If the user asks for only the script, honor that format while resolving essential missing claims before presenting it as final.
