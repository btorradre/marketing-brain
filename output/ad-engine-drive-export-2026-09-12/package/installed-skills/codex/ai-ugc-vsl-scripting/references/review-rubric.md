# Semantic review before delivery

Review the script against its actual brief. This is an editing aid, not a score to publish or a reason to force every script into the same structure.

## 1. Grounding and narrator

Can every product fact, real experience, result, quoted statement, credential, price, deadline, and guarantee be traced to supplied or verified material? Does the speaker have a legitimate basis for the knowledge attributed to them?

**Repair:** remove or qualify unsupported assertions; change the narrator format if appropriate; ask for an essential missing fact. Do not replace missing evidence with certainty, a metaphor, or an invented number.

## 2. Explanation completeness

At the product introduction, can the target listener explain why this feature or combination is relevant? Can they follow the connection from the familiar problem to the proposed action? Did the script explain a necessary term rather than merely name it?

**Repair:** add the smallest missing causal relationship. A complete explanation can require an additional sentence; brevity does not excuse a logical jump.

## 3. Explanation proportionality

Does every explanatory passage answer a necessary question? Is the same proposition being repeated with different adjectives or several analogies? Has a useful explanation become a category lecture?

**Repair:** keep the clearest causal explanation and the most relevant consequence. Shorten secondary examples before removing the reason the product makes sense. Move on when the next decision is understandable.

## 4. Sentence-level comprehension

Can each sentence be understood in one hearing? Is its main proposition identifiable? Are the subject and pronouns clear? Have fragments separated ideas that need to stay connected? Does a connector express an actual relationship?

**Repair:** split independent ideas; reconnect cause and immediate consequence; name the referent; replace unnecessary terminology; remove ornamental transitions. Do not mechanically shorten every sentence or add “so” to every opening.

## 5. Belief progression and attention

What belief or emotional recognition changes in each passage? Is the next passage a natural response? Are promised answers delivered without artificial delay? Does the script explain prior failure rather than just declare the old approach wrong?

**Repair:** supply the missing reason or change the order. Remove repeated anticipation that postpones an answer already earned by the viewer. Emotional scenes should change the stakes or make the consequence recognizable.

## 6. Product bridge and proof

Does the chosen bridge fit awareness and destination? Do the criteria explain the product's relevant features? Does the proof support the exact promise rather than a stronger one?

**Repair:** establish the feature's job, narrow the claim to its evidence, or choose an appropriate earlier reveal. Do not force a late reveal or invent a unique mechanism for a straightforward demonstration.

## 7. Closure and action

Does the outcome resolve the opening frustration or activity? Is the next step actually available? Does an article ad promise the article, and a product ad explain its product sufficiently?

**Repair:** connect the result to the opening scene and state the actual action. Do not add a sale, urgency, or guarantee by default.

## 8. Length and requested scope

Does spoken copy fit the requested word limit or a transparent duration estimate? Are visual notes excluded from spoken word counts? Does a narrow revision remain narrow?

**Repair:** narrow the promise for a short ad; remove redundant setup, extra examples, and secondary objections. Preserve enough explanation to make the pitch intelligible. If an exact runtime depends on delivery, label the estimate accurately.

## 9. Full-VSL extraction, budget and continuity

For a full VSL or structural review: were the source beats mapped before drafting, with measured versus estimated timing distinguished? Does the allocated runtime sum correctly, with only one hook selected and pauses/holds accounted for? Are UMP, UMS and product entry distinct? Are repeated proof elements and loop payoffs visible? Can each beat be explained as the narrator's response to the preceding information or feeling?

**Repair:** correct source coverage and timing labels, restore the missing problem-to-solution bridge, or replace an announced transition with the naturally arising question. Do not flatten different archetypes into a fictitious average sequence. Preserve the user's approved copy during analysis; a timing finding does not authorize an unrelated rewrite.

## Adversarial checks for explanation depth

Use the checks relevant to the draft:

- Remove the ingredient name. Is there still a clear action the viewer understands, or was the name doing all the explanatory work?
- Remove the analogy. Can you state the literal supported relationship? If not, the analogy may be concealing a gap.
- Ask why the previously tried option leaves this gap. Does the script give a reason, or only dismiss it?
- Point to each product feature. Which requirement introduced earlier does it meet? For product-first scripts, where is its relevance subsequently established?
- Delete the second explanation of the same idea. Did any necessary meaning disappear? If not, keep it deleted.
- Read the preceding sentence before each “so” or “because.” Does the conclusion actually follow?
- Read only the opening and the outcome. Does the outcome resolve that specific situation?

Deliver when the brief is satisfied and the applicable failures are repaired. Describe material limits briefly; do not claim the script is proven to convert, medically validated, or verified at an exact runtime from this review alone.
