# Believable human B-roll and source selection

Use this guidance wherever human or phone-style footage fits the story: hooks, discovery, attempts, explanations, product use, authentic proof, enjoyable outcomes and CTA as well as agitation. Natural capture style does not determine the emotional tone or story stage. Select the action for the current line first.


For the user's TikTok B-roll sourcing request, apply [TikTok B-roll Sourcing](../../tiktok-broll-sourcing/SKILL.md). Every clip selected through that specialization requires extreme visible intensity (EV 5/5), an exact action/context match, a raw organic look and a verified text-free selected interval. Its explicit extreme-only sourcing standard takes precedence over the quieter examples below for that task; the general guidance remains available for other briefs.

## Choose the observed moment

Write the scene in this order:

1. Story role and exact cue: what the line means here.
2. Subject: approved protagonist or relevant illustrative person; age/life context where supplied.
3. Situation: a specific room/activity and the reason a camera could plausibly be there.
4. Visible action: one gesture or interrupted task with a readable result; calibrate intensity to the beat. The opening uses the user's intense first-frame hook direction, while everyday supporting scenes can be quieter.
5. Capture: camera holder, distance, height, lens behavior and motion.
6. Light and surroundings: ordinary available light, plausible lived-in details, skin/clothing texture.
7. Timing: entry state, useful action, exit state; realistic duration before generation.
8. Identity/reference constraints and things that would contradict the scene.

Choose natural imperfection selectively. Slight reframing, uneven window light, a normal room and an unperformed expression often suffice. Random shake, exaggerated noise, simulated glitches, glamour skin and shallow-focus beauty lighting can all make the result less plausible.

## Capture modes

| Mode | Plausible image | Useful for |
|---|---|---|
| Propped phone | Steady medium/wide; person moves within frame | Bedroom routine, kitchen waiting, meal interruption |
| Family-member phone | Observational angle and slight natural handheld drift | Family participation, walking, everyday consequences |
| Handheld selfie | Face close to camera, plausible arm distance | Personal admission, reaction, conversational payoff |
| Mirror phone | Phone/reflection geometry and gaze agree | Clothing check, dressing, fit; avoid impossible reflections |
| First-person detail | Hands and actual objects from a plausible viewpoint | Preparing a drink, retrieving an item, product routine |
| Candid close detail | Observable part of a task; contextual objects explain it | Fork set down, waistband adjustment, product interaction |

Don't make every person stare at the lens. In a lived moment they should attend to the task. Keep deliberate talking-head footage consistent; supporting scenes can vary angle, location and outfit while preserving identity when required.

## Found footage

Search using `specific action + context + relevant audience`, not just the product category. Candidate research phrases might be “after dinner uncomfortable setting fork down”, “night waking getting out of bed”, “trying to finish small meal”, or “searching for keys inside tote”. They describe observable behavior, not medical diagnoses.

Inspect the actual candidate clip through the intended in/out. Record source URL/creator, original file, useful timecode, visible action, framing, length, embedded text/watermarks, audio relevance and usage/permission status if known. A downloaded competitor ad is reference evidence, not automatically an approved production asset. Don't represent found footage as a customer endorsement.

Reject clips whose expressions, setting, product, age/context, action or time of day contradict the narration. Inspect what happens immediately before and after the selected interval; a smiling reaction or unrelated reveal can reverse the meaning. Prefer another source or an explicitly generated reenactment when no usable fit exists.

## Generated footage

Use GPT Image 2 for the approved first image and Google Omni for the motion. Preserve existing authorized provider settings where relevant. A sample direction:

> Vertical propped-phone view from the far end of an ordinary dining table. The approved mature protagonist sits beside a small partly eaten dinner. She quietly puts the fork down, shifts back from the table and pauses with one hand resting against her side. She looks at the plate, not the lens. Mixed window and room light, real skin texture and normal home details. One continuous restrained action, no cinematic camera move or dramatic pain performance. Preserve the approved identity. Requested motion duration: [actual needed duration].

This is an appearance/behavior brief, not evidence that a real event occurred. Do not manufacture authentic customer results, practitioner credentials, measured before/after footage or real laboratory evidence.

Inspect the selected image, then the generated motion. Check hands, facial expression, body/phone/reflection geometry, product identity, action completion and whether the motion reads naturally at normal speed. A good first frame is not sufficient QA for a video.
